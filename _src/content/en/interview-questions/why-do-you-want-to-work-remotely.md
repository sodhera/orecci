## What remote employers worry about

When a company hires someone remote, it can't see them work. So the question "Why do you want to work remotely?" is really three questions:

- **Will you get work done without someone watching?**
- **Will you communicate clearly**, mostly in writing, so others aren't left guessing?
- **Will you stay part of the team**, or disappear?

Remote work is often framed around benefits to you: no commute, more time at home, cheaper living. Those are real, but they don't answer the employer's worries. Mention one briefly at most, and spend the rest of the answer on evidence.

## The answer in three parts

1. **Why remote suits your work.** "I do my best deep work with fewer interruptions, and I've found remote lets me plan my day around that."
2. **Proof you're good at it.** Two of these three: time management, written communication, staying connected. Use specific habits and results.
3. **Why this role.** Connect it back. "And this role is mostly focused writing and client calls, which is exactly the work I do well remotely."

## Evidence that persuades

| What they worry about | Evidence you can give |
| --- | --- |
| Getting work done alone | "I plan my week on Monday and share it with my manager. I hit every sprint deadline last year while fully remote." |
| Written communication | "I write a short end-of-day update in our team channel: done, next, blocked. My manager said it meant she rarely needed to check in." |
| Time zones | "My last team was split across three time zones. I moved my day an hour later twice a week so we overlapped." |
| Staying connected | "I booked a 15-minute call with each new teammate in my first month, and I still run our optional Friday coffee chat." |
| Tools | "I've used Slack, Notion and Zoom daily for two years, and I set up our team's shared task board." |
| Home setup | "I have a quiet room, a reliable connection and a backup hotspot." |

If you've never worked remotely, use remote study, freelance work, a distributed volunteer project, or times you worked independently in an office.

## Example answers

These are examples with made-up details. Replace them with your own experience.

### Example answer for someone who has worked remotely

> "I've worked remotely for three years, and it's where I do my most focused work. What makes it work is structure: I plan my week every Monday, post a short daily update, and keep my calendar open for my team during our overlap hours. Last year I led a website migration with people in four countries without ever meeting in person, and we launched a week early. This role is largely independent project work with a few key meetings, which suits how I work best."

### Example answer for someone new to remote work

> "I haven't had a fully remote job yet, but during my final year all my project work was remote, with a team in two cities. I set up our task board and wrote the weekly summary for our supervisor. I found I was more productive without constant interruptions, and I liked that writing things down made decisions clearer. I'd bring those habits, and I'd make an effort to get to know the team in my first weeks since we wouldn't share an office."

### Example answer for a customer-facing remote role

> "I want to work remotely because I do my best customer calls and follow-up writing in a quiet, focused space. In my current hybrid role, I schedule my calls on home days and my customer response times are fastest those days. I keep in close touch with my team through a short daily check-in. I'm also available for occasional in-person meetups if the team holds them."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I hate commuting" | All about you | Mention it in one clause, then talk about the work |
| "I want flexibility to do other things" | Sounds like you'll be distracted | Talk about planning your day around focused work |
| "I don't like offices or people" | Remote still means teamwork | Show how you stay connected |
| No evidence | Anyone can say they're self-motivated | Give one habit and one result |
| Ignoring time zones or availability | Raises practical doubts | Say which hours you can overlap |

## Practice it out loud

Remote interviews usually happen on video, so practice there too. Say your answer to your camera, not the screen, in about 60 seconds. Watch the recording for pace and eye contact. ## Questions people ask

### Is it okay to mention I'd save on commuting?

Yes, briefly, as a side benefit: "and I'd use the time I save on commuting for…" Don't make it the main reason.

### What if I've never worked remotely?

Use any time you worked independently or with people elsewhere: remote study, freelance work, a distributed volunteer team. Then name the habits you'd bring.

### Should I say I'm open to coming into an office?

If you are, yes. Many remote roles have occasional meetups, and saying you'd attend shows you value the team.

### What if the job is hybrid?

Explain what kind of work you'd do on home days versus office days. That shows you've thought about how to use both well.

### How long should my answer be?

About 45 to 60 seconds, with at least one concrete example of working well remotely.
