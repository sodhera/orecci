## Why it's a tricky question

"What is your dream job?" sounds friendly, but there's a risk built in. If your dream job is something completely different, such as running your own café while you're interviewing for an accounting role, the interviewer hears: "I'll leave as soon as I can." If you say "this job is my dream job," it sounds flattering and hard to believe.

What they're really asking is:

- **What do you enjoy?** Which kinds of tasks give you energy?
- **What do you want to achieve?** The impact you want, on people, products or problems.
- **Does this job fit?** Is this role a step toward that, or a detour?

## Describe the work, not the title

The safest and most honest way to answer is to describe the ingredients of your dream job rather than naming one.

| Instead of naming… | Describe… |
| --- | --- |
| "Creative director at a big agency" | "Work where I come up with ideas and see them reach real customers" |
| "Running my own business" | "A role where I own a whole area end to end and see the results of my decisions" |
| "Working at a famous tech company" | "Building products used by millions, with people who are better than me" |
| "Head nurse" | "Caring for patients and helping a team give better care" |
| "Teacher at an international school" | "Teaching in a place where I can try new methods and see students change" |

The right column can be honest and still fit the job you're interviewing for, as long as it's true.

## A three-part answer

1. **The work you love (one or two sentences).** The kind of tasks and setting.
2. **The impact you want (one sentence).** What you want your work to change.
3. **The link to this job (two sentences).** Which parts of this role are already that, and what it would teach you.

About 45 seconds. If you've already answered [where you see yourself in five years](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/where-do-you-see-yourself-in-five-years/), keep the two answers consistent.

## Example answers

These examples use invented details to show the shape.

### Graduate, marketing assistant role

> "My dream job is one where I get to understand why people choose what they buy, and then use that to shape campaigns. I'd love to eventually lead campaigns end to end, from research to results. This role is a great start for that, because you've said the assistant does the customer survey work and helps report on campaign results. That's exactly the part I want to learn properly first."

### Experienced nurse, applying to a community health role

> "My dream job is one where I can follow patients over time rather than seeing them for a few days and never knowing what happened. I want to help people stay well, not just recover. That's why this community role appeals to me so much: I'd be seeing the same patients over months and working on prevention as well as treatment."

### Software engineer

> "My ideal job is building tools that other developers depend on, where reliability matters more than flashy features. I want to work on things that quietly make many people's work easier. Your platform team does exactly that, and the on-call and testing practices you described are the parts I want to get better at."

### Career changer (from retail to HR)

> "My dream job is helping people find the right place to work and then grow there. In retail management, my favourite part was always hiring and training new staff. This HR coordinator role is the most direct way into that work, and it would give me the grounding in employment processes that I don't have yet."

### When your dream is genuinely different

If your real dream is far from this job, you don't have to lie, but choose what to share. Talk about the skills and type of work your dream shares with this role. Someone who dreams of opening a restaurant but is applying for a purchasing role might say:

> "Long term, I'd love to understand every part of how a food business runs. Right now I want to get really good at the supply side, which is why this purchasing role makes sense."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naming a completely unrelated job | Signals you'll leave | Describe the parts that overlap with this role |
| "This is my dream job" | Rarely believable | Say which parts of it match your ideal work |
| Naming a competitor | Awkward, and suggests you'd rather be there | Describe the work, not the company |
| "Not working at all" or a joke about winning the lottery | Avoids the question | Give a real answer, briefly |
| Only talking about pay or perks | Sounds like the job doesn't matter to you | Focus on work and impact |
| Naming the interviewer's job | Can feel like a threat in a small team | Describe growth in your own area |

## How to prepare

1. Write down three moments at work or study when you lost track of time because you were absorbed.
2. Find what they have in common: a type of task, a kind of problem, a kind of person you were helping.
3. Turn that into one sentence: "My dream job is one where I…"
4. Read the job post and find two parts that match.
5. Say the answer out loud. It should take under a minute and sound like you, not like a cover letter.

## Questions people ask

### Can I say my dream job is to start my own business?

You can, but it often worries employers that you'll leave. If it's true, frame it around the skills you want to build here first, and only share it if you're comfortable with the risk.

### Is this the same as 'Where do you see yourself in five years?'

Close. The five-year question is about a time frame; the dream job question is about the kind of work you love. Both should point toward this role.

### What if I'm not sure what my dream job is?

That's normal, especially early in your career. Say what you've enjoyed so far and what you want to find out. "I know I enjoy working with data and explaining it to people. I want to find out whether I'm best in analysis or in client work, and this role has both."

### Should I mention salary in my dream job?

No. Talk about the work and the impact. Salary is a separate conversation.

### How honest should I be?

Fully honest about what you enjoy, and selective about what you share. You don't have to mention a dream that has nothing to do with this job.
