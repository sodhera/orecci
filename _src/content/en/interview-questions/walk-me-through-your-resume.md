## How this differs from "Tell me about yourself"

They overlap, but they're not the same question, and answering one when asked the other is a common slip.

| | Tell me about yourself | Walk me through your resume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order | Present first, then past, then future | Chronological, oldest to newest |
| Coverage | Only the most relevant parts | Every role on the page, briefly |
| What it explains | Who you are and why this job | How each step led to the next |
| Length | 60–90 seconds | 90 seconds to 2 minutes |

The key extra in a resume walkthrough is the **transitions**. The interviewer can read your job titles; what they can't see is why you moved, and whether your career has a direction.

## A timing plan

Spend time in proportion to relevance. Older and less relevant roles get one sentence; your current role gets the most.

| Part | What to cover | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | Education or first relevant job, and why you chose it | 10–15 seconds |
| Earlier roles | One line each: what you did, one result, why you moved | 10–20 seconds each |
| Current or most recent role | What you do, your best result, what you've learned | 30–40 seconds |
| Why this role | How it's the logical next step | 10–15 seconds |

For three or four jobs, that comes to about 90–120 seconds, or roughly 200–260 words. The [speech length calculator](/speaking-coach/tools/speech-length-calculator/) converts your draft's word count into minutes.

## Transition lines that explain your moves

Each move should sound like a choice that made sense. Some lines to adapt:

- "After two years I'd learned the basics, and I wanted to work with bigger clients, so I moved to…"
- "That role was mostly reporting, and I realised I wanted to be closer to the decisions, which is why I took…"
- "The company was acquired, and my team was merged, so I used that moment to move into…"
- "I was promoted to team lead after eighteen months, which is where I first managed people."
- "I wanted to try a smaller company where I'd own more, so I joined…"

## Example walkthrough

Made-up career to show the structure. Use your own resume.

### Marketing manager, four steps

> "I studied business at university and specialised in marketing in my final year, because I liked the mix of numbers and creativity.
>
> "My first job was as a marketing assistant at a regional furniture retailer. I ran the email newsletter and grew the list from about 8,000 to 20,000 in two years. I wanted to work on digital campaigns at a bigger scale, so I moved to an agency.
>
> "At the agency I managed paid social campaigns for six clients, mostly in retail. That's where I learned to run budgets and report results to clients every week. After three years I wanted to go back in-house, where I could see a brand through long-term rather than jumping between clients.
>
> "So for the last four years I've been marketing manager at a home goods brand. I lead a team of three and manage our agencies. The result I'm proudest of is relaunching our website and email programme, which brought online sales from a small share of revenue to about a third.
>
> "This role is the next step: leading a bigger team and owning the whole marketing plan, not just the digital side, in a category I know well."

### Graduate, two steps

> "I did a degree in environmental science, and in my second year I realised the part I enjoyed most was the data work, so I took every statistics module I could. My summer placement was with a water company, where I cleaned and analysed river sampling data and presented findings to the team at the end. For my dissertation I built a model of flood risk for two local towns. I'm applying for this graduate analyst role because it's the same kind of work, real data that drives decisions, at a much larger scale."

## Handling the awkward parts

- **Gaps.** Mention them in their place, in one calm sentence: "I took eight months out to care for a parent, then returned to…" Don't apologise. See [why is there a gap in your resume](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/why-is-there-a-gap-in-your-resume/).
- **Short jobs.** Group them or give one line: "I had two short contracts in 2023, both in customer support, before landing at…" If there are several, see [why have you changed jobs so often](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/why-have-you-changed-jobs-so-often/).
- **Unrelated jobs.** Give them one sentence and pull out a transferable skill: "I spent a year in hospitality, which is where I got comfortable with difficult customers."
- **A long career.** Group your early years: "I spent my first ten years in operations roles at two manufacturers, which gave me…" Then slow down for the last ten.
- **Being let go.** If a role ended in a layoff, say so plainly at that point and move on.

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading duties off the page | They can already read it | One result per role, not a list of tasks |
| Equal time on every job | Old roles drown out recent ones | Weight time toward recent, relevant roles |
| No reasons for moves | Your career looks random | One transition line per move |
| Going past three minutes | They lose the thread | Aim for under two; let them ask for more |
| Ending at your current job | No link to why you're here | Finish with why this role is next |
| Negative reasons for leaving | Sounds bitter | Frame each move as moving toward something |

## How to practise it

Put your resume in front of you and say the walkthrough out loud with a timer running. Most people run long on their first job and rush their current one; fix that ratio first. Then practise without the resume. For the first few tries, it helps to record yourself and check the transitions sound like choices, not accidents. In Speaking Coach's mock interviews you can add your CV, so the interviewer asks about your actual roles and the moves between them.

## Questions people ask

### How long should "Walk me through your resume" take?

Between 90 seconds and two minutes for most people. With a long career, group early roles so you stay under about two and a half minutes.

### Should I start from university or my first job?

Start where your relevant story starts. For graduates and people early in their careers, that's usually university. With ten or more years of experience, start from your first relevant job.

### Do I need to mention every job on my resume?

Mention every role briefly so there are no unexplained jumps, but give most of your time to the recent, relevant ones. Very short or unrelated roles can be grouped into one sentence.

### Is it okay to look at my resume while I answer?

A glance is fine, especially in a video interview. But practise enough that you don't read from it, so you can keep eye contact and explain your moves naturally.

### What if they say "Tell me about yourself" instead?

Give the shorter present-past-future version instead. See [tell me about yourself](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-yourself/) for that structure.
