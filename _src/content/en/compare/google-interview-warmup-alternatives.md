## First: where did Warmup go?

When we checked in October 2026, the old address, grow.google/interview-warmup, redirected to a Grow with Google article called "How to Prepare for an Interview". That article doesn't mention Interview Warmup. For AI practice, it points to **Gemini Live** in the Gemini mobile app, which it says can "simulate a real-time, conversational interview, providing instant, structured feedback on your responses."

If you used Warmup before, you'll remember what Google's launch post described: question sets built around Google Career Certificate fields (such as data analytics, IT support, project management and UX design) plus general questions; answers transcribed in real time; and insights that highlight job-related terms, your most-used words and talking points like experience and skills. Answers weren't graded, and you could repeat them as often as you liked.

That was a gentle, private place to start. It also left gaps. We make Speaking Coach, one of the tools below, so take our view with that in mind.

## Ordered by the next step most people need

**1. Follow-up questions: an AI interviewer that reacts.**
Huru's site says it is free for job seekers and that its live interviewer adapts to your answers and asks follow-ups, with Supportive, Standard or Demanding pressure. Each answer is scored, with a rewritten version. Speaking Coach, which we make, does the same in voice on iPhone and Android: add your CV or the job post, and the interviewer asks about your real experience and follows up on what you said. Gemini Live can do this too if you tell it the role and ask for one question and one follow-up at a time.

**2. Measured delivery: pace, fillers and pauses.**
Warmup's "most-used words" was the nearest it came. For actual numbers, the free Speaking Coach app in ChatGPT reviews a recording in English: measured pace, filler words and pauses, feedback quoting your words and one change to try, two reviews per connection per day. For a quick check with no account, our [free speech review](/speaking-coach/tools/free-speech-review/) runs in your browser.

**3. Your own field.**
If your job wasn't covered, start from the job post. Tools that read it (Huru, Speaking Coach) ask about what the role needs. Or pick your role in our free [interview questions by role](/speaking-coach/tools/interview-questions-by-role/) finder and practice those out loud.

**4. Human pressure.**
A real person changes how you speak. Aced (formerly Exponent) runs free peer mock interviews with monthly credits, mainly for tech, product and finance roles. For other fields, a friend with one rule ("always ask me one follow-up") works.

**5. Structured lessons.**
If you want to learn before you practice, Big Interview offers video lessons and practice questions, from $39 for a month as of October 2026.

## A one-week plan for Warmup graduates

1. **Day 1:** List the 10 questions your role is most likely to ask.
2. **Days 2 to 4:** One AI mock a day with follow-ups. After each, rewrite your weakest answer in three lines.
3. **Day 5:** One measured review of your best answer. Fix the single biggest issue (pace or fillers).
4. **Day 6:** A human mock, peer or friend.
5. **Day 7:** Say your opening answer twice, then rest.

## Questions people ask

### Is Google Interview Warmup still available?

When we checked in October 2026, its old address redirected to a Grow with Google interview tips article that doesn't mention Warmup and suggests Gemini Live for practice. Google may still host it elsewhere, but we couldn't find it.

### What is the best free alternative to Interview Warmup?

For a conversation with follow-ups, Huru says it is free for job seekers. For measured pace and filler feedback, the Speaking Coach app in ChatGPT is free with two reviews a day, in English.

### Can Gemini Live replace Interview Warmup?

Partly. Google's own tips page suggests it for interview practice, and it can ask follow-ups if you prompt it. It doesn't give you Warmup's insight lists or measured pace and filler counts.

### Which alternative works for jobs outside tech?

Tools that read your job post or CV, like Huru and Speaking Coach, work for any role. Our [questions by role](/speaking-coach/tools/interview-questions-by-role/) tool covers nurses, teachers, cashiers, drivers and many more.
