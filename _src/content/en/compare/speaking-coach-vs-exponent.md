## First: Exponent is now called Aced

We make Speaking Coach, so read this comparison with that in mind. When we checked in October 2026, tryexponent.com redirected to aced.io, and the site's header reads "Aced (formerly Exponent)". Everything below about Exponent comes from Aced's own pages.

## What each one covers in a tech interview

A typical product or engineering loop has several parts. These two tools barely overlap.

| Interview part | Aced (Exponent) | Speaking Coach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coding, SQL | Courses, question bank, peer mocks with a shared code editor | Not covered |
| System design | Course and peer mocks | Not covered: it won't judge a design |
| Product sense and strategy cases | PM course, question bank | Can play an interviewer asking you to talk through a case; it won't grade the framework |
| Behavioral ("Tell me about a conflict") | Question bank, an engineering behavioral course | Core use: interviewer reads your CV and follows up on your stories |
| "Tell me about yourself", "Why this company?" | Question bank | Core use: daily spoken reps, feedback on pace and fillers |
| Talking while you think | Peer mocks, where a person watches you solve | Pace, pauses and freezes measured on every answer |
| Human judgment of your level | Paid 60-minute coaching sessions with tech coaches | None |
| Languages | Not stated | 20 languages |

As of October 2026, Aced lists courses for product management, software engineering, system design, data science, machine learning, data engineering, engineering management and more, plus a question database by role and type with "100+ live video answers".

## Where Aced is the right tool

- **You need to learn the content.** No amount of speaking practice will teach you how to design a rate limiter or structure a product-metrics answer. A course and real questions will.
- **You want a human to interview you, for free.** Aced's practice page says you're matched with a peer preparing for similar interviews; each of you interviews the other for about 30 minutes over a live video call. Free users get monthly practice credits.
- **You want an experienced coach.** Aced sells 60-minute coaching sessions with senior tech coaches; its page says most people book three to five.

Limits worth knowing: its practice page says peer mocks don't work on mobile, and transcripts and AI feedback on peer interviews need a paid membership.

## Where Speaking Coach is the right tool

Technical candidates often know their stories and still lose the behavioral round. They tell them out of order, give the background for 90 seconds, or say "we" so often the interviewer can't tell what they did. That's a delivery problem, and you fix it by saying the answer many times to someone who interrupts.

Speaking Coach does that part. Add your CV and the job post, and the interviewer asks about your actual projects, then follows up: "What did *you* decide?", "What would you do differently?" Afterwards you see your pace, filler words and long pauses, and tips quoting your words. You get a 5-session plan for your interview day and a reminder the day before. You can practice any time, on your phone, without booking a partner.

## The final week: using both

| Day | Aced | Speaking Coach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | One peer mock in your weakest technical area | 10 minutes: "Tell me about yourself" until it fits in about 90 seconds |
| 6 | Course videos on that weak area | Your two strongest behavioral stories, Realistic pressure |
| 5 | Questions from the database for your target company | Conflict and failure stories; use **Retry this moment** on the weakest |
| 4 | Second peer mock, or a paid coach session if senior | "Why this company?" and your questions for them |
| 3 | Review notes only | Full mock at Tough pressure |
| 2 | Light review | One run of each core story, no new material |
| 1 | Rest | 5 minutes: your opening answer, once |

For the behavioral part, the [STAR method](/speaking-coach/guides/star-method/) gives each story a shape. For coding rounds, our guide to the [technical interview](/speaking-coach/interviews/technical-interview/) covers thinking out loud.

## Questions people ask

### Is Exponent the same as Aced?

Yes. As of October 2026, tryexponent.com redirects to aced.io and the site calls itself "Aced (formerly Exponent)".

### Are Aced's peer mock interviews free?

Its practice page says free users get practice credits every month, and members get unlimited access. Transcripts and AI feedback need a paid membership.

### Can Speaking Coach help with system design interviews?

Not with the design itself: it won't tell you whether your architecture is right. It can help you practice explaining a design out loud clearly and handling interruptions.

### Which should I use for a product manager interview?

Both, for different parts. Aced for frameworks, cases and real PM questions; Speaking Coach to rehearse your product stories and behavioral answers out loud. Our [product manager interview questions](/speaking-coach/interview-questions-by-role/product-manager/) page lists common ones.

### Do I need paid coaching?

Not always. Free peer mocks plus daily spoken practice cover most needs. A paid human session is worth it near the end for senior roles, where someone who has hired at that level can calibrate you.
