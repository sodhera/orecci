## How a campus drive usually runs

Campus recruitment, common in India and many other countries, brings employers to your college to hire final-year students for the next year. The exact process depends on the company and your college's placement cell, but most drives follow some version of this:

| Round | What it checks | How to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-placement talk | Nothing scored; the company presents itself | Attend, note the role, location, and one fact to use later |
| Aptitude test | Quantitative, logical reasoning, verbal English, sometimes coding | Timed practice daily; speed matters as much as accuracy |
| Group discussion (GD) | Communication, listening, teamwork | Practise with friends on current topics |
| Technical interview | Core subjects, your project, problem solving | Revise four or five core subjects; know your project in depth |
| HR interview | Attitude, goals, fit, flexibility | Prepare spoken answers to common fresher questions |

Some companies skip the GD; some combine technical and HR into one conversation. Ask seniors who sat for the same company last year. Their memory of the rounds is the most useful preparation you can get.

## The aptitude round

This is usually an online test and the biggest filter, since many students apply. It isn't about speaking, but missing it means you never reach the rounds that are. Do one timed section a day for the four weeks before placements, and review every wrong answer.

## The group discussion

Ten or so candidates discuss a topic for 10–20 minutes while assessors watch. It tests whether you can make a clear point, listen and build on others. This round has its own guide: [how to prepare for a group discussion](/speaking-coach/interviews/group-discussion/).

## The technical interview

The interviewer usually starts with what you know best, then pushes until you reach the edge of it. Expect:

- **Your favourite subject.** If you name one, expect detailed questions on it. Choose one you can defend. See [what is your favorite subject](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-is-your-favorite-subject/).
- **Your final-year project.** What problem it solved, your part, the technology, what went wrong. See [tell me about your final year project](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-your-final-year-project/).
- **Core subjects for your branch.** For computer science, often data structures, databases, operating systems, networks and one programming language.
- **A small problem to solve**, on paper or a shared screen, while explaining your thinking.
- **Anything on your resume.** If you list a skill, expect a question on it.

When you don't know, say so: "I haven't studied that in depth. What I do know is…" Bluffing in a technical round is usually caught within one follow-up.

## The HR interview: what freshers are asked

The HR round decides between candidates who passed the technical round. The questions are predictable, which is good news: you can prepare every one.

- Tell me about yourself.
- Why should we hire you?
- What are your strengths and weaknesses?
- Where do you see yourself in five years?
- What are your short-term goals?
- Are you planning higher studies or an MBA?
- Are you willing to relocate?
- Are you comfortable with a service agreement or bond? (Some companies have one; ask what it involves before agreeing.)
- Why do you want to join our company?
- Do you have any questions for us?

The higher studies question catches many freshers. If you are planning a master's in two years, a company may worry you'll leave. Be honest but show commitment to the role first. There's a full answer guide at [are you planning higher studies](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/are-you-planning-higher-studies/).

## Example: "Tell me about yourself" for a fresher

An example with made-up details. Change every fact to your own.

> "I'm Arjun, a final-year B.Tech student in electronics and communication at a college in Pune. Over the last two years I moved towards software: I did an internship at a logistics start-up where I built a small dashboard that tracked delivery delays, and my final-year project is a mobile app that helps farmers check market prices by SMS. I enjoy turning messy data into something simple people can use, which is why your data engineering role interests me. Outside class, I coordinate our college's coding club."

About 90 words, so 40–45 seconds. Present, past, why this role, one personal note. No reciting your marks; they're on your resume.

## A four-week preparation plan

1. **Week 4 before:** Start daily aptitude practice. List core subjects and revise one per two days. Update your resume and check you can explain every line.
2. **Week 3:** Write three-line notes on your project: problem, your part, result. Explain it out loud to a friend from a different branch.
3. **Week 2:** Prepare the HR questions above. Say each answer out loud, timing it to about a minute. Do two practice GDs with friends.
4. **Week 1:** Mock interviews with seniors, a placement cell mentor or friends. Research the companies on the schedule. Keep aptitude practice going.
5. **The day before:** Print copies of your resume, prepare documents the placement cell asks for, and lay out formal clothes.

Many students speak in English in interviews that they otherwise speak in another language. If English is not your first language, slow down rather than speed up when nervous; clear and steady beats fast and fluent-sounding. ## On the day

- Arrive early; placement days run late and schedules shift.
- Keep water and a snack. Some drives last into the evening.
- If you're shortlisted for several companies on one day, tell the placement coordinator straight away so they can arrange the order.
- After each round, write down the questions you were asked. They help you and your juniors.

## Questions people ask

### What are the rounds in a campus placement?

Usually an aptitude test, sometimes a group discussion, a technical interview and an HR interview. The exact rounds depend on the company; seniors and the placement cell can tell you.

### What do HR interviewers ask freshers?

Mostly questions about you: tell me about yourself, strengths and weaknesses, short-term goals, higher studies, relocation, and why this company. Prepare spoken answers to each.

### How do I prepare for a campus technical interview?

Revise four or five core subjects for your branch, know your final-year project in depth, and be ready to explain everything on your resume. Practise solving small problems while talking through your approach.

### How do I answer if my marks are low?

Be brief and honest, then move to what you did well: a project, an internship or a skill you built. See [why is your GPA low](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/why-is-your-gpa-low/) for examples.

### Should I speak in English in a placement interview?

Usually yes, unless the interviewer switches language. Speak slowly and clearly; interviewers are listening for clear communication, not a particular accent.
