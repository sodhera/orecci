## How to use it

1. **Words to minutes:** type the word count of your script. Most writing apps show it in a status bar or under a word count menu. You'll see the speaking time at each pace.
2. **Minutes to words:** type the time you've been given. You'll see the word budget at each pace.
3. Pick the pace that matches how you speak. If you don't know, measure it once with the [pace checker](/speaking-coach/tools/pace-checker/).

## The formula

**Minutes = words ÷ words per minute.** Going the other way, **words = minutes × words per minute.**

A worked example: a 630-word script at 140 words per minute is 630 ÷ 140 = 4.5 minutes. Multiply the part after the decimal point by 60 to get seconds: 0.5 × 60 = 30. So 630 words is 4 minutes 30 seconds of speaking. In reverse, a 4-minute slot at the same pace holds 4 × 140 = 560 words.

The calculator runs this sum at three paces at once, because your pace is the one number you can't be sure of until you've said the speech out loud.

## How long is a speech of 1, 2, 3, 5 or 10 minutes?

Each of these lengths has its own page, with a timing plan and a sample to read against a stopwatch:

- [1-minute speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-1-minute-speech/): 110 to 170 words, about 140. Room for one point.
- [2-minute speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-2-minute-speech/): 220 to 340 words, about 280. One point and one story.
- [3-minute speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-3-minute-speech/): 330 to 510 words, about 420. An opening, two points and a close.
- [5-minute speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-5-minute-speech/): 550 to 850 words, about 700. One message and three short points.
- [10-minute speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-10-minute-speech/): 1,100 to 1,700 words, about 1,400. Sections, signposts and time for slides.

## Which pace to plan for

- **Slow (110):** eulogies, emotional toasts, speaking to a large room, or an audience listening in a second language. Also a safe choice if you know you rush when nervous, because nerves usually speed you up.
- **Average (140):** most presentations, class talks, work updates and interview presentations. Conversational English is commonly cited at about 120 to 160 words per minute, and presentations often land around 130 to 150.
- **Fast (170):** casual, energetic delivery. Rarely the right plan for a speech, but useful as a worst case: if you rush, this is how short it gets.

## Scripts, podcasts, slides and reading aloud

The same sum works for anything spoken. What changes is the column to read.

- **Voice-over or video script.** There is no audience to wait for, so start at average (140). The picture sets the length, so read the script aloud against the video before you record. If viewers also have to read words on screen, plan at slow.
- **Podcast script.** Use average for the scripted parts: the intro, a solo segment, a sponsor read. Unscripted conversation can't be estimated from a word count, so time only what you've written.
- **Presentation with slides.** Plan at slow (110). Each slide change, gesture to a chart or "as you can see here" costs a few seconds that aren't words.
- **Reading a text aloud.** For a reading at a ceremony, a statement or a story for a class, plan at slow. Written sentences are longer than spoken ones, and listeners can't go back and re-read. Mark where you'll breathe.

## Planning tips

- **Leave room.** Write for about 80 to 90 percent of your slot. A best man speech with a 5-minute slot works better at around 550 to 600 words than at 700, because laughs and pauses take time.
- **Read it out loud once with a timer.** The calculator gives an estimate; your own run is the real number. If they differ by more than a minute, trust the run.
- **Cut by section, not by word.** If you're over, drop a whole story or point rather than trimming sentences everywhere.

## Questions people ask

### How many minutes is 500 words?

About 3 minutes 34 seconds at an average speaking pace of 140 words per minute. It ranges from 2:56 at a fast 170 to 4:33 at a slow 110.

### How many words is a 5-minute speech?

About 700 words at 140 words per minute, and between 550 and 850 depending on your pace. For a speech with pauses or laughs, write 560 to 630. The [5-minute speech guide](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-5-minute-speech/) has a timing plan.

### How do you convert words to minutes?

Divide the word count by your speaking pace in words per minute. For example, 1,000 words ÷ 140 is a little over 7 minutes: about 7 minutes 9 seconds.

### How many words per minute is a normal speaking pace?

Conversational English is commonly cited at about 120 to 160 words per minute, and presentations often land around 130 to 150. The calculator's average of 140 sits in the middle of both ranges.

### Why did my speech run longer than the calculator said?

Pauses, audience reactions, ad-libbed lines and slide changes all add time. Nervous speakers also add filler words. Time a full run out loud to see your real length.

### Does it work for languages other than English?

The 110, 140 and 170 paces are based on English. Speakers of other languages fit a different number of words into a minute, so time a run out loud and adjust.
