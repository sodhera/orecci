## Thirty seconds is short

Most elevator rides last between 10 seconds and a minute. That's enough for a greeting, one question, and an answer. You're not trying to start a friendship. You're trying to make a small shared space feel friendly instead of tense.

If you came here for the other kind of elevator talk, a 30-second summary of who you are and what you do, see the [elevator pitch](/speaking-coach/presentations/elevator-pitch/).

## The 30-second shape

1. **Acknowledge them** as they step in: a nod, "Morning," or "Which floor?"
2. **One light line or question,** if the mood suits.
3. **Let them answer** and react briefly.
4. **Close as the doors open:** "Have a good day," "See you later," "Good luck with it."

That's the whole thing. The close matters as much as the opener, because it gives the moment a clean ending.

## What to say, by who's in the lift

These are example lines. Pick by how well you know the person.

| Who | Line |
| --- | --- |
| A colleague you know | "How did the workshop go yesterday?" |
| A colleague you know by sight | "We keep sharing lifts. I'm Lina, from legal." |
| Your manager | "Morning. Ready for the review later?" |
| A senior leader | "Morning. Good to see you in the building today." |
| A stranger in an office building | "Which floor?" then "Busy day?" |
| Someone in a hotel lift | "Are you here for the conference too?" |
| A neighbour in your building | "Hi, I'm in 4B. Have you been here long?" |
| Someone carrying a cake, flowers or boxes | "Someone's having a good day. Is it a birthday?" |

## Lines that fit any short ride

- "Busy day?"
- "Nearly Friday."
- "Is it still raining out?"
- "Going down for lunch? Anywhere good?"
- "That smells amazing." (to someone carrying food)

Each one can be answered in one sentence, which is what you want. If they give a longer answer, follow up once: "Oh, the Thai place? Is it good?"

## How much time you have

Roughly, a short ride gives you one exchange; a longer one gives you two or three.

| Ride | What fits |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 floors | A greeting and a smile |
| 4–10 floors | A greeting and one question |
| Longer, or a slow lift | A question and one follow-up |
| Stuck or delayed | A shared comment: "Well, this is an adventure." Then stay calm and friendly |

If you're in the middle of a sentence when the doors open, wrap up: "Anyway, I'll let you go. Good luck with it!"

## When silence is fine

Not every elevator needs small talk. Silence is normal, and expected, when:

- **The lift is crowded.** Group small talk in a packed lift is rare. A nod is plenty.
- **It's early morning,** and people are still waking up with coffee.
- **Someone's on the phone** or wearing headphones.
- **They're clearly preoccupied:** reading, upset, rushing.
- **The ride is one or two floors.**

In these cases, a nod or a quick "Morning" acknowledges them without asking anything. It's friendlier than looking at your phone and pretending they're not there, and less demanding than a question.

## Talking to your boss or a senior leader in the lift

This is the moment people worry about most. Keep it short and normal.

> "Morning. How's the week treating you?"
> "Good to see you. I enjoyed the all-hands on Monday. The update on the new office was useful."

Don't use 30 seconds to pitch an idea or raise a problem. It puts them on the spot. If you want to follow up, ask: "Could I grab 15 minutes next week about the onboarding project?" Then leave it. See [small talk with your CEO or senior leaders](/speaking-coach/small-talk/talking-to-senior-leaders/).

## What not to do

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting a long story | The doors will cut you off | One line, one question |
| Talking business someone else could overhear | Lifts are not private | Save it for the office |
| Complaining about the company | Anyone could be listening | Keep it neutral |
| Loud phone calls | Everyone hears | Pause the call or keep your voice down |
| Ignoring someone you work with daily | Feels cold | At least a nod and "Morning" |

## If the elevator gets stuck

It's rare, but it happens. Keep calm and friendly, press the alarm or call button, and follow the instructions on the panel. A light line helps everyone: "Well, at least we'll get to know each other." If someone is anxious, talk to them calmly about something ordinary, like where they're headed today.

## Questions people ask

### What do you say in an elevator with a coworker?

A greeting and one easy question: "Morning, busy day?" or ask about something you know they're working on. End with "Have a good one" as the doors open.

### Is it rude not to talk in an elevator?

No. Silence is normal, especially in a crowded lift or early in the morning. A nod or "Morning" is polite enough.

### What do I say to my boss in the elevator?

Keep it short and light: "Morning. How's your week going?" Don't use the ride to pitch an idea or raise a problem.

### How do I end an elevator conversation?

Use the doors as your cue: "This is me. Have a good day." If you're mid-sentence, wrap up with "Anyway, good luck with it."

### What do I say if we're stuck in the lift together?

Stay calm, use the alarm or call button, and keep it light: "Well, this is an adventure." Talk about something ordinary if the other person is nervous.
