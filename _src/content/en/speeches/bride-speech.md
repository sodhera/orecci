## Should the bride give a speech?

There's no rule that she must, and no rule that she can't. Many brides now speak, either alone or in a joint speech with their partner. If you have something you want to say to the people in the room, say it. If you'd rather not, a short "thank you all for coming" when the toasts are done is perfectly fine.

The usual worry is: "What do I say that the groom isn't already saying?" Coordinate. Split the thank-yous, or give one joint speech and each take a part.

## What to include

1. **Thank everyone for coming (15 seconds).** Mention people who travelled far.
2. **Thank the people who made it happen (45 seconds).** Parents, anyone who hosted or paid, the wedding party. By name, but briefly.
3. **A word for your partner's family (15 seconds).** A warm welcome, by name.
4. **Your friends (30 seconds).** One or two lines for your maid of honour or bridesmaids, or for a group of friends.
5. **Your new spouse (45 to 60 seconds).** The heart of the speech. Something specific about them.
6. **A toast (10 seconds).** To your partner, or to everyone in the room.

That's three minutes, or about 400 words. Check with the [speech length calculator](/speaking-coach/tools/speech-length-calculator/).

## Solo or joint speech?

| Option | Works well when | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bride speaks alone | You want to say things in your own voice, and the groom speaks separately | Repeating the same thank-yous he's giving |
| Joint speech | You both want to speak but not for long, or you want one "couple's thank-you" | Clear handovers; decide who says which line |
| Bride speaks at the rehearsal dinner instead | You want a smaller audience | Keep a one-line thank-you for the reception anyway |
| No speech | You'd rather not | Nothing; nobody will mind |

## Example bride's speech (about 3 minutes)

This is a made-up example. Swap in your own names and moments.

> "Hi, everyone. I wasn't going to speak today, and then I realised there was no way I was letting James have the last word on our wedding day.
>
> Thank you all for being here. Auntie May flew from Singapore. The Patels drove up from Cornwall with a newborn. We know what it took for many of you to come, and it means the world.
>
> Mum and Dad, you've given me everything, including this day, and including a tolerance for loud family dinners, which has turned out to be the main qualification for joining the Robinsons. Thank you.
>
> Sue and Peter, thank you for welcoming me so completely. Sue, you sent me a birthday card six months after we started dating. I knew then I was in.
>
> To my girls, Leah, Nadia and Grace: you've held my hand, my hair and, this morning, my bouquet while I cried in a toilet cubicle. I love you.
>
> And James. The first time we met, you told me you were terrible at keeping plants alive. Four years later, our flat looks like a jungle, because you learned, for me. That's who you are. You don't just say things. You learn how to do them. I can't wait to grow everything else with you.
>
> Please raise your glasses to my husband. To James."

## Example: a joint speech (each speaker in turn)

> **Bride:** "We'll keep this short, because we know there's dancing waiting."
>
> **Groom:** "First, thank you all for coming, especially those who travelled from abroad."
>
> **Bride:** "To our parents, thank you for everything, and for not killing each other over the seating plan."
>
> **Groom:** "And to the wedding party, who've been incredible all year."
>
> **Bride:** "Most of all, thank you for being the people who made us who we are."
>
> **Both:** "To all of you."

## Lines for different parts of the speech

These are example lines you can adapt.

**To your parents:** "Dad, you taught me to change a tyre and to never settle. I've only needed one of those so far."

**To your spouse:** "I used to think marriage was about finding the right person. Now I think it's about being the right person, every day. You make me want to."

**To your friends:** "Every important moment in my life, you've been there with a bottle of something and a terrible plan. Today was a good plan."

**If someone is missing:** "Grandad would have loved this. He'd have been the first on the dance floor. We're thinking of him tonight."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A long list of individual thanks | Guests stop listening after six names | Thank by group, then name the few who matter most |
| Repeating the groom's speech | The room hears the same thank-yous twice | Agree who thanks whom |
| Inside jokes with the bridesmaids | Most guests miss them | One line of context, or keep it for later |
| Thanking vendors at length | It's not the audience they care about | Thank them in person or online |
| Skipping the part about your partner | It's the line everyone is waiting for | Make it specific and sincere |

## Delivery on the day

- **Speak early in the reception**, before too much champagne and before the dancing.
- **Use a card with bullet points**, not a full script, unless reading helps your nerves.
- **Pause after the line to your partner.** Let it land.
- **If your voice wobbles,** stop, smile, breathe. See [how to stop your voice shaking](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-stop-voice-shaking/).

Practise out loud a few times in the week before. To practise in front of a listening audience and see your pace and filler words afterwards, try the "Open a presentation or toast" scene in Speaking Coach.

## Questions people ask

### Does the bride have to give a speech?

No. It's a choice. Many brides now speak, either alone or with their partner. A one-line thank-you is also fine.

### When does the bride give her speech?

Usually during the reception speeches, after the father of the bride and before or after the groom. Some couples give one joint speech instead.

### How long should a bride's speech be?

Two to four minutes. If you're also giving a joint speech, keep your solo part to two minutes.

### What should the bride say to the groom in her speech?

Something specific: a moment, a habit, or what you've learned about each other. One true detail is more moving than general praise.

### Who should the bride thank in her speech?

The guests, both families, anyone who hosted or paid, and the wedding party. Thank vendors privately, not in the speech.
