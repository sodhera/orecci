## The four-part structure

| Part | Goal | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | Who you are, said in a way that hints at the story to come | 15–30 seconds |
| One story | The bride at her most herself | 60–90 seconds |
| The couple | What you saw change when they met; a line to the partner | 45–60 seconds |
| The glass | A wish and the toast | 15–20 seconds |

That lands around three minutes, which is the right length for most receptions. The best man often has the longer slot; you don't need to match him.

## How to open

The first 20 seconds decide whether the room settles in. Skip "For those who don't know me…" as a full sentence and get to something with a picture in it. Four openings that work:

| Opening type | Example line |
| --- | --- |
| The first meeting | "I met Priya on our first day of university, when she knocked on my door to ask if I had a spare kettle. Twelve years later, I'm still lending her things." |
| The relationship | "I'm Leah, Anna's younger sister, which means I have 28 years of material and a mother in the second row." |
| The confession | "When Jess asked me to give this speech, she gave me one rule: no stories from Ibiza. So this will be short." |
| The quiet start | "I'm Rosa, and I've been Mia's best friend since we were seven. I want to tell you about one afternoon that says everything about her." |

Pick the one that sounds like you on a normal day. If you are not a joke teller, the quiet start is perfect.

## Choosing the one story

Most maid of honor speeches go wrong by trying to cover the whole friendship: school, university, the bad flat, the trip, the breakup. The room can't hold that many scenes, and it turns into a tour of your shared past.

Instead, pick one story that passes these checks:

- It shows a quality her partner would recognise: loyalty, stubbornness, generosity, chaos.
- A stranger at table 12 can follow it without background.
- It ends with a small moment you can describe in one sentence: what she said, what she did.
- Her parents can hear it.

Tell it in the present tense if that helps you slow down: "So it's midnight, the car is in a ditch, and Anna gets out and starts directing traffic."

## Speaking to the couple, not only the bride

The turn to the partner is what makes the speech feel like a wedding speech instead of a birthday toast. Say what you noticed when they got together, then speak to the partner by name.

> "I knew Tom was different the first time Anna cancelled on me to stay home with him and watch a documentary about bridges. Anna doesn't stay home. Tom, thank you for making her happy in a way that is quieter than any of us expected. You've got a good one. She'll lend you her kettle."

If you're the sister, add the family: "Tom, our family has been waiting for someone who could keep up with her at Christmas quizzes. Welcome."

## Raising the glass

Give the room a clear signal so people know when to stand and drink. Pause, pick up your glass, then:

> "Will you all please stand and raise your glasses. To Anna and Tom."

Say the names slowly. Wait for people to repeat them before you sit down.

## Example maid of honor speech: best friend (about 2 and a half minutes)

A made-up example to show the shape.

> "Hi everyone, I'm Rosa. I've been Mia's best friend since we were seven, and I want to tell you about one afternoon that says everything about her.
>
> We were nineteen, and I'd just failed my driving test for the third time. I called Mia crying. She didn't say 'you'll pass next time.' She showed up with a learner sign, borrowed her dad's car, and made me drive around an empty supermarket car park until it got dark. She brought snacks. She gave me marks out of ten. I passed the next week. That's Mia: she doesn't comfort you, she fixes it, and then she makes it fun.
>
> When she met James, I noticed something new. For the first time, someone was fixing things for Mia. He learned her coffee order in a week. He learned her mum's name before I did. James, you make my best friend feel looked after, and she's spent her whole life looking after everyone else. Thank you.
>
> Will you all stand and raise your glasses. To Mia and James."

## Mistakes that are easy to make

| Mistake | Why it falls flat | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Our friendship has meant so much to me" as the theme | The speech becomes about you | Make the story show her |
| Inside jokes and nicknames | Most guests can't follow | Explain in one line or cut |
| Mentioning exes, even as a joke | The partner's family hears it differently | Leave it out entirely |
| Forgetting the partner | Feels like a toast to the bride alone | One direct line to them |
| Reading a poem or quote at length | Borrowed words feel distant | One short line of your own |

## Getting through it without your voice cracking

Print the speech in 16-point type, double spaced, and mark where you will breathe. If you think you'll cry at a particular line, rehearse that line ten more times than the rest until it stops surprising you. Our guide on [how to stop your voice shaking](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-stop-voice-shaking/) has breathing steps you can do at the table. If you'd like a listener before the day, you can rehearse with Speaking Coach in the app; afterwards it shows your pace, filler words and where you paused for long.

## Questions people ask

### How long should a maid of honor speech be?

Two to four minutes. Shorter is fine if it has a real story; longer than five usually means two stories are fighting for space.

### Can the maid of honor and best man give a joint speech?

Yes, and some couples prefer it. Split it cleanly: one tells a story about each partner, then you share the toast.

### What if I'm not close to the partner?

Ask the bride for one moment when the partner showed who they are, or talk to their best friend. You only need one real detail.

### Should I use notes?

Yes. Cards or one printed page are normal at weddings. Look up at the end of each sentence and for the toast.

### What if I cry during the speech?

Stop, breathe, smile and carry on. Guests are on your side; a pause for emotion at a wedding reads as love, not failure.
