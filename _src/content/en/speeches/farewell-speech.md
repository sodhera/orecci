## Leaving is not retiring

A farewell speech is shorter and lighter than a retirement speech. You're not closing a career, you're closing a chapter, and many people in the room will be in your network for years. The goal is for people to remember you warmly and want to stay in touch. If you are retiring, see [what to say in your own retirement speech](/speaking-coach/speeches/retirement-speech/).

## The structure

| Part | What to say | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | A light line, thank whoever organised the send-off | 10–20 seconds |
| Thanks | The team as a whole, then one or two people with a reason | 40–60 seconds |
| A shared memory | One moment most of the room was part of | 30–45 seconds |
| What you'll take | One thing you learned here | 15–20 seconds |
| Stay in touch | How to reach you, and an invitation | 10–15 seconds |
| Close | A wish for the team | 10 seconds |

That's around two to two and a half minutes. Under three minutes is the target: people are standing, holding drinks, and want to come and say goodbye to you in person.

## Choosing the shared memory

Pick something most people in the room lived through, not a private joke with your closest colleague.

- The launch that went wrong at 6 p.m. on a Friday and the pizza that followed.
- The office move when nobody could find the kettle for a week.
- The client visit that turned into an impromptu team lunch.
- The first time the new system worked.

Tell it in three or four sentences. End on the part that shows the team, not you.

## Example farewell speeches

These are made-up examples. Keep the shape and swap in your own details.

### Leaving for a new job (about 2 minutes)

> "Thanks, Joe, for organising this, and for the cake, which I'm told is not from the vending machine this time.
>
> I've been here four years, and the thing I'll miss most is how this team handles a crisis. I'm thinking of the Henderson launch, when the site went down at six on a Friday and nobody went home. Lena ordered pizza, Ahmed rewrote the checkout page, and Craig kept the client on the phone telling stories about his dog. We fixed it by nine. That night is what I'll tell people about when they ask what a good team looks like.
>
> Sarah, thank you for giving me the chance to lead my first project, and for telling me the truth when it wasn't going well. Ahmed, thank you for answering every question I've asked for four years, even the same one twice.
>
> I'm starting at a smaller company next month, and I'll be stealing several of your habits. My personal email is on the card going round, and I'm on LinkedIn. Please keep in touch, and let me know when the next launch is so I can send pizza. Thank you, everyone."

### Moving cities (about 1 minute)

> "I wasn't planning to leave, but my partner got a job in Manchester and apparently I'm going too. I want you to know this isn't about the work: this is the best team I've worked in. Thank you for teaching me how to run a meeting that ends on time. I'll be back for the summer party, and I expect a seat. Please stay in touch."

### Leaving a small team after a short time (about 45 seconds)

> "I've only been here a year, so I'll keep it short. You took me in when I didn't know anything about logistics, and you were patient every single time I mixed up the depots. Thank you, especially Grace, for the daily check-ins. I'm heading back to study, and I'll miss this place. My number's in the team chat; use it."

## If you're leaving on difficult terms

You don't have to pretend everything was perfect. You do have to keep the speech about the people you're grateful to.

- Thank the colleagues you genuinely valued, by name.
- Skip the reason you're leaving, or give it in a neutral half-sentence ("It's time for something new").
- Never use the speech to settle a score. The right place for honest feedback about the company is an [exit interview](/speaking-coach/interviews/exit-interview/), said calmly to the right person.

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it backfires | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking up the new job | Sounds like you're glad to escape | One sentence, then back to them |
| Explaining in detail why you're leaving | Invites questions and awkwardness | "It's time for something new" |
| Thanking 25 people by name | Someone is always left out | The team, then two or three names |
| Inside jokes | Newer colleagues feel excluded | A memory the whole room shares |
| Going past three minutes | People want to talk to you, not listen | Cut the second memory |

## If your farewell is on a video call

Remote teams often say goodbye at the end of a regular meeting. Keep it to one minute, turn your camera on, and look at the camera lens, not the faces on screen, when you say thank you. Say how to reach you out loud, and post it in the chat as well. Our tips on [eye contact on video calls](/speaking-coach/guides/eye-contact-on-video-calls/) help here.

Say the speech out loud a couple of times before the day. Two minutes feels long until you try it. If you want a quick check of pace and filler words, the [free speech review](/speaking-coach/tools/free-speech-review/) shows both.

## Questions people ask

### How long should a farewell speech be?

Under three minutes, ideally around two. A short, specific speech is remembered better than a long one.

### Do I have to give a speech when I leave?

No, but if the team organises a send-off, a few words are expected. Even 30 seconds of thanks is enough.

### Should I mention my new company?

You can name it in one sentence if people ask. Don't make it part of the speech's message.

### What if I get emotional?

Pause, smile and keep going. Colleagues will take it as a sign that the job mattered to you.

### Should I send a farewell email too?

Yes, if you're leaving a larger organisation. The speech is for the people in the room; the email reaches everyone else and gives your contact details in writing.
