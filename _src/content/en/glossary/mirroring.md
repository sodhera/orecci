## What mirroring means

People often fall into step with each other without trying: leaning in at the same time, speaking at a similar pace. Psychologists call this unconscious copying the chameleon effect. In a 1999 paper with that title, Chartrand and Bargh reported that people unintentionally matched the movements of strangers they worked with, and that being mimicked made the interaction smoother and increased liking.

Mirroring as a conversation skill is doing a light version of that on purpose. It isn't copying. It's adjusting toward the other person so the conversation feels in sync.

## Types of mirroring, with examples

| Type | What you match | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Words | Their key terms | They say "my team"; you say "your team", not "your staff" |
| Verbal mirror | Their last few words, as a question | "It's been a chaotic month." "Chaotic?" |
| Pace | How fast they talk | A slow, thoughtful speaker; you slow down too |
| Energy | Their volume and enthusiasm | They speak quietly about a sad event; you lower your voice, not raise it |
| Posture | How they sit or stand | They lean back and relax; a few seconds later you loosen your posture a little |

## Example: a verbal mirror

Example exchange at a networking event, made up:

> **Them:** "I've just moved over to the data side."

> **You:** "The data side?"

> **Them:** "Yeah, I was in sales for six years, and I kept building spreadsheets nobody asked for, so they finally let me do it full-time."

Two words got a whole story. Follow up on what they share, and use mirrors sparingly: two or three in a conversation, not every reply. Pair them with [active listening](/speaking-coach/glossary/active-listening/) and [paraphrasing](/speaking-coach/glossary/paraphrasing/).

## Example: mirroring pace and energy

Example support call, made up. The customer speaks slowly and flatly.

> **Customer:** "I've been on hold for forty minutes."

> **Mismatched reply (bright and fast):** "No problem at all, let's get that sorted for you!"

> **Mirrored reply (calm, same pace):** "Forty minutes. That's too long. Let's fix this now."

The mirrored reply reuses their words and their pace, so it sounds like someone who heard them.

## Light mirroring vs overdoing it

| What you mirror | Light | Overdone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Words | Reusing one or two of their key terms | Parroting whole sentences, or borrowing slang that isn't yours |
| Verbal mirrors | Two or three in a conversation | An echo on every turn, which sounds like a technique |
| Pace and voice | Speeding up or slowing down a little | Imitating their accent, which can come across as mocking |
| Energy | Matching calm, warmth or enthusiasm | Matching anger, which escalates; stay calm and slightly slower |
| Posture | The same general mood, after a delay | Copying every gesture straight away, which people notice |

Once someone notices the mirroring, it stops building [rapport](/speaking-coach/glossary/rapport/) and starts to feel manipulative.

## How to practise mirroring

1. For the first minute, only listen. Notice their pace, their volume and two or three words they keep using.
2. Match one thing. Pace is the easiest place to start.
3. Use their words for the things that matter to them.
4. Try one verbal mirror: repeat their last one to three key words as a question, then wait.
5. Watch what happens. If they say more, it worked. If they look puzzled, drop it and ask an ordinary question.

There are more ways to draw people out in [how to keep a conversation going](/speaking-coach/small-talk/how-to-keep-a-conversation-going/).

## Questions people ask

### What is mirroring in communication?

It is subtly matching the other person's words, pace, energy or posture so the conversation feels in sync and they feel heard.

### What is an example of mirroring?

Someone says "It's been a chaotic month" and you reply "Chaotic?" Or they speak slowly and quietly, so you slow down and lower your voice too.

### Is mirroring manipulative?

It can feel that way if it's obvious or used to pressure someone. Done lightly, it's a normal part of paying attention.

### What is verbal mirroring?

Repeating the last one to three key words someone said, as a question, to invite them to keep talking.

### Is mirroring the same as paraphrasing?

No. Mirroring repeats a few of their exact words or matches how they speak. Paraphrasing restates their meaning in your own words.

### Does mirroring work on video calls?

Energy, pace and word mirroring work well. Posture matters less on camera.
