## What a call to action is

In a speech or presentation, the call to action (often shortened to CTA) is the ask at the end: the sentence that tells the audience what to do next. In marketing the same phrase means a button or line such as "Sign up". Here it means the spoken version.

It matters most in a [persuasive speech](/speaking-coach/glossary/persuasive-speech/), where it is the whole reason for speaking. It is also the fifth and final step, "Action", of [Monroe's motivated sequence](/speaking-coach/glossary/monroes-motivated-sequence/).

## What makes a call to action work

- **One action.** Three asks at once usually means none get done.
- **Specific.** "Support the project" is vague. "Reply yes to my email by Friday" is not.
- **Doable soon.** Something people can start today or this week.
- **Tied to your main point,** so it feels like the obvious next step.
- **Said last.** If there's a Q&A, repeat the ask after it, so it's the final thing people hear.

## A three-part formula

| Part | Question it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | What exactly should they do? | "Put your name on the volunteer sheet" |
| Time | By when? | "before you leave today" |
| How | Where is it, and how long will it take? | "It's by the door and takes ten seconds." |

Put together: "Before you leave today, put your name on the volunteer sheet. It's by the door and takes ten seconds."

## Call to action examples for different talks

Made-up examples:

| Talk | Call to action |
| --- | --- |
| Pitch to managers | "Give us four weeks and one designer to run the pilot. I'll report back on the 30th." |
| Team update | "I need a yes or no on the new vendor by Thursday at noon." |
| Fundraiser | "There's a card on your table. Fill it in before dessert." |
| Class presentation | "Next time you buy a phone, ask the shop how many years of updates it gets." |
| Wedding toast | "Please raise your glasses to Sam and Priya." |
| Training session | "Tomorrow, use the checklist on one customer call and tell me what didn't fit." |
| School election speech | "Voting opens at lunch on Friday. Tick the box next to Maya Lee." |
| Community meeting | "The petition is on the table at the back. Sign it on your way out tonight." |

A toast is a good reminder that almost every talk has a call to action, even if it's only "raise your glass".

## Weak versus strong

| Weak | Stronger |
| --- | --- |
| "Think about it." | "Pick one meeting this week to cancel, and see if anyone notices." |
| "Any questions?" as the last line | Take questions, then close with your ask. |
| "We should all do better on this." | "Starting Monday, every ticket gets an owner within an hour." |
| "Let me know what you think." | "Email me your biggest objection by Friday." |
| "Please support us." | "Scan the code on the screen now and choose a monthly amount." |

## How to write yours

1. Finish this sentence: "After my talk, I want them to…"
2. Cut it down to one action, with a time and a place.
3. Say it out loud. If it takes more than two sentences, cut again.
4. Put it last. Pause before it, say it slowly, then stop.

The call to action is one part of the close. For the summary before it and the final line after it, see [how to end a presentation](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-end-a-presentation/).

## Questions people ask

### What is a call to action in a speech?

It is the sentence near the end that tells the audience exactly what to do next, such as sign, vote, approve or try something.

### What is an example of a call to action in a persuasive speech?

"Voting opens at lunch on Friday. Tick the box next to Maya Lee." It names one action and says when. The [persuasive speech guide](/speaking-coach/speeches/persuasive-speech/) shows where it fits in the outline.

### Where does the call to action go in a speech?

At the end, after your strongest point. You can preview it early ("By the end I'll ask you for one thing") so people listen for it.

### How long should a call to action be?

One or two sentences.

### Does every speech need a call to action?

Persuasive talks and pitches do. Informative talks can end with a takeaway instead, though a small next step ("try this tonight") still helps.

### Is a call to action pushy?

Not if it fits what you've just argued. Leaving people unsure what to do is more frustrating than asking clearly.
