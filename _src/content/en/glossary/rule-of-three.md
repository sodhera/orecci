## What the rule of three means

The rule of three says: when you can choose how many, choose three. It works at three sizes:

- **Three words:** "faster, cheaper and safer."
- **Three phrases or sentences:** "We listened. We changed the plan. We shipped on time."
- **Three main points:** the body of a whole talk.

## Why three works

- **Three is the smallest number that makes a pattern.** One item is a point. Two is a pair or a contrast. The third confirms the pattern, or breaks it for a laugh.
- **Listeners can't reread.** They hold on to what you said by its shape. Three short items fit in one breath; a list of seven blurs.
- **It has a built-in ending.** The beat of one, two, three tells people the list is finished, which is why it suits closing lines.
- **The last place carries the weight.** People hear the third item as the conclusion, so put your strongest there.

## Famous examples of the rule of three

| Phrase | Who and when |
| --- | --- |
| "Veni, vidi, vici" ("I came, I saw, I conquered") | Attributed to Julius Caesar, around 47 BC |
| "Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness" | The US Declaration of Independence, 1776 |
| "government of the people, by the people, for the people" | Abraham Lincoln, the Gettysburg Address, 1863 |

Look at what they share: the three parts match in form, and the weightiest comes last.

## Example lines for your own talk

These are made-up examples of the pattern for different occasions.

| Occasion | Example line |
| --- | --- |
| Wedding toast | "Sam is loyal, Sam is funny, and Sam is, finally, on time." |
| Team update | "This week we fixed the login bug, cleared the backlog and hired our new designer." |
| Sales pitch | "It's quicker to set up, cheaper to run and easier to train people on." |
| Interview answer | "I'd bring three things: process, patience and a lot of spreadsheets." |
| Eulogy | "She taught us to cook, to argue fairly and to forgive." |
| Presentation close | "Start small, measure weekly, and share what you learn." |

Notice the trick in the toast: the first two items set a pattern and the third bends it for a laugh.

## How to build a line of three

1. Write down everything you could say on the point.
2. Pick the three strongest. If you only have two, use two.
3. Make them match: three verbs, three nouns or three short clauses.
4. Put the shortest first and the strongest last.
5. Say it slowly, with a short [pause](/speaking-coach/glossary/pause/) before the third.

For a whole talk, use three main points in a 5- to 10-minute slot and announce them with [signposting](/speaking-coach/glossary/signposting/): "I'll cover three things." A final line in three short parts is also a reliable way to [end a presentation](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-end-a-presentation/).

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventing a weak third item | The filler point is the one people remember | Use two strong points |
| Every sentence in threes | It starts to sound like a sales script | Save it for key moments |
| Three long clauses | The rhythm disappears | Keep each item to a few words |

## Questions people ask

### What is the rule of three in simple terms?

Say things in threes. Three points, three examples or three words are easy to follow and sound complete.

### What is an example of the rule of three?

"Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness", from the US Declaration of Independence. An everyday one: "It's quicker to set up, cheaper to run and easier to learn."

### Why does the rule of three work?

Three is the smallest number that forms a pattern. A list of three is short enough to follow by ear and long enough to sound complete.

### What is a tricolon?

The rhetorical name for three parallel words or phrases in a row, such as "quick, clear and kind."

### Do I always need three main points?

No. If you have two strong points, use two. The rule is a tool, not a requirement.

### Can I use the rule of three in interviews?

Yes. "I'd say three things" helps you and the interviewer keep track. Back each with an example.
