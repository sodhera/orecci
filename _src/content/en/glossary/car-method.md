## What CAR stands for

| Part | What it answers | Length | Lines that start it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Challenge** | What was the problem, and why did it matter? | One or two sentences | "The problem was…" / "We were losing…" |
| **Action** | What did *you* do, step by step? | Most of the answer | "So I…" / "First I… then I…" |
| **Result** | What changed, ideally with a number? | One or two sentences | "Within a month…" / "As a result…" |

The interviewer's question is usually a [behavioral one](/speaking-coach/glossary/behavioral-interview/): "Tell me about a time…" or "Give me an example of…".

## Example CAR answers

Made-up details. Each runs about 30 to 45 seconds out loud.

### "Tell me about a problem you solved" (clinic receptionist)

> **Challenge:** "Our clinic's phone line had a 20-minute wait on Monday mornings, so patients gave up and walked in instead, which jammed reception."
>
> **Action:** "I pulled three weeks of call logs and saw most Monday calls were repeat prescription requests. I set up a separate voicemail option for those and trained the two receptionists to clear it by noon."
>
> **Result:** "Within a month the Monday wait was under five minutes."

### "Tell me about a time you worked under pressure" (café supervisor)

> **Challenge:** "Two of our five staff called in sick on the Saturday of a street festival, our busiest day of the year."
>
> **Action:** "I cut the menu to the six best sellers, moved myself to the till, and asked a regular part-timer to come in for the lunch rush."
>
> **Result:** "We served more customers than the same day last year, and the shortened menu became our festival menu."

### "Give an example of teamwork" (graduate)

> **Challenge:** "Our four-person marketing project had two weeks left and no agreed idea."
>
> **Action:** "I suggested we each pitch one idea in two minutes and vote. Then I split the work by strength: I took the survey, since I'd done statistics."
>
> **Result:** "We handed in two days early and got the top mark in our year."

## Before and after: turning a vague answer into CAR

> **Before:** "I'm really good at solving problems. At my last job there were lots of issues with the system, and I helped fix a lot of them, and people were happy."
>
> **After:** "Our order system crashed every Friday afternoon, and sales lost about two hours each time. I traced it to a weekly report that ran at 3 p.m., moved it to 2 a.m., and set an alert. It hasn't crashed on a Friday since."

The "before" has no problem anyone can picture, no steps and no outcome. The "after" has all three in under 50 words.

## CAR vs. STAR vs. STARR

| | CAR | [STAR](/speaking-coach/glossary/star-method/) | [STARR](/speaking-coach/glossary/starr-method/) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parts | Challenge, Action, Result | Situation, Task, Action, Result | STAR + Reflection |
| Typical length | 30–60 seconds | 1–2 minutes | 1–2 minutes |
| Best for | Phone screens, follow-ups, quick rounds, resume bullets | Most behavioral questions | Mistakes, failure, conflict |
| Risk | Too thin if the context is unusual | Too long if the setup runs on | Reflection sounds rehearsed |

Use STAR when the situation needs explaining, such as a technical system or a regulated industry. Use CAR when the problem is easy to picture. For the same story told both ways, see the [CAR method guide](/speaking-coach/guides/car-method/).

## How to practice

1. Write five stories: a problem solved, a deadline, a conflict, a mistake, something you led.
2. Give each one Challenge line, two or three Action verbs ("I traced… I moved… I set up…") and one Result line.
3. Say each out loud and time it. Over 60 seconds? Cut the Challenge, not the Result.
4. Ask someone to follow up with "What exactly did you do?" If you can answer in detail, the Action is real.

## Questions people ask

### What does CAR stand for?

Challenge, Action, Result. Some versions use Context instead of Challenge; the idea is the same.

### What is the CAR method in an interview?

A three-part way to answer "Tell me about a time…" questions: the problem, what you did, and what changed.

### Is CAR better than STAR?

Neither is better. CAR is shorter; STAR gives more room for context. Use whichever keeps your answer clear.

### How long should a CAR answer be?

About 30 to 60 seconds. Let the interviewer ask for more detail.

### What if my result has no number?

Use a concrete change instead: what people said, what stopped happening, or what the team did differently afterwards. Don't invent a number.
