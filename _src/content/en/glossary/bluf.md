## What BLUF means

The bottom line is whatever the listener most needs: the decision, the answer, the request or the headline. BLUF means it comes first, and background comes after, only as much as they need. The opposite is the chronological story, which saves the point for the end.

## Where BLUF comes from

The phrase comes from US Army correspondence guidance, Army Regulation 25-50, which asks for writing that is concise and puts the main point at the beginning. From military writing it spread to business, and it works even better spoken: a listener can't skip ahead to find your point.

## BLUF examples, before and after

All four are examples with made-up details.

### In an email

**Before:**

> "Hi Priya, hope you're well. As you know, we've been reviewing our suppliers this quarter. After several calls and a look at last year's costs, we think there may be a case for switching. Would you have a view?"

**After:**

> "Hi Priya, I need your approval by Friday to switch our packaging supplier to Northway. It saves about 8% a year and delivery times stay the same. The comparison is below."

### In a work update

**Before:**

> "So on Monday we started testing, and Ana found a problem with the payment page on older phones, and it turns out it's the vendor's library, so they're patching it Thursday, so we'll probably need to move the launch."

**After:**

> "Bottom line: launch moves from the 3rd to the 10th, and I need your OK to tell the client today. The reason is a payment bug on older phones; the vendor will patch it Thursday. Everything else is on track."

### In an interview answer

Asked "Have you managed a budget?":

**Before:**

> "Well, in my last role the team was small at first, and finance handled most things, but then we grew, so by the second year I was more involved in the planning side."

**After:**

> "Yes. For two years I ran a marketing budget of £120,000 and came in under it both years. I can walk you through how I planned it."

### In a presentation

**Before:**

> "Today I'll take you through our method, then the survey results, then some themes, and at the end, a recommendation."

**After:**

> "My recommendation is to close the Saturday phone line and move those staff to live chat. In the next ten minutes I'll show you the three findings behind it."

In every "after", the listener could stop you after one sentence and still have what they need.

## A simple BLUF format

1. **Bottom line:** the answer, decision or request in one sentence.
2. **Why:** one or two sentences of reason.
3. **What's next or what I need:** a date, an owner or an approval.

For meetings, see [how to give a clear team update](/speaking-coach/presentations/team-meeting-update/). If you tend to tell the whole story first, the guide on [how to stop rambling](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-stop-rambling/) has drills.

## BLUF vs. PREP

| Method | Stands for | What it gives you | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BLUF | Bottom line up front | One rule: the main point goes first | Emails, updates, requests, briefings |
| [PREP](/speaking-coach/glossary/prep-method/) | Point, Reason, Example, Point | The four parts of a whole short answer | Opinion questions, impromptu answers |

They work together: a PREP answer opens with its point, so it is already BLUF. For longer reports and decks, the [pyramid principle](/speaking-coach/glossary/pyramid-principle/) adds a structure for the supporting points.

## Questions people ask

### What does BLUF stand for?

Bottom line up front.

### What does BLUF mean in an email?

That the first line states the point or the action you need, before any background. Some teams write "BLUF:" as the first word. If your readers don't know the term, put the point first without the label.

### How do you write a BLUF statement?

Ask what you would say if you had ten seconds. Write that sentence first: the decision, answer or request, with a date if there is one.

### Is BLUF a military term?

Yes. It comes from US Army writing guidance and is now widely used in business too.

### Isn't BLUF rude?

Not if the tone is warm. Getting to the point respects people's time. Add one friendly line if the relationship needs it.

### When shouldn't I use BLUF?

When the listener needs context before the news can land, such as very bad personal news. Even then, don't delay long.
