## Questions you'll usually hear

| Question | What the recruiter is checking |
| --- | --- |
| "[Tell me about yourself.](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-yourself/)" | Can you summarise your background clearly in a minute or two? |
| "Why are you interested in this role?" | Did you read the job post, and is your interest real? |
| "Walk me through your current job." | Does your experience match the must-haves? |
| "[What are your salary expectations?](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-are-your-salary-expectations/)" | Are you in the budget range? |
| "When could you start?" | Notice period and timing |
| "Are you interviewing elsewhere?" | How fast they need to move |
| "Do you have any questions for me?" | Interest, and whether you've thought about the next steps |

A phone screen rarely goes deep. The recruiter is usually filling in a short checklist for the hiring manager, so clear, direct answers matter more than detail.

## How speaking on the phone differs

- **No visual cues.** You can't see them nodding or wanting to cut in, so keep answers to about 60 to 90 seconds and stop clearly.
- **Silence feels longer.** If you need to think, say so: "Good question. Let me think for a second."
- **Your voice carries everything.** Standing up and smiling make you sound more awake and warmer.
- **Interruptions are messier.** If you both talk at once, stop and say "Sorry, go ahead."
- **Notes are allowed.** Keep the job post, your key examples and your salary range in front of you, but don't read from them.
- **Sound quality matters.** Find a quiet room and test your headphones beforehand.

More preparation, including a checklist for the hour before, is in [how to prepare for a phone interview](/speaking-coach/interviews/phone-interview/).

## Questions people ask

### How long does a phone screen last?

Usually 15 to 30 minutes. Some run shorter if the recruiter already has what they need.

### Is a phone screen a real interview?

Yes. It's often the first cut, so treat it seriously even if it feels casual.

### What should I ask at the end of a phone screen?

Ask about the next steps and timeline, and one question about the team or role.

### Do I talk about salary on a phone screen?

Often, yes. Have a researched range ready, and know how you'll answer if asked first.
