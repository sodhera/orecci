## Free, free for a while, or free to download

"Free" in app stores usually means free to install. We sorted every tool below into three honest groups after opening its own site or App Store page in November 2026:

- **Free to use, with a daily or no limit.** You can practice every week without paying.
- **Free for a few sessions.** Enough to try it, not to build a habit.
- **Free to download.** The useful parts are paid, or the store page doesn't say what's free.

We make Speaking Coach. Its phone app is paid, with no free tier; two of the free tools below are ours, and we say so where they appear.

## Free to use, week after week

**PowerPoint Speaker Coach.** Microsoft's support page says it is in free preview for everyone, even without an Office subscription, on Windows, Mac, the web, iPhone, iPad and Android. It flags pace, pitch, filler words, informal speech and reading your slides aloud, then gives a report. English only, and only when your Microsoft 365 language is English. The catch: it is built around a slide deck, so it's less useful for a toast or an interview answer.

**The free voice review on this site (ours).** You answer out loud for up to 2 minutes, or 3 for a speech, on our [free speech review](/speaking-coach/tools/free-speech-review/) page or on the English interview, speech, presentation and small-talk pages. You get your measured pace, filler words and long pauses, plus feedback that quotes your own words: one thing to keep, one change, and one sentence rewritten more clearly. Two free reviews per network per day, English only, no account and no install. Audio goes to Speaking Coach's server for transcription and isn't kept.

**Speaking Coach in ChatGPT (ours).** The same kind of review inside ChatGPT: record or upload your voice, get measured pace, fillers and pauses with one change to try, then try again and compare. English, two reviews per network per day, subject to shared capacity. See [Speaking Coach in ChatGPT](/speaking-coach/chatgpt/).

**Huru.** If your speaking goal is a job interview, this is the most generous free tool we found. Its site says "Every feature is free for job seekers": a live AI interviewer that asks follow-ups, three pressure levels and sessions of 5 to 20 minutes, in five languages. It has an iOS app and a web app; we saw no native Android app listed.

**The browser tools here.** The [pace checker](/speaking-coach/tools/pace-checker/) and [filler word counter](/speaking-coach/tools/filler-word-counter/) run in your browser using its own speech recognition. They measure, they don't coach, and they have no limit.

**Toastmasters.** Its joining page says "Visiting is completely free", in person or online. Joining costs $72 every six months plus a $25 new member fee, so it moves to paid once you commit.

## Free for a few sessions

**Yoodli.** The Starter plan gives 5 lifetime roleplay sessions; Pro is $8 a month billed annually. Good for trying an AI audience on the web, not for daily practice.

**Interviews by AI.** The Basic plan is $0 with three practice questions a month, answered by audio or text, with instant AI feedback. Pro is $9 a month for unlimited questions and resume upload.

**Bodyswaps.** The home page offers a free trial of its AI roleplays, which run in a browser with no download. Prices aren't listed.

## Free to download

**Speeko** lists free speaking exercises and tools; Pro adds lessons and roleplays. **Orai** and **Stimuler** are free to install with in-app purchases; Stimuler's page says its core app is free, and Orai's doesn't say which features are free. All three are English only on the App Store.

## A free practice routine that works

You don't need a paid app to improve. This plan uses only free tools:

1. **Monday:** Record a 2-minute talk on your phone's voice recorder and listen once. Our guide to [recording yourself](/speaking-coach/guides/record-yourself-speaking/) says what to listen for.
2. **Tuesday:** Run it through the free voice review. Write down the one change it suggests.
3. **Wednesday:** Practice only that change, five short runs, with the pace checker open.
4. **Thursday:** Second voice review. Compare the numbers and the quoted sentence with Tuesday's.
5. **Weekend:** Give the talk to one real person, or visit a Toastmasters club.

## When paying is worth it

Free tools listen; very few talk back. If you freeze when someone asks a follow-up question, interrupts or pushes back, you need practice with a partner who plays that person. Speaking Coach's phone app does that for interviews, presentations and small talk in 20 languages, as a subscription; see the App Store or Google Play for the price in your country. ## Questions people ask

### Is there a completely free public speaking app?

Yes, a few. As of November 2026, PowerPoint Speaker Coach is free in preview, Huru is free for job seekers, and the free voice review on this site gives two reviews a day with no account. Most others are free to download with paid features.

### Is Orai free?

Orai is free to download, but its App Store page doesn't say which features are free. Paid options listed in November 2026 were $12.99 a month, $49.99 a year or $99.99 lifetime.

### Is Yoodli free?

Partly. Its pricing page lists a free Starter plan with 5 lifetime roleplay sessions; after that you need Pro or Advanced, both billed annually.

### Which free app gives feedback on what I said, not just my pace?

The free voice review on this site and Speaking Coach in ChatGPT quote your own words and suggest one change, in English. Speaker Coach flags phrases like informal speech and reading slides, but doesn't rewrite your sentences.

### Is the free voice review private?

Your audio goes to Speaking Coach's server to be turned into text and isn't kept. The pace checker and filler word counter on this site use your browser's own speech recognition instead.
