## What the foreman wants to know

Construction interviews are often five or ten minutes, sometimes on site. The foreman or superintendent is checking four things: will you work safely, will you listen, can you handle the physical work, and will you show up. They don't need long stories. Short, honest answers with one example each are best.

## Site safety

Be specific. "I'm always safe" isn't an answer; naming your gear and a real moment is.

Example answer:

> "Hard hat, safety glasses, high-vis, steel toes and gloves every day, plus hearing protection near saws and a harness when I'm working at height. On my last site I noticed a guardrail missing on the second floor after a delivery. I told the foreman straight away and put up tape until it was fixed. Nobody was hurt, and that's the point."

If you have safety training, say so. OSHA's Outreach Training Program gives a 10-hour course card for general hazard awareness and a 30-hour course for supervisors and workers with safety responsibilities. OSHA says the program is voluntary, though some states, cities and employers require it. Don't claim a card you don't have; if you're planning to get one, say when.

## Following a foreman

They want someone who listens, asks when unsure and doesn't argue in front of the crew, but who will still say something about a real hazard.

| Situation | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| You're told to redo work | "No problem. Can you show me what you want different, so I get it right this time?" |
| You don't understand an instruction | "Just to check: you want the forms stripped on this side first?" |
| You think there's a faster way | Do it their way, then suggest it privately at a break |
| You think something's unsafe | Stop, tell the foreman why, and don't do it until it's made safe |

> "If my foreman told me to do something I thought was unsafe, like working near an open edge with no protection, I'd stop and tell him why. Most of the time there's a fix, like a harness or a guardrail. I'd never just do it to avoid looking slow."

## Physical work in bad weather

Be honest. If you've done the work, say where and for how long.

> "I spent two summers on a roofing crew and last winter on a concrete crew, so I've worked in 95-degree heat and on freezing mornings. In heat I drink water all day and take breaks in the shade when we can. In cold I dress in layers and keep dry gloves in my bag. I know my limits, and I'd tell the foreman if someone on the crew looked like they were struggling with the heat."

## Reliability

For laborers, showing up on time every day may matter more than anything else you say. Back it up.

> "I haven't missed a day without notice in two years. I live 20 minutes from this site, I have my own truck, and I leave early so traffic doesn't make me late. If I'm ever sick, I'd call the foreman before the shift, not text halfway through."

If transport is a concern, have a plan ready: a carpool, a bus route, a backup.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Saying you'd ignore PPE in the heat because it's uncomfortable.
- Arguing with the foreman in your example stories.
- Overstating what equipment you can operate. They'll ask you to show them.
- Vague answers about attendance, or no plan to get to site.

Even in a short interview, saying your answers out loud once beforehand helps. You can use the [interview answer review](/speaking-coach/tools/interview-answer-review/) to hear how you sound and cut the "ums". If you're applying through an [apprenticeship program](/speaking-coach/interview-questions-by-role/apprentice/), expect longer questions about why you want the trade.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need experience to get a construction laborer job?

Not always. Many crews hire people with no site experience who are reliable and willing to learn. Talk about physical work you've done, like landscaping, warehouse work or moving, and any safety training.

### What should I bring to a construction interview?

ID, a list of past jobs with dates, any certifications or cards, and your own basic PPE if you have it. Dress for a site visit, with sturdy boots.

### How do I answer "what tools can you use"?

List the hand and power tools you're comfortable with, and say which ones you've only used a few times. Be honest. They may ask you to use one.

### What if I've been fired from a site before?

Be brief and honest. Say what happened, what you learned and what you do differently now. See [have you ever been fired](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/have-you-ever-been-fired/) for how to phrase it.
