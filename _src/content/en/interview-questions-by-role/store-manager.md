## What the interviewer is listening for

A store manager runs a small business with someone else's money. District and regional managers want to hear that you know your numbers, that you act on them early, and that your team still shows up for you on a bad week. The strongest answers sound like a Monday morning conversation about last week's results.

## Hitting sales targets

When they ask about a store behind plan, don't say "motivate the team". Break sales into the parts you can move, pick the weak one, and say what you did about it.

| Lever | What it means | An action you can name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traffic | People coming in | Window and entrance changes, local events, staff on the floor at peak |
| Conversion | Share of visitors who buy | Greeting every customer, zoning staff by department, fitting-room checks |
| Average transaction | Spend per sale | Add-on training, bundles at the till, daily goals per person |

Example answer for a manager who has run a store:

> "We were 9% behind plan at week six. Traffic was flat, so the gap was conversion. I moved two of my strongest sellers from the stockroom to the floor between 12 and 6, started a five-minute huddle with one add-on item of the day, and posted daily progress in the break room. We closed the quarter 2% under plan instead of 9, and conversion stayed higher into the next quarter."

## Staffing and scheduling

They want to see that you schedule to traffic, not to habit, and that you protect the hours that make money.

1. Start from the hour-by-hour traffic or sales data for the last four weeks.
2. Put your best sellers on the peaks, and tasks like freight and replenishment on the quiet hours.
3. Keep a short list of people who want extra shifts, for call-outs.
4. Post the schedule early so people can plan their lives. It cuts call-outs more than any rule.

> "When our hours were cut by 40 a week, I took them from the first two hours after opening, where we averaged under ten customers, and moved freight to that window. Our peak coverage stayed the same and sales held."

## Shrink

Shrink covers theft by customers, theft by staff, and paperwork and process errors. Interviewers notice when candidates only talk about shoplifters. Show you looked at all three.

> "Our shrink was well above the district average. The count showed most of it in two categories near the entrance, so we moved them behind the counter. We also found unrecorded damages, so I set up a daily damage log signed off by a key holder. At the next count we were back in line with the district."

Follow your company's policy on approaching suspected shoplifters. Many retailers don't allow staff to chase or detain anyone, and saying "I'd tackle them" is a quick way to lose the room.

## A store visit from district leadership

This tests whether your store is ready every day or only when someone is watching. A good answer is mostly about routine.

> "I run the store as if the visit is tomorrow, so the night before is a walk-through, not a rescue. I check the priority displays against the plan, fix the fitting rooms and stock gaps, and make sure each shift lead can answer two questions: how are we doing against plan today, and what are we working on. During the visit I take notes, I don't argue with feedback, and I send a short follow-up with what we'll fix and by when."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No numbers at all | Store managers are judged on numbers | Have sales, payroll and shrink figures ready |
| "My team loves me" | Unprovable, and not the job | Show one person you developed and where they went |
| Blaming the district or the weather | Sounds like you don't own results | Name what you changed within your control |
| Describing tasks, not outcomes | Lists of duties don't separate you | Each story ends with a result |

Before the interview, say your turnaround story out loud and keep it near 90 seconds. The [interview answer review](/speaking-coach/tools/interview-answer-review/) shows your pace and filler words. ## Questions people ask

### What numbers should I memorise before a store manager interview?

Sales against plan, conversion if your store tracks it, average transaction, payroll as a share of sales, and your last shrink result. Know the period each one covers.

### How do I answer if I've only been an assistant manager?

Use the times you ran the store alone: closing weeks, holiday cover, a period between managers. Say what you decided, not just what you carried out.

### Will they ask me to do a store walk?

Some retailers do. Walk the floor the way you would on your own shift: name what's working, the three things you'd fix first, and why.

### How do I talk about firing someone?

Briefly and respectfully. Say what the issue was, how you documented and coached first, and that you followed policy. Never criticise the person.
