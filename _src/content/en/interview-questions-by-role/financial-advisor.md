## What the hiring manager is listening for

A branch manager or a partner at an advisory firm is asking two questions behind every interview question: can you bring in clients, and can those clients trust you? Advisors who can sell but don't put clients first are a risk. Advisors who are trustworthy but can't build a book don't last. You need to show both.

Interviewers also want to hear how you talk to clients. Your tone on a hard call matters as much as your technical knowledge.

## Building a client book

Give a plan with a niche, sources and numbers. "Everyone with money" is not a niche.

| Part of the plan | Example to adapt |
| --- | --- |
| Niche | "Nurses and teachers in their 40s planning for retirement, because I understand their pensions." |
| Sources | Warm contacts, workplace seminars, accountants and estate lawyers, existing firm clients without a plan |
| Activity | "Ten new conversations a week, two seminars a month." |
| Tracking | Conversations, first meetings, plans delivered, clients signed |
| Timeline | What you'd expect in months 1–3, 4–12, and year 2 |

> "I'd start with the people I know through my old job in hospital administration, and run a lunchtime session on pensions at two hospitals. I'd track every first meeting so I know which source leads to signed clients, and drop what doesn't work by month four."

## A client panicking in a downturn

This is often asked as a role play. The best answers listen first, reconnect the client to their plan, and avoid both dismissing and agreeing with the panic.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "First I'd let them talk and say back what I'm hearing: 'You're worried this keeps falling and you won't have enough to retire on.' Then I'd bring them back to the plan: 'When we built this, we planned for drops like this one. Your next five years of income are in cash and bonds, so nothing you need soon is being sold at these prices.' I'd ask what would make them feel safer, and if a small change fits the plan, we'd talk about it. I wouldn't make a big decision on the call, and I'd book a meeting for that week."

Don't promise the market will recover by a date. Don't tell them they're being irrational.

## Putting the client first

Many firms will ask what fiduciary duty or acting in the client's best interest means to you. The exact legal standard depends on your country, license and firm, so explain it in practical terms:

- Recommend what fits the client's goals, time frame and risk, even if it pays you less.
- Be clear about how you're paid and about any conflicts.
- Document why a recommendation suits the client.
- Say no when a client wants something unsuitable, and explain why.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "A client wanted to move her whole savings into an annuity a friend had bought. It would have paid me a good commission, but she was 45 with high-interest debt. I recommended clearing the debt and building an emergency fund first, and we looked at the annuity again a year later. She's referred two colleagues to me since."

## Rejection

> "In my first year in sales, I booked about one meeting for every fifteen calls. I stopped measuring days by sales and started measuring them by conversations. It kept me going on bad weeks, and I noticed which openings worked better."

If you haven't sold before, use any target-driven work, or talk about how you'd structure your days. The [stress and pressure](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/how-do-you-handle-stress-and-pressure/) page has more on this type of question.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Talking only about markets.** Clients hire advisors for their judgement and calm, not for stock tips.
- **No prospecting plan.** "I'm good with people" isn't a plan.
- **Promising returns,** even as an example in the interview.
- **Treating the downturn call as a sales opportunity.** Lead with the client's worry.

The downturn call is easy to describe and hard to do in the moment. Speaking Coach can play the anxious client so you can practise staying steady.

## Questions people ask

### What licenses do I need to become a financial advisor?

It depends on your country, what you'll sell and the firm. Check the job post and the regulator for your market, and be ready to say which exams you've passed or booked.

### How do I answer if I'm changing careers into financial advice?

Lead with the network and trust you've built in your previous field, and a niche that comes from it. Then show you've thought through the first year of building a book.

### What's the difference between interviewing at a big firm and an independent one?

Big firms often have a structured training program and focus on your activity plan. Independent firms may focus more on whether you'll fit their clients and how you'd serve existing ones.

### Should I talk about my own investing?

Only briefly, if asked. Focus on how you'd help clients, not on your own returns.
