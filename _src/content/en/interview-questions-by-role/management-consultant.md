## What consultants are listening for

Most consulting interviews have two parts. The fit part tests whether you can lead, influence and handle setbacks, and whether a client would trust you in a room. The case tests whether you can break a messy problem into pieces, think out loud and communicate clearly. In both, interviewers are listening to how you communicate as much as what you say: top-down, structured, concise.

Interviewers often dig into one story for ten minutes or more. Choose stories with enough detail to survive that.

## Why consulting

Three parts, about 60 seconds:

1. **What you want from the work:** variety of problems, working with clients, learning fast.
2. **Evidence:** something you've done that's like consulting (a project with a client, a strategy competition, a problem you solved at work).
3. **Why this firm:** a person you met, a practice area, or how they staff projects.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "I like working on a problem that matters to someone and then moving to a new one. In my engineering job I led a three-month project to cut machine downtime, and the part I enjoyed most was presenting options to the plant manager and seeing him act on them. Consulting would let me do that across industries. When I spoke with someone from your operations practice, she described being on the factory floor in her first month, and that's the kind of work I want."

## Leadership and other fit stories

Prepare four or five stories that can cover most fit questions: leadership, persuasion, failure, teamwork, achievement. For each, go deeper than a normal behavioural interview.

| Interviewer's follow-up | What they're testing | Be ready with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What exactly did you say to him?" | Whether you really led | The actual words you used |
| "Why did you choose that approach?" | Judgement | The options you considered and rejected |
| "How did the team react?" | Self-awareness | One person who pushed back and how you handled it |
| "What would you do differently?" | Learning | A real change, not "nothing" |

Example leadership story outline (made-up details):

> "I was the student lead for a pro bono project helping a food bank cut volunteer no-shows. Two weeks in, a teammate stopped contributing. I asked him privately what was going on; he was overloaded with exams. We moved him to the data analysis, which he could do on his own schedule, and I took the interviews. We delivered a new reminder system, and no-shows dropped from about one in four to one in ten over the next two months."

See [showing leadership](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-a-time-you-showed-leadership/) for more on these stories.

## Talking through a case structure out loud

Most candidates practise frameworks on paper. The interview is spoken, so practise the spoken version.

1. **Restate the question.** "So the client is a regional grocery chain, profits are down 20% over two years, and they want to know why and what to do."
2. **Ask two or three clarifying questions.** Goal, timeframe, anything unusual about the business.
3. **Ask for a moment.** "Can I take a minute to structure my thoughts?" Then take it.
4. **Lay out the structure top-down.** "I'd look at this in three areas: revenue, costs, and changes in the market. Within revenue, I'd split into number of customers and spend per visit."
5. **Say where you'd start and why.** "I'd start with revenue, since the drop is large and grocery margins are thin."
6. **Summarise as you go.** After each finding, say what it means for the answer.

Say your structure in the order you'd present it to a client, the [pyramid principle](/speaking-coach/glossary/pyramid-principle/) way: main point first, then supporting points. For more on case formats, see the [case interview](/speaking-coach/interviews/case-interview/) guide.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Forcing a memorised framework** that doesn't fit the question.
- **Thinking silently for too long** mid-case. Narrate your reasoning.
- **Fit stories with "we" throughout.** Interviewers want what you did.
- **Ending a case without a recommendation.** Always close with "My recommendation is…" and the next step.

Cases reward people who've practised speaking their thinking. Speaking Coach can give you a case prompt and push on your structure out loud.

## Questions people ask

### How many fit stories should I prepare?

Four or five strong ones usually cover everything. Make sure each can stand up to several minutes of follow-up questions.

### Are fit questions really as important as the case?

Yes. A weak fit interview can sink a strong case performance, so prepare them with the same care.

### How long should I take to structure a case?

Usually about a minute after your clarifying questions. Ask for the time, use it, then present your structure clearly.

### What if my structure is wrong?

Interviewers care more about how you adapt. If new information doesn't fit, say so, adjust your structure out loud, and move on.
