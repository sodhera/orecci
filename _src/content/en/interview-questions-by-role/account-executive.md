## How AE interviews usually run

Most account executive hiring processes include a recruiter screen, a hiring manager interview about your numbers, a panel with peers or leaders, and a practical round: a mock discovery call or a short pitch. The practical round often decides it, because it's the only time they hear you sell.

Have these facts ready before any call: your average deal size, typical cycle length, last year's quota and attainment, and what you sold to whom. If a number is confidential, give a range and say so.

## Your sales cycle

Walk through it in stages and name what you do at each. Keep it to about 90 seconds.

| Stage | What you say you do |
| --- | --- |
| Prospecting | "About half my pipeline came from SDRs, half from my own outreach to existing customers' peers." |
| Discovery | "I'm looking for a problem that costs them money, and who feels it most." |
| Demo or proposal | "I only show the three things tied to what they told me." |
| Mutual plan | "I write the steps to signature with the champion, with dates." |
| Close | "Legal and procurement usually take two to three weeks, so I start them before the final yes." |

## Closing a big deal

Pick the deal that best matches the job: similar size, similar buyer. Tell it as a story with turns, not a list of steps.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "My largest deal was a three-year contract with a regional hospital group, about four times my average deal. My champion was the IT director, but the CFO had killed a similar purchase the year before. I asked my champion what the CFO had objected to: no clear payback. So we built the business case together using their own overtime figures, and I asked for 20 minutes with the CFO to walk through it. She questioned two assumptions and we fixed them on the spot. It closed in seven months, about a month later than I'd forecast, because procurement added a security review I should have started earlier."

That last sentence matters. A small honest miss makes the rest believable.

## A lost deal post-mortem

Interviewers ask this to see whether you learn or blame. Choose a loss where the lesson was yours, not "they had no budget."

1. **The deal in one line.** Size, buyer, how far it got.
2. **The signal you missed.** "My only contact was a manager; I never met the person who signed."
3. **What happened.** "A competitor got to the VP directly."
4. **What you changed.** "Now I ask in the second meeting who else needs to say yes, and I don't forecast a deal above 50% until I've met them."

## The mock discovery call

This is where many candidates lose the offer, usually by pitching in the first two minutes. The interviewer wants to see you uncover a problem.

Lines you can use:

> "Before I tell you anything about us, can I ask how you handle this today?"

> "You said onboarding takes three weeks. What does that cost you?"

> "Who else would care if this got fixed?"

> "If we solved this, what would need to be true for you to move forward this quarter?"

End by summarizing what you heard and proposing a next step with a date. If you'd like more on pitching once discovery is done, see [how to give a sales pitch](/speaking-coach/presentations/sales-pitch/).

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inflating attainment | Managers check references and ask follow-ups | Give the real number and the context |
| Pitching in the discovery call | Shows you sell before you listen | Ask four or five questions first |
| A lost deal with no lesson | Sounds like you blame the buyer | Name what you'd do differently |
| Every deal "closed in record time" | Unbelievable | Include one honest delay |

A mock call only improves when you say it out loud to someone who plays a hard buyer. In Speaking Coach you can describe the buyer and the AI partner plays them; feedback afterwards quotes what you said and suggests what to try instead.

## Questions people ask

### What if I missed quota last year?

Say so, give the number, and explain the context in one sentence without blaming the territory. Then show what your pipeline looks like now or what you changed.

### How long should my sales cycle answer be?

About 90 seconds. Name the stages, add one detail per stage, and let them ask about the part they care about.

### Should I bring a brag book?

Some sales managers like a short one: quota attainment by year, a few deal summaries and a recommendation. Keep confidential client names out of it.

### How do I prepare for a mock discovery call?

Research the company you'll pretend to sell to, write ten open questions, and practice asking them out loud. Plan your closing line: a summary plus a proposed next meeting.

### Do AE interviews include "sell me this pen"?

Sometimes, especially for earlier-career roles. The same rule applies: ask what they need before you describe the pen. See [sell me this pen](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/sell-me-this-pen/).
