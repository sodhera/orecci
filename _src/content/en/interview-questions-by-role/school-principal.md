## Who is on the panel, and what each wants

Principal interviews are almost always panels, sometimes in several rounds: district leaders, teachers, parents, occasionally students. You are answering all of them at once.

| Panel member | What they're listening for |
| --- | --- |
| District leader | Results, judgment, follow-through on hard personnel decisions |
| Teachers | Will you protect their time, give real feedback and back them with parents? |
| Parents | Will you listen, keep children safe and communicate clearly? |

Assistant principal interviews ask the same questions, with more weight on discipline, operations and supporting the principal's priorities.

## Instructional leadership

Every candidate says they're an instructional leader. Prove it with your calendar.

> "At my last school I blocked Tuesday and Thursday mornings for classrooms and put my phone in my desk. Each week I visited every teacher in one grade for 15 minutes and gave one piece of feedback within 24 hours, always something they could try the next day. That year we focused on student discussion, and by spring our walkthrough notes showed students talking in pairs in most classrooms, compared with a few in the fall."

Mention the cycle: observe, give one clear next step, check back. Panels like a principal who coaches, not one who evaluates once a year.

## A struggling teacher

This question has two parts, and you need both: how you support, and what you do if support doesn't work.

1. **See it yourself.** Several short visits, not one bad lesson or a parent complaint.
2. **Name one priority.** Usually classroom management or lesson clarity; trying to fix everything at once fails.
3. **Give real support.** A mentor, a model lesson to watch, co-planning, a coach.
4. **Set a timeline and check back** with visits and evidence.
5. **If it doesn't improve,** move to the formal process, documented and fair.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "A second-year teacher was losing her class in the afternoons. I visited four times and saw the transitions were the problem, so we focused only on that. A colleague with strong routines let her observe, and I came back twice a week for three weeks. By the end, transitions went from ten minutes to about three. I told her clearly at the start that if things didn't improve, we'd move to a formal plan. She didn't need it."

## School culture

Culture answers go wrong when they're all values and no practice. Describe what someone would see walking into your school.

> "Culture is what happens when no one's watching. At my school I wanted students to feel known, so every adult took responsibility for five students they weren't teaching and checked in with them weekly. Morning arrival had staff at every door greeting students by name. For staff, I started each meeting with a teacher sharing something that worked. Within a year, our staff survey showed more teachers saying they felt recognized."

## Using data

Show you use data to decide, not to decorate a slide. Name the data, the question you asked, the decision and the result.

> "Our reading scores looked flat overall, but when I broke them out by grade, third grade had dropped sharply. We looked at the schedule and found reading intervention clashed with lunch for half the grade. We moved it, and the spring benchmark showed most of those students back on track."

## Your first 90 days

Expect this. A good answer is mostly listening, then a few early wins, then a direction. Avoid promising big changes before you know the school. See [how to answer the first 90 days question](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-would-you-do-in-your-first-90-days/).

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking only about vision | Panels need to see how it happens on a Tuesday | Describe the routine behind the value |
| Avoiding the "what if they don't improve" part | Suggests you won't make hard calls | Say you'd move to a formal plan, fairly |
| Criticizing your current principal | Teachers on the panel imagine you talking about them | Talk about what you learned |
| Long answers with no result | The panel loses the thread | End each story with what changed for students |

Principal interviews often run long, with several panels. Practice the struggling-teacher and data answers out loud until they fit in about 90 seconds. With Speaking Coach you can add the job post and the AI interviewer asks the follow-up a district leader would.

## Questions people ask

### How do I prepare for a principal interview with no principal experience?

Use assistant principal, coach or department lead examples, and be clear about what you led yourself. Panels expect first-time principals to draw on those roles.

### How do I answer "What is your leadership style?"

One sentence, then a story that proves it. See [the leadership style question](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-is-your-leadership-style/).

### Should I bring a 30-60-90 day plan?

If you've studied the school's public data, a one-page plan can help. Keep it humble: most of the first month is listening.

### What should I ask at the end?

Ask what the district sees as the school's biggest challenge, how much freedom principals have over hiring and budget, and what the staff are proud of.
