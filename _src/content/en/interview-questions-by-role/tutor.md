## What tutoring interviews look like

Tutoring interviews are often short: a tutoring company coordinator, a school, or a parent hiring privately. Many include a mini lesson or a "teach me this" moment. That moment matters more than anything on your CV, because it's the job.

## Explaining a concept two ways

This is the signature tutor question. Pick a concept you know well and prepare two genuinely different explanations, not the same one in different words. Then say how you'd check understanding.

| Approach | When it helps | Example for negative numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual | Students who need to see it | A number line: subtracting moves left, past zero |
| Real-life story | Students who switch off at symbols | Money: you have $5, you spend $8, you owe $3 |
| Hands-on | Younger students or kinesthetic learners | Counters in two colors that cancel each other out |
| Pattern | Students who like rules | 3 − 1 = 2, 3 − 2 = 1, 3 − 3 = 0, so 3 − 4 = ? |

Example answer (made-up details):

> "First I'd draw a number line and ask her to start at 3 and take 5 steps left. She'd land on −2. If that didn't click, I'd switch to money: 'You have three dollars and buy something for five. What happens?' Most students say 'I owe two.' Then I'd connect the two: owing two is −2 on the line. To check, I'd give her 4 − 7 and ask her to explain her answer to me, not just say it."

Practice explaining your concept out loud, with a timer. Two clear minutes is plenty. The guide on [explaining things simply](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-explain-things-simply/) has more techniques.

## An unmotivated student

Interviewers want to see you look for the reason, not just push harder.

> "I tutored a 15-year-old whose parents booked me for chemistry. He came to the first session with his hood up and said he'd fail anyway. I asked what he was good at. He played a lot of a building game, so we used it to talk about elements as building blocks. I also made the first goal tiny: get one practice question right by the end of the session. He did, and I texted his mum about it with him watching. By week four he was bringing his own questions."

Show three moves: find out why, make an early win possible, and connect the subject to something they care about.

## Measuring progress

Parents and companies pay for results, so be specific about how you track them.

1. **Baseline** in the first session: a short diagnostic or past test.
2. **A clear goal** with the parent: a grade, a test date, a topic mastered.
3. **Quick checks** each session: three questions at the start on last week's topic.
4. **Regular updates:** a short note after each session or every few weeks.
5. **Retest** with the same type of questions to show change.

> "After each session I send parents three lines: what we covered, what went well and what to practice. Every four sessions we redo a short quiz in the same format as the first, so they can see the change."

## Online sessions

Show you've thought about attention and tools, not just video.

- Test audio, camera and the shared whiteboard before the student joins.
- Have the student write or draw on the whiteboard, not just watch you.
- Check understanding more often than in person, because you can't see their page.
- Use the student's name and ask a question every couple of minutes.
- Keep sessions shorter or add a break for younger students.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Giving the same explanation twice, louder.** The question asks for a second way.
- **Promising grade jumps.** Promise a plan and regular evidence, not a result.
- **Talking the whole time.** Good tutoring has the student doing most of the work.
- **Pretending to know.** If you don't know an answer, say you'll find out and show them how you look it up.

Explaining out loud is a different skill from knowing the subject. Speaking Coach's AI partner can play a confused student and ask follow-ups on what you actually said, so you can rehearse both explanations before the interview.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need teaching qualifications to tutor?

It depends on the company or school. Many value subject knowledge and clear explanation more than certificates. Check the job post.

### What if they ask me to teach a topic I haven't prepared?

Say what you'd ask first to find the student's level, then explain one small part well. A clear start beats a rushed full lesson. The [complex explanation question](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-a-time-you-explained-something-complex/) has more examples.

### How do I answer a parent who wants faster results?

Show them the progress evidence, explain what's realistic before the test date and suggest one thing that would help, such as extra practice between sessions.

### What should I ask at the end?

Ask how students are matched to tutors, what materials are provided, how sessions are scheduled and how progress is reported to families.
