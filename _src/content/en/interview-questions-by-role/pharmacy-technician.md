## What the pharmacist is hiring for

A pharmacy technician interview is often with the pharmacist you'll work beside. They're imagining a Monday afternoon: a long queue, phones ringing, a rejected claim and a patient getting louder at the counter. They want someone who won't cut corners when it's busy, who handles the counter kindly and who knows exactly when to say, "Let me get the pharmacist for you."

## Accuracy under volume

Don't say "I'm detail-oriented." Describe your checks, in order.

1. **Read the label against the prescription** before you start.
2. **Scan the barcode** on the stock bottle and check the drug, strength and form match.
3. **Count twice** or use the counting tray method your pharmacy uses.
4. **Check look-alike, sound-alike drugs** with extra care; say one pair you watch for if you know them.
5. **Stop and ask** when anything doesn't match, however busy it is.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "On a busy Monday I was filling a prescription for a 25 mg tablet and the barcode scan flagged the bottle as 50 mg. They're stored side by side and the bottles look almost the same. I put it back, pulled the right strength and told the pharmacist, who moved them to different shelves that afternoon. Since then I read the strength out loud to myself when I pull look-alike bottles."

The point is that you slow down for the check, not for everything else.

## Insurance rejections

Show that you understand the common reasons and what to do about each, and that you explain it simply to the patient.

| Rejection | What you do | What you tell the patient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refill too soon | Check the last fill date; tell the pharmacist if the patient's dose changed | "Your insurance will cover it from Thursday. Would you like us to have it ready then?" |
| Prior authorization required | Notify the prescriber's office with the details | "Your insurance needs your doctor to approve this first. We've sent them the request." |
| Not covered on the plan | Check for a covered alternative and let the pharmacist contact the prescriber | "This one isn't covered, but there may be a similar one that is. We're checking with your doctor." |
| Patient details don't match | Confirm date of birth, ID number and the cardholder's details | "Can I check your insurance card? One detail doesn't match." |

## An upset patient at pickup

The interviewer wants calm, honesty about the wait and a next step. Say the actual lines.

> "A woman came to pick up her son's inhaler and it wasn't ready because the claim had been rejected. She was frustrated because she'd taken time off work. I said, 'I'm sorry, I know you came specially for this.' I explained in one sentence that the insurance needed a new approval, and told her we'd sent the request to the doctor that morning. Because her son needed it today, I asked the pharmacist whether there were options to tide him over. He spoke to her directly. She left with a plan and a callback time."

Notice the hand-off: anything involving what the patient should do about their medication went to the pharmacist. More general techniques are in [how to handle an angry customer](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/how-would-you-handle-an-angry-customer/).

## When to refer to the pharmacist

This question is often the deciding one. Interviewers want a clear line, and a simple phrase you use.

**You handle:** prescription intake, filling under the pharmacist's checks, insurance processing, stock, the register, and questions like "Is it ready?" or "How much is it?"

**You refer:** anything that needs clinical judgment, such as:

- "Can I take this with my other medicine?"
- "Which cold medicine should I buy?"
- "Is this side effect normal?"
- "I missed a dose, what should I do?"
- Counseling on a new prescription.

> "My rule is simple: if the question starts with 'should I' or 'can I,' it goes to the pharmacist. I say, 'That's a great question for the pharmacist; let me get her for you,' so the patient doesn't feel brushed off."

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Offering medical advice to seem helpful.** In this interview it's a red flag.
- **"I've never made a mistake."** Talk about a near miss you caught.
- **Rushing the scenarios.** Saying "I'd double-check" sounds better when you say how.
- **Forgetting privacy.** Keep patient names and medications quiet at the counter.

Rehearse the upset-patient answer out loud so the calm, plain wording comes naturally. Speaking Coach's AI partner can play the patient at the counter and react to what you actually say.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need to be certified to interview as a pharmacy technician?

Requirements vary by employer and by where you live. Check the job post, and if you're studying for a certification, say when you expect to finish.

### What if I have no pharmacy experience?

Use retail or customer service experience for the counter questions, and examples of careful, repetitive work for the accuracy questions. See [how to answer questions about repetitive work](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/how-do-you-handle-repetitive-work/).

### What's the difference between this and a pharmacist interview?

Technician interviews focus on accuracy, insurance and counter service; pharmacist interviews focus on clinical judgment and counseling. See [pharmacist interview questions](/speaking-coach/interview-questions-by-role/pharmacist/).

### How do I answer "Why do you want to be a pharmacy technician?"

Give a concrete reason, such as liking precise work that helps people, and connect it to a moment you saw at a pharmacy or in your own life.
