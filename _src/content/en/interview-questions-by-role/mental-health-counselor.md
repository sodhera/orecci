## What a clinical director is listening for

Counselor interviews are usually run by a clinical director or supervisor, sometimes with a peer clinician, and some include a vignette: a short client scenario you talk through. They're checking clinical reasoning, safety, ethics and fit with their population. They also listen to how you talk, because warmth and clarity in the interview suggest how you'll sound in session.

Every example should be de-identified. Age range, setting and presenting concern are enough.

## Your theoretical approach

Avoid listing every model you've studied. Name one or two, say why, and show them in action.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "My main approach is cognitive behavioral therapy, with person-centered work as the foundation. Clients I see in community settings often come in overwhelmed, and CBT gives us something concrete to work on early. But it only works once they feel heard, so the first sessions are mostly listening. With one young adult client dealing with panic attacks, we spent two sessions on his story before introducing a thought record. By then he was ready to use it, and by our eighth session he was riding the bus to work again."

What makes it work: plain words, a reason, a de-identified example and an outcome.

If you're integrative, say what guides when you use what. "Integrative" alone sounds like you haven't decided.

## A client in crisis

This is the most important answer in the interview. Show that you follow a structured process, use the agency's protocol and don't act alone.

1. **Stay calm and ask directly.** Asking clearly about suicidal thoughts is standard practice; show you're comfortable doing it.
2. **Assess risk** with the screening tool or framework your training and the agency use: thoughts, plan, means, intent, history, protective factors.
3. **Collaborate on a safety plan** with the client: warning signs, coping steps, people to contact, crisis lines, reducing access to means.
4. **Consult** your supervisor, the same day.
5. **Follow the agency protocol** for higher risk, including emergency services when needed.
6. **Document** the assessment, plan and consultation.
7. **Follow up** at the agreed time.

A line that lands: "In a crisis I don't make the decision alone. Consulting is part of the process, not a sign I'm unsure."

## Boundaries

Expect a scenario: a gift, a social media request, a client asking personal questions, or running into a client in the supermarket. Show you know your ethics code and explain kindly.

| Situation | A reasonable answer |
| --- | --- |
| A small handmade gift at termination | "I'd follow agency policy, think about its meaning to the client, and often accept a small token with thanks. I'd talk to my supervisor if unsure." |
| A social media friend request | "I don't accept, and I'd explain why at the next session so it doesn't feel like rejection." |
| A client asks about your personal life | "A brief, warm deflection, and I'm curious about why they're asking." |
| Running into a client in public | "I let them decide whether to acknowledge me, and I never mention our work." |

## Self-care

Interviewers ask about self-care because burnout affects client care. Be specific and honest.

Example answer:

> "I use supervision for the hard cases, not just paperwork. After a difficult session I take five minutes to write my notes and close the file before the next client. Outside work I run three mornings a week and keep weekends free of email. When my caseload had several crisis clients at once last year, I told my supervisor, and we adjusted my new intakes for a month."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing many models with no example | Sounds like a textbook | One or two models, one client story |
| Handling a crisis alone in your answer | Sounds unsafe | Assess, safety plan, consult, document |
| "I don't really need self-care" | A red flag for burnout | Specific habits and how you use supervision |
| Identifiable client details | An ethics breach in the interview itself | De-identify everything |

## Questions people ask

### How should a pre-licensed counselor describe supervision?

Explain your licensure stage, how often you'd need supervision and what you look for in a supervisor. Ask how the agency provides it.

### How do I answer a vignette question?

Think out loud: what you'd assess first, the risks you'd consider, what you'd do in the first session and when you'd consult. There usually isn't one right answer; they want your reasoning.

### What if my orientation is different from the agency's model?

Say what you know about their model, where it overlaps with your approach and how you'd learn it. Show openness, not a debate.

### What should I ask at the end?

Ask about caseload size, productivity expectations, supervision, crisis procedures and how clinicians are supported after a critical incident.
