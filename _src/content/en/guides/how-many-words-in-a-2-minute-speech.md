## The word count for 2 minutes

| Pace | Words in 2 minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Slow (110 words per minute) | 220 |
| Average (140 words per minute) | 280 |
| Fast (170 words per minute) | 340 |
| **Write this many** (80–90% of average) | 224–252 |

Slow and fast are 120 words apart, which is most of a minute at an average pace. That is why guessing is risky at this length: a draft that feels like two minutes can turn out to be three. The [speech length calculator](/speaking-coach/tools/speech-length-calculator/) converts any word count for you.

**In pages,** 280 words is a bit more than half a single-spaced page, or about one page double-spaced. That uses the rough rule of thumb of 500 words to a single-spaced page of 12-point text, and fonts and margins shift it. A useful warning sign: if your two-minute speech fills a whole single-spaced page, it is about 500 words, and that takes 3 minutes 34 seconds at an average pace.

## What fits in 2 minutes

One point and one story. The story is what the second minute buys you. In a [1-minute speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-many-words-in-a-1-minute-speech/) you can only mention that something happened; in two you can tell it.

Two minutes suits:

- a **short toast**, such as a [guest's toast at a wedding](/speaking-coach/speeches/wedding-toast/), a birthday or an anniversary
- an **answer in class** or a short spoken assignment
- an **update in a weekly meeting**, when one item needs explaining ([how to give a clear update](/speaking-coach/presentations/team-meeting-update/))
- a **contest speech** with a two-minute limit, or your pitch beside a [research poster](/speaking-coach/presentations/poster-presentation/)

The shape is the same for all of them: open, tell the story, say what it shows, close. Two stories don't fit. Neither does one story plus a list of thank-yous.

## A timing plan for 2 minutes

| Part | Time | Words | What goes in it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 15 seconds | 35 | Get the room's attention and say who you are |
| The story | 45 seconds | 105 | One scene: where you were, what went wrong, what someone did |
| What it shows | 30 seconds | 70 | The point, said plainly and tied to the person or topic |
| The close | 15 seconds | 35 | A wish, an ask or the toast itself |
| Pauses | 15 seconds | 0 | The laugh, the breath before the close, glasses going up |
| **Total** | **120 seconds** | **245** | |

Words are counted at 140 per minute.

## A 280-word sample to time yourself

This made-up toast, from a son at his parents' thirtieth anniversary dinner, is exactly 280 words. Read it out loud with a stopwatch, straight through, without stopping for laughs.

> Could I have everyone's attention for two minutes? I promise it will be two. For the few of you I have not met, I am Tomas, the younger son, the one who still brings his laundry home.
>
> When I was nine, our car broke down on the way to the coast. It was raining, the phone had no signal, and my brother and I were certain the holiday was over. Dad opened the hood and stared at the engine as if it might apologize. Mom did not say a word. She took the picnic out of the trunk, spread the blanket across the back seat, and announced that lunch was served. We ate cheese sandwiches in a parking lot for two hours while the rain drummed on the roof, and it is still the best meal I remember. When the tow truck finally came, none of us wanted to leave.
>
> That is what thirty years of these two looks like from the back seat. Things break. Plans fall apart. And one of them always finds the blanket while the other glares at the problem until it gives up. They have never once agreed on directions, and they have never left each other stranded.
>
> Mom, Dad, you taught us that a good marriage is not the one where nothing goes wrong. It is the one where somebody always packs sandwiches. I hope the next thirty years bring you fewer breakdowns, better weather, a car that starts, and many more lunches in strange places.
>
> So please stand if you can, and raise your glasses with me. To Elena and Viktor, thirty years in, and still on the road. To Mom and Dad.

If that took you exactly 2 minutes, your pace is 140 words per minute. If not, divide 16,800 by your time in seconds.

| Your time | Your pace | Two minutes of talking at that pace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1:45 | 160 words per minute | 320 words |
| 2:00 | 140 words per minute | 280 words |
| 2:20 | 120 words per minute | 240 words |
| 2:33 | 110 words per minute | 220 words |

Then write 80 to 90 percent of the last column, because in a real room this toast will not take two minutes. People laugh, you pause before "Mom, Dad", and it takes a while for everyone to find a glass. To fit a strict two-minute slot, cut the third paragraph (53 words). That leaves 227 words: about 1 minute 37 seconds of talking and 23 seconds for everything else.

If you'd rather measure from a speech of your own, the [pace checker](/speaking-coach/tools/pace-checker/) works from your voice or from a word count and a time.

## Slides for a 2-minute speech

None for a toast or a class answer. For a two-minute presentation, plan on one slide, two at most. At a poster session, the poster is your slide. It depends on what's on it: one clear chart can carry the full two minutes, while a slide of bullet points gets read instead of heard.

## What to cut when you're over

If you're 30 words over, that is about 13 seconds: trim the start of the story. If you're 80 over, that is more than half a minute, and a whole part has to go. Cut in this order:

1. **The second story.** Keep the one with a scene in it.
2. **The run-up.** "This was back in, I think, 2014, or maybe 2015" can go. Start where something happens: "When I was nine, our car broke down."
3. **Inside jokes that need explaining.** The explanation costs more seconds than the joke earns.
4. **The thank-you list.** One thank-you, by name.
5. **The explanation after the point.** If the story worked, you don't need "what I mean by that is".

Don't cut the pauses. For more on building a toast, see [how to give a toast](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-give-a-toast/).

## Questions people ask

### How many words is a 2-minute speech?

About 280 words at an average pace of 140 words per minute, and between 220 and 340 depending on your pace. For a toast, write 225 to 250.

### How many pages is a 2-minute speech?

A bit more than half a single-spaced page, or about one page double-spaced, if a single-spaced page holds roughly 500 words. Treat that as a rough guide and count words.

### Is 300 words too long for a 2-minute speech?

Slightly. 300 words takes about 2 minutes 9 seconds at 140 words per minute before any pauses, and only fits if you speak fast (1:46 at 170). Cut it to about 250.

### How long is a 250-word speech?

About 1 minute 47 seconds at 140 words per minute. That makes 250 words a good length for a two-minute slot with a few pauses.

### How many words is a 90-second speech?

About 210 words at 140 words per minute, and between 165 and 255 depending on your pace.
