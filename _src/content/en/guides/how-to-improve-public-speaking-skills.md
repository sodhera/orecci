## How the plan works

Reading about speaking doesn't change how you speak. Saying things out loud, hearing them back and adjusting does. This plan gives you one drill a day, each under 10 minutes, and each one aimed at a single skill. You touch every major skill once in the first three weeks, then combine them in the last.

What you need:

- A phone to record voice or video.
- A **baseline topic**: something you could talk about for two minutes, such as your job, a project or a hobby. You'll use it on day 1 and day 30.
- A timer.

Do the drill out loud every time, even when it feels silly. Whispering or running it in your head doesn't train the same muscles.

## Week 1: Find out how you sound

| Day | Skill | Drill (about 10 min) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Baseline | Record a two-minute talk on your baseline topic. Don't watch it yet. |
| 2 | Self-review | Watch day 1 once. Write down three things you'd change. Pick the biggest. |
| 3 | Pace | Read a 150-word paragraph out loud and time it. Run your day 1 recording through the [free speech review](/speaking-coach/tools/free-speech-review/) to see your words per minute. |
| 4 | Fillers | Talk for one minute on any topic. Count your ums, likes and so's. Repeat, replacing each with a silent pause. |
| 5 | Pauses | Read a paragraph and mark a pause at every full stop. Read it pausing two counts at each mark. |
| 6 | Volume | Stand at one end of a room. Read to a chair at the other end so it would "hear" every word. |
| 7 | Rest and review | Re-record one minute of your baseline topic. Note one thing that's already better. |

## Week 2: Structure your message

| Day | Skill | Drill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Point first | Answer five everyday questions ("What did you do this weekend?") with your main point in the first sentence. |
| 9 | Openings | Write three different opening lines for your baseline topic: a question, a story, a number. Say each out loud. |
| 10 | Closings | Write and say a final line that sums up your point and asks the audience for something. |
| 11 | Rule of three | Give a one-minute talk with exactly three points, signposted "first, second, finally". |
| 12 | Stories | Tell a 60-second story from your week: time and place, what happened, why it mattered. |
| 13 | Cutting | Take your two-minute baseline and say it in one minute without losing the point. |
| 14 | Review | Record your baseline topic with a new opening and closing. Compare with day 7. |

If you want the deeper version of any day, see [how to start a speech](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-start-a-speech/) and [how to structure a presentation](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-structure-a-presentation/).

## Week 3: Voice, body and nerves

| Day | Skill | Drill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | Clarity | Say three tongue twisters slowly, then at normal speed. Read a paragraph exaggerating word endings. |
| 16 | Vocal variety | Read the same sentence five ways: excited, serious, surprised, quiet, slow. |
| 17 | Emphasis | Read a paragraph and stress one word per sentence. Change which word and hear the meaning change. |
| 18 | Breath | Breathe in for four counts, out for six, three times. Then say one long sentence on a single breath without rushing. |
| 19 | Hands | Record a one-minute video. Start with hands relaxed at your sides; gesture only to show size, number or direction. |
| 20 | Eye contact | Put three sticky notes around the room. Deliver one sentence to each in turn. |
| 21 | Nerves | Give your baseline talk to one real person, standing up. Do the slow-breathing routine first. |

## Week 4: Think on your feet and put it together

| Day | Skill | Drill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22 | Impromptu | Pick a random object. Talk about it for 60 seconds with no preparation. |
| 23 | Questions | Have someone ask you three questions about your baseline topic. Answer each in under 30 seconds. |
| 24 | Not knowing | Practice saying "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" to a question you can't answer. |
| 25 | Recovery | Mid-talk, stop for five seconds on purpose. Look at your notes, find your place, carry on. |
| 26 | Full talk | Write a three-minute talk on a new topic. Say it through twice. |
| 27 | Notes | Cut your notes for the day 26 talk down to six words per point. Give it from those. |
| 28 | Audience | Give the day 26 talk to two or more people. Ask for one thing that worked and one to change. |
| 29 | Rehearse | Give the day 26 talk again with their change. |
| 30 | Compare | Record your baseline talk again. Watch day 1 and day 30 back to back. |

## What to look for on day 30

Watch both recordings and compare, using the same checklist each time:

| Check | Day 1 | Day 30 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Words per minute (aim for about 130–150 for a talk) | | |
| Filler words in the first 30 seconds | | |
| Is the main point in the first two sentences? | | |
| Clear final line? | | |
| Pauses between points? | | |
| Hands and eyes steady? | | |

Most people find the biggest change in structure and fillers. Pace and voice tend to take longer. For how to watch a recording without cringing your way through it, see [how to record yourself speaking](/speaking-coach/guides/record-yourself-speaking/).

## Keep it going after 30 days

- **One talk a week.** A team update, a toast, a story at dinner: treat it as practice.
- **Re-run the weakest week.** If day 30 shows fillers are still your issue, repeat week 1.
- **Practice with someone who reacts.** Drills alone build skill; speaking to a listener builds nerve. Speaking Coach gives a daily 30-second speaking prompt and a streak, and its voice partner can play an audience that asks questions afterwards.

## Questions people ask

### How long does it take to get better at public speaking?

Most people notice a difference within a few weeks of daily out-loud practice, especially in structure and filler words. Comfort in front of large groups usually takes longer.

### Can I improve public speaking without an audience?

Yes, up to a point. Recording yourself fixes pace, fillers and structure. You still need real listeners eventually to get used to the pressure.

### What's the fastest way to improve?

Record yourself, pick the single biggest problem, and drill only that for a week. Fixing one habit at a time is faster than trying to fix everything at once.

### Should I join a speaking club?

A club gives you a regular audience and feedback, which this plan can't. Many people combine both: daily drills at home, a club or group talk once a week.

### What if I miss a day?

Pick up where you left off. The order matters more than the dates.
