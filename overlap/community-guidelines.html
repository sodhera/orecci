Draft Community Guidelines

Overlap
Last Updated: September 01, 2026
Status: Pre-launch working draft

Draft notice: These baseline safety expectations describe the current product. A final enforcement, appeals, and response-time process must be defined before public launch.

Overlap is for making plans with people you know and trust. Treat every member and event with care, honesty, and respect.

1. Be Respectful and Get Consent

  • Invite people honestly and respect declines, blocks, privacy choices, and event boundaries.
  • Do not harass, threaten, stalk, shame, intimidate, or repeatedly contact someone who does not want contact.
  • Do not share another person’s private details, exact location, photos, messages, or identity without permission.

2. Keep People Safe

  • No sexual exploitation, child endangerment, human trafficking, non-consensual sexual content, credible threats, or encouragement of self-harm.
  • No hate, dehumanization, or discrimination targeting protected characteristics.
  • No illegal goods or services, dangerous instructions, weapons threats, or events intended to facilitate unlawful activity.
  • Do not impersonate people or organizations, create deceptive events, or use spam and scams to obtain money, access, or personal information.

3. Host and Attendee Responsibilities

Hosts should use accurate event information, protect private addresses, approve guests thoughtfully, communicate material changes, and cancel events that cannot safely proceed. Attendees should respect the host, venue, guest list, property, and consent of others.

Overlap does not verify every person or event. Meet in an appropriate place, tell someone you trust about plans when useful, arrange your own transport, and leave if something feels unsafe.

4. Report, Block, and Emergencies

Use Report event for spam, harassment, unsafe activity, or misleading information. Use Block to stop access between accounts. Reports are reviewed as an operator workflow; they are not monitored as an emergency service.

If someone may be in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or an appropriate local authority first.

5. Enforcement

Depending on severity and context, action may include a warning, content or link removal, event cancellation, feature restriction, account suspension, or account termination. Serious risks may be preserved or disclosed when legally required. A final appeals and response-time process must be defined before launch.
