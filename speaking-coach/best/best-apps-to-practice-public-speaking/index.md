## Four ways an app can help, and what each misses

Public speaking practice has two parts: getting the words and delivery right, and getting used to people watching you. Apps are very good at the first and only partly good at the second. They fall into four groups:

| Type | Examples | What it gives you | What it can't do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lessons and daily drills | Speeko, Orai, Vocal Image | Short exercises and tips, a habit | Prepare a specific speech |
| Recording analyzers | Orai, Speeko, Yoodli | Pace, filler words, clarity, after you speak | React to you |
| Virtual audiences | Ovation, VirtualSpeech | A room that looks real, in VR or a browser | Feel like real faces you know |
| Practice partners | Speaking Coach, Yoodli | Questions and follow-ups on what you said | Replace a live room |

## The apps

**Orai.** Record a speech, scripted or freestyle, and get AI feedback on filler words, pace, clarity, energy, confidence and conciseness, with transcripts, playback and short lessons. Its App Store listing showed $12.99 a month, $49.99 a year or $99.99 lifetime, English only. Best for: rehearsing a fixed speech alone.

**Speeko.** Tracks your voice in real time with short alerts, then reviews pace, filler words, intonation, sentiment and word choice. It has 2-minute daily exercises, vocal warm-ups and digital notecards, on iOS, Android, Mac and web. Its subscriptions page listed $29.99 a month, or $8.33 a month on the annual plan with a 7-day trial. Best for: building the habit over weeks, before a talk is even scheduled.

**Vocal Image.** Voice tests, warm-ups, diction drills with clarity and speed scores, and an AI roleplay partner. Best for: a voice that shakes, trails off or sounds flat.

**Yoodli.** Its public speaking page describes practice on your own topics and decks, a speaking report on pacing and filler words, and optional AI follow-up questions to prepare for Q&A. Individuals get 5 lifetime sessions free; Pro is $8 a month billed yearly. Best for: talks that end with questions, on a computer.

**Ovation.** Practice in VR, on desktop or on the web, in rooms like a boardroom, lecture hall or debate stage, with AI avatars who ask questions about what you said. Feedback covers filler words and pacing, with transcripts. Its individual plan was $15 a month billed yearly, with a 7-day trial. Best for: people who freeze at the sight of a room and own a VR headset or a decent computer.

**VirtualSpeech.** Runs in the browser with VR as an option: present to a virtual audience or rehearse conversations with AI characters. Its pricing page listed $45 a month or $399 a year for individuals. Best for: someone whose employer might pay, since its site is mainly aimed at organizations.

**Speaking Coach (ours).** An AI voice partner plays your audience. The "Open a presentation or toast" scene lets you practice the opening that matters most; for a talk with slides, you upload a PDF, swipe through it while you speak, and then answer the questions your audience would ask. Afterwards you get your pace, filler words, long pauses and tips that quote your own words, and you can retry the moment that slipped. It runs on iPhone and Android in 20 languages; it's a subscription. Best for: practicing the questions and the opening, and for speeches in languages other than English.

## The non-app option: Toastmasters

A Toastmasters club gives you what no app does: a real room of people, every week or two, who are also nervous. As of October 2026 its site says clubs meet in person, online or hybrid, guests can visit free, and membership is $72 billed every six months plus club dues and a one-time $25 fee. The downside is time: a fixed evening, every couple of weeks. If that doesn't fit, our [Toastmasters alternatives](/speaking-coach/compare/toastmasters-alternatives/) page covers other ways to get a live audience.

## Picks by fear level and time

| | 5 minutes a day | An hour a week | One evening before the event |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Terrified** (avoid speaking at all) | Speeko or Vocal Image drills, alone | A recording analyzer, then one session with a partner app | Practice only your first 30 seconds, ten times |
| **Nervous but doing it** | Orai on your actual speech | A partner app on Gentle, then Realistic | Full run-throughs with a partner app or a friend |
| **Fine, want to get better** | Daily prompts in any app | Toastmasters, or Ovation for a realistic room | One run in front of two or three real people |

If fear is the main issue, read our [guide to public speaking anxiety](/speaking-coach/guides/public-speaking-anxiety/) first. Apps work best as gradual exposure: alone, then with an AI listener, then with a few people, then the real room.

## How to practice a specific speech with any app

1. Write the speech, then check its length with the [speech length calculator](/speaking-coach/tools/speech-length-calculator/). A 3-minute toast is roughly 390–450 words at a typical pace.
2. Say it out loud once without stopping, even if it goes badly.
3. Run it through a recording analyzer. Fix the one thing that stands out most: usually pace or fillers at the start.
4. Practice the opening and closing until they're automatic. The middle can be looser.
5. Do one run with a listener, AI or human, who reacts or asks a question.

## Questions people ask

### What is the best app to practice public speaking for free?

Most apps here are paid. Yoodli's free plan gives 5 lifetime sessions, and PowerPoint's Speaker Coach is free for slide rehearsals. The free [speech review](/speaking-coach/tools/free-speech-review/) on this site checks your pace and filler words in your browser.

### Does VR public speaking practice work?

It helps some people get used to the feeling of a room watching them. It still isn't a real audience, so treat it as a step before speaking to people, not a replacement.

### Is an app better than Toastmasters?

They do different jobs. An app lets you practice any time and repeat the hard parts. Toastmasters gives you real people and regular deadlines. Many people use both.

### Can these apps help with a wedding speech?

Yes. A recording analyzer helps with pace and fillers, and a partner app helps you rehearse the opening and stay calm if you lose your place. See our [speeches section](/speaking-coach/speeches/) for structure and examples.

### Which public speaking app works in my language?

Of those we checked, Orai is English only. Speaking Coach works in 20 languages. Speeko, Yoodli, Ovation and VirtualSpeech didn't list languages on the pages we read.
