## Short verdicts

**Speeko.** Tracks your voice in real time, then reviews pace, filler words, intonation, sentiment and word choice, with 2-minute exercises, vocal warm-ups and digital notecards. New since October: its subscriptions page says the main features are "free forever" with basic insights, while personalized feedback and real-time guidance are paid: $29.99 a month on its site ($24.99 in the App Store) or $99.99 a year, with a 7-day trial. **Pick it if** you want to build the habit over weeks. **Skip it if** you need to rehearse one speech this weekend; Orai suits that better.

**Orai.** Record a scripted or freestyle speech and get feedback on filler words, pace, clarity, energy, confidence and conciseness, with a transcript and playback. $12.99 a month, $49.99 a year or $99.99 lifetime; English only. **Pick it if** you're nervous and want to practice where no one can hear. **Skip it if** you dread the questions afterwards.

**Yoodli.** Its public speaking page describes practicing on your own topics and decks, a speaking report on pacing and filler words, and opt-in AI follow-up questions to "simulate Q&A with tough, skeptical audiences". Free for 5 lifetime sessions; Pro is $8 a month billed yearly. **Pick it if** your talk has slides and a Q&A and you rehearse at a computer. It's the strongest web option here. **Skip it if** you want to practice on your phone.

**PowerPoint Speaker Coach.** Free in preview with a Microsoft account, English only. On-screen guidance as you rehearse, then a report. **Pick it if** your talk is already in PowerPoint.

**Ovation.** Rooms like a boardroom, lecture hall, courtroom or debate stage, with AI avatars; a student quoted on its site says they ask questions based on what you're talking about. Feedback includes pauses, filler words and transcripts. Web, desktop or VR, at $15 a month billed yearly with a 7-day trial. **Pick it if** the sight of a room is what freezes you.

**VirtualSpeech.** Present your own slides to a virtual audience that reacts and asks about what you said, in the browser with optional VR. Its site is aimed at companies and universities; its pricing page lists $45 a month or $399 a year for individuals. **Pick it if** your employer will pay.

**Vocal Image.** Voice tests, warm-ups, diction drills with clarity and speed scores, and an AI roleplay. **Pick it if** your voice shakes, trails off or sounds flat.

**The Speaking Coach app (ours).** An AI voice partner plays your audience. The "Open a presentation or toast" scene drills the opening that matters most; for a talk with slides, you add a PDF, swipe through it while you speak, then answer the questions your audience would ask. You get your pace, filler words, long pauses and tips that quote your own words, then retry the moment that slipped. 20 languages; a subscription. **Skip it if** you want a virtual room you can see; Ovation does that.

**Free option on this site:** the [free speech review](/speaking-coach/tools/free-speech-review/) gives you an AI voice review of up to 3 minutes of a speech: measured pace, fillers and long pauses, plus feedback that quotes your words, with one thing to keep, one change and one sentence rewritten more clearly. English only, two reviews per network per day, no account; audio is sent to Speaking Coach's server for transcription and not kept.

## The non-app option: Toastmasters

A Toastmasters club gives you what no app does: a real room of people, every week or two, who are also nervous. As of November 2026 its site says clubs meet in person, online or hybrid, guests visit free of charge, and membership is $72 every six months plus any club dues and a one-time $25 new-member fee. The cost is time: a fixed evening, every couple of weeks. Our [Toastmasters alternatives](/speaking-coach/compare/toastmasters-alternatives/) page covers other ways to get a live audience.

## How to practice a specific speech

1. Check the length with the [speech length calculator](/speaking-coach/tools/speech-length-calculator/). A 3-minute toast is roughly 390–450 words at a typical pace.
2. Say it out loud once without stopping, even if it goes badly.
3. Get a review of it and fix the one thing that stands out most: usually pace or fillers at the start.
4. Practice the opening and closing until they're automatic.
5. Do one run with a listener, AI or human, who reacts or asks a question.

## Questions people ask

### What is the best app to practice public speaking?

As of November 2026: Speeko for a daily habit, Orai for a fixed speech, Yoodli for Q&A on a computer, Ovation for a virtual room, and the Speaking Coach app for audience questions on your phone in 20 languages.

### Is there a free app to practice public speaking?

Speeko has a free tier, PowerPoint Speaker Coach is free in preview, and Yoodli gives 5 lifetime sessions. The AI voice review on this site gives two free English reviews a day.

### Does VR public speaking practice work?

It helps some people get used to a room watching them. It still isn't a real audience, so treat it as a step before speaking to people.

### Is an app better than Toastmasters?

They do different jobs. An app lets you practice any time and repeat the hard parts; Toastmasters gives you real people and deadlines. Many people use both.

### Which public speaking app works in my language?

Of those we checked, Orai and Speaker Coach are English only, and Speeko's App Store listing shows English. The Speaking Coach app works in 20 languages, and VirtualSpeech lists work conversations in 20+ languages. Yoodli and Ovation don't list languages on the pages we read.
