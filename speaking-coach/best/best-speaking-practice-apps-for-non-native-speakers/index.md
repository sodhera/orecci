## First, name the problem

Non-native speakers who freeze at work usually have one of three problems, and each needs a different app:

- **"People ask me to repeat myself."** That's pronunciation or clarity. A pronunciation app helps.
- **"I can write it but I can't say it fast enough."** That's fluency. You need a lot of low-stakes conversation.
- **"I'm fine with friends but blank in meetings and interviews."** That's pressure, not language. You need practice in that exact situation, with someone pushing back.

Many people assume the first problem when they have the third. If colleagues understand you in the kitchen but you stall when your manager asks "So what's the plan?", an accent course won't fix it.

## Short verdicts

**ELSA Speak.** Its App Store listing describes detailed pronunciation feedback, a choice of American, British, Australian and other accents, roleplays, interview and business coaches, and IELTS, TOEFL, TOEIC, EIKEN and CEFR prep with Premium. $19.99 a month, or yearly plans listed from $71 to $129.99, with a 7-day trial. **Pick it if** people genuinely struggle with certain sounds you make, or you need an exam score. **Skip it if** your accent is clear and you simply sound different from a native speaker; our guide to [speaking clearly with an accent](/speaking-coach/guides/speaking-clearly-with-an-accent/) explains the difference.

**Praktika.** "Lifelike AI tutors" with different accents who listen, respond and correct you, 1,000+ lessons including careers and job interviews, and the option to upload a resume to rehearse interview answers. It lists 13 languages to learn, from English and Spanish to Arabic, Polish and Dutch. One month of Premium is listed at $9.99. **Pick it if** you need fluency and also want some interview lessons. **Skip it if** you already speak well and only freeze under pressure.

**Speak.** Conversation practice with an AI tutor, with feedback on pronunciation and phrasing. English speakers can learn Spanish, French, Korean, Japanese, Italian and Chinese; non-English speakers can practice English. 7-day free trial, then a subscription. **Pick it if** you want structured lessons that end in real conversation.

**Cambly.** Small groups with native English-speaking tutors and private lessons, plus a Professional Development course. Its English page showed discounted prices from $12 a month for small groups and $28 a month for private lessons. **Pick it if** you want a person to explain *why* a phrase sounds odd. That's still something AI apps do less well.

**Huru.** A free AI interviewer with follow-up questions in English, Spanish, Portuguese, German and French. **Pick it if** you're interviewing in one of those languages and have no budget.

**The Speaking Coach app (ours).** Plays the other side: an interviewer who reads your CV, a manager asking for an update, a stranger at a networking event. It follows up on what you said, then shows your pace, filler words and long pauses, with tips that quote your own sentences, and lets you retry that moment. Screens, voice partner and feedback all work in 20 languages, including Hindi, Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Indonesian, Filipino and Vietnamese, and it never rates your accent or personality. A subscription. **Skip it if** you want pronunciation drills; it doesn't score pronunciation at all.

**Vocal Image.** Voice analysis, diction drills, an accent test and American and British accent reduction courses, plus an AI roleplay. Its listing shows 11 app languages. **Pick it if** you want accent and voice work in one app.

**Duolingo Max** adds Roleplay and Video Call for English speakers learning Spanish, French, German, Italian or Portuguese (Video Call also for Japanese, Korean and Chinese). It's for learners, not people who already speak the language well.

Most speaking-skills apps are English only: Orai's listing shows English, and PowerPoint Speaker Coach works only in English for now, with Spanish, French and Japanese planned. If you need to interview in German or present in Japanese, your options narrow to language apps (Praktika, Speak, Duolingo) for fluency, Huru for interviews in its five languages, and the Speaking Coach app in its 20. The free AI voice review on this site is English only.

## A weekly plan that works

1. **Twice a week, 10 minutes:** fluency practice in a conversation app or with a tutor. Don't stop for every mistake.
2. **Once a week:** one session of a real work situation: your next meeting update, an interview question, a client call.
3. **Before anything important:** say your first two sentences out loud five times. Most freezes happen at the start.
4. **Every two weeks:** record one minute and check your pace with the free [pace checker](/speaking-coach/tools/pace-checker/). Nervous non-native speakers often rush, which hurts clarity more than accent does.

## Questions people ask

### What is the best speaking practice app for non-native speakers?

As of November 2026: ELSA Speak for pronunciation, Praktika or Speak for fluency, Cambly for a human tutor, and Huru or the Speaking Coach app for interviews and meetings under pressure.

### Do I need to reduce my accent to do well at work?

Usually not. What matters is being understood easily. If people rarely ask you to repeat yourself, work on pace and structure instead.

### Is there an app that doesn't judge my accent?

Yes. The Speaking Coach app never rates your accent or personality, and Orai measures delivery, such as pace and fillers, rather than pronunciation.

### Can I practice job interviews in Spanish or Hindi?

The Speaking Coach app works in both, among its 20 languages. Huru supports Spanish but not Hindi, according to its site, and Praktika lists Spanish but not Hindi.

### How long until I stop freezing?

There's no fixed number. Most people notice a difference once they've practiced the specific situation out loud several times, rather than only reading about it.
