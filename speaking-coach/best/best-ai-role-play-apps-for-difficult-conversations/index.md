## Why rehearse a conversation at all

Hard conversations go wrong in a predictable place: not your opening line, which you've rehearsed in the shower, but the second minute, when the other person says "The budget's frozen" or "That's not fair" and you either cave or get defensive. A role-play app is useful only if it does that part: plays the other person, out loud, and pushes back in a realistic way.

We checked every app below on its own App Store page or site in November 2026 and state only what we saw. We make Speaking Coach, one of the options, and say where another tool fits better.

## The apps, one by one

**Speaking Coach (ours).** The scenes are built for exactly this: Ask for a raise, Give tough feedback, Say no, Set a boundary, Deliver bad news, Apologize properly, Explain a mistake, Disagree in a meeting, Handle a rude customer and Raise it with your flatmate. You can also describe your own situation ("my landlord is keeping my deposit") and the voice partner plays that person. Choose Gentle, Realistic or Tough. If you freeze, say "I need a moment" to pause or "Help me" for a sentence to start from. Afterwards you get your pace, filler words, long pauses and tips that quote what you said, then 90 seconds to retry the moment that went wrong. iPhone and Android, 20 languages, subscription.

**iGrow.** The App Store listing describes an AI character that "pushes back the way the real person will", with named characters for salary talks, interviews and management conversations. You can speak or type. After each session you get a score, what you did well and "the exact phrase to say differently". Three free practice conversations, then Pro at $7.99 a month or $59.99 a year after a 7-day trial. Best for: trying it free in English before you commit.

**Persway.** Built around persuasion: it generates a playbook from your goal, with a conversation plan, techniques and objection-handling tips, then lets you role-play with AI and get voice-powered feedback during the conversation. The listing says a subscription is required for full access: $9.99 a week or $19.99 a month, with a 3-day trial. Best for: negotiation-heavy talks like a raise or a deal, if you want a plan as well as practice.

**TalkSimulator.** A simple app whose listing names a boss, a landlord and a professor as scenarios, for salary negotiations, conflict and emotional conversations. The AI taking the other person's role is a Pro feature ($7.99 or $29.99; the page doesn't say which is one-time). The listing doesn't say whether you speak or type.

**Speeko.** Mainly a public speaking app, but its listing includes "Convos", AI roleplays for job interviews, difficult conversations and small talk, with delivery feedback. Best for: people who also want daily speaking exercises.

**Yoodli.** Its home page shows a feedback conversation with "a high performer who missed a target", scored on empathy, specific feedback and agreed next steps, and lists Difficult Conversations Training and Manager Coaching. It is aimed mainly at companies. Individuals get 5 free lifetime sessions. Best for: an employer training a group of new managers.

**Bodyswaps.** A soft-skills simulation platform sold mostly to universities, hospitals and companies; its examples include breaking bad news to a patient and handling a guest complaint. It offers a free trial and runs in a browser.

**ChatGPT voice.** A general assistant rather than a practice app: describe the scene and ask it to play the other person. Tell it to push back, or you'll rehearse an easier version of the talk than the real one. It won't measure your pace or fillers. ## Three conversations and what to rehearse

| Conversation | Line to open with (example) | Pushback to practice | What usually goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asking for a raise | "I'd like to talk about my pay. Since March I've taken over two client accounts." | "There's no budget this year." | Accepting the first no without asking what would change it |
| Giving tough feedback | "Can I give you some feedback on Tuesday's client call?" | "Nobody told me that was a problem." | Softening it until the point disappears |
| Saying no | "I can't take this on this week without dropping the Friday report. Which one matters more?" | "It'll only take an hour." | Apologizing three times, then saying yes |

Rehearse the pushback column, not the opener. Run each scene at least three times: once gentle, once realistic, once tough. If your mind goes blank at the pushback, our guide to [when your mind goes blank](/speaking-coach/guides/mind-goes-blank-while-speaking/) has lines that buy you time.

## How to rehearse well

1. **Write two sentences of context.** Who the person is, what they want, what they're likely to say. Better context makes a better partner.
2. **Say your opening out loud once.** Under 20 seconds. Then stop polishing it.
3. **Set the other person to tough.** The real conversation is usually easier than a tough rehearsal.
4. **Listen to the replay for one thing.** Did you over-apologize, ramble or back down? Fix only that.
5. **Retry the hardest moment, not the whole talk.**

## Questions people ask

### Can AI help me practice asking for a raise?

Yes. Speaking Coach, iGrow and Persway all name asking for a raise as a scenario, as of November 2026. Practice the pushback ("there's no budget") rather than your opening, and run it at a tougher level than you expect.

### Is there a free app to practice difficult conversations?

iGrow gives 3 free practice conversations, Yoodli 5 free lifetime sessions, and Bodyswaps a free trial. ChatGPT's voice mode is free if you prompt it to play the other person and push back.

### Do I need to speak out loud, or can I type?

Speaking is better. Typing lets you edit before you "say" it, which a real conversation never allows. iGrow offers both; Speaking Coach and Persway are voice-first.

### What if the AI is too easy on me?

Raise the pressure setting if the app has one (Speaking Coach has Gentle, Realistic and Tough), or tell it directly: "Don't agree with me. Push back twice before you consider it."

### Is this useful for personal conversations, not just work?

Yes. Speaking Coach has scenes for setting a boundary and raising something with your flatmate, and you can describe any situation; Persway names relationship talks. For anything involving safety or abuse, talk to a qualified professional rather than an app.
