Interview types and how to prepare for each

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence

Phone screens, video calls, panels, case interviews and more: how each one works and how to get ready for it.

  • How to prepare for a phone interview: tips and common questionsPrepare for a first-round phone screen: a quiet voice-only setup, a one-page note sheet, the questions screeners ask, and how to sound clear without a face.
  • Video interview tips: how to prepare for a Zoom or Teams callPrepare for a live video interview on Zoom, Teams or Meet: camera and light setup, where to look, handling lag and pauses, and what to say when the tech fails.
  • What is a behavioral interview? How to prepare with STARBehavioral interviews ask 'tell me about a time'. Build a bank of 8–10 stories mapped to the competencies the job needs, and tell each one with STAR in 90 seconds.
  • How to prepare for a panel interview: tips for answering a groupAnswer several interviewers at once: who to look at, how to handle handoffs between panelists, and how to read the hiring manager, HR, peer and senior roles.
  • What to expect in a second interview and how to prepare for itA second interview goes deeper: harder follow-ups, meeting the team, sometimes a task. What changes from round one and how to prepare in the three days before.
  • How to prepare for a technical interview: thinking out loudIn a technical interview your reasoning is scored as much as your answer. How to narrate your thinking, ask clarifying questions, handle silence and admit gaps.
  • Final round interview: what to expect and how to prepareThe final round is usually with senior leaders: fewer skill questions, more judgement and fit, a closing case for you, and often the first real salary talk.
  • One-way video interview tips: how to do a recorded interviewIn a one-way video interview you record answers to a timer with nobody on the other side. How to fit the clock, answer without follow-ups, and use retakes well.
  • What is a case interview? How to prepare and structure answersA case interview gives you a business problem to solve out loud. How to clarify, structure, ask for data, do the maths aloud and deliver a recommendation.
  • Group interview tips: how to stand out without talking overIn a group interview, several candidates are assessed together. How to speak early, build on others, include quieter people and stand out without dominating.
  • How to prepare for a campus placement interview: complete guideCampus placements usually run aptitude, group discussion, technical and HR rounds. What each round checks, what freshers are asked, and a four-week prep plan.
  • How to prepare for a US visa interview: questions and tipsGeneral tips to speak clearly and briefly at a US visa interview: the kinds of questions asked, how to answer honestly in plain English, and where to find the rules.
  • What happens at an assessment centre? How to prepare for the dayGraduate assessment centres combine a group exercise, presentation, role-play and interview in one day. What each tests, what to say, and how to pace yourself.
  • How to give a presentation in a job interview: tips and timingAsked to present in a job interview? A timed structure for a 10 or 15-minute slot, how many slides to use, how to rehearse to time, and how to handle the Q&A after.
  • How to prepare for a group discussion (GD) round in an interviewThe leaderless GD round in campus and MBA selection: when to open, how to add points and enter a crowded discussion, how to summarise, with example lines.
  • What is a stress interview? How to stay calm and answerA stress interview uses deliberate pressure: silence, interruptions, challenges, rudeness. Why some employers do it, the common tactics, and steady lines to reply.
  • How to prepare for an internal interview at your companyInterviewing for another role or team at your own employer: how to use inside knowledge, why not to assume they know your work, and how to answer 'why move?'
  • Promotion interview: how to prepare and make your case to move upInterviewing for a higher level in your own team? Show you already work at the next level: evidence to gather, how to answer 'why you?', and leading former peers.
  • What is an informational interview? Questions to askAn informational interview is a short career chat where you ask the questions. How to request one, a 20-minute plan, the best questions to ask, and how to follow up.
  • What to say in an exit interview without burning bridgesLeaving a job? What to share and keep to yourself in an exit interview, how to turn criticism into useful feedback, and example answers to the usual questions.
