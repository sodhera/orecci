How to Stop Your Voice Shaking When You're Nervous

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

A nervous voice shakes mostly because of tense muscles and short, high breaths. Steady it by breathing low and slowly before you start, warming up your voice, beginning your first sentence slowly at an easy, comfortable volume, and keeping your first lines short. A two-minute routine of breathing, humming and saying your opening line out loud helps most people.

Why your voice shakes

Your voice depends on steady airflow and relaxed muscles in your throat, jaw and chest. Nerves disturb both. When you're anxious, many people:

  • Breathe high and fast, into the upper chest, so there's less steady air behind the voice.
  • Tense the shoulders, neck and jaw, which tightens the muscles around the voice box.
  • Start too fast or too loud, pushing out the first sentence to get it over with.
  • Hold their breath before speaking, then start on the tail end of an exhale.

The shake is usually most noticeable in the first 30 to 60 seconds. As you settle and your breathing evens out, it often fades. So most of the work goes into the start.

The four fixes

Breathe lowBreathe into your belly, not your chest. Exhale longer than you inhale.Gives your voice steady air and tends to calm the body

Low breathing in practice

Put one hand on your belly and one on your chest. Breathe in through your nose for about four seconds, so the belly hand moves out and the chest hand stays mostly still. Breathe out through your mouth for about six seconds. Repeat five times. This is often called diaphragmatic breathing.

During your talk, take a small low breath at each full stop. Don't wait until you're out of air. The breathing while speaking guide has drills for this.

Why a slower start works

When you're nervous you'll want to rush the opening. But a fast, breathless first sentence is exactly where the voice wobbles. Plan a short first sentence, under ten words, and say it slowly. "Good morning. I'm Leila, and I run the support team." Then pause. That pause is your chance to breathe and settle.

A 2-minute routine before you speak

Do this somewhere private, like a corridor, bathroom or your car, in the last ten minutes before you speak.

TimeStep
0:00–0:30Shake out. Roll your shoulders back three times. Open your mouth wide, then relax your jaw. Let your arms hang loose.
0:30–1:00Breathe. Three slow breaths: in through the nose for 4, out through the mouth for 6. Feel the belly move.
1:00–1:20Hum. Hum on a comfortable note, lips closed, for a few breaths. You should feel a buzz around your lips and nose.
1:20–1:40Lip trill or slide. Blow air through loose lips like a motorboat, sliding gently up and down in pitch. If that feels silly, slide on "mmm" instead.
1:40–2:00Say your first two sentences out loud, slowly, at the volume you'll use.

If you can't speak out loud, do the breathing and a silent jaw and shoulder release. Even that helps.

In the moment: if your voice starts shaking

  1. Pause. Stop at the end of the phrase.
  2. Breathe out fully, then in low. Let the old, tight breath go first.
  3. Slow down the next sentence and drop your volume slightly to a comfortable level.
  4. Plant your feet shoulder-width apart and let your knees soften.
  5. Have water nearby. A sip gives you a natural pause.
  6. Keep going. The audience usually notices far less than you feel. Most people focus on what you're saying.

Holding something light, like notes or a clicker, can give your hands a job, though it may also show a hand tremor. A lectern or a table to rest your hands on can help.

Make the start familiar

Shaking eases with familiarity, so the most useful practice is the first minute, said out loud, many times. Rehearse your opening more than any other part, ideally standing up and at the volume you'll use. Questions people ask

Why does my voice crack when I'm nervous?

Nervous tension tightens the muscles that control pitch, and shallow breathing gives less steady air. Together they can make the voice break or jump, especially at the start.

Can people hear my voice shaking?

Often less than you think. You hear and feel the shake from inside your body. Listeners usually notice your words and pace far more.

Does drinking water help a shaky voice?

A sip of room-temperature water can ease a dry mouth and gives you a natural pause. It won't fix shallow breathing on its own, so combine it with slow breaths.

What if my voice shakes all the time, not just when I'm nervous?

If your voice shakes or changes even when you're relaxed, or it's getting worse over time, see a doctor or a speech-language professional. This guide covers nerves only.

Will my voice stop shaking with practice?

For most people, nerves ease with repeated speaking, and the voice steadies with them. Practicing your opening out loud is the most direct way to get there. The public speaking anxiety guide covers the bigger picture.
