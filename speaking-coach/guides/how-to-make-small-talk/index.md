# How to Make Small Talk With Anyone Without It Feeling Awkward

Start small talk with a comment or easy question about something you both share right now: the place, the event, the food, the week. When they answer, add a small detail about yourself and ask a follow-up about what they said. Skip money, politics, health and complaints about people who aren't there.

## Small talk has a simple shape

Small talk feels awkward when you think you need something clever to say. You don't. It's a short warm-up that tells the other person you're friendly and gives you both a chance to find something more interesting. It follows the same four steps almost every time:

1. **Opener:** a comment or question about the shared moment.
2. **Answer plus a bit:** they reply; you react and add something small about yourself.
3. **Follow-up:** you ask about a detail in what they said.
4. **Name swap:** "I'm Sam, by the way." Then either go deeper or wrap up.

The whole thing can take 90 seconds. If it goes well, you're into a real conversation. If not, you exit politely and nobody's hurt.

## Openers by setting

These are example lines; change the details to fit. The best openers are about something you can both see or are both doing.

| Setting | Opener | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Office kitchen** | "Is that the new coffee machine? Any good?" | Shared object, easy answer |
| **Office, someone new** | "I don't think we've met. I'm Priya, I'm on the finance team." | Clear, gives them your name to return |
| **Monday at work** | "Did you get up to anything at the weekend, or was it a quiet one?" | Offers two easy answers |
| **Before a meeting** | "Have you been in one of these reviews before? This is my first." | Admits something small, invites help |
| **Conference or event** | "Which talk are you here for?" | Everyone has an answer |
| **Networking event** | "How do you know the organisers?" | Opens a story, not a job title |
| **Queue for food or drinks** | "Any idea what's good here?" | Shared, harmless, quick |
| **House party** | "How do you know Jess?" (the host) | The classic for a reason |
| **Party, standing alone** | "I only know the host. Mind if I join you?" | Honest, and most people say yes |
| **Wedding table** | "Are you bride's side or groom's side?" | Built for exactly this |

For more lines by situation, see [how to start a conversation](/speaking-coach/small-talk/how-to-start-a-conversation/).

## What to say after they answer

Most small talk dies at step 2. Someone says "Yeah, it's okay" and both of you stall. The fix is to always give back a little more than you got.

**Add a detail about yourself.** It gives them something to ask about.

> They say: "I'm here for the design talk."
>
> Flat: "Oh, cool."
>
> Better: "Oh nice, I'm here for that one too. I'm hoping it covers mobile, because that's most of my work."

**Ask about a detail, not the topic.** Pick up on something specific they mentioned.

> They say: "I've just come back from a week in Lisbon."
>
> Generic: "Did you have a nice time?"
>
> Specific: "Was that for work or a holiday? I've heard the hills are brutal."

**Use "how" and "what" questions.** They're harder to answer with one word than "did" or "do you".

| Closed | Open |
| --- | --- |
| "Do you like working here?" | "What's been the best part of working here so far?" |
| "Did you have a good weekend?" | "What did you get up to at the weekend?" |
| "Are you enjoying the event?" | "What's been the most useful session so far?" |

If a conversation goes quiet after a few minutes, the guide on [keeping a conversation going](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-keep-a-conversation-going/) covers what to do next.

## Topics to skip

| Skip | Why | Safer swap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salary or what things cost them | Feels like judging | Ask about the work itself |
| Politics and religion | High chance of offence with strangers | Local news that isn't divisive, like a new café opening |
| Health, weight, pregnancy | Personal, often painful | Let them bring it up |
| Complaints about colleagues or the host | You don't know who's friends with whom | Something you've enjoyed |
| "So, are you married? Kids?" | Can land badly | "What keeps you busy outside work?" |
| Long stories about your problems | Too heavy for a first chat | Save it for friends |

The full list of good topics is in [small talk topics](/speaking-coach/small-talk/small-talk-topics/).

## Things that make small talk feel awkward

- **Interviewing.** Question after question with nothing about you feels like an interrogation. Alternate: ask, share, ask.
- **Waiting for your turn.** If you're planning your next line, you'll miss the detail you could have asked about.
- **Fearing silence.** A two-second pause is normal. Smile, take a sip, and say the next thing.
- **Staying too long.** Small talk is meant to be short. Ending it well is a skill: "It's been great chatting. I'm going to grab some food. Hope the talk's good."

## Questions people ask

### How do you start small talk without it being weird?

Comment on something you both share right now, then ask a simple question about it. Ordinary openers aren't weird; trying too hard to be clever is.

### What do you talk about with someone you have nothing in common with?

Ask about what they do with their time and why. People are usually more interesting on "why" than on "what", and you don't need shared interests to be curious.

### How long should small talk last?

Anywhere from 30 seconds in a lift to five minutes at an event. If it's flowing, let it turn into a real conversation; if not, exit after a few minutes.

### How do I make small talk with coworkers I barely know?

Use what you share: the office, the week, a project, lunch. "How's your week going?" followed by a real follow-up works better than any clever line.

### What if I'm an introvert?

Plan two openers and one exit line before you go, and aim for a few good conversations rather than talking to everyone. See [small talk for introverts](/speaking-coach/small-talk/small-talk-for-introverts/).
