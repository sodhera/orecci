How to Structure a Presentation: Simple Outlines That Work

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Pick one of four outlines: problem-solution for persuading, past-present-future for updates and change, three points for explaining, or story for inspiring. Every outline gets an opening of about 10% of your time, a body of about 80%, and a close of about 10%, and every section should support one main message you can say in a sentence.

Start with one sentence

Before you pick an outline, write your main message as one sentence. Not the topic ("Q3 results") but the point ("Q3 was strong, but we need two more hires to keep it going"). Every section of your outline should support that sentence. Anything that doesn't, cut.

Then decide what you want the audience to do or think afterwards. That decides the outline.

If you want them to…Use
Agree to a decision or changeProblem-solution
Understand where things stand and what's nextPast-present-future
Learn or remember a few thingsThree points
Feel something and remember itStory

Use clear signposts between points: "That's the first. The second is…". Put your strongest point last, or first if you might run short of time.

How to build it in 30 minutes

  1. Write your one-sentence message (2 min).
  2. Pick the outline from the table above (1 min).
  3. Fill each part with one line saying what it covers (5 min).
  4. Add one example, number or story per body section (10 min).
  5. Write your first and last sentences in full (5 min). See how to start a speech and how to end a presentation.
  6. Say it out loud once with a timer (7 min). Note which part ran over.

Slides come after the outline, not before. Building slides first is how presentations end up structured around the slides instead of the message.

Short talks need less, not faster

If you have five minutes, don't squeeze a 10-minute outline into it by speaking faster. Cut sections. A 5-minute three-point talk often works better as a two-point talk. For more on very short formats, see how to structure a 5-minute presentation.

Questions people ask

What is the basic structure of a presentation?

Opening, body and close. The opening hooks the audience and says where you're going, the body makes your case in two to four sections, and the close sums up and asks for something.

How many main points should a presentation have?

Two to four for most talks, three being the most common. In a 5-minute talk, two strong points are often better than three thin ones.

Where does Q&A go in a presentation?

Usually after your summary but before your final line. That way you still end on your own words, not on the last question.

How many slides do I need for a 10-minute presentation?

There's no fixed rule. Many people use about one slide per minute, but fewer slides with less on each is usually easier to follow.

Should I write out my whole presentation?

Writing it out once helps you find the words. Then cut it to an outline of key phrases and speak from that, so it sounds like talking, not reading.
