How Many Words Is a 3-Minute Speech? (With a Timed Sample)

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

A 3-minute speech is about 420 words at an average speaking pace of 140 words per minute, and between 330 words (slow) and 510 words (fast). For a toast or any speech with pauses and laughs, write about 340 to 380 words, which is 80 to 90 percent of 420. That is room for an opening, two points and a close.

The word count for 3 minutes

PaceWords in 3 minutes
Slow (110 words per minute)330
Average (140 words per minute)420
Fast (170 words per minute)510
Write this many (80–90% of average)336–378

Conversational English is commonly cited at about 120 to 160 words per minute, and 140 sits in the middle. At three minutes, slow and fast are 180 words apart: a whole extra point. Time the sample below to find your pace, or convert any draft with the speech length calculator.

In pages, 420 words is most of a single-spaced page, or about a page and a half double-spaced, if a single-spaced page of 12-point text holds roughly 500 words. That is a rough rule, so count words.

What fits in 3 minutes

An opening, two points and a close. A 2-minute speech holds one story and what it shows; the third minute buys a second point that the first one sets up. Three minutes suits:

  • a wedding toast from someone close: the maid of honor, a sibling or a parent (maid of honor speeches). Guests should keep to one or two minutes (toasts from guests).
  • a three-minute thesis-style talk, explaining your research to people outside your field
  • a lightning talk at an event that gives three minutes instead of the more usual five (lightning talks)

The two points do two different jobs:

SpeechPoint onePoint two
Wedding toastA story that shows who your friend isWhat you've seen since they met their partner
Thesis-style talkThe problem, and why people outside your lab should careWhat you found, and what it changes
Lightning talkThe mistake that taught you somethingWhat the audience can try this week

What doesn't fit is a third point: after the opening, close and pauses, each would get about 90 words, enough to state a point but not to show it. Three points make a 5-minute speech.

A timing plan for 3 minutes

PartTimeWordsWhat goes in it
Opening20 seconds45Who you are, or the question your talk answers
Point one55 seconds130One scene or one example, told in full
The bridge10 seconds25One sentence that says what point one shows
Point two50 seconds115What changed, what you found, or what to do
The close20 seconds45The toast, the takeaway or the ask
Pauses25 seconds0Laughs, breaths, glasses going up
Total180 seconds360

Words are counted at 140 per minute and rounded to the nearest 5. People leave out the bridge, but it is what makes two points sound like one speech.

A 420-word sample to time yourself

This made-up maid of honor toast is exactly 420 words: three full minutes at an average pace, with no pauses. Read it aloud against a stopwatch.

Good evening, everyone. If we haven't met, I'm Jess, Amara's maid of honor, and for the last twelve years her emergency contact. Theo, I'm afraid that job is yours now. I checked, and the paperwork is mostly the vows you just made.

Amara and I met in our first week at university. I was the girl in room fourteen who cried every night and told everyone it was a cold. Amara lived across the hall, and we had spoken exactly once, about a fire alarm. On the Thursday of that first week, at eleven at night, she knocked on my door holding two bowls of instant noodles and the worst movie she could find. She didn't ask what was wrong. She sat on the floor, handed me a fork, and talked through the whole movie. It was about a volcano erupting under a shopping mall, and she knew every line. The next Thursday she came back. And the one after that. She came every week until Christmas, and by then I had stopped crying and started looking forward to Thursdays. Twelve years later, Noodle Thursday still exists, in three different cities and one very small apartment. The noodles have improved. The movies have not.

That is who Amara is. She doesn't make speeches about caring for people. She just turns up, every single week, with a fork and a terrible movie.

So when she met Theo, I watched him closely. On their third date, she told him about Noodle Thursday, which I thought was a risky move. The following Thursday, Theo knocked on her door with a bag of fresh ramen from the market and a movie so bad that even she wanted to turn it off. He didn't make a speech either. He just came back the next week, and the week after. That is when I knew. In four years he has missed two Thursdays, and both times he sent noodles in a taxi. Theo, you understood that loving Amara means joining the things she has built, not replacing them. You have never once tried to make her life smaller. You make room.

Amara, Theo, I hope your marriage is full of ordinary Thursdays, the kind nobody writes speeches about, because those are the ones that hold everything up. I hope you always knock on each other's door first, and I hope the movies never get any better. Will you all please stand and raise your glasses? To Amara and Theo, and to every Thursday still to come.

The noodle story is point one, "That is who Amara is" is the bridge, and Theo is point two. For a real three-minute slot, drop the paperwork joke (20 words), the volcano line (15) and the taxi line (17). That leaves 368 words: 2 minutes 38 seconds of talking and 22 seconds for laughs and glasses.

Slides for a 3-minute speech

None for a toast. For a three-minute talk, one slide is often enough, and some formats allow no more: the Three Minute Thesis (3MT) competition, which began at the University of Queensland, permits a single static slide, with no transitions, animations or props. Elsewhere, use three at most, depending on the slides: a title and one image or number per point.

What to cut when you're over

At this length, 45 extra words is about 20 seconds, a ninth of your time. Cut in this order:

  1. The third point. Keep the two that connect.
  2. The run-up. Skip "So, to give you some background" and start inside the scene.
  3. The thank-you list or the method. One thank-you by name, or one sentence on how you did the research.
  4. The recap before the close. The bridge already said it; keep the bridge.

Questions people ask

How many words is a 3-minute speech?

About 420 words at an average pace of 140 words per minute, and between 330 and 510 depending on your pace. For a speech with pauses or laughs, write 340 to 380.

How many pages is a 3-minute speech?

Most of a single-spaced page, or about a page and a half double-spaced.

Is 500 words too long for a 3-minute speech?

Yes, for most speakers. It takes about 3 minutes 34 seconds at 140 words per minute, and fits only at a fast 170 (2:56) with no pauses. Cut it to about 360.

How long is a 400-word speech?

About 2 minutes 51 seconds at 140 words per minute: 2:21 at a fast pace, 3:38 at a slow one. With laughs or applause, trim it to about 360.
