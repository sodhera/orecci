How to Improve Public Speaking Skills: A 30-Day Practice Plan

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

You improve at public speaking by speaking out loud often, in short focused drills, and reviewing a recording. A good plan works one skill at a time: record a baseline talk on day 1, spend about 10 minutes a day on pace, fillers, structure, voice, body and thinking on your feet, then record the same talk on day 30 and compare.

How the plan works

Reading about speaking doesn't change how you speak. Saying things out loud, hearing them back and adjusting does. This plan gives you one drill a day, each under 10 minutes, and each one aimed at a single skill. You touch every major skill once in the first three weeks, then combine them in the last.

What you need:

  • A phone to record voice or video.
  • A baseline topic: something you could talk about for two minutes, such as your job, a project or a hobby. You'll use it on day 1 and day 30.
  • A timer.

Do the drill out loud every time, even when it feels silly. Whispering or running it in your head doesn't train the same muscles.

Week 1: Find out how you sound

DaySkillDrill (about 10 min)
1BaselineRecord a two-minute talk on your baseline topic. Don't watch it yet.
2Self-reviewWatch day 1 once. Write down three things you'd change. Pick the biggest.
3PaceRead a 150-word paragraph out loud and time it. Run your day 1 recording through the free speech review to see your words per minute.
4FillersTalk for one minute on any topic. Count your ums, likes and so's. Repeat, replacing each with a silent pause.
5PausesRead a paragraph and mark a pause at every full stop. Read it pausing two counts at each mark.
6VolumeStand at one end of a room. Read to a chair at the other end so it would "hear" every word.
7Rest and reviewRe-record one minute of your baseline topic. Note one thing that's already better.

Week 2: Structure your message

DaySkillDrill
8Point firstAnswer five everyday questions ("What did you do this weekend?") with your main point in the first sentence.
9OpeningsWrite three different opening lines for your baseline topic: a question, a story, a number. Say each out loud.
10ClosingsWrite and say a final line that sums up your point and asks the audience for something.
11Rule of threeGive a one-minute talk with exactly three points, signposted "first, second, finally".
12StoriesTell a 60-second story from your week: time and place, what happened, why it mattered.
13CuttingTake your two-minute baseline and say it in one minute without losing the point.
14ReviewRecord your baseline topic with a new opening and closing. Compare with day 7.

If you want the deeper version of any day, see how to start a speech and how to structure a presentation.

Week 3: Voice, body and nerves

DaySkillDrill
15ClaritySay three tongue twisters slowly, then at normal speed. Read a paragraph exaggerating word endings.
16Vocal varietyRead the same sentence five ways: excited, serious, surprised, quiet, slow.
17EmphasisRead a paragraph and stress one word per sentence. Change which word and hear the meaning change.
18BreathBreathe in for four counts, out for six, three times. Then say one long sentence on a single breath without rushing.
19HandsRecord a one-minute video. Start with hands relaxed at your sides; gesture only to show size, number or direction.
20Eye contactPut three sticky notes around the room. Deliver one sentence to each in turn.
21NervesGive your baseline talk to one real person, standing up. Do the slow-breathing routine first.

Week 4: Think on your feet and put it together

DaySkillDrill
22ImpromptuPick a random object. Talk about it for 60 seconds with no preparation.
23QuestionsHave someone ask you three questions about your baseline topic. Answer each in under 30 seconds.
24Not knowingPractice saying "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" to a question you can't answer.
25RecoveryMid-talk, stop for five seconds on purpose. Look at your notes, find your place, carry on.
26Full talkWrite a three-minute talk on a new topic. Say it through twice.
27NotesCut your notes for the day 26 talk down to six words per point. Give it from those.
28AudienceGive the day 26 talk to two or more people. Ask for one thing that worked and one to change.
29RehearseGive the day 26 talk again with their change.
30CompareRecord your baseline talk again. Watch day 1 and day 30 back to back.

What to look for on day 30

Watch both recordings and compare, using the same checklist each time:

CheckDay 1Day 30
Words per minute (aim for about 130–150 for a talk)
Filler words in the first 30 seconds
Is the main point in the first two sentences?
Clear final line?
Pauses between points?
Hands and eyes steady?

Most people find the biggest change in structure and fillers. Pace and voice tend to take longer. For how to watch a recording without cringing your way through it, see how to record yourself speaking.

Keep it going after 30 days

  • One talk a week. A team update, a toast, a story at dinner: treat it as practice.
  • Re-run the weakest week. If day 30 shows fillers are still your issue, repeat week 1.
  • Practice with someone who reacts. Drills alone build skill; speaking to a listener builds nerve. Speaking Coach gives a daily 30-second speaking prompt and a streak, and its voice partner can play an audience that asks questions afterwards.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to get better at public speaking?

Most people notice a difference within a few weeks of daily out-loud practice, especially in structure and filler words. Comfort in front of large groups usually takes longer.

Can I improve public speaking without an audience?

Yes, up to a point. Recording yourself fixes pace, fillers and structure. You still need real listeners eventually to get used to the pressure.

What's the fastest way to improve?

Record yourself, pick the single biggest problem, and drill only that for a week. Fixing one habit at a time is faster than trying to fix everything at once.

Should I join a speaking club?

A club gives you a regular audience and feedback, which this plan can't. Many people combine both: daily drills at home, a club or group talk once a week.

What if I miss a day?

Pick up where you left off. The order matters more than the dates.
