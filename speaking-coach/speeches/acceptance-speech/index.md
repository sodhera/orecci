# How to Give an Acceptance Speech for an Award or Honor

A good acceptance speech lasts 1 to 2 minutes: thank the people giving the award, name two or three people who made it possible and group everyone else, say in one or two sentences what the honour means to you, and close with a line of thanks or a look ahead. Prepare it even if you don't expect to win, and never read a list of 30 names.

## The four-part acceptance speech

| Part | What to say | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Thank the givers | The organisation, the judges, whoever presented it | 10–15 seconds |
| 2. Thank the people | Two or three names with a reason, then a group | 30–45 seconds |
| 3. What it means | Why this matters to you, or who it's really for | 20–30 seconds |
| 4. Close | A look ahead, a dedication, or a simple "thank you" | 5–10 seconds |

That's 65 seconds to under two minutes. If the event has many awards, the organisers may give you a limit. Stick to it; the next winner is waiting.

## Thanking people without a list of 30 names

Long name lists are the most common reason acceptance speeches drag. The audience doesn't know most of the names, and someone always gets missed.

1. **Pick two or three people** who made the biggest difference, and say *what* they did. "Maria, who read every draft and told me the truth about chapter three."
2. **Group everyone else with a detail.** "The night shift on Ward 4, who covered for me every time I had a deadline."
3. **Thank family last and briefly,** if they're there. "And my wife, who heard about this project at every dinner for two years."

A thank-you with a reason sounds sincere. A name without one sounds like a list.

## Saying what it means

This is the part people remember. Keep it to one or two sentences, and make it about something bigger than you: the team, the people you serve, the path that got you here.

Example lines (made up):

> "This award is for a project, but the project was about getting families answers faster. Knowing that it worked means more to me than the trophy."

> "When I started volunteering here, I was the one who needed help. Being recognised by the same people who helped me is something I'll keep with me."

> "I'm the first person in my family to finish a degree. My parents are in the third row, and this is really theirs."

## Example acceptance speeches

These are made-up examples. Adjust the details and keep the shape.

### Employee award at a company event (about 75 seconds)

> "Thank you, Rachel, and thank you to the leadership team for this. I genuinely didn't expect it.
>
> This was a team year. Dev rebuilt our reporting from scratch so we could actually see where customers were getting stuck. Priya took the hardest accounts without being asked. And the whole support team kept answering the phone through two system migrations, with remarkable patience.
>
> What I'm proudest of isn't the numbers. It's that our customers stopped waiting three days for a reply. That's what this is really for.
>
> I'll put this on my desk, but it belongs to all of you. Thank you."

### Community or volunteer award (about 60 seconds)

> "Thank you to the Riverside Community Trust for this honour.
>
> Six years ago I turned up at the food bank because a neighbour asked me to help unload a van. I never left. I want to thank Joan, who has run the Tuesday shift since before I arrived, and taught me everything, including how to stack tins so they don't fall. And thank you to every volunteer who gives up a morning each week. You're the reason the doors open.
>
> I'll accept this on behalf of all of you, and I'll see you on Tuesday."

### Student or academic award (about 45 seconds)

> "Thank you to the faculty and to the scholarship committee. I want to thank Dr Mensah, whose office hours I attended more than any student should, and my study group, who kept me going through finals week. And to my mum, who drove me to the library every Saturday for four years: this is yours too. Thank you."

## If you didn't expect to win

Prepare anyway. If you're shortlisted, write a 60-second version on a card and keep it in your pocket. If you're truly surprised, use this minimum version, which works anywhere:

1. "Thank you, I really didn't expect this."
2. Thank the organisers.
3. Name the one person who most deserves credit, and say why.
4. "Thank you all."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it falls flat | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I didn't prepare anything" | Sounds unprepared or falsely modest | Have a 60-second card ready |
| A list of every name | The audience tunes out; someone is missed | Two or three with reasons, then groups |
| False modesty ("I don't deserve this") | Can sound like you're disagreeing with the judges | Accept it with thanks |
| Turning it into a career story | The audience is waiting for the next award | One sentence on what it means |
| Unfolding a long page of notes | Signals a long speech | A single small card |

## Delivery

Walk up, shake hands or take the award, then turn to the audience before you speak. Pause for two seconds; it gives people time to settle. Put the award down or hold it at your side so your hands are free. Speak slower than feels natural, and look at the people you're thanking when you name them.

Practise your speech out loud and time it. A speech that feels like 60 seconds in your head is often 90 when spoken. In the Speaking Coach app you can rehearse it with the "Open a presentation or toast" scene and see your pace and filler words afterwards.

## Questions people ask

### How long should an acceptance speech be?

One to two minutes for most events. If there's a stated limit, finish a little under it.

### Who should I thank in an acceptance speech?

The organisers or judges first, then two or three people who made the biggest difference, then groups like your team or family.

### Should I prepare a speech if I might not win?

Yes. A short card in your pocket costs nothing and prevents rambling if your name is called.

### Is it okay to read my acceptance speech?

A small card with key points is fine. Look up when you thank people by name.

### How do I end an acceptance speech?

With a simple "thank you" after a forward-looking or dedicating line. Then step back from the microphone.
