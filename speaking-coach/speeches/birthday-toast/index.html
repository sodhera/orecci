What to Say in a Birthday Toast: Short Examples for Friends

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

A birthday toast should take 30 to 90 seconds: get everyone's attention, say one specific thing about the birthday person (a quick story or a quality you love), add a wish for the year ahead, and raise your glass with their name. One true detail is all it takes; a birthday dinner isn't the place for a long speech.

The 4-beat birthday toast

BeatWhat to doTimeExample line (made up)
1. Get attentionStand, or tap your glass gently, and wait5 seconds"Can I say a few words?"
2. The detailOne quality or a 2–3 sentence story20–60 seconds"Lena is the only person I know who plans surprise parties for other people's dogs."
3. The wishWhat you hope for their year5–15 seconds"I hope this year someone plans something for you, for a change."
4. The glassRaise it, say their name5 seconds"To Lena!"

At dinner, 30 to 60 seconds is ideal. Go up to 90 seconds for a milestone or if you're the host. For a big milestone party with a programme of speeches, see our 50th birthday speech guide.

Picking the detail

Ask yourself: what would everyone at this table nod along to? Good details are:

  • A habit: "Marco has texted me 'leaving now' while still in the shower for twelve years."
  • A moment: "The night my flat flooded, she turned up with towels and a bottle of wine, in that order."
  • A quality, shown: not "she's generous" but "she has never once let anyone pay for their own birthday drink, including on her own birthday."

Skip anything only you two understand, anything about exes or age-related jokes the person might not enjoy, and anything that would embarrass them in front of family.

Short birthday toast examples

These are made-up examples. Keep the shape, swap in your details.

Funny, for a best friend (about 40 seconds)

"Okay, everyone, glasses up for a second. I've known Sam for fifteen years, and in that time he has been late to every single thing I've ever invited him to, including his own surprise party, which is why it wasn't a surprise. But he's never once missed something that mattered. When I moved house, he was there at 7 a.m. Somehow. Sam, I hope this year is everything you want, and that it arrives on time. To Sam!"

Heartfelt, for a friend (about 45 seconds)

"I want to say something about Priya. Three years ago, I was new in this city and knew nobody. Priya invited me to a dinner just like this one, with people she'd known for years, and sat me next to her so I wouldn't feel lost. Half the people at this table I know because of her. That's what she does: she builds tables. Priya, happy birthday. Thank you for building this one. To Priya."

For your partner (about 30 seconds)

"I'll keep this short because he hates attention. Ben, you make every ordinary Tuesday better: you make the coffee, you remember the bins, you laugh at my jokes even the second time. I'm so glad I get to spend another year with you. Happy birthday. To Ben."

For a parent (about 60 seconds)

"Mum turns 60 today, and she asked for no speeches, so this is a toast. Technically.

When I was ten, I wanted to learn the trumpet. Mum didn't know anything about trumpets, but she found a teacher, drove me to lessons every Thursday, and sat through two years of very loud practice in a small flat. She never complained. She just closed the kitchen door.

That's her: quietly making things possible for everyone else. Mum, this year, let us make things possible for you. To Mum."

For a sibling (about 30 seconds)

"As her older brother, I've had 28 years to collect embarrassing stories about Ella, and I'm going to use none of them. Mostly because she has more on me. Ella, you're the funniest person I know and the first person I call. Happy birthday. To Ella."

For a coworker at a team lunch (about 20 seconds)

"Quick one: happy birthday to Daniel, who has fixed my laptop four times this year and only laughed at me twice. Here's to a great year. Cheers, Daniel."

How to raise the glass

  1. Stand up if it's a seated dinner. Standing is the signal that a toast is coming.
  2. Wait for quiet. A gentle "Can I say something?" works better than clinking a glass with a knife.
  3. Look at the birthday person for the detail and the wish.
  4. Turn to the table as you raise your glass, so everyone joins in.
  5. Say their name last. "To Priya." Then drink and sit.

In a loud restaurant, speak slower and a little lower rather than louder. Our tips on projecting your voice help in a noisy room.

Questions people ask

How long should a birthday toast be?

30 to 90 seconds. A minute is perfect for most birthday dinners.

What do you say when raising a glass for a birthday?

End with their name: "To Priya!" or "Happy birthday, Sam. Cheers!" Keep it short and clear so everyone can join in.

Who gives the birthday toast?

Usually the host, a partner or a close friend. If nobody has planned one, ask the birthday person's partner or best friend before standing up.

Can a birthday toast be funny?

Yes. Light teasing works if it's affectionate and everyone can follow it. Ending on something sincere makes the joke land better.

What if I'm nervous?

Keep it to three sentences: one detail, one wish, their name. Short toasts are always welcome.
