How to Give a Toast at Any Occasion: A Simple 4-Part Formula

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Any toast fits four parts: connect (who you are or why you're speaking), the reason (one specific thing about the person or occasion), the wish (what you hope for them), and the raise (glasses up and their name). Most toasts take 30 to 90 seconds. Stand, wait for quiet, and end on the name.

The four-part formula

PartWhat it doesExample lineTime
1. ConnectGets attention and tells people who you are or why you're speaking"For anyone who doesn't know me, I'm Sam, and I've shared an office with Lisa for six years."5 to 10 seconds
2. ReasonOne specific thing about the person or occasion"Every time a project went wrong, Lisa was the one who stayed late and said, 'Right, what do we know?'"15 to 45 seconds
3. WishWhat you hope for them, looking forward"In the new job, I hope they let you stay a little less late."5 to 15 seconds
4. RaiseGlasses up, the name, the toast"Please raise your glasses: to Lisa."5 seconds

That adds up to 30 to 75 seconds. For a big occasion, like a wedding or milestone birthday, part 2 grows into one or two full stories, and the toast becomes a speech. For everything else, short is right.

The formula at eight occasions

These are made-up example toasts. Swap in your own names and details.

A dinner with friends

"Before we eat, can I just say something? It's been a year since we've all been around one table. Thanks to Maya for cooking enough for twenty. Here's to doing this more than once a year. Cheers."

A new job or promotion

"I'm Tom, I trained with Aisha when we were both juniors. She was the only one of us who actually read the manual. Now she's running the department. Aisha, I hope they know how lucky they are. To Aisha."

A work milestone or project launch

"Six months ago, this was a whiteboard and a lot of arguments. Tonight it's live, and the first customers are already using it. Thank you, all of you, especially the people who fixed the bugs at midnight. Here's to the launch."

A housewarming

"Thanks for having us, Dan and Rosa. It took you two years of viewings to find this place, and now I see why. May it be full of people, noise and good food. To the new house."

A family holiday meal

"I just want to say how good it is to have everyone here, including Grandad, who drove himself despite our protests. Here's to family, and to whoever's doing the washing up."

A graduation

"Jake, four years ago you couldn't boil an egg. Today you have a degree in chemistry, which I'm told is mostly the same skill. We're so proud of you. To the graduate."

A farewell or leaving drinks

"Ella, you've been the person we all asked when we didn't want to ask the manager. We'll be lost, but they're lucky. Good luck in Berlin. To Ella."

Honouring someone who has died, at a gathering

"Before we start, I'd like us to remember Dad. He'd have loved this table, and he'd have told the same story about the 1987 cup final. Please raise a glass to him. To Dad."

Toast etiquette in brief

Customs vary by family, country and occasion, but these are widely followed:

  1. Wait for the right moment. Usually when everyone has a drink, before or after the main course, not mid-bite.
  2. Get attention gently. Stand up and say "Could I have everyone's attention for a moment?" Tapping a glass with a knife can work but risks breaking it.
  3. Stand if the group is large; sitting is fine at a small table.
  4. Keep your glass in hand but don't wave it around while you speak.
  5. Any drink works. Water or a soft drink is fine for the toast.
  6. If you're the one being toasted, smile and listen. Traditionally you don't drink to yourself; you can raise your glass in thanks afterwards.
  7. Look at the person when you say their name, then at the room as you raise your glass.

Mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurtsInstead
Starting over noiseHalf the room misses itWait for quiet, even if it takes ten seconds
A list of qualities"Kind, funny, generous" sounds genericOne specific moment that shows one quality
Talking about yourselfThe toast becomes your speechOne line about who you are, then them
Inside jokesMost of the table is lostSomething everyone can follow
No clear endingPeople don't know when to drinkAlways finish with "To [name]"
Going long at a casual eventPeople are hungryUnder a minute

If you're asked with no warning

Use the same formula, compressed: who you are, one thing about them, a wish, glasses up. It takes 20 seconds. For more structures, see how to give an impromptu speech.

Questions people ask

What do you say when giving a toast?

Say who you are, one specific thing about the person or occasion, what you wish for them, and then "Please raise your glasses: to [name]."

How long should a toast be?

Thirty to sixty seconds for most occasions. Weddings, milestone birthdays and retirements can run two to five minutes.

How do you start a toast?

Stand, wait for quiet and say "Could I have everyone's attention for a moment?" Then introduce yourself in one line if not everyone knows you.

How do you end a toast?

Raise your glass and say the name of the person or the thing you're toasting: "To Lisa" or "To the new house." Then drink and sit down.

Do you have to drink alcohol to toast?

No. Any drink is fine, and so is raising an empty hand. The gesture matters, not what's in the glass.
