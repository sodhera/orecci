How to Give an Impromptu Speech When Asked With No Notice

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

When you're asked to say a few words with no notice, pick one ready-made structure and fill it in: past-present-future for celebrations, PREP (point, reason, example, point) for opinions, or thanks-story-toast for people. Take a breath, say one clear opening line, keep it under a minute, and finish on purpose.

The first ten seconds

Someone has just said, "Oh, you should say something!" and the room is looking at you. Before any structure, do this:

  1. Stand up, or step forward, slowly. It buys you three seconds without looking like you're stalling.
  2. Smile and look at the person you're speaking about, or at the room.
  3. Take one breath.
  4. Say a holding line that's true and gives your brain a moment: "I wasn't expecting this, but I'm glad to have the chance."

By then, you've picked your structure. The audience doesn't expect brilliance from an unplanned speech. They expect something short, warm and finished.

Past, present, future

"When I met Anna, five years ago, she was the new hire who reorganised the whole stockroom in her first week without being asked. Today she's leaving us as the person who trained half this team. And wherever she goes next, I'd bet they'll have a much tidier stockroom within a week. To Anna."

PREP

For a meeting or when someone asks your view. See the full PREP method.

"I think we should delay the launch by two weeks. The reason is that support isn't trained yet. Last time we launched before training, we had three days of complaints we couldn't answer. So, two more weeks, and we launch properly."

Thanks, story, toast

"First, thank you to Mark and Jess for having us all tonight. I've known Jess since we were seven, and the first thing she ever said to me was 'Do you want to share my crisps?' She's been sharing everything with everyone ever since: her home, her time, her terrible jokes. So please raise a glass: to Jess."

Problem, solution, benefit

"Six months ago, it took us four days to answer a customer email. The team rebuilt how we sort the inbox, and now it's under one day. That means customers stop chasing us and we stop apologising. Thank you, all of you."

One word

"If I had to pick one word for Dad, it'd be steady. When the car broke down on holiday in 1998, he was steady. When I failed my driving test twice, steady. And tonight, at seventy, he's still the steadiest person I know. To Dad."

How to end

Unplanned speeches usually go wrong at the end, not the start. Without a planned last line, people trail off: "So, yeah… anyway… that's it." Decide your ending before you start:

  • A toast: "Please raise a glass: to [name]."
  • A wish: "Good luck with whatever comes next."
  • Your point again: "So, two more weeks, and we launch properly."
  • Thanks: "Thank you, everyone."

Then stop talking and sit down, or step back. Silence after your last line is fine; the applause will fill it.

If your mind goes blank

  • Repeat your last sentence in different words. It gives your brain a second to catch up.
  • Go back to the person: "What I really want to say is that Sam is great at this job." You can always finish there.
  • Skip to the ending. A short speech that ends well is better than a long one that wanders.

Build the skill before you need it

You get better at impromptu speaking by doing it, not by reading about it. Once a day for a week, pick a random topic (your morning, a person you admire, a recent decision) and speak for 60 seconds using one of the structures above. Questions people ask

How long should an impromptu speech be?

Thirty seconds to two minutes. When nobody expected you to speak, short and finished beats long and wandering.

What is the easiest impromptu speech structure?

Past, present, future. It works for almost any celebration or person, and you only need one thought for each part.

How do I start an impromptu speech?

Take a breath and say a simple, true opening line, like "I wasn't expecting this, but I'm really glad to be asked." Then go into your structure.

Can I say no if I'm asked to speak?

Yes. A warm "I'll just raise a glass" and a one-line toast is a perfectly good answer.

How can I practise impromptu speaking?

Pick a random topic every day, give yourself ten seconds, and talk for one minute using a structure. Over a week, starting gets easier.
