What to Say in a Christmas or Holiday Party Speech at Work

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

A work holiday party speech should take two to three minutes: welcome everyone, name two or three real moments from the year, thank people specifically, and send them off to enjoy the night. Skip targets, forecasts and anything that sounds like a performance review. Short is the kindest thing you can do.

What people want from this speech

At a holiday party, people want to hear three things: that their year was noticed, that the person speaking means it, and that the drinks and food are coming soon. Nobody wants a strategy update. The bar for a good speech is low and the bar for a long one is very low. Two minutes, said warmly, is better than ten minutes of slides.

The structure

PartWhat to sayTime
1. WelcomeHello, glad you're here, a nod to partners or guests15 seconds
2. The year in three momentsSpecific wins or hard moments the team got through45 to 60 seconds
3. ThanksNamed people or teams, and what they did30 to 45 seconds
4. OrganisersThe people who arranged tonight10 seconds
5. Send-offA toast or "enjoy your night"10 seconds

That's about two to two and a half minutes, or 250 to 350 words. Use the speech length calculator to check your draft.

Example speeches

These are made-up examples. Replace the details with your own year.

Example: team manager (about 2 minutes)

"Hi, everyone. Can I steal two minutes before the food comes out? I promise it's two.

First, welcome, and a special welcome to the partners and families who've heard us complain about this place all year. Thank you for putting up with us.

This year we did three things I'm really proud of. We moved the whole support system in March, and nobody lost a single ticket. We hired six new people and every one of them is still here and, as far as I know, happy. And in October, when the warehouse flooded, half of you came in on a Saturday to move stock without anyone asking. I'll never forget that.

A few thank-yous. Priya, for leading the migration with a calm I still don't understand. Tom and the night shift, for covering every gap we had in the summer. And Grace and Leo, for organising tonight, including the karaoke, which I'm told I'm not allowed to skip.

That's it. Thank you for a great year. Eat, drink, and please be kind to whoever ends up singing first. Cheers."

Example: company founder or CEO, larger party (about 2.5 minutes, key lines)

"Good evening, everyone. I'll keep this short, because the band is waiting and they charge by the hour.

Three moments from this year. The day we shipped the new app and the support queue stayed quiet. The week the Leeds office opened and twelve people became forty. And the Tuesday in June when the whole company stopped for an hour to clap Ana's team for landing our biggest customer.

None of that happened because of me. It happened because of the people in this room…"

Example: a non-religious, inclusive version (about 1 minute)

"Thanks for coming, everyone. Whatever you're celebrating this season, or if you're just celebrating a few days off, I hope you get some proper rest. This year was busy, and you made it work. Thank you. Now please, go and enjoy the food."

Keeping it inclusive

Many workplaces include people of different faiths and none, and for some people the season is hard. A few simple habits:

  • "Holiday party", "end-of-year party" or "season" work for everyone. If your workplace calls it the Christmas party, that's fine; just don't assume everyone celebrates it.
  • Thank partners and families, but don't assume everyone has one there.
  • Acknowledge a tough year gently if there were layoffs or losses: "It hasn't been an easy year, and I know some people we'd want here aren't." One line, then move on.
  • No jokes about drinking, people's personal lives or anyone's appearance.

What to leave out

Leave outWhyInstead
Revenue, targets, next year's goalsIt's a party, not an all-handsOne line at most: "It was a strong year"
Thanking only senior peopleEveryone else noticesName teams and people at every level
Long lists of namesUnnamed people feel left out; named people get embarrassedTwo or three names with specifics, then "and everyone in this room"
Inside jokes from leadership meetingsMost people don't get themMoments the whole team shared
Announcements about bonuses or reorganisationsWrong place and timeSave for a proper meeting

Timing and delivery

  1. Speak early, once most people have arrived and have a drink, but before dinner or dancing gets going. Later, attention drops fast.
  2. Get attention cleanly. Ask the DJ to pause the music. Wait for quiet before you start.
  3. Know your thank-you names by heart. Reading names off your phone looks like you didn't think about them.
  4. End on a clear line and step away. Let people get back to the party.

Questions people ask

How long should a holiday party speech be at work?

Two to three minutes. For a small team, one minute is fine. If you're past five minutes, people are waiting for you to stop.

What should a manager say at the Christmas party?

Welcome everyone, mention two or three specific highlights from the year, thank people by name for real things they did, thank the organisers, and send everyone off to celebrate.

Should I say "Merry Christmas" at a work party?

If your workplace calls it a Christmas party and the culture is comfortable with it, it's usually fine. "Happy holidays" or "enjoy the break" includes everyone.

How do I mention a difficult year in a party speech?

Acknowledge it in one honest sentence, thank people for how they handled it, and move on to the positives. The party isn't the place to dwell on it.

Should I give out awards in the holiday party speech?

You can, but keep them short and light. If there are several, present them separately after the main speech; see how to present an award.
