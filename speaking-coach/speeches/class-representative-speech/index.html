Class Representative (CR) Speech: Examples and How to Write One

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

A class representative (CR) speech should last about a minute: say who you are, show you understand the job, make two or three small promises you can keep, give one reason your classmates can trust you, and ask for their vote. A CR's main job is passing information between the class and its teachers, so promise reliable updates and fair representation, not changes only the school can make.

What a class representative does

Your classmates know what the job involves, so your speech has to show that you do too. Depending on where you study, the role may be called class representative, CR, class rep, class monitor or course rep, and your school or college sets the exact duties. Most versions include some of these:

  • Passing on information: notices, timetable changes, test and assignment dates.
  • Speaking for the class: taking problems to a class teacher, lecturer or department, and bringing the answer back.
  • Collecting views: asking classmates what they think before a meeting or decision.
  • Helping things run: taking attendance or keeping order when asked, or helping with a class event.

If you're not sure what your CR does, ask a teacher or last year's CR before you write anything.

Running for a school-wide post instead? Use the student council speech guide.

The 1-minute CR speech structure

You'll be speaking to people who already know you, often in your own classroom. You don't need a long introduction, and they'll judge your promises against what they've seen you do.

PartWhat to sayTime
1. Name and whyYour name and one line on why you want the role10 seconds
2. What you've noticedOne or two real problems in this class, in your classmates' words10–15 seconds
3. Two or three promisesWhat you'll do, and how20–25 seconds
4. Why trust youSomething they've already seen you do10 seconds
5. CloseYour name, the role and a clear ask5 seconds

That's about a minute, or 120 to 150 words at a normal pace. If you're given less time, keep parts 1, 3 and 5.

Promises a CR can actually keep

A CR can't cancel tests or change the syllabus, and everyone knows it. Promise things that depend on you.

PromiseHow you'd keep it
"You'll hear every notice the same day."Post notices and timetable changes in the class group or on the board as soon as you get them
"One list of every deadline."Keep a shared list of test, assignment and lab dates, updated weekly
"Your problem gets raised."Take issues to the class teacher or department within a week, without names if people prefer
"You'll hear the answer, even if it's no."Report back after every meeting with a teacher
"I'll ask about test clashes."When two tests fall on the same day, ask whether one can move

Notice the last one: you promise to ask, not to win.

Lines to avoid

LineWhy it backfiresSay instead
"I'll get tests cancelled."Everyone knows a CR can't"You'll always know what's coming."
"I'll fight the teachers for you."A CR works with teachers, and one may be in the room"I'll take your problems to the teachers clearly."
"Vote for me, I'm your friend."Sounds like you'll represent only your friends"I'll be the CR for everyone, including people I don't sit with."
"My friends put my name in, so…"Nobody votes for someone who doesn't want the jobOne real reason you want it
Jokes about another candidate or a teacherYou look unkind in front of the people decidingTalk only about what you'll do

Three example speeches

These are made-up examples. Keep the shape and use your own class's real problems.

1. Running for CR in school (about 60 seconds)

"Good morning, everyone. I'm Ananya. Most of you know me as the person who always has a spare pen, and I'd like to be your class representative this year.

I asked around last week, and two things kept coming up. We often find out about tests late, and when something's broken, like the fan by the window, nobody's sure who should tell the teacher.

So here's what I'll do. Every notice and test date will go on the board the same day I hear it. And if something needs fixing, tell me, and I'll take it to our class teacher that week and tell you what they said.

I can't promise less homework. I can promise you'll always know what's coming.

Please vote Ananya for class representative. You already trust me with your pens."

2. Running for CR in college (about 75 seconds)

"Hi everyone. I'm Rohan. Some of you know me as the person who shares lecture notes the same night, and I'd like to do that as your CR, but for everything.

Last semester we missed a lab change twice, and half of us heard about an assignment deadline from someone in another class. It's a communication problem, and that's the CR's job.

If you choose me, three things. One: every message from our lecturers or the department goes into our class group within the hour, with the date and room in the first line. Two: a shared sheet with every assignment, lab and test date, updated each week. Three: before any meeting with the department, I'll ask what you want raised, and afterwards I'll post what they said, even when the answer is no.

I've kept the notes group going for a whole year, so you've already seen how I work.

Vote Rohan for CR. You'll never be the last to know."

3. A thank-you speech after being elected (about 35 seconds)

You may be asked to say a few words as soon as the result is announced, so have this ready. For longer thank-yous, see how to give a thank-you speech.

"Thank you, everyone, for voting, and thank you to Meera and Karan for standing too. You both had good ideas, and I'd like to use some of them, starting with Meera's suggestion box.

By Friday every test date we know about will be on the board, and I'll ask our class teacher about the fan. If you want something raised, catch me after class.

I'm everyone's CR now, including those of you who didn't vote for me. Thank you."

Delivering it to your own classmates

Speaking to people you sit with every day can feel harder than speaking to strangers.

  1. Start without a warm-up. Skip "So, um, hi guys". Your first words should be your name or your first point. More ideas in how to start a speech.
  2. Use your normal voice, slightly slower. A speech-competition voice sounds like an act to people who know you.
  3. Speak to the back row. If the people at the back can hear you, everyone can.
  4. Hold a small card with three keywords, not a full page, and look up for every promise.
  5. Expect comments from friends. If someone shouts a joke, smile, pause and carry on. Don't answer back.

Say it out loud at least three times and time it. You can also practise in Speaking Coach in front of an AI audience and see your pace and filler words afterwards.

Questions people ask

What should I say in a CR speech?

Your name, why you want the role, one or two problems in your class, two or three promises you can keep, and a clear ask. Stick to things a CR controls.

How long should a class representative speech be?

About one minute, or 120 to 150 words, unless your teacher sets a different limit.

What do I say if I'm asked to speak with no preparation?

Use three lines: your name, the one thing you'll do as CR, and the ask. For example: "I'm Sam. If you choose me, you'll hear every notice and test date the same day I do. Vote Sam for CR."

Is a class representative the same as a class monitor?

It depends on the school. In some, the monitor keeps order and the representative speaks for the class; in others, one person does both. Ask your teacher what the role involves.
