How to Give Closing Remarks at an Event or Conference

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Closing remarks should take two to five minutes: recap two or three moments from the event, thank speakers, organisers, sponsors and the audience, say what happens next, and end with one clear final line. Don't summarise every session; pick the moments people will repeat afterwards.

What closing remarks are for

By the end of an event, people are tired, checking trains and thinking about dinner. Your closing remarks have to do four things quickly: remind them what they got, thank the people who made it happen, tell them what to do next, and send them off on a high. The welcome speech opened the door; the close shows people out warmly.

The structure

PartWhat to sayTime
1. Signal the end"Before we close…" so people know where they are5 seconds
2. The recapTwo or three moments or ideas from the day45 to 90 seconds
3. ThanksSpeakers, organisers and volunteers, sponsors, audience45 to 60 seconds
4. Next stepsFeedback form, slides, next event, the after-party20 to 30 seconds
5. Final lineA clean, memorable finish10 seconds

Total: about two and a half to four minutes, or 300 to 500 words.

The recap: moments, not minutes

The temptation is to summarise every session. Don't. Nobody needs a list, and speakers whose talks you skip will feel overlooked. Instead, pick:

  • A line someone said that people were repeating at lunch.
  • A moment the room shared: a demo that went wrong, a standing ovation, a question that changed the discussion.
  • A theme that came up across several sessions.

Then connect them in one sentence: "If there's one thing that ran through today, it's that small teams are doing the most interesting work."

Take notes during the day for this. The best closing remarks are written at 3 p.m., not the week before.

Thanks: who, in what order

  1. Speakers, as a group, with one or two named moments if time allows.
  2. Organisers and volunteers, by name if it's a small team. These people are usually forgotten.
  3. Sponsors and venue, briefly and by name.
  4. The audience, last, sincerely. "You're the reason this works."

Ask the audience to applaud once, after the full list, rather than after each name. It saves time and avoids uneven applause.

Example closing remarks

These are made-up examples. Use your own event's moments and names.

Example: one-day conference (about 3 minutes)

"Before we let you go, I want to take two minutes.

This morning, Marta told us that the best clients are the ones you say no to. By lunch, I'd heard three people quoting her. This afternoon, the panel on pricing got so heated that we ran fifteen minutes over, and nobody left. And the question from the back row, about how to charge for revisions, is one I suspect we'll be arguing about next year.

If there's a thread through today, it's this: small studios are not just surviving. They're setting the rules.

Thank you to all eleven of our speakers. Thank you to Leah, Sam and the eight volunteers who've been here since 6 a.m. and who you'll see packing chairs in a minute. Thank you to Northfield Print and Atlas Paper for sponsoring us, and to the library for the room. And thank you to all of you, for asking such good questions.

The slides and recordings will be on the website by Friday. You'll get a short feedback form tonight; please fill it in, because it decides next year's speakers. And the drinks are next door, starting now.

See you next October. Safe travels home."

Example: internal offsite or workshop (about 90 seconds)

"That's it for today. Three things we agreed: we're merging the two support queues by March, Priya's team owns the new onboarding, and we're cancelling the Tuesday meeting. Forever. You heard it here.

Thank you to Tom for organising, and to everyone for actually putting laptops away. Notes will be in the shared folder tomorrow. Get home safely."

Example: ceremony or community event (about 1 minute)

"Graduates, families, friends, that brings us to the end of tonight's ceremony. To everyone who crossed this stage: you did this while working, raising families and carrying more than most people know. Be proud. Thank you to our staff, our volunteers and the families who supported them. Please join us in the hall for refreshments. Congratulations, class of 2026."

The final line

Your last sentence is the one people walk out with. Plan it word for word. Good options:

  • Callback to something from the day: "So go and say no to a client this week."
  • A clear send-off: "Safe travels, and see you next year."
  • A short thank-you: "Thank you, all of you. Goodnight."

Then stop. Don't add "So, yeah, that's it." Step back, and let the applause or music take over. For more on endings, see how to end a presentation.

Mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurtsInstead
Summarising every sessionLong, and people were thereTwo or three moments
Forgetting volunteers or organisersThey notice, and it stingsWrite names on your card
Thanking with applause after each nameTakes forever, uneven reactionsOne round of applause at the end
Logistics buried in the middlePeople miss the one thing they needPut next steps just before the final line
A weak endingThe event fades instead of finishingPlan the last sentence and say it slowly
Running longPeople have trains to catchUnder five minutes, always

Preparing

  1. Write the thanks and next steps in advance.
  2. During the event, note two or three moments as they happen.
  3. Thirty minutes before closing, write the recap and the final line.
  4. Say it once out loud, quietly, to check timing.

Questions people ask

How long should closing remarks be?

Two to five minutes. For a short meeting or workshop, under a minute is fine.

What do you say in closing remarks?

Recap two or three highlights, thank speakers, organisers, sponsors and the audience, explain any next steps, and end with one clear final line.

Who should give the closing remarks?

Usually the host, organiser or chair, often the same person who gave the welcome. That creates a clean frame for the event.

Should I summarise every session in my closing remarks?

No. Pick the moments people talked about most and one theme that connected them. Everything else is in the slides.

How do I end closing remarks?

With a short, planned line: a callback to the day, a send-off or a simple thank-you. Then step back and stop talking.
