What to Say in an Anniversary Speech for Parents or Spouse

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

An anniversary speech tells the couple's story in one or two moments, says what their marriage has shown you, and ends with a toast. As a child, focus on what you saw growing up; as a spouse, speak to your partner directly. Three to five minutes is plenty for a 25th or 50th.

What an anniversary speech is for

At a wedding, people toast what a couple hopes to be. At an anniversary, you get to toast what they actually were: the years, the hard parts, the habits. That's what makes it moving. The best anniversary speeches are specific about ordinary moments, not general about love.

A structure for a son or daughter

  1. Open with a picture (20 seconds). Something specific: "In the hallway at home there's a photo of Mum and Dad at a bus stop in 1976, soaked."
  2. Their story in one or two moments (90 seconds). How they met, a hard year they got through, a move or a business. Not a timeline.
  3. What you saw growing up (60 seconds). The small habits that showed you what a marriage is. The cup of tea, the way they argued and made up.
  4. What it means for you (30 seconds). What you carry into your own life.
  5. Speak to them directly, then toast (20 seconds). Turn to them. Then raise your glass.

Example: a daughter at her parents' 40th (about 3 minutes)

This is a made-up example to show the shape. Use your own family's details.

"Thank you all for coming. For those I haven't met, I'm Sarah, the eldest, which means I've been watching these two for thirty-eight of their forty years.

Mum and Dad met at a petrol station in 1986. Dad had run out of fuel; Mum had the jerry can. She says she felt sorry for him. He says it was fate. Forty years later, they still disagree about that, and about almost everything else, including how to load a dishwasher.

But here's what I saw growing up. Every single night, whoever got home first put the kettle on for the other one. When Dad was made redundant in 2003, and things were tight for two years, the kettle still went on. When Mum was in hospital, Dad brought a flask. I didn't understand it as a kid. Now I think that's what a marriage is. It's not the big gestures. It's the kettle.

Tom and I have a kettle now too. We're working on it.

Mum, Dad, thank you for showing us. Everyone, please raise your glasses: to Linda and Graham, and forty years of tea."

Example: a spouse at a 25th anniversary (about 2 minutes)

"I'll keep this short, because Michael knows I cry at adverts.

Twenty-five years ago I married a man who promised me we'd travel the world. We've been to Portugal twice. But we've also raised two kids, buried both our fathers, moved house four times and painted every one of those houses the wrong colour first.

Michael, you are still the person I want to tell things to first. When something funny happens at work, I'm already writing the message to you in my head. I'd marry you again tomorrow. I'd just check the paint colours more carefully.

Everyone, to my husband, and to the next twenty-five."

The 50th: honouring a long life together

A golden anniversary often has three or four generations in the room. Some tips:

  • Name the generations. "Fifty years, four children, nine grandchildren and, as of March, one great-grandchild."
  • Mention the time it started. A detail from the year they married (the wedding cost, the first flat's rent, the car) lets older guests smile and younger ones picture it.
  • Acknowledge who isn't there. If a parent or friend has died, one gentle sentence is enough: "Grandma Rose would have loved this."
  • Keep it shorter if they're frail or tired. Three minutes, a story, a toast.

Where to find your material

Ask other family members two questions: "What's a story about them I might not know?" and "What do they always say to each other?" Look through photos for a moment that tells a story. Ask your parents directly how they met, even if you think you know; the version you get will often have a new detail.

Mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurtsInstead
A year-by-year timelineGuests drift off by the 1990sTwo moments, told well
"They've never had a cross word"Nobody believes it, and it's not the interesting partShow how they got through something hard

Losing composure without a planEmotion is fine; freezing is hardHave a glass of water and a pause ready

If you get emotional

You will, probably at the part where you speak to them directly. That's fine and the room will be with you. Pause, breathe, and carry on. Questions people ask

How long should an anniversary speech be?

Three to five minutes for a child, two to four for a spouse, one to two for a friend. With several speakers, aim for the shorter end.

Who gives the speech at a parents' anniversary party?

Usually one of the children, often the eldest or whoever organised the party. Siblings can share it, and the couple sometimes replies with a short thank-you.

What do I say in a 50th anniversary toast?

Mention how long they've been together, one story from the early years, what their marriage has meant to the family, then raise a glass to them by name.

Can I give a funny anniversary speech?

Yes. Humour about their habits and differences works well. Make sure the speech also has one sincere moment, usually near the end.

What do I say to my spouse at our anniversary party?

Speak to them directly. Mention something you've been through together, something you still love about them, and that you'd choose them again.
