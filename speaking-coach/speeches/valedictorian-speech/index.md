# How to Write a Valedictorian Speech: Structure and Ideas

A valedictorian speech speaks for the whole graduating class, not just the top students: thank the people who got you there, share two or three moments everyone in the class lived through, draw one idea from them, and send the class off. Plan for 5 to 7 minutes unless the school says otherwise, replace every cliché with a specific memory, and get the draft approved early.

## What makes this role different

You were chosen for grades, but you're speaking for everyone: the student who barely passed, the athlete, the quiet kid in the back of art class. That changes the speech in three ways:

- **It's "we", not "I".** Your own achievements stay out of it, except perhaps one self-deprecating line.
- **Memories must be class-wide.** If only the honours students would get the reference, cut it.
- **The audience is wider than the class.** Parents, grandparents, staff and younger siblings are listening too.

### Valedictorian vs salutatorian

| | Valedictorian | Salutatorian |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional role | The farewell: sending the class off | The welcome: greeting families and guests |
| Where it often falls | Near the end of the speeches | Near the start of the ceremony |
| Tone | Reflective, forward-looking | Warm, grateful, a little lighter |

Schools vary, so ask what yours expects. If you're the salutatorian, spend more of your time on welcoming and thanking, and keep the reflection shorter.

## Structure and timing

| Part | What it does | Time (6-minute speech) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | A specific moment or image, not a definition or quote | 30 seconds |
| Thanks | Families, teachers, staff, in a few lines | 45 seconds |
| Shared moments | Two or three memories the whole class shares | 2–2.5 minutes |
| The idea | What those moments add up to | 1 minute |
| Send-off | Speaking directly to the class | 45 seconds |
| Last line | Short, clear, said slowly | 10 seconds |

At a ceremony pace of about 120 words per minute, six minutes is roughly 700 words. Check with the school for your exact slot: some ceremonies give student speakers as little as three minutes.

## Swap clichés for specifics

Graduation clichés fill time without saying anything. Every one has a specific version that only your class could say.

| Cliché | What to say instead (made-up example) |
| --- | --- |
| "Webster's dictionary defines success as…" | Open with a scene: "Four years ago, 312 of us stood in this gym for orientation, and the fire alarm went off." |
| "This isn't the end, it's the beginning." | "Tomorrow, for the first time in thirteen years, nobody will ring a bell to tell us where to go." |
| "We've grown so much together." | "In ninth grade we couldn't agree on a class colour. Last month we raised the money for the senior trip in three weeks." |
| "Follow your dreams." | "Most of us don't know what we want yet. That's fine. Try one thing this summer you've never tried before." |
| "The future is ours." | "The world will ask us what we're going to do. A better question to answer is what we'll try first." |
| "Thank you to everyone who made this possible." | "Thank you to Mr Ortega, who opened the library at 7 a.m. every exam season and never asked why." |

## Finding the shared moments

Make a list of 15 things that happened to your year, then cross out anything that only one group experienced. Good sources:

- Events that disrupted everyone: a move to a temporary building, a snow week, a strange timetable year.
- Traditions or jokes the whole year knows: a school mascot, a famous cafeteria dish, a phrase a teacher always used.
- Moments where the class came together: a fundraiser, a game, a protest, a memorial.

Ask five classmates from different friend groups what they remember most. Their answers will often surprise you and make the speech more inclusive.

## Example passages

These are made-up examples, to show the tone, not to copy.

**Opening:**

> "Four years ago, almost to the day, we sat in this gym for orientation. Ten minutes in, the fire alarm went off. Three hundred nervous fourteen-year-olds filed onto the football field and stood in the rain while someone found out it was burnt toast. Nobody knew anybody. By the time we were let back in, we'd all met at least one person. Looking back, that's how this school worked: something went wrong, and we ended up together."

**Send-off:**

> "Class of 2026: most of us will leave this town, and some of us will stay. Some of us know exactly what we want, and some of us are making it up. Whichever you are, remember the field in the rain. You'll stand in a lot of strange places with people you don't know yet. Talk to them. That's how it starts."

## The approval process and timing

Many schools review student speeches before the ceremony. Ask early:

1. **When is the draft due, and to whom?**
2. **How long is my slot?** Write for one minute under it.
3. **Is there a rehearsal at the venue?** Go, and test the microphone.
4. **Are there topics to avoid?** Follow the guidance, and ask for reasons if a cut changes your meaning.

If the approved version is what you deliver, you avoid surprises on the day.

## Delivering it

The venue will be large, echoing and full of people moving, phones out. That means:

- **Slow down,** more than you think. Our guide to [using pauses](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-use-pauses/) shows where to stop for emphasis.
- **Wait out cheers and laughter** before your next line.
- **Use printed notes** in a folder, large type. A middle path is best: memorise the opening and send-off, and read the rest with frequent eye contact. See [memorise or use notes](/speaking-coach/guides/memorize-speech-or-use-notes/).
- **Look up for the send-off.** Speak it to your class, not the page.

Rehearse out loud at least five times, standing, and time each run. If you want feedback on your pace and filler words before the ceremony, you can practise with Speaking Coach in the app.

## Questions people ask

### How long should a valedictorian speech be?

Usually 5 to 7 minutes, but schools vary widely. Ask for your exact slot and write for a minute less.

### Should I talk about my own achievements?

Barely. You're speaking for the class. A brief, modest line is fine; the rest should be about everyone.

### Can I include inside jokes?

Only jokes the whole class shares. If the families won't get it, add one line of context or cut it.

### How do I start a valedictorian speech?

With a specific moment your class shared, told as a short scene. Avoid opening with a quote or a definition.

### What if my speech isn't approved?

Ask what needs to change and why. Usually a small edit keeps your message while meeting the school's rules.
