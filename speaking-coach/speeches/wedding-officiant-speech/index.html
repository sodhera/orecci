Wedding Officiant Script: Full Ceremony Wording and 3 Samples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 16, 2026

A wedding officiant says, in order: a welcome, the couple's story, the declaration of intent ("Do you take...?"), the vows in short repeat-after-me phrases, the ring exchange wording, the pronouncement, "You may kiss" and the presentation of the couple. A short ceremony takes about 5 minutes. A personal one with a story and a reading takes 15 to 25 minutes, and the officiant speaks for about half of that.

Full wedding officiant script (fill in the blanks)

It runs about 10 to 17 minutes from welcome to presentation, or 15 to 25 with the music at each end. Round brackets are directions, not lines. Each … in the vows is a pause for the partner to repeat.

1. Processional cue (20 seconds)

"Good afternoon, everyone. We're about to begin, so please take your seats and silence your phones."

(When the music starts) "Please stand, if you're able."

(When the couple reach the front) "Thank you. Please be seated."

2. Welcome (1 to 2 minutes)

"Welcome. My name is [your name], and I'm [how you know the couple]. We're here because [Partner A] and [Partner B] have decided to marry, and they wanted the people who matter most to them in the room when they do. Thank you all for coming, especially those who travelled from [place]."

(Optional, if someone important has died) "Today we also remember [name], who is very much part of this day."

3. Reading cue (optional, 1 to 2 minutes)

"[Partner A] and [Partner B] have chosen a reading for today. [Reader's name], would you come up and share it?"

(After the reading) "Thank you, [reader's name]."

4. The couple's story (3 to 5 minutes)

"[Partner A] and [Partner B] met [where and when], when [one vivid detail of how they met]. [Partner A] remembers [first impression]. [Partner B] remembers it differently: [their version].

[The moment one of them knew, in their own words if they've told you.]

If you've spent time with them, you've seen [one habit guests will recognise]. That's why today makes sense to everyone who knows them: [what they give each other, in one sentence]."

5. Declaration of intent (1 minute)

"[Partner A], do you take [Partner B] to be your [wife / husband / spouse], and do you promise to love and support [Partner B] through whatever life brings?"

([Partner A]: "I do." Then ask [Partner B] the same question.)

6. Vows, repeat after me (2 to 4 minutes)

"Please face each other and hold hands. [Partner A], repeat after me.

I, [Partner A], … take you, [Partner B], … to be my [wife / husband / spouse]. … I promise to listen to you, … to laugh with you, … to tell you the truth, … and to choose you, … every day of my life."

(Lead the same phrases for [Partner B].)

(Or, if they wrote their own vows) "[Partner A], please read your vows first."

7. Ring exchange (1 to 2 minutes)

"May I have the rings, please? [Partner A], place the ring on [Partner B]'s finger and repeat after me. I give you this ring … as a sign of my promise … and of my love."

(Lead the same line for [Partner B].)

8. Pronouncement and kiss (30 seconds)

"[Partner A] and [Partner B], you've made your promises and exchanged rings in front of the people who love you. It is my great joy to pronounce you married. You may kiss."

(Step to one side. Let the cheering run.)

9. Presentation of the couple (10 seconds)

"Everyone, please stand, if you're able. For the first time as a married couple: [Partner A] and [Partner B]!"

Three sample wedding officiant speeches

Three complete example scripts, each with its own length and tone.

Sample 1: short officiant script (about 5 minutes)

For a courthouse-style ceremony or an elopement. No story, no reading; about 5 minutes with the entrance, rings and kiss.

"Welcome, everyone. This will be short, because [Partner A] and [Partner B] asked for exactly that. They already know what they want: each other. All that's left is to say so out loud.

[Partner A], do you take [Partner B] as your [wife / husband / spouse]?" ([Partner A]: "I do." Then [Partner B].)

"[Partner A], say after me: [Partner B], I choose you. … I will be honest with you, … kind to you, … and on your side, … for the rest of my life." (Then [Partner B].)

"Now the rings. As you place the ring, say: This ring is my promise to you." (Each partner in turn.)

"You walked in as two people in love. With real delight, I pronounce you married. Go ahead and kiss. Everyone: [Partner A] and [Partner B]!"

Sample 2: officiant speech for a friend's wedding, with light humour

Made-up names and details, to show how a filled-in script sounds. About 8 minutes with the entrance.

"Good afternoon, everyone, and welcome. I'm Chris. I've known Alex since we were eleven, and Jordan since the day Alex wouldn't stop talking about her. I've never done this before, so if I cry before they do, please pretend you didn't see.

They met at a pub quiz in 2019. The deciding question was the capital of Australia. Alex's team wrote Sydney. Jordan's wrote Canberra. Jordan won the quiz and, she says, the argument that followed, which went on until closing time.

The arguing never stopped. But each of them is always, quietly, looking out for the other. Alex refills Jordan's glass before she notices it's empty. Jordan remembers every one of Alex's work deadlines. They pay attention.

Alex, do you take Jordan to be your partner in marriage, and will you keep paying attention, even when Jordan is right about geography?" (Alex: "I do.")

"Jordan, do you take Alex to be your partner in marriage, and will you be gracious about Canberra?" (Jordan: "I do.")

"Alex, one phrase at a time: Jordan, I promise to be on your team, … to keep your glass full, … and to love you … long after closing time." (Then Jordan.)

"Rings, please. As you put yours on the other's finger, say: Wear this, … and know I'm always in your corner."

"By the power of a very long friendship, and with everyone here as witnesses, I pronounce you married. Well? Kiss! Friends, family: Alex and Jordan!"

Sample 3: non-religious ceremony script with no readings

For a couple who want it sincere and plain: no readings, no prayers, no mention of faith. About 7 minutes with the entrance.

"Friends and family, welcome. There are no readings today and no borrowed words. [Partner A] and [Partner B] wanted this ceremony made of two things only: the promises they make and the people who hear them.

A marriage isn't built in one afternoon. They have been building theirs for [number] years, in [two ordinary things they do for each other]. Today they give it a name.

So I'll ask you first. Will you, their family and friends, stand by them in the years ahead? If so, say: We will." (Guests: "We will.")

"[Partner A], is it your intention to marry [Partner B], freely and with your whole heart?" ([Partner A]: "It is." Then [Partner B].)

"[Partner A], please say these words: [Partner B], you are the person I choose. … I offer you my patience, … my honesty … and my company, … on ordinary days and hard ones." (Then [Partner B].)

"As you give each ring, say: Take this ring … as a reminder of what we said here."

"You have chosen each other in front of everyone who matters to you. With great happiness, I declare you married. Please seal it with a kiss. Everyone, please welcome [Partner A] and [Partner B]!"

The legal part, briefly

Who can legally officiate a wedding, and what must be said or signed, depends on the country, and often the state or region, where it takes place. In some places a friend can become authorised to officiate; in others an official does the legal part and the friend leads a symbolic ceremony around it. Ask the local marriage licence office well in advance who may officiate, whether exact words are required and who signs what. These scripts are example wording, not legal advice, and do not make a marriage legally valid.

How to write the couple's story

  1. Ask each partner separately how they met, when they knew and what they love about the other.
  2. Tell scenes, not a timeline: how they met, the moment one of them knew, one habit guests will recognise.
  3. End on why this marriage makes sense, in a sentence or two.
  4. Leave out exes and anything the couple hasn't approved.

Three to five minutes is roughly 400 to 650 words; check your draft with the speech length calculator.

Delivery tips for the day

  • Pace. Go slower than feels natural. Keep vow phrases to four to eight words, and wait for each one to be repeated.
  • Where to stand. Between the couple and a step behind, facing the guests. Outdoors, voices get lost: see how to project your voice.
  • When someone cries. Stop, wait a few breaths, then offer the phrase again.
  • The kiss photo. As you say the line, step aside so the photographer gets the couple, not you.
  • Your script. Large type, in a folder, rehearsed aloud three times. See memorising or using notes.

Questions people ask

What does an officiant say at a wedding?

In order: a welcome, the couple's story, the declaration of intent ("Do you take...?"), the vows, the ring exchange, the pronouncement, "You may kiss" and the presentation of the couple.

How long should an officiant's speech be?

Four to seven minutes for the welcome and the couple's story together. A full personal ceremony usually runs 15 to 25 minutes, and the officiant speaks for 8 to 12 of them.

What is a simple officiant script?

A welcome, one "Do you take...?" question for each partner, one line of vows each, the rings and the pronouncement. Sample 1 on this page takes about 5 minutes.

What do you say to officiate a wedding for a friend?

Say how you know the couple in one line, tell their story in scenes only a friend would know, then lead the vows and rings in short phrases. Sample 2 on this page is an example.

What does a priest or other religious officiant say?

Religious officiants usually follow the wedding service of their own tradition and add a short personal address. The scripts here are for non-religious and friend-led ceremonies.
