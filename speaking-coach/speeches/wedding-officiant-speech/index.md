# What to Say as a Wedding Officiant: Ceremony Script Tips

As a wedding officiant you guide the ceremony: welcome the guests, tell a short version of the couple's story, lead the vows and ring exchange in small phrases they can repeat, declare them married and present them. Most personal ceremonies run 15 to 25 minutes, and your own speaking is about half of that.

## First, the legal part (briefly)

Who can legally officiate a wedding, and what must be said or signed, depends on where the wedding takes place. The rules differ a lot. In some places a friend can become authorised to officiate; in others the legal part has to be done by an official, and the friend leads a symbolic ceremony around it. Check with the local marriage authority where the wedding will happen, well in advance. This page covers what to say, not the legal requirements.

## The ceremony order

Most non-religious and lightly religious ceremonies follow this shape. Adjust it with the couple.

| Step | What happens | Who speaks | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Processional | Wedding party and couple enter | No one (music) | 3 to 5 minutes |
| 2. Welcome | You greet guests and say why you're here | You | 1 to 2 minutes |
| 3. The couple's story | How they met, who they are together | You | 3 to 5 minutes |
| 4. Reading (optional) | A poem or passage | A friend or relative | 1 to 2 minutes |
| 5. Declaration of intent | "Do you take…?" | You, then each partner | 1 minute |
| 6. Vows | Personal or traditional | You lead, couple repeats or reads | 2 to 4 minutes |
| 7. Rings | Exchange with a short phrase | You lead, couple repeats | 1 to 2 minutes |
| 8. Pronouncement and kiss | You declare them married | You | 30 seconds |
| 9. Presentation | You introduce the couple | You | 10 seconds |
| 10. Recessional | Couple exits | No one (music) | 2 minutes |

## What to say at each step

These are example lines. Rewrite them with the couple's names, tone and wishes.

### The welcome

Start by asking guests to be seated and setting the tone.

> "Good afternoon, everyone, and welcome. My name is Chris, and I've known Alex since we were eleven and Jordan since the day Alex wouldn't stop talking about her. We're here because two people we love have decided to promise each other the rest of their lives, and they wanted all of you to see them do it."

If someone important has died, this is the moment for one gentle line: "We know Jordan's mum, Elaine, is with us in spirit today."

### The couple's story

This is the part only a friend can do. Keep it to three to five minutes, and tell it in scenes, not as a timeline:

1. **How they met**, in one vivid detail.
2. **The moment one of them knew**, if they've told you.
3. **What they're like together** that guests will recognise.
4. **Why this marriage makes sense**, in one or two sentences.

> "Alex and Jordan met at a pub quiz in 2019. Their teams were tied going into the final round, and the deciding question was the capital of Australia. Alex's team wrote Sydney. Jordan's wrote Canberra. Jordan won the quiz, and, she says, the argument that followed, which went on until closing time.
>
> If you've spent an evening with them since, you know the arguing never really stopped. But you'll also have noticed that each of them is always, quietly, looking out for the other. Alex refills Jordan's glass before she notices it's empty. Jordan remembers every one of Alex's work deadlines. That's what I want to say about them: they pay attention."

Avoid exes, embarrassing stories and anything the couple hasn't approved. Send them this section in advance.

### Declaration of intent

> "Alex, do you take Jordan to be your wife, to love and support her, through whatever life brings?"
>
> (Alex: "I do.")

### Leading the vows

If they're repeating vows after you, give them **short phrases of four to eight words**. Long phrases get mangled under nerves.

> "Please repeat after me. I, Alex, … take you, Jordan, … to be my wife. … I promise to love you, … to laugh with you, … and to stand beside you, … for all the days of my life."

If they're reading their own vows, hold the cards for them and hand each one over at the right moment.

### Rings

> "Rings are a circle, with no beginning and no end. Alex, please place the ring on Jordan's finger and repeat after me: With this ring, … I marry you."

### Pronouncement and presentation

> "Alex and Jordan, you've made your promises in front of the people who love you most. It's my absolute joy to pronounce you married. You may kiss."
>
> (Pause. Let the cheering happen.)
>
> "Everyone, for the first time as a married couple: Alex and Jordan!"

## How to prepare

1. **Meet the couple** and ask: how did you meet, when did you know, what do you love about each other? Ask each partner separately, too; you'll get better material.
2. **Agree on the tone:** religious elements, readings, humour, how long.
3. **Write the full script** and send it to the couple for approval, except perhaps the story if they want a surprise. Even then, have one person check it.
4. **Print it in a folder or binder,** large type. A binder looks better than loose paper, and you can rest it on your hand. For more on notes, see [memorising or using notes](/speaking-coach/guides/memorize-speech-or-use-notes/).
5. **Rehearse at the venue** during the wedding rehearsal, including where everyone stands.
6. **Practise out loud at home**, at least three times, at a slower pace than feels natural.

## Delivery

- **Speak to the back row.** Outdoor ceremonies swallow voices. Use a microphone if one is offered, and read [how to project your voice](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-project-your-voice/).
- **Step aside for the kiss** so photographers get the couple, not you.
- **Pause after the pronouncement.** Let the cheering run.
- **Keep your eyes up** during the story; look down for the vows.

## Questions people ask

### How long should a wedding ceremony be when a friend officiates?

Most run 15 to 25 minutes, including the processional and recessional. Your speaking part is usually 8 to 12 minutes.

### What does the officiant say at the start?

Welcome the guests, introduce yourself and how you know the couple, and say why everyone is gathered. Keep it under two minutes.

### Should the officiant tell jokes?

A little humour in the couple's story works well. Keep it kind and approved by the couple. The vows and pronouncement should be sincere.

### How do I lead the vows without them stumbling?

Break the vows into short phrases of four to eight words and pause for them to repeat each one. Practise it with them at the rehearsal.

### Do I have to sign anything as officiant?

Often yes, but it depends on the law where the wedding takes place. Check with the local marriage authority well before the date.
