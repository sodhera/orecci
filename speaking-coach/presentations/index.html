Presentations and pitches

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence

How to structure a talk, how long each part should run, and the questions your audience is likely to ask.

  • How to Prepare a Presentation for a Job Interview (Examples)Prepare a job interview presentation: pick the right angle for the brief, build a 10–15 minute outline, and get ready for the panel's questions. With examples.
  • How to Write an Elevator Pitch About Yourself (30 Seconds)Write a 30-second elevator pitch about yourself: who you are, what you do, what you want. Word counts, a fill-in template and examples for recruiters and new contacts.
  • How to Prepare for a Thesis Defense: Presentation and Q&APrepare for your master's thesis or PhD defense: a slide structure that fits 20–45 minutes, how to time it, and how to answer committee questions calmly.
  • How to Pitch to Investors: What to Say in Your Startup PitchWhat to say when you pitch your startup to VCs or angels: problem, solution, traction and the ask, a spoken example, timing, and how to answer investor questions.
  • How to Give a Sales Pitch That Sounds Like a ConversationPitch a product to a prospect in a meeting or call without sounding scripted: ask discovery questions first, describe value in their words, and handle objections calmly.
  • How to Give a Good Presentation at School Without NotesGive a class presentation without reading your notes: learn it as a few key points, use slides as prompts, handle nerves, and know what teachers usually grade.
  • How to Structure a 5-Minute Presentation (Word Count Too)Fit any topic into 5 minutes: about 650–750 spoken words, 3–5 slides, a second-by-second structure, and a checklist of what to cut when you run over.
  • How to Give a Conference Talk: From Abstract to Q&AGive a 20–40 minute conference talk: write an abstract that promises one takeaway, build a story arc, rehearse on a schedule, and handle audience questions.
  • How to Give a Keynote Speech: Structure, Story and DeliveryDeliver a 30–60 minute keynote as the headline speaker: pick a big theme, build it from personal stories, plan changes of pace, and use the stage with confidence.
  • How to Give a TED-Style Talk: One Idea in 18 Minutes or LessGive a TEDx or TED-style talk: find one idea worth sharing, build it step by step, decide whether to memorise, open strongly and stay under 18 minutes.
  • How to Give a Product Demo That Doesn't Bore Your AudienceRun a live product or software demo customers stay awake for: follow one user's story, show outcomes before features, narrate clicks, and recover calmly when it breaks.
  • How to Give a Clear Update in a Team Meeting (With Examples)Give a short status update in standup or a weekly meeting: done, next, blocked, in under 60 seconds. Examples by role, rewrites and how to share bad news.
  • How to Present to Executives or a Board: Get to the PointPresent to senior leaders or a board: lead with the decision you need, tell a one-page story, expect interruptions, and give short answers to tough questions.
  • How to Present a Quarterly Business Review (QBR) to ClientsRun a QBR with a client or internally: results against goals, what changed, next quarter's plan, a timed agenda, and how to keep it a dialogue.
  • How to Do a Group Presentation: Split Parts and HandoffsPresent as a group or team: split sections by strength, write smooth handoff lines between speakers, rehearse together, and agree who answers which questions.
  • How to Present a Webinar That Keeps People WatchingHost or present a webinar people don't leave: a minute-by-minute run of show, talking to a camera, keeping your energy up with no audience, and handling chat Q&A.
  • How to Run a Project Kickoff Meeting: What to Present and SayWhat to say when you kick off a project with a team or client: goals, scope, roles, timeline, risks and ways of working, with a 60-minute agenda and example lines.
  • How to Present a Research Poster: What to Say in 2 MinutesWhat to say when someone stops at your academic poster: a 30-second and 2-minute walkthrough, example scripts, how to start the conversation and common questions.
  • How to Present a Training Session or Workshop to AdultsDeliver a training session or workshop to colleagues: a teach-show-practice structure, timings for 60 and 90 minutes, how to check understanding, and how to pace it.
  • How to Give a Lightning Talk: 5 Minutes, One Point, No FillerGive a 5-minute lightning talk at a meetup or conference: pick one point, plan auto-advancing slides like Ignite's 20 x 15 seconds, and rehearse tightly to time.
