Are you planning higher studies or an MBA? How to answer

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Say honestly that your focus for the next few years is building experience in this job, and if you might study later, frame it as something you'd consider after gaining real work experience, possibly with the company's support. The interviewer is checking whether you'll leave within a year, so show commitment to the role without making promises you won't keep.

Why HR asks this in campus placements

In campus HR rounds, especially in India, this question is almost standard. The reason is simple: a company hiring freshers spends months training them. If you leave after a year for an MS abroad, a CAT-route MBA or a government exam, that investment is lost. Some candidates do exactly that, so HR asks everyone.

What they want to hear is not "never". It is that your next step is this job, and that you plan to stay long enough to learn and contribute.

Match your answer to your real situation

Don't lie. Interviewers ask follow-ups, and an offer built on a promise you plan to break is a bad start for you too. Find your situation below.

Your real situationWhat to sayWhat to avoid
No plans to study furtherThat you want to grow through work, and why"Never", which can sound unthinking
Might do an MBA somedayThat you'd want several years of work experience first, which makes an MBA more valuableNaming exam dates or target colleges
Interested in a part-time or executive programmeThat you'd look at options that fit around work, ideally ones the company supportsImplying the job is a stopgap
Want to study but undecidedThat right now you want to understand the industry before choosing a specialisationSaying "I'll see how it goes here"
Already applied or preparing seriously for an examThink hard before taking a seat in this process; if you continue, be honest about timelinesSaying no and leaving in six months

On the last row: if you are already set on leaving soon, a campus offer you'll quit can affect your college's relationship with that company and the juniors who come after you. That is worth weighing.

A simple structure

  1. Clear focus now. "My focus for the next few years is to work and learn on the job."
  2. The reason. Why work experience matters to you at this stage.
  3. Honest about the future, if relevant. "I may look at an MBA later, but only after I've got real experience, and ideally in a way that fits around work."
  4. Tie it back to this company. Something specific about the training, projects or growth here.

Keep it to 30 to 45 seconds. A long answer to this question sounds like you are trying to convince yourself.

Example answers

These use made-up details. Adapt to your own situation and the company.

No plans for further study

"Not right now. After four years of engineering, I want to learn how real systems are built and maintained, and I think I'll learn more from two or three years on actual projects than from another degree. That's a big reason I'm interested in your graduate programme, because of the rotation across product teams."

Considering an MBA later

"Possibly an MBA someday, but not for at least four or five years. An MBA is much more useful once you've worked and know what you want from it. Right now I want to go deep in analytics, and if later I study further, I'd ideally do it in a way that builds on what I've done here, like an executive programme."

Asked "Why not MS, like many of your classmates?"

"I did think about it. But I'd rather know what I want to specialise in before spending two years and a lot of money on it. Working first will tell me that. If I do study later, it will be with a much clearer goal."

Asked about government exams

"No, I'm not preparing for any. I chose to sit for placements because I want to work in the private sector, and specifically in a role like this one."

Prepare for the pushback

HR interviewers often push once to see if your answer holds. "Are you sure? Most of your batch wants to go abroad." Stay calm and repeat the core of your answer in different words. Don't add new details under pressure; that's when people over-promise.

Questions people ask

Is it bad to say I want to do an MBA someday?

No. Many good employees do one later. The key is "later": after real work experience, not next year.

What if the company has a service agreement or bond?

Some campus employers ask freshers to commit to a minimum period. Know the terms before the interview, and make sure your answer about further study fits them.

Should I mention that I've already taken the GRE or GMAT?

If they ask directly, don't deny it. Explain that you took it to keep options open and that your plan now is to work. Then be sure that is true.

Can I ask whether the company supports higher studies?

Yes, near the end of the interview, if you frame it as long-term growth: "Do people here ever do part-time programmes while working?"

What if I genuinely don't know my plans?

That's normal. Say your focus is on working and learning now, and that you'll decide about further study once you know which area you want to specialise in.
