A short self-portrait, not a career story

Many people answer this the way they'd answer "Tell me about yourself" and launch into their work history. That's a different question. Here, the interviewer wants to know what you're like to work with, in a few traits, backed by evidence.

Here is how this question compares with its close relatives:

QuestionWhat they wantLength
Tell me about yourselfYour story: now, before, next60–90 seconds
How would you describe yourself?Two or three traits with proof20–40 seconds
Describe yourself in three wordsExactly three words, one line eachAbout 30 seconds
How would your coworkers describe you?How others experience you30–45 seconds

Choose traits, then find the proof

A trait is how you work. Proof is what you did that shows it.

  1. Write down five traits that are true of you at work.
  2. Cross out any the job doesn't need.
  3. For each of the rest, write one thing you did that a colleague would remember.
  4. Keep the two or three with the most specific proof.

A trait without proof is a claim. A trait with proof is evidence. The difference sounds like this:

Claim onlyTrait with proof
"I'm organised.""I'm organised. I'm the one who keeps the team tracker, and people check it before they ask me."
"I'm a good communicator.""I explain things clearly. New starters on my team get my one-page guide to our systems on day one."
"I'm calm.""I stay calm when things go wrong. When our booking system crashed on a Saturday, I moved us to paper in ten minutes."
"I'm curious.""I'm curious. I read our customer complaints every week, which is how I spotted the delivery problem before anyone asked."

A simple formula

"I'd describe myself as [trait] and [trait]. [Proof for the first]. [Proof for the second]. Both matter a lot in a role like this."

Optional third trait if it adds something new. Then stop. Silence after a confident answer is fine; let them pick what to ask about.

Example answers

Made-up examples to show the formula. Pick traits and proof from your own work.

Project coordinator

"I'd describe myself as organised and persistent. I keep a running list of every open item on a project, and I'm usually the person who notices when something has been sitting untouched for a week. And I follow up until things are actually done. On my last project, chasing three suppliers every two days is what got our launch out on time. Both feel like the core of this job."

Retail sales associate

"I'm friendly and I'm steady under pressure. I like talking to customers, and I've had regulars ask for me by name at the shoe shop where I work now. On Saturdays when the queue is out the door, I'm usually the one on the till, because I keep going without getting flustered."

Software developer

"I'm careful and I like to explain things. I write tests before I change anything risky, which has caught a few bugs in our payment code that would have been painful in production. And I enjoy explaining technical things to non-developers. I run a short demo for the support team every release so they know what changed."

Recent graduate

"I'd say I'm reliable and quick to learn. I worked part-time all through university and never missed a shift. And when my placement team switched to a new reporting tool halfway through the summer, I learned it in a weekend and ended up showing the others how to use it."

Personal or professional?

Keep it mostly professional, since you're being assessed for a job. A personal touch can work as a final line if it reveals a useful trait: "Outside work I run long distances, so I'm used to steady progress over months." Avoid anything about age, relationships, religion or health.

How to practise it

Because this answer is short, small habits stand out: filler words at the start, rising tone at the end of each sentence, rushing. Say yours out loud a few times and record one. Questions people ask

How is "How would you describe yourself?" different from "Tell me about yourself"?

"Tell me about yourself" wants your story, usually present, past and future. "How would you describe yourself?" wants a few traits, each backed by proof, in about 30 seconds.

How many traits should I mention?

Two or three. Each needs a short piece of proof, and more than three makes the answer sound like a list.

Can I describe myself as introverted?

Yes, if you pair it with a strength: "I'm on the quiet side, which means I listen carefully. Clients often tell me I'm the first person who actually understood their problem."

Should I use the same traits as my strengths answer?

They can overlap. Strengths are about what you're good at; this question is about what you're like. Using one shared trait links the two answers nicely.
