What is the last book you read? How to answer in an interview

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Name a book you've genuinely read recently and could discuss for two minutes if asked. Say in one sentence what it's about, share the single idea that stuck with you, and give an example of how that idea shows up in your work or thinking. It doesn't have to be a business book, but avoid anything you'd struggle to talk about or that invites a debate.

Why they ask

It sounds like small talk, and sometimes it is. But it also shows:

  • Curiosity. Do you keep learning outside work?
  • How you think. Can you pull a clear idea out of something you read and explain it?
  • Honesty. Did you really read it? Follow-ups catch people out quickly.
  • A bit of personality, which helps the interviewer remember you.

The real test isn't the title. It's whether you can talk about it clearly and connect it to something.

How to choose the book

  1. Pick something you finished, or most of it, in the last year. "Last book" doesn't have to mean literally the last one, but it should be recent.
  2. Make sure you can talk for two minutes. If you can't explain the main idea and one detail, choose another.
  3. Have one idea you can link to work. Not the whole book, just one idea.
  4. Avoid books that start debates. Strongly political or religious books, or anything that might make the interviewer uncomfortable.
  5. Don't pick a book just to impress. If the interviewer has read it and asks about chapter three, you need to be ready.

Which kind of book to choose

A structure for your answer

  1. Title and author, plus one sentence on what it's about.
  2. The idea that stuck with you.
  3. How it connects to your work, or how you've used it.
  4. Optional: why you'd recommend it, in one line.

About 45 seconds is enough. If they want more, they'll ask.

Example answers

These are examples of the structure. Use a book you have actually read; the interviewer may have read it too.

Example answer with a work-related book

"The last one I finished was Atomic Habits by James Clear. It's about building habits through very small changes. The idea that stuck with me was making the good habit the easy option. I used it at work: I put our team's weekly checklist at the top of the shared drive instead of in a folder, and people started actually using it."

Example answer with a novel

"I just finished Klara and the Sun by Kazuo Ishiguro. It's told by a robot who looks after a sick child. What stayed with me was how much Klara notices by watching people carefully. I work in customer support, and it made me think about how much I can learn by listening closely instead of rushing to answer."

Example answer with a biography

"I read a biography of a scientist I'd only heard of in school. What surprised me was how many failed experiments came before the famous result. It made me more patient with early drafts. When a first version of a report doesn't work, I see it as part of the process."

Example answer if you don't read many books

"Honestly, I read more long articles than books. The last thing I read properly was a long piece on how hospitals reduce waiting times. One idea was moving simple checks earlier so patients wait less later. I've been thinking about how that might apply to our booking process."

Mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurtsTry instead
Naming a book you haven't readFollow-ups expose it fastOnly name what you've read
A plot summary with no pointDoesn't show thinkingOne idea, then the link
A controversial bookStarts a debate you don't needChoose something neutral

Practice it out loud

Explaining an idea from a book is harder out loud than in your head. Say your answer once, then imagine two follow-ups: "What did you disagree with?" and "Would you recommend it to our team?" Answer both aloud. The interview answer review can show if you're rushing or filling pauses with "um."

Questions people ask

Does it have to be a business book?

No. A novel or biography can be more memorable. What matters is that you can say one idea from it and why it stayed with you.

Is it okay to mention an audiobook?

Yes. Just say "I listened to…" Most interviewers won't mind how you read it.

What if I haven't read anything recently?

Mention the last thing you read in depth, like a long article, a report or a course, and treat it the same way: one idea, one link.

Should I choose a famous book?

Only if you have your own angle on it. Popular books are fine, but many candidates mention them, so your example of using the idea matters more.

What if the interviewer hasn't heard of it?

That's fine. Your one-sentence summary will explain it, and it may make your answer more memorable.
