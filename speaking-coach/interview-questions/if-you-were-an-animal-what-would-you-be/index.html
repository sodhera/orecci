Why interviewers ask odd questions

Curveball questions like "If you were an animal…", "How many tennis balls fit in this room?" or "What would you do with a million dollars?" are less common than standard questions, but some interviewers still use them, especially in graduate, sales and creative roles. They're not trying to trick you. Usually they want to see:

  • How you think when you can't prepare. Do you freeze, laugh nervously, or calmly reason it out?
  • A bit of personality. A moment off-script.
  • Whether you can connect anything back to the job.

There's rarely a right answer. There's a right way of answering.

The 3-step formula for any odd question

  1. Pause and pick. Take two seconds. Say "Good question, let me think," if you need to. Then commit to one answer, not three.
  2. Give one reason. Tie it to a trait that's true of you.
  3. Link to work. One sentence with a quick, real example.

That's about 15 to 25 seconds. Stop there. If they want more, they'll ask.

Animal answers with work links

You don't need a clever animal. You need a reason that fits you and the job. Some examples:

AnimalTraitFits roles like
OctopusHandles many tasks at onceCoordinator, assistant, operations

Avoid animals that suggest traits you don't want to highlight, like a sloth or a lone wolf, unless you can make it a clear joke and still land a good point.

Example answers

These are examples. Pick an animal and a reason that are genuinely true of you.

Example answer for a coordinator role

"Probably an octopus, because I'm good at keeping a lot of things moving at once. In my last role I coordinated three product launches in the same month, and I kept them on track with one shared plan and a quick check-in each morning."

Example answer for a nursing or care role

"An elephant, because they remember everything and look after their group. I'm known on my ward for remembering small things about patients, like who takes their tea without milk, and it makes a real difference to how settled they feel."

Example answer for a graduate in sales

"A dolphin. They're curious and they work in groups. I like meeting people and finding out what they need. At my part-time job at the phone shop, I was usually the one who asked customers what they actually used their phone for, and that's how I sold the most protection plans on my team two months running."

For estimation questions, the reasoning is the answer. Say your assumptions out loud.

Questions people ask

Is there a wrong animal?

Not really, but avoid animals with obviously negative traits unless you turn it into a strength. The reason and the work link matter far more than the animal.

Why do companies ask these questions?

Usually to see how you think on the spot and to get a sense of your personality. Some interviewers just like them as a break from standard questions.

What if I can't think of anything?

Say "Let me think for a second," take a breath, and pick the first reasonable answer. A calm, simple answer is fine.

Should I prepare an animal in advance?

It doesn't hurt to have one in mind, but it's more useful to practise the formula so you can handle any odd question.

Can I make it funny?

A little humour is fine if it suits you, but always end with a real point about how you work.
