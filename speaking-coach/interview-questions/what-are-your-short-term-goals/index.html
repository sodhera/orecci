What are your short-term goals? Answers for freshers

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Give one or two specific goals for the next one to two years that you can reach in this job: a skill you want to learn properly and a contribution you want to make. Say how you'll work toward them. Short-term goals should be concrete and close to the role, unlike five-year goals, which can be broader.

Short-term versus long-term goals

Freshers are often asked both questions in the same interview, and many give the same answer to both. The interviewer is listening for whether you can tell them apart.

Short-term goalsLong-term goals
Time frameNext 6 months to 2 years3 to 10 years
SizeSpecific and measurableBroader direction
Typical contentLearn a skill, finish training, own a task, earn a certificationLead a team, become an expert, move into a specialised area
Where they happenInside this jobOften beyond it
What they showYou're ready to learn and contribute nowYou have direction and a reason to stay

For the long-term version, see where do you see yourself in five years.

What a good short-term goal sounds like

A good short-term goal for a fresher has three features:

  • It's about this job. A goal you'd reach by doing the work they're hiring for.
  • It's specific. "Learn SQL well enough to build reports on my own" is specific. "Grow as a professional" is not.
  • It has a how. One sentence about how you'll get there: training, a mentor, practice, a certification.

Pair a learning goal with a contribution goal. Learning shows you're coachable; contribution shows you're thinking about what you'll give back.

A simple structure

  1. Learning goal. "In my first year, I want to…"
  2. How. "I plan to do that by…"
  3. Contribution goal. "By the end of my second year, I'd like to be…"
  4. Link to the company. "Your training programme/team structure makes that possible because…"

That's around 30–45 seconds, which is the right length for this question in a campus or fresher interview.

Example answers by field

These examples use invented details. Adapt them to your own course and the role.

Engineering graduate, software trainee role

"My first short-term goal is to finish your training programme well and become comfortable working in a real codebase, not just college projects. I want to learn how code is reviewed and tested in a team. By the end of my first year, I'd like to be trusted to take a small feature from start to finish on my own. Your structure of pairing freshers with a senior developer is one of the reasons I applied."

Commerce graduate, accounts or finance trainee

"In the next year, I want to learn the full monthly closing process hands-on, because so far I've only studied it in theory. I also want to clear the next level of my professional exams within 18 months. By then, I'd like to be handling a set of client accounts on my own. I know your firm supports trainees through exams, which matters to me."

MBA or management graduate, sales trainee

"My short-term goal is to learn your products and customers well enough to meet my targets within my first six months. I want to understand why customers say yes and why they don't. In the second year, I'd like to be one of the trainees considered for a key account role."

Arts or humanities graduate, content or communications role

"In the first year, I want to learn how to write for different channels: web, email and social. I've mostly written long essays, so writing short and clearly is a skill I want to build. I'd also like to learn your analytics tools so I can see what's working. By the second year, I'd like to run a small campaign myself."

Nursing graduate

"My main short-term goal is to settle in safely as a new nurse: complete my preceptorship, become confident with the ward routines, and know when to ask for help. In the second year, I'd like to take on a link nurse role in an area like infection control, which I enjoyed on placement."

Goals to avoid saying

GoalWhy it's riskyBetter version
"To do an MBA next year"Signals you'll leave soon"To learn the business side here, and maybe study later"
"To get promoted quickly"Sounds like it's only about titles"To be trusted with my own accounts by year two"
"To earn well and settle down"Not about the jobA learning or contribution goal
"To learn new things"Too vagueName the skill and how you'll learn it
"To move abroad"They wonder if you'll stayLeave it out unless the role involves it

How to prepare

  1. Read the job description and list the skills you don't have yet. One of these is your learning goal.
  2. Find out how the company trains freshers: a programme, a mentor, certifications. Mention it.
  3. Write your answer in four sentences, using the structure above.
  4. Say it out loud three times and time it. In a campus placement interview, short and clear answers stand out.

Questions people ask

How many short-term goals should I mention?

Two is ideal: one learning, one contribution. More than three sounds like a list rather than a plan.

Can my short-term goal be a certification?

Yes, especially if it's relevant to the job. Say how it will help you do the work better, not just that you want the certificate.

Is it okay to say I don't know yet?

It's better to give a modest, specific goal than to say you don't know. "To learn the job properly and become reliable on my own" is a fine start if you add how.

What's the difference between short-term goals and career goals?

Career goals usually mean long-term direction. Short-term goals are the next steps toward it. You can connect them: "Long term I want to lead projects; in the short term I want to learn how projects are planned here."

Should my answer match my answer to 'Tell me about yourself'?

Yes. If you ended tell me about yourself with what you want to do next, your short-term goals should continue that thread.
