What is your greatest achievement? How to answer, with examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Choose one achievement that shows a skill this role needs, explain briefly what made it hard, and put a number on the result. Graduates can use a project, a part-time job, a volunteer role or a sport, as long as the effort and the outcome were real and mostly yours.

"Greatest" means most relevant

The interviewer isn't asking for the biggest thing you've ever done. They want to see what you count as success, how you work toward it, and whether that matches the job. The achievement that impresses them is the one that looks like the work they need done.

So "greatest" here means: the most relevant thing you're genuinely proud of.

Pick the right achievement

Score each candidate against five questions:

TestAsk yourself
RelevantDoes it show a skill this job asks for?
RecentIs it from the last few years, so it reflects you now?
YoursDid you drive it, rather than just being there?
MeasurableCan you put a number or a clear before-and-after on it?
HardWas there a real obstacle, so it's a story and not a fact?

The one that scores highest wins, even if something else felt bigger at the time.

How to put a number on it

If you think your achievement has no numbers, look again:

  • Before and after: "Month-end used to take nine days; now it takes five."
  • Saved: hours per week, money per month, steps in a process.
  • Grew: members, sales, followers, attendance, repeat customers.
  • Reduced: errors, complaints, waiting time, returns.
  • Reached: people trained, customers served, events run.
  • Ranked: top of the class, second out of 40 teams, fastest on the shift.

Estimates are fine if honest: "about a third", "roughly 200 people". Don't invent precision you don't have, because the follow-up will be "How did you measure that?"

A five-part answer

  1. What it was, in one sentence.
  2. Why it was hard: the obstacle, the stakes or the starting point.
  3. What you did: two or three specific actions.
  4. The result, with a number.
  5. Why it matters here: one line linking it to the role.

About 60–90 seconds. It's essentially a STAR answer with extra weight on the result.

Example answers

Made-up examples to show the structure. Use your own achievement and real figures.

Experienced hire: accounts payable

"My greatest achievement at work was cutting invoice errors in our accounts payable team. When I joined, about one in ten supplier invoices needed correcting after payment, which meant awkward calls and late fees. I mapped where the errors came from, and most were from purchase orders that didn't match. So I set up a three-way check before payment and trained the team of five on it. Within six months, corrections dropped to about one in fifty, and we stopped paying late fees completely. I think that matters here, because your job post talks about cleaning up the payables process."

Graduate: a part-time job

"I'm proudest of something from my part-time job at a garden centre. I noticed new seasonal staff were taking weeks to learn the tills and the plant care basics, so I wrote a two-page starter guide and offered to run a short session each spring. My manager let me try it with eight new starters. They were working on their own in about a week instead of three, and the manager now gives the guide to every new hire. It taught me I like making things easier for other people, which is a lot of what a training coordinator does."

Graduate: a university society

"As treasurer of our university volunteering society, I organised a charity quiz night when our usual summer event was cancelled. I had five weeks, no budget and a committee of three. I booked a free room by partnering with the students' union, got local cafés to donate prizes, and sold tickets through every society's mailing list. We had 160 people and raised just over 2,000 for a local shelter, which was more than the event it replaced. It's the first time I managed something from start to finish, and it's the part of this events role I'm most looking forward to."

Career returner

"During my career break I chaired the parents' association at my children's school. The playground needed resurfacing, and the school had no budget for it. Over a year, I led a group of six parents through two grant applications and a summer fair. We raised enough for the whole project, and it opened that autumn. It reminded me I'm good at keeping a small team moving toward a long deadline, which is what this project officer role needs."

What about personal achievements?

A marathon, learning a language or raising a family are real achievements. Use them only if the interviewer asks broadly ("in your life") or if you have nothing work-related that fits. Then link it to work: what it shows about discipline, planning or persistence.

Mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurtsTry instead
A team win where your part is unclearThey can't tell what you didSay exactly which part was yours
A degree or award with no storyIt's on your CV alreadyTell what was hard about earning it
Something from ten years agoSuggests nothing sincePick something from the last few years
Inflated numbersFalls apart in follow-upUse honest estimates and say they're estimates
Several achievements in one answerNone get proper attentionOne achievement, told well

How to practise it

Tell your achievement out loud and listen for the result. Is it crisp, with a number, or does it trail off? Then answer the likely follow-ups: "How did you measure that?" and "What would you do differently?" Questions people ask

What if I don't think I have a great achievement?

You almost certainly have something: a process you improved, a person you trained, a project you finished under pressure. It doesn't need to be impressive in general, only relevant and real.

Can I use an academic achievement?

Yes, especially as a graduate. Focus on what was hard about it, like a dissertation with a difficult dataset, rather than just the grade.

Should my greatest achievement be work-related?

Ideally, yes. If the interviewer asks about your life in general, a personal achievement is fine, as long as you link it to a skill the job needs.

How long should the answer be?

About a minute to a minute and a half. Enough to explain what made it hard and what you did.
