Are you willing to work overtime or weekends? How to answer it

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Answer honestly with what you can actually do: 'Yes, when the work needs it, with some notice' is a common, fair answer. If you have fixed limits, like childcare or a second job, say so calmly and offer what you can. Then ask how often overtime or weekend work really happens, so you both know what you're agreeing to.

What they're really asking

Sometimes this question is a real scheduling check: the store needs weekend staff, the hospital runs 24 hours, the accounts team works late at month end. Sometimes it's a test of commitment. Either way, the interviewer wants:

  • A clear answer they can plan around.
  • Flexibility when the job really needs it.
  • Honesty, so there are no surprises after you start.

Saying "Yes, absolutely, any time" to get the offer, then turning down every weekend shift, causes more trouble than an honest answer now.

Decide your answer before the interview

Answer these for yourself first:

  1. How many extra hours a week could you do without strain?
  2. Are there days or times you can't work at all, and why?
  3. How much notice do you need?
  4. Does it change if it's paid, or swapped for time off later?

Then pick the answer that matches:

Your situationWhat you could say
Fully flexible"Yes. I understand the role has busy periods, and I'm happy to put in extra hours when they come up."
Flexible with notice"Yes, with some notice. If I know a day or two ahead, I can arrange things."
Weekends fine, evenings limited"Weekends work well for me. On weekday evenings I have family commitments after 6, so I'd prefer to start earlier on busy days."
A fixed commitment"I can't work Sunday mornings because of a long-standing commitment, but I'm flexible on Saturdays and evenings."
Occasional only"For deadlines or emergencies, yes. I wouldn't want it every week, and from what you've said I don't think that's the case here?"
Not at allBe honest, and consider if the job fits: "I'm not able to work weekends. If that's essential for this role, it's better we both know now."

You don't have to explain why you have a limit. "Family commitments" or "a regular commitment" is enough.

Ask how often it happens

After answering, turn it into a question. It's fair, and it tells you a lot about the job:

  • "How often does the team usually work late or at weekends?"
  • "Is it around particular times, like month end or holidays?"
  • "How are weekend shifts shared out across the team?"
  • "How is overtime handled: paid, or time off in lieu?"

Ask about pay calmly and at the right time; in some interviews it fits better near the end or once there's an offer. If the answers are vague, ask what last month looked like.

Example answers

These are examples with made-up details. Change them to match what you can genuinely do.

Example answer for a retail or hospitality role

"Yes, I can work weekends. I know they're the busiest times in a store. I'm also fine staying late if someone calls in sick, as long as it's not every week. Could I ask how weekend shifts are usually split across the team?"

Example answer for an office role

"When there's a deadline or a busy period, yes, I'll stay until the work is done. In my last role I worked late most nights in the week before our year-end close. I do have childcare on weekday evenings, so with a bit of notice I'd usually start early instead. How often does the team need to work extra hours?"

Example answer with a firm limit

"I'm happy to work Saturdays and some evenings. I can't work Sundays, because I care for a family member that day. Outside that, I'm flexible, and I'd always help out on a busy weekday."

Example answer for a graduate

"Yes. I expect the first year to involve some long weeks while I learn. I'd like to understand what's normal here, though. Is late work mostly around particular projects, or is it a regular thing?"

Practice it out loud

This answer is short, but people often sound apologetic when they state a limit. Practice it out loud until your limit sounds as calm as your yes. Try it once with someone who pushes back ("What if we really need you that Sunday?"). Speaking Coach can play an interviewer at Gentle, Realistic or Tough pressure for this.

Questions people ask

Will saying no cost me the job?

It might, if weekend work is essential. But it's better to find out now than after you've started. If it's not essential, a clear answer with what you can do often works fine.

Should I ask if overtime is paid?

Yes, it's a fair question, but timing matters. Ask how often it happens first, and ask about pay later in the interview or when you discuss the offer.

Do I have to explain my reason for a limit?

No. "I have a regular commitment" or "family commitments" is enough. The interviewer needs to plan, not know your private life.

What if the job ad already says weekends?

Then assume they're needed and say yes if you can. Use the question to ask how often and how shifts are shared.

Can I change my answer later?

You can, but it's harder once you've agreed. Give the answer you can keep.
