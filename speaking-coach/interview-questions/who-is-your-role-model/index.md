# Who is your role model? How to answer this interview question

Choose someone you know well enough to describe specifically, often a manager, teacher, family member or colleague rather than a celebrity. Name the one quality you admire, give a moment that showed it, and then explain how you copy that quality in your own work, with an example. The quality and your example matter far more than who the person is.

## What the interviewer is really asking

"Who is your role model?" is common in campus placements, graduate schemes, teaching, healthcare and public service interviews. Variants include "Who inspires you?" and "Who has influenced you most?" The person you name matters less than:

- **What you value.** The quality you pick says what kind of worker you want to be.
- **Whether you act on it.** Admiring someone is easy; copying them is the point.
- **How well you think.** Can you describe a quality precisely, with a real example?

So the answer has two halves: **them**, then **you**. Most weak answers stop after the first half.

## Choosing your role model

| Type of person | Strength | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A past manager or senior colleague | Directly relevant to work | Keep it professional; no gossip |
| A teacher, coach or professor | Shows how you learn | Link it to work, not only school |
| A parent or family member | Personal and believable | Keep it short and not too private |
| A well-known figure in your field | Shows you know the field | Know real details, not just their fame |
| A public figure or celebrity | Easy to recognise | Avoid political or divisive names |

People you know personally usually make better answers, because you can describe specific moments only you saw. If you pick a public figure, make sure you can name a specific decision or habit, not just their success.

## A structure for your answer

1. **Who, in one line.** "My first manager at the hotel, who ran the front desk."
2. **The one quality.** Name it precisely. Not "she was amazing," but "she stayed calm and fair with angry guests."
3. **A moment that showed it.** Ten to twenty seconds of story.
4. **How you copy it.** A habit you've adopted and one time you used it.

Aim for 45 to 75 seconds.

## Name the quality precisely

Vague qualities sound generic. Turn them into something you could see someone do:

| Vague | Precise |
| --- | --- |
| "Hard-working" | "She was always the first to check the next day's bookings before leaving." |
| "A great leader" | "He told us the bad news first, in person, and then the plan." |
| "Kind" | "She remembered every patient's name and one detail about them." |
| "Smart" | "He asked one more question than anyone else before deciding." |
| "Inspiring" | "She did the hardest shift herself before asking us to do it." |

## Example answers

These are examples with made-up people and details. Use someone real from your own life, described honestly.

### Example answer: a past manager

> "My role model is my first manager at the hotel. What I admired most was how she handled angry guests. She never argued and never gave in to everything. She'd repeat the problem back to them, say what she could do, and do it fast. I copy that now. Last month a customer called about a double charge and was shouting. I repeated the problem back, told him I'd fix it within the hour, and called him back in forty minutes. He ended the call by thanking me."

### Example answer: a teacher, for a graduate

> "My chemistry teacher at school. She explained every hard idea three ways, until everyone in the room got it. She never made anyone feel slow for asking again. When I tutored younger students at university, I tried to do the same, and my tutoring group's results improved over the term. In this role, where I'd explain products to customers, I'd bring that same patience."

### Example answer: a family member

> "My father, who ran a small hardware shop for twenty years. What I copy is how he kept his word. If he said an order would arrive Tuesday, it did, or he called the customer before Tuesday. At work, I try to do the same: if I'm going to miss a date, I say so early, not on the day."

### Example answer: someone in your field

> "I admire the nurse educator who trained us in my first hospital placement. She always asked 'What would you check next?' instead of giving us the answer. I've started doing the same with new healthcare assistants on my ward, and they come back to me with fewer basic questions."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only describing them | Misses the point of the question | Spend half the answer on how you copy them |
| Choosing someone divisive | Can distract the interviewer | Pick someone whose quality matters more than their fame |
| A long list of qualities | Nothing sticks | One quality, described precisely |
| "I don't have one" | Sounds incurious | Pick someone you've learned one thing from |
| Picking the company's CEO to flatter | Sounds like an obvious move | Only if you genuinely know their work |

## Practice it out loud

This answer can sound emotional when the person matters to you, which is good, but keep it focused. Say it out loud and check the time: if the story about them runs past 30 seconds, cut it. Speaking Coach's AI interviewer can follow up with "When did you act the way they would?", which is the part interviewers care about most.

## Questions people ask

### Can my role model be a family member?

Yes. Many good answers use a parent or grandparent. Keep it about one quality and how it shows in your work, not your family story.

### Is it okay to pick a famous person?

Yes, if you can name a specific decision or habit of theirs. Avoid political or controversial figures, which can distract from your answer.

### What if I don't have a role model?

Say you've learned different things from different people, then give one specific example. "I don't have one single role model, but one person taught me…" works.

### Should my role model be in the same field as the job?

It helps, but it's not necessary. What matters is that the quality you copy is useful in this job.

### How long should my answer be?

About 45 to 75 seconds. Half on them, half on you.
