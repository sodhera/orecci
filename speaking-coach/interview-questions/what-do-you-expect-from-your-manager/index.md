# What do you expect from a manager? How to answer this question

Name two or three things that help you do your best work, such as clear goals, regular honest feedback and room to own your work, and for each say what you do with it. Frame them as how you work best, not as demands. End by showing you adapt to different managers and asking how this manager likes to work.

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence. Updated 2026-10-22. Source: https://www.orecci.com/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-do-you-expect-from-your-manager/

## What's behind the question

Often the person asking is your future manager. They're asking, in effect: "What will working with you be like for me?" They're listening for two risks:

- **High maintenance.** Someone who needs constant reassurance, check-ins or praise.
- **A bad fit.** Someone who wants a style they don't offer, such as total freedom on a team that works closely.

A good answer shows you know how you work best, you're realistic, and you'll meet a manager halfway.

## The three things most people work best with

Pick two or three that are true for you. Each one has a high-maintenance version and a balanced version.

| What you need | Sounds high-maintenance | Sounds balanced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear goals | "I need detailed instructions for every task." | "I work best when I know what success looks like. Then I'm happy to work out the how." |
| Feedback | "I like to get feedback every day." | "I appreciate honest feedback, including when something isn't working, so I can fix it early." |
| Autonomy | "I don't like being checked on." | "I like owning my work, with regular check-ins so we stay aligned." |
| Availability | "I need my manager to be there when I have questions." | "I try to solve things myself first, and it helps to know when I can bring the bigger questions." |
| Recognition | "I need to feel appreciated." | "I like knowing when something I did made a difference, so I know to do more of it." |
| Growth | "I expect to be promoted quickly." | "I'd like to talk about where I want to grow and get stretch work when I'm ready." |
| Advocacy | "I expect my manager to fight for me." | "I appreciate a manager who shares context from above, so I understand the why behind decisions." |

The pattern: say what you need, then say what you do with it. That second half is what removes the high-maintenance tone.

## A simple structure

1. **Two or three things,** each in the balanced form above.
2. **A short example** of one of them working well in the past.
3. **Flexibility.** "I've worked with quite different managers, and I adjust."
4. **Turn it into a question,** if the timing fits. "How do you like to work with your team?"

Keep it to 45 to 60 seconds.

## Example answers

These are examples with made-up details. Use what's true for you.

### Experienced professional

> "Three things help me most. Clear priorities, so I know what matters this quarter. Honest feedback, including the uncomfortable kind. My last manager told me early on that my reports were too long, and fixing that made a big difference to how they were used. And room to own my projects, with a weekly check-in so nothing surprises either of us. I've worked with hands-on and hands-off managers and adapted to both. How do you usually work with your team?"

### Graduate or first job

> "As someone early in my career, I'd value a manager who's clear about what good work looks like and gives me feedback often enough that I can improve quickly. I'll make sure I've tried to solve problems myself before I ask, and I'll bring questions together rather than one at a time. During my internship my supervisor and I had a fifteen-minute check-in every Friday, and that worked really well."

### Someone leaving a difficult manager

> "I work best with clear expectations and regular, direct feedback. I'm someone who'd rather hear that something isn't right on day two than at a review six months later. I also like being trusted with a piece of work end to end. I'm flexible about the format, whether that's weekly one-to-ones or quick conversations as things come up."

Notice the third example says nothing about the old manager. You can describe what you want without explaining what went wrong before. If they ask directly, see [what you thought of your last boss](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-did-you-think-of-your-last-boss/).

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Nothing, I'm fully independent" | Sounds unrealistic, or like you'd resist management | Name what helps you, even if it's light-touch |
| A long list of expectations | Sounds demanding | Two or three, with what you do with each |
| Describing your old boss's faults | Sounds bitter, and they may wonder what you'd say about them | Describe what you want, not what you didn't get |
| "Someone who lets me work from home whenever" | Sounds like you're negotiating perks | Raise practical terms separately with HR |
| Copying their management style from their LinkedIn | Can sound like flattery | Be honest about your own preferences |

## Use it to learn about them too

This question is a rare chance to check fit both ways. After your answer, a short question back is natural: "How do you usually give feedback?" or "How often do you meet one-to-one with your team?" Their answer tells you a lot about the job. You can also save those for [the questions at the end](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/do-you-have-any-questions-for-us/).

## Practise the tone

This answer can sound very different depending on how you say it. The same words can come out confident or needy. Say it out loud and listen: does it sound like a description of how you work, or a list of requests? Speaking Coach can play a hiring manager, follow up on what you said ("What if I'm not available much?"), and show you afterwards where you hedged or used fillers.

## Questions people ask

### Should I say I want regular feedback?

Yes, most managers like hearing that. Add what you do with it, so it doesn't sound like you need constant reassurance.

### Is it okay to say I don't like micromanagement?

Say it positively: "I work best when I'm trusted to own my work, with regular check-ins." That says the same thing without sounding difficult.

### What if I don't know what I want from a manager?

Think about the best manager, teacher or coach you've had. What did they do that helped? Use that.

### Should I ask about their management style in return?

Yes, if it fits naturally. It shows interest and gives you real information about the job.

### What if the interviewer won't be my manager?

Answer the same way. HR and recruiters use this question to match you with the right team.
