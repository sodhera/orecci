Where do you see yourself in 5 years? How to answer with examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Describe the skills and responsibility you want to have in five years, in a direction this role naturally leads, and say how this job starts that path. Be specific about the work and loose about titles, and don't suggest you'll leave, start your own business or take the interviewer's job.

What the interviewer is checking

They're not asking for a prediction. They want to know three things:

  • Will you stay? Hiring and training someone takes months. A five-year plan that clearly happens somewhere else is a warning.
  • Does your ambition fit what they can offer? Someone who wants to manage a team in two years won't be happy in a company with no management roles.
  • Have you thought about your career at all? "I don't know" sounds like you'll drift.

The best answers sit where your real goals and the role's real path overlap.

Find the path before you answer

Before the interview, find out where this role usually leads:

  1. Look at the company's careers page for senior versions of the same job title.
  2. Check where people in this role at this company are now, using their professional profiles.
  3. If you get a recruiter screen first, ask: "Where have people in this role gone next?"
  4. Note any qualification the job post mentions as a bonus, such as a professional certificate or licence.

If the role is a specialist job with no obvious ladder, plan your answer around depth: harder problems, bigger projects, becoming the person others ask.

A three-part answer

  1. The near term (first one to two years): get very good at this job. Name one or two skills.
  2. The longer term (three to five years): more responsibility, broader scope, maybe leading others, described as work, not a title.
  3. The link: why this company is a good place for that path.

Keep it to 30–45 seconds. This is a question where a short, calm answer sounds more confident than a detailed plan.

Example answers

Made-up examples to show the structure. Build yours from your own goals and the company's actual path.

A graduate at an accounting firm

"In the first couple of years, I want to get solid on audit work and pass my professional exams. I know you support trainees through them, which is a big reason I applied. By five years, I'd like to be running audits for my own set of clients and helping train the new graduates, the way someone will be training me. I'd like to do that here, because your clients are mostly mid-sized manufacturers, and that's the sector I want to know inside out."

A specialist who doesn't want to manage

"I want to get deeper rather than broader. In five years I'd like to be the person the team comes to for the hardest database performance problems, and to have led at least one big migration end to end. I'm not aiming for people management right now. What I like about this role is that your senior engineers lead technical projects without having to manage people, so the path I want exists here."

Someone aiming for management

"Over the next year or two, I want to become one of the strongest account managers on the team and learn how you run renewals, since that's different from what I've done. After that I'd like to start mentoring newer account managers, and in five years I'd hope to be leading a small team. I saw that two of your current team leads started as account managers, so that path seems realistic here."

A career changer

"Five years from now, I want to be a fully qualified, experienced paralegal, specialising in employment law. My background in HR means I already know the workplace side, and this role would give me the legal training and real case work to build on it. Long term, I can see myself supporting the senior partners on the bigger employment cases."

If you're genuinely unsure

Honesty is fine, as long as it has a direction:

"I'm early enough that I don't want to fix on a job title. What I do know is I want to be much stronger at analysis than I am now, and to be trusted with bigger decisions. This role is about the data behind those decisions, so it's the right place to start."

How to practise it

This answer often falls apart on the follow-up: "And how does this job get you there?" or "What if that doesn't happen here?" Say yours out loud, then answer those two follow-ups. Questions people ask

Is it okay to say I want to be a manager?

Yes, if the company has management roles and you frame it as leading people well, not just a title. Mention what you'd learn first.

What if I plan to leave in two years?

Don't say so. Focus on what you want to learn and achieve in this role. Plans change, and many people stay longer than they expect.

Should I mention personal goals like family or travel?

No. Keep the answer about work. Personal plans can raise questions about commitment that have nothing to do with your ability.

How is this different from "What is your dream job?"

The five-year question is about a realistic path from this role. The dream job question is about what you love doing, which should still overlap with this job.

What if the job has no promotion path?

Talk about depth instead: becoming expert, handling harder cases, training others, owning bigger projects. Many specialist roles grow this way.
