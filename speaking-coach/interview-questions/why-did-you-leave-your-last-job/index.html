Why did you leave your last job? Answers if you already left

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Give the real reason in one neutral sentence, add what you've done since you left if it's been a while, and turn to why this job fits. Use plain words like "my contract ended", "I resigned to" or "we moved", never criticise your old employer, and keep the whole answer under 30 seconds.

Why this is different if you've already gone

If you're still employed, the question is about what you're moving toward. That version is covered in why are you leaving your current job. Once you've left, the interviewer has an extra concern: did you leave on good terms, and why don't you have a job now? Your answer has to settle that, quickly, without sounding rehearsed.

The tone matters as much as the words. A short, flat, factual answer tells them there's nothing to dig into. A long story with feelings in it invites follow-ups.

The structure

  1. What happened (one sentence). The neutral version of the real reason.
  2. What you've done since (one sentence, only if relevant). Especially if more than two or three months have passed.
  3. Why this job (one or two sentences). Turn toward the role you're interviewing for.

Twenty to thirty seconds is enough. That's around 50–75 words.

Example answers by situation

These examples use made-up details. Adapt the facts, keep the shape.

You resigned without another job lined up

"I resigned in June. I'd been in the same role for four years, and I wanted to make a clean move into project coordination rather than juggle a search on top of a full workload. Since then I've completed a project management course and helped run a community event for 200 people, and this role is exactly the kind of coordination work I've been aiming for."

The key is to show it was a decision, not an escape. Say what you used the time for.

Your contract or fixed-term role ended

"It was a twelve-month maternity cover contract, and it ended as planned when the person I was covering came back. They asked me to stay on for an extra month to hand over. I'm now looking for a permanent role in payroll, which is why this one caught my eye."

This is the easiest version. Say "contract ended" early so they don't wonder.

You moved for a partner, family or a fresh start

"My partner was offered a job here, so we relocated in August. I left my previous team on good terms, and my old manager is one of my references. I've been looking specifically at healthcare administration roles in the area, and yours is the one I'm most excited about."

Mention that you're now settled, so they don't worry you'll move again.

You left a role that wasn't the right fit after a short time

"I joined as a sales assistant, but within a few months the role turned into mostly cold calling, which wasn't what I'd applied for. I gave notice after five months and left on good terms. I learned to ask more about the day-to-day in interviews, which is why I asked earlier how this team splits its time."

Own the decision without blaming anyone, and show what you learned.

You were laid off

"My role was one of twelve cut when the company closed its London office in March. I've spent the time since finishing my CIPD certificate and helping a small charity with their hiring process. I'm looking for a people-advisor role where I can use both."

For more on this, see how to explain a layoff. If you were let go for performance, the approach is different; see have you ever been fired?

Neutral words that do the work

Instead ofSay
"My manager was impossible""The role had changed from what I'd joined for"
"They didn't value me""I'd gone as far as I could in that team"
"I was totally burned out""I took a planned break after a demanding few years"
"I couldn't stand it any more""I decided to make a clean break and focus on finding the right next role"
"They let me go out of nowhere""My role was cut in a restructure"
"I just quit""I resigned to …" (and finish the sentence)

Mistakes to avoid

  • Criticising the old employer. Even if it's true, the interviewer imagines you saying the same about them.
  • Giving three reasons. Pick the main one. A list sounds like you're building a case.
  • Leaving a silence after "I resigned". Always finish the sentence with to do what.
  • Hiding a short stay. If you left after a few months, say so before they do the maths.
  • Saying more than they asked. If they want details, they'll ask a follow-up.

Get the delivery right

On this question, people often rush, trail off or add nervous extras like "but it's fine, honestly". Say your answer out loud five times and record the last one. If you hear yourself justify the decision twice, cut the second justification. The interview answer review shows your pace and filler words so you can check you sound settled.

Questions people ask

Is it bad to say I left without another job?

No, as long as you explain it as a decision and say what you've done since. Many people leave to change direction, care for family or rest.

How do I explain leaving after only a few months?

Say it was a short stay, give one neutral reason, and show what you learned about choosing roles. Then point to why this role is a better fit.

Should I mention a toxic workplace?

Not in those words. Use "the culture wasn't the right fit for how I work" and move on. If pressed, give one factual detail without naming anyone.

What if my old employer might give a bad reference?

Offer a different reference from that job, such as a peer or another manager who knew your work. Keep your explanation consistent with what they're likely to hear.

Can I just say "personal reasons"?

You can, but add one sentence about readiness: "a personal matter, now resolved, and I'm ready for full-time work". On its own it can sound evasive.
