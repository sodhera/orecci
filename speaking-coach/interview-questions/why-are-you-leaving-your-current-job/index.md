# Why are you leaving your current job? How to answer it honestly

Frame the move as going toward something this job offers, such as more scope, a different kind of work or a skill you want to build, and leave criticism of your current employer out. Give one or two sentences on why now, then turn to why this role fits.

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence. Updated 2026-10-22. Source: https://www.orecci.com/speaking-coach/interview-questions/why-are-you-leaving-your-current-job/

## The rule: toward, not away

You're still employed, so you're choosing to leave. The interviewer wants to know what you're choosing. If your answer is all about what's wrong where you are, they'll wonder what you'll complain about next, and whether the problem was you.

A move toward something sounds like a plan. A move away sounds like an escape. The facts can be identical.

Your answer should be honest, and it should be short. This is not the place for the full story.

## Turning real reasons into safe answers

Most people leave for a mix of reasons, some of them uncomfortable. Here's how to say the true thing without the complaint.

| The real reason | Say something like |
| --- | --- |
| My manager micromanages | "I'm ready for more ownership. I'd like to run projects end to end, which this role offers." |
| There's no promotion path | "I've grown as far as I can in this role, and there's no next step in my team for the next few years." |
| I'm underpaid | "I'm looking for a role that matches the scope I've grown into." Save pay for the salary question. |
| The work is boring | "I've learned the core of the job well, and I want to work on X, which is a bigger part of this role." |
| The company is struggling or restructuring | "The company is restructuring and my team's future is unclear, so it's a good moment to look for something longer-term." |
| The hours are unsustainable | "I want a role where I can do focused, high-quality work at a steady pace." Only if this role really offers that. |
| Long commute or moving home | Say it plainly. It's neutral and easy to understand. |
| I want to change industry or role | "I want to move into X, and this job lets me use my experience in Y while I do." |

## A three-sentence answer

1. **Credit your current job:** one thing you've gained there.
2. **What you want next:** the thing it can't give you.
3. **Why this role:** how this job offers exactly that.

Keep it to 20–40 seconds. Long explanations make a simple decision sound complicated.

## Example answers

Made-up examples to show the pattern.

### Ready for more scope

> "I've been at my current company four years and I've learned a lot, especially about running monthly reporting for a large business. But my role has stayed fairly narrow, and there isn't a senior analyst position opening up in our team. This role would let me own the forecasting work as well as the reporting, which is exactly the step I want to take."

### Changing direction

> "I've enjoyed sales, and I've hit my targets, but the part I like most is helping customers after they've bought, getting them set up and making sure they actually use the product. I want to do that full-time, and this customer success role is built around it. My sales background means I understand renewals and expansion, too."

### The company is changing

> "My company was bought by a larger group earlier this year, and our team is being merged into theirs. Nothing's been decided about roles yet, but it's made me think about what I want next. I'd like to stay in logistics planning but in a company that's growing, and your expansion into the north is a big reason I applied."

### Short tenure (under a year)

> "I'll be honest, I took my current role expecting it to be mostly project work, but it's turned out to be almost entirely support. I've done it well and learned a lot about our systems, but I want to get back to building things, which is what this role is."

## Follow-up questions to expect

- **"Have you talked to your manager about it?"** If you have, say what happened briefly. If you haven't, say you're keeping the search private for now, which is normal.
- **"What would make you stay?"** Answer honestly but don't make it sound like a negotiation: "If a role like this opened up internally, I'd consider it, but it isn't going to."
- **"Would you accept a counteroffer?"** "I'm not looking to use this as leverage. I'm looking for the right next role."
- **"Does your employer know you're looking?"** Saying no is fine. You can ask them to keep your application confidential.

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criticising your boss or coworkers | They'll imagine you saying the same about them | Describe what you want, not what you're escaping |
| A long, emotional story | Makes a normal move sound like a crisis | Three sentences, then turn to the new role |
| "Money" as the only reason | Suggests you'll leave for the next raise | Talk about scope; handle pay separately |
| Sharing confidential company news | Shows poor judgement | Stick to what's public or say "the company is changing" |
| Sounding unsure why you're leaving | Makes them unsure about you | Decide your one-line reason before the interview |

If you've already left your job, the question changes; see [why did you leave your last job](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/why-did-you-leave-your-last-job/).

## How to practise it

Say your answer out loud and listen for one thing: does any sentence complain? If so, rewrite it as something you want. Then check the length, because this is an answer that should take under 40 seconds. Practising against follow-ups helps most here, since that's where people slip into criticism. ## Questions people ask

### How honest should I be about why I'm leaving?

Honest about the reason, selective about the detail. Say the true reason in its most constructive form, and leave out the frustrations behind it.

### Can I say I'm leaving because of a bad manager?

Not directly. Say what you want instead, such as more independence or clearer feedback, and make sure this role offers it.

### Is it okay to say I'm leaving for more money?

It's a fair reason, but it shouldn't be the only one you give. Mention growth or scope first, and save numbers for the [salary question](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-are-your-salary-expectations/).

### What if I've only been in my current job a few months?

Be brief and honest about the mismatch, show you gave it a real try, and explain why this role is a better fit. One short stint is easy to explain; a pattern needs more care.

### Should I mention I'm interviewing elsewhere?

Only if asked. If you are, it's fine to say so without naming companies.
