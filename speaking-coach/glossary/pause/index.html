What is a pause in public speaking? Definition and example

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

A pause in public speaking is a deliberate silence you use on purpose: to let a point land, separate ideas, give the audience time to think, or replace a filler word. Most useful pauses last from half a second to about three seconds, even though they can feel much longer to you than to the audience.

Types of pause

PauseTypical lengthUse it toExample moment
Phrase pauseUnder 1 secondSeparate ideas, take a breathBetween sentences
Emphasis pause1–2 secondsMake the next words landBefore a key number or name
Reflective pause2–3 secondsLet the audience thinkAfter a question you asked them
Opening pause2–3 secondsGet attention before you startAfter you reach the front, before your first line
Transition pause1–2 secondsSignal a new sectionBetween main points

Example

Example: the middle of a short talk, made up. A slash (/) marks a short pause; a double slash (//) marks a longer one.

"Last year we lost twelve customers. / We asked each one why. // Eleven gave the same answer. // They never heard back from us after the sale."

Read it without the pauses and it's a list of facts. With them, the audience leans in before the reason and has a moment to feel it.

A filler word is often a pause you didn't let yourself take. Replacing "um" with a half-second silence is one of the quickest ways to stop saying um. Speaking Coach's feedback after each session points out long pauses or freezes, alongside your pace and filler words.

Questions people ask

How long should a pause be in a speech?

Usually half a second to three seconds. Longer pauses work only for big moments, such as after a story's ending.

Why do pauses feel so long?

Under pressure, your sense of time speeds up. A two-second pause can feel like ten to you and sound natural to the audience.

Will pausing make me sound unsure?

Not if you hold eye contact and continue with a steady voice. A pause sounds unsure mainly when it's filled with "um" or followed by an apology.

Where should I pause in a speech?

Before and after key points, between sections, after questions to the audience, and before your first and last lines. See how to use pauses.
