# What is a behavioral interview? Definition and examples

A behavioral interview asks how you actually handled situations in the past, on the idea that past behaviour is a good guide to how you'll act in the job. Questions usually start with "Tell me about a time…" or "Give me an example of…", and you answer with one specific story, often in STAR format.

## How it differs from other formats

| Format | Typical question | What you give |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Behavioral** | "Tell me about a time you missed a deadline." | A real story from your past |
| **Situational** | "What would you do if you were going to miss a deadline?" | A plan for a made-up scenario |
| **Technical** | "Walk me through how you'd debug this." | Skills shown live |
| **Case** | "Why are this company's profits falling?" | Structured reasoning out loud |

Many interviews mix formats. A behavioral round is often called a [competency-based interview](/speaking-coach/glossary/competency-based-interview/) when the questions map to a published list of skills.

## Sample behavioral questions

- [Tell me about a time you faced a challenge.](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-a-time-you-faced-a-challenge/)
- [Tell me about a time you had a conflict at work.](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-a-time-you-had-a-conflict/)
- Tell me about a time you made a mistake.
- Give me an example of when you showed leadership without a title.
- Describe a time you had to learn something quickly.
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.

## What the interviewer listens for

- **One specific moment,** not "Usually, I…". Generalities don't count as evidence.
- **Your actions,** said with "I", even inside a team story.
- **A result,** and ideally what you'd do differently now.
- **Follow-up answers** that hold up: "What did your manager say?" "What would you change?"

The [STAR method](/speaking-coach/glossary/star-method/) is the usual way to keep these answers short and complete.

## Questions people ask

### How do I prepare for a behavioral interview?

Write down five to eight real stories (a success, a failure, a conflict, a deadline, leadership, learning fast) and practise telling each in under two minutes, out loud.

### Can I use the same story for more than one question?

Yes, if it fits. A strong project story can show teamwork and handling pressure, but tell it from a different angle each time.

### What if I don't have work experience?

Use school projects, part-time jobs, volunteering or sports. What matters is a real situation and what you did.

### Is a behavioral interview harder than a normal one?

It's harder to bluff, because follow-ups dig into details. With prepared stories, many people find it easier.
