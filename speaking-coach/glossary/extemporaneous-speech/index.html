Extemporaneous Speech: Meaning, Examples and How to Prepare

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

An extemporaneous speech is a speech you prepare and rehearse but don't memorise or read: you know your structure and key points and speak from brief notes, choosing the exact words as you go. It sits between an impromptu speech, which has little or no preparation, and a manuscript speech, which is read word for word. Most good class presentations, lectures and work talks are given this way.

What extemporaneous means

The word comes from the Latin ex tempore, a phrase meaning "impromptu", which is why dictionaries such as Wiktionary still define it as offhand, without advance thought. In public speaking it means something narrower: planned and practised, but not scripted. You fix the order of your points and your examples. You never fix the sentences, so the wording changes slightly every time you give it.

The short form "extemp" usually means a competition event. In the National Speech & Debate Association's United States Extemporaneous Speaking, students answer one of three questions, get 30 minutes to prepare, and give a seven-minute speech without notes.

Example note card

Example: notes for a 5-minute class talk on why the town library should stay open late. Made-up details.

  • Open: my sister's exam week, library closed at 6
  • Point 1: who uses it after 6 (students, shift workers)
  • Point 2: cost: 2 staff × 3 hours, about £180 a week
  • Point 3: what a nearby town tried
  • Close: sign the petition at the front desk

Five lines, key words only. You rehearse until each line reliably triggers 45 to 60 seconds of talk. More on writing them in speaker notes.

Example: one point, said two ways

Here is the card line "Point 1: who uses it after 6" spoken in two rehearsals. Both are fine. That's the point.

Rehearsal 1: "Who uses the library after six? Mostly two groups: students revising for exams, and people coming off day shifts."

Rehearsal 2: "After six, the library fills up with two kinds of people. Students who can't work at home, and shift workers who can't come in the day."

A memorised speech would demand the exact words of one version. Extemporaneous delivery lets you use whichever comes out, and keep eye contact while you do.

Example topics to practise

  • Why our town should keep the library open late
  • One change that would improve our school canteen
  • How I'd spend a small team budget
  • A skill everyone should learn before 18
  • What I learned from my first job
  • Should public transport be free for students?

How to prepare an extemporaneous speech

  1. Write your outline: an opening, two or three points and a close. See how to structure a presentation.
  2. Talk it through out loud from the outline, without writing sentences.
  3. Cut the outline to key words on one card or a few slides.
  4. Rehearse three to five times and let the wording change.
  5. Memorise only your first and last lines.

Questions people ask

What does extemporaneous speech mean?

A speech that is prepared and rehearsed but delivered from brief notes, with the exact words chosen as you speak.

Is extemporaneous the same as impromptu?

No, though people sometimes use them interchangeably. Impromptu has little or no preparation; extemporaneous is well prepared but not scripted.

Can you use notes in an extemporaneous speech?

In class and at work, yes: brief key words, not full sentences. Competitive extemp is different; there, the speech is given without notes.

Why do speech teachers prefer extemporaneous delivery?

It keeps you organised while letting you look at the audience and adjust to them, which reading a script doesn't.

What is "extemp" in speech and debate?

A competitive speech event. In the National Speech & Debate Association's version, students answer one of three questions, prepare for 30 minutes and speak for up to seven minutes without notes.
