Small Talk: Meaning, Definition and Examples of What to Say

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

Small talk is light, polite conversation about safe, everyday topics, such as the event you're both at, the weekend or the weather, usually with people you don't know well. Its job isn't the information: it shows you're friendly, finds common ground and opens the door to a real conversation, which is why it matters before interviews, meetings and at networking events.

Meaning

Small talk is short, low-stakes and polite. Anyone can answer the topic without risk, and nobody expects a deep or clever reply. You usually make small talk: "We made small talk while we waited for the lift." A more casual word for it is chit-chat.

What it's for:

  • Signalling goodwill. "I'm friendly and happy to talk" matters more than what you say.
  • Finding common ground you can go deeper on.
  • Filling in-between moments: before a meeting, in a queue, in a lift.
  • Building trust slowly. Many short chats with a neighbour or coworker add up to a relationship.

Good topics, with example lines

TopicExample lineWhy it works
The setting"How do you know the hosts?"You both share it, and it has a story behind it
A shared moment"Did you catch the first talk this morning?"Easy to answer, invites an opinion
Plans"Anything good planned for the weekend?"Points to the future and to interests
Food and drink"Have you tried the dumplings? I'm going back for more."Light, and gives them something to do
Work, lightly"What are you working on at the moment?"Less of a status question than "What do you do?"

The weather is fine as a first line, but it won't carry a conversation. More ideas in small talk topics.

Examples of small talk by setting

  • At work, by the coffee machine: "How's your week going so far?" / "Are you on the launch too, or just hearing about it?"
  • Before a job interview, walking from reception: "Yes, thanks, the directions were easy. Have you been here long?" More in small talk before an interview.
  • Before a video call starts: "Where are you dialling in from today?"
  • With a neighbour or in a queue: "Is it always this busy on Saturdays?" / "Your dog's lovely. What's her name?"

A small talk conversation, start to finish

Example, made up, at a friend's party:

A: "How do you know the hosts?"

B: "I work with Priya. We're both in the design team."

A: "Oh, nice. I went to university with her. What kind of design do you do?"

B: "Mostly packaging, for food brands."

A: "So you're why I pick biscuits by the box. What's the hardest part?"

B: "Honestly? Fitting everything on the back."

A: "I'll never read a label the same way again. Good to meet you. I'm going to grab a drink before the speeches."

The shape: a shared opener, a detail of your own, a follow-up on what they said, and a friendly exit.

Topics most people avoid at first

Money, politics, religion, health, looks and relationship status ("Why aren't you married?") are risky with someone you've just met. Save them for when you know each other.

Small talk vs. related terms

TermWhat it isExample
Small talkLight, polite chat with people you don't know well"Did you have far to come?"
IcebreakerA question or activity that starts talk in a group"Tell us one thing you did this summer."

Common mistakes

MistakeInstead
A one-word answer: "Fine."Answer plus a detail: "Good, busy. We're moving offices."
Firing question after questionShare something of your own after two questions
Talking only about yourselfFollow up on what they just said
No way out"It's been good talking to you. I'm going to say hi to Sam."

How to practise small talk

  1. Prepare three openers for your next event: the setting, plans, and them.
  2. This week, answer every "How are you?" with a detail instead of "Fine".
  3. After each answer you get, ask one follow-up.
  4. Choose one exit line and say it until it's automatic.
  5. Try openers out loud in the small talk drill, or with Speaking Coach, where a stranger says hi and you keep it going.

Questions people ask

What is small talk in simple words?

Small talk is a short, friendly chat about easy topics, like the weather or the weekend, with someone you don't know well.

What is an example of small talk?

"How do you know the hosts?" at a party, or "How's your week going?" at the coffee machine, then a follow-up on their answer.

Is small talk pointless?

No. The words matter less than the signal: you're open and friendly. Many deeper conversations start with something trivial.

Why do some people find small talk hard?

There's no clear script, the stakes feel unclear, and silences are noticeable. Having two or three openers ready helps.

How long should small talk last before a meeting?

Usually a minute or two. Follow the other person's lead; if they move to business, go with it.
