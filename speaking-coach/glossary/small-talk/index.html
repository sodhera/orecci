What is small talk? Definition and examples of good topics

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Small talk is light, low-stakes conversation about shared, safe topics, such as the event you're both at, weekend plans, the food or the weather, usually with people you don't know well. Its job isn't the information: it shows you're friendly, finds common ground and opens the door to a real conversation.

What small talk is for

  • Signalling goodwill. "I'm friendly and happy to talk" matters more than what you say.
  • Finding common ground. Each light question tests for a shared interest you can go deeper on.
  • Filling in-between moments: the minute before a meeting starts, a queue, a lift, the walk to a table.
  • Building trust slowly. Many short chats with a neighbour or coworker add up to a relationship.

Good topics, with example lines

TopicExample lineWhy it works
The setting"How do you know the hosts?"You both share it, and it has a story behind it
A shared moment"Did you catch the first talk this morning?"Easy to answer, invites an opinion
Plans"Anything good planned for the weekend?"Points to the future and to interests
Food and drink"Have you tried the dumplings? I'm going back for more."Light, and gives them something to do
Work, lightly"What are you working on at the moment?"Less of a status question than "What do you do?"
The local area"Have you lived around here long?"Leads to places, moves and recommendations

The weather is fine as a first line. It just won't carry a conversation on its own, so move on to something they can say more about.

Topics most people avoid at first

Money and salaries, politics, religion, health, weight or appearance, and relationship status ("Why aren't you married?") are risky with someone you've just met. They can come up later, once you know each other.

How to start, keep going and end gracefully is covered in how to make small talk, and you can try openers out loud in the small talk drill.

Questions people ask

Is small talk pointless?

No. The words matter less than the signal: you're open and friendly. Many deeper conversations start with something trivial.

Why do some people find small talk hard?

There's no clear script, the stakes feel unclear, and silences are noticeable. Having two or three openers ready helps.

How long should small talk last before a meeting?

Usually a minute or two. Follow the other person's lead; if they move to business, go with it.

What do you say after the first question?

Pick up on something in their answer: "Oh, you just moved here? From where?" A follow-up shows you listened.
