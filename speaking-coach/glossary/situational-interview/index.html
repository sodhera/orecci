Situational Interview: Meaning, Questions and Example Answers

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

A situational interview is a job interview that asks hypothetical questions about what you would do in a situation the job is likely to bring, such as "What would you do if a customer demanded a refund you couldn't give?" It tests your judgement and priorities, so a good answer walks through your first steps in order and says why. Behavioral questions ask what you did in the past; situational questions ask what you would do.

Meaning

The interviewer describes a realistic scenario from the job and asks how you'd handle it. These are also called hypothetical or scenario-based questions, and most interviews mix a few in with others. They suit graduates and career changers, because they test judgement rather than track record.

Typical opening"What would you do if…""Tell me about a time when…""Our client's profits are falling. Why?"
Answer shapeSteps and reasonsOne real story, often STARStructure, numbers, recommendation
TestsJudgement and prioritiesTrack recordAnalysis out loud

One skill, both ways: "Tell me about a time a customer got upset with you" (behavioral) or "What would you do if a customer got upset with you?" (situational).

How to answer a situational question

  1. Clarify if you need to: "Is the customer in the shop or on the phone?"
  2. Name the goal: what matters most here.
  3. Give your first two or three steps, in order.
  4. Say why, briefly.
  5. Back it up with "I did something similar when…", if you can.

Example answers

Examples with made-up details.

Two managers, two urgent tasks:

"First I'd tell both managers about the clash, because they may not know. I'd ask which deadline has the bigger impact, and meanwhile start on whichever is quickest to unblock. In my last job this happened during month-end, and a two-line message to both managers sorted it in ten minutes."

An angry customer:

"My first goal is to calm things down, not win the argument. I'd let them finish, say I can see why they're frustrated, and not blame my colleague. Then I'd tell them what I can do right now. If they need more than I'm allowed to give, I'd bring in my manager rather than promise something I can't deliver."

A graduate with no work experience ("Your manager is away and a client wants a decision today"):

"I'd reply within the hour so they know it's being handled, then check whether anyone else can decide. If no one can, I'd tell the client exactly when they'll hear back. I ran our university society's events while the president was abroad, with the same rule: never leave someone waiting without a time."

Questions people ask

What is a situational interview question?

A hypothetical question about a work scenario, such as "What would you do if…". You answer with the steps you'd take and why.

What's the difference between situational and behavioral questions?

Situational questions ask what you would do in a future scenario. Behavioral questions ask what you did in a real past situation.

Can I use STAR for situational questions?

Not directly, since nothing has happened yet. Answer with steps and reasons, then back it with a short real example if you have one.

Is there a right answer to a situational question?

Not one exact answer, but better ones protect the customer, the team and the organisation, in a sensible order.

What if I've never faced the situation?

Say so briefly, then reason it through out loud. Interviewers want to hear your judgement.
