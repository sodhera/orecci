What is a stress interview? Definition and example questions

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

A stress interview is one in which the interviewer deliberately puts pressure on you, with blunt challenges, interruptions, long silences or rapid questions, to see how you react. It is more likely in jobs where pressure is part of the work, such as sales, trading or front-line customer roles. The test is usually your composure, not a perfect answer.

Definition

In a normal interview, the interviewer tries to put you at ease. In a stress interview, they do the opposite on purpose. The behaviour is staged: they want to see whether you stay polite, think clearly and stand by your answers under pressure, the way you would need to with an angry client or a tough negotiation.

Example questions

  • "Why should we hire you over someone with more experience?"
  • "Your CV looks average. Convince me otherwise."
  • "What would you do if I told you that answer was wrong?"
  • "You seem nervous. Are you?"
  • "Why did it take you so long to find a job?"

How to stay steady

  1. Pause before answering. Two seconds reads as composure, not weakness.
  2. Don't take the bait. Answer the content, not the tone.
  3. Hold your position politely if you're right; concede briefly if you're not.
  4. Keep answers short. Pressure makes people ramble.

Questions people ask

Are stress interviews still used?

Less commonly than standard interviews, but some employers still use stress tactics for high-pressure roles.

How do I know it's a stress interview?

A sudden shift to coldness, interruptions or provocative comments, often with no reason given, usually signals it.

Should I point out that it's a stress test?

No. Just respond calmly. Naming it can sound defensive.

What if I lose my composure?

Take a breath, say "Let me answer that again," and give a short, calm answer. Recovering well counts too.
