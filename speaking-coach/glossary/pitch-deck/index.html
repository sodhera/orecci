Pitch Deck: Meaning, Slide Order and Examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

A pitch deck is a short set of slides, usually 10 to 15, that backs up a spoken pitch, most often a startup founder asking investors for money. It covers the problem, the solution, the market, the business model, traction, the team and the ask. When you present it, the slides are the outline and you are the pitch: say each point in one sentence and let the slide prove it.

Meaning

"Deck" simply means a set of slides. A pitch deck is the visual half of a pitch; the other half is what you say. It is also called an investor deck, a startup deck or a pitch presentation. The term is used most for startups raising money, but also for the slides behind a sales pitch, a competition entry or a budget request at work.

Types of pitch deck

TypeWho sees itHow it differs
Presented investor deckInvestors in a meetingSparse slides; you do the explaining
Send-ahead or reading deckInvestors reading aloneMore text, because it must make sense without you
Demo day or competition deckA room or judging panelA few minutes, so fewer slides
Sales deckA potential customerProblem, product and price; no fundraising ask
Internal pitch deckManagers or a budget committeeAsks for budget, people or a go-ahead

Pitch deck example: slides and what to say

Example: a made-up company renting reusable takeaway boxes to restaurants; all numbers are invented. The third column is what you say. The slide holds the chart or screenshot that proves it.

#SlideExample line to say
1Title"We rent reusable takeaway boxes to restaurants, so their packaging stops ending up in the bin."
2Problem"A busy takeaway buys around 2,000 single-use boxes a month, and the price has gone up twice this year."
3Solution"Customers return the box to any partner restaurant. We collect, wash and bring it back."
4Product"This is the restaurant dashboard: boxes out, boxes back, boxes due."
5Market"Our city has about 900 independent takeaways. We're starting with the 120 closest to our washing site."
6Business model"Restaurants pay a monthly fee per box in use, less than they spend on single-use boxes now."
7Traction"Eighteen restaurants pay us today, and 91 percent of boxes come back."
8Competition"The alternatives are cheap plastic or pricier compostable boxes. Ours get cheaper with every reuse."
9Team"I ran operations for a laundry chain. My co-founder built route planning for a courier firm."
10Financials"A restaurant becomes profitable for us in its fourth month."
11The ask"We're raising £400,000 to reach 150 restaurants by next autumn."

If you can't say a slide in one sentence, it is probably doing two jobs. For the full spoken pitch and the questions investors ask, see how to pitch to investors.

Pitch deck vs. elevator pitch vs. business plan

Pitch deckElevator pitchBusiness plan
Form10 to 15 slides plus your talkSpoken, no slidesA long written document
LengthOften 10 to 20 minutes30 to 60 secondsMany pages
JobWin the money or the next meetingStart a conversationShow in detail how the business will run

Mistakes when presenting a pitch deck

MistakeInstead
Reading the slide word for wordSay the point; let the slide back it up
One huge market figureCount real buyers you could reach
Saving your best number for slide 9If traction is your strongest point, move it up
Ending on "Any questions?"End on the ask, then take questions

How to practise presenting your deck

  1. Write one sentence per slide: what you'd say if you could say only one thing.
  2. Run the deck out loud with a timer. About a minute per slide puts 11 slides near 12 minutes.
  3. Practise jumping: have a friend call out "team" or "traction" mid-run and go straight there.
  4. Learn the first 60 seconds well enough to say them without looking at the screen.
  5. Do one run with someone playing an investor who interrupts. In Speaking Coach you can add your slides as a PDF, swipe through them while you speak, then answer the questions your audience would ask.

Questions people ask

What is a pitch deck in simple words?

A short slideshow that explains a business idea, usually to investors, while the founder talks them through it.

How many slides should a pitch deck have?

Often 10 to 15. Fewer is fine if each slide earns its place; a demo day version can be shorter still.

What is the difference between a pitch deck and a business plan?

A business plan is a long written document. A pitch deck is a short visual summary meant to get a meeting or support a conversation.

How long should a pitch deck presentation be?

Often 10 to 20 minutes, but ask how long you have and plan to finish early. Prepare a 3-minute version for events.

Should I send my pitch deck before the meeting?

Many founders send a fuller version ahead and present a cleaner one in the room. Ask what the investor prefers.
