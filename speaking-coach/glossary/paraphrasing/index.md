# What Is Paraphrasing in Conversation? Meaning and Examples

Paraphrasing in conversation means repeating the key points of what someone said back to them, briefly and in your own words, to check you understood. It is an active listening skill: you speak, but only to reflect their message. It is not listening without speaking, nodding in agreement or repeating their words exactly.

## Meaning

To paraphrase is to rephrase what a person tells you: the same meaning, fewer words, your own wording. You repeat back what a client, coworker or friend has said in order to:

- **check** you understood before you respond
- **show** them they were heard
- **let them correct you** or add what they left out

Example: a friend talks for a minute about a job offer in another city. You say, "So the move is exciting, but you're worried about money." That is one sentence they can confirm.

### Which of these is paraphrasing?

| Behaviour | Is it paraphrasing? |
| --- | --- |
| Repeating the key points of the conversation back to the person, in your own words | **Yes** |
| Listening without speaking | No. That is silent listening |
| Nodding in agreement | No. A nod shows attention, not what you understood |
| Repeating their sentence word for word | No. That is parroting |
| Multitasking during the conversation | No. That is not listening |

## Paraphrasing vs. repeating, summarising and reflecting feelings

Example: a coworker says, "I'm swamped this week. Two people are off and the audit is on Friday."

| Skill | What you do | Example reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Repeating** (parroting) | Say their exact words back | "You're swamped this week." |
| **Paraphrasing** | Restate the key points in your own words | "So you're covering for two people with the audit due Friday." |
| **Summarising** | Pull together the main points of a longer talk | "So far: you're short-staffed, the audit is Friday, and the report can wait." |
| **Reflecting feelings** | Name the emotion you hear | "That sounds stressful." |
| **Interpreting** | Add a conclusion they didn't make | "So you hate this job." |

Repeating can sound robotic. Interpreting may annoy them. The middle three show you listened.

## Sentence starters

- "So what you're saying is…"
- "It sounds like…"
- "If I've got this right…"
- "Let me check I understood…"
- "So the main thing is…"
- "In other words…"

When the stakes are high, end with a check: "Is that right?"

## Example exchanges

Made-up examples.

### With a customer or client

> **Customer:** "I've called twice, nobody calls back, and the part still isn't here."
>
> **You:** "So you've chased this twice and you still have no part and no answer. Is that right?"
>
> **Customer:** "Yes. And I need it by Friday."

### With a coworker

> **Coworker:** "I've rewritten this deck three times and every time someone new has comments."
>
> **You:** "So the goalposts keep moving because more people keep joining the review."
>
> **Coworker:** "Exactly. I need one person to sign it off."

### In an interview

> **Interviewer:** "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a decision and what happened."
>
> **You:** "So you'd like an example where I disagreed with a decision, and how it turned out?"
>
> **Interviewer:** "That's right."

### With a friend

> **Friend:** "I like the new flat, but it's a long way from everyone and I've barely been out."
>
> **You:** "The flat's good, but you're feeling cut off."
>
> **Friend:** "A bit, yeah. Mostly at weekends."

Each paraphrase draws out what you need next: a deadline, a fix, a feeling.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Try instead |
| --- | --- |
| Parroting their exact words | Change the wording, keep the meaning |
| Adding your own conclusion | Stick to what they said |
| Paraphrasing after every sentence | Once, at a key moment |
| A paraphrase longer than the original | One sentence |
| Offering a fix before they confirm | Wait for a yes or a correction |

Paraphrasing is one part of [active listening](/speaking-coach/glossary/active-listening/). It works best followed by a [follow-up question](/speaking-coach/glossary/follow-up-question/) or a next step.

## Questions people ask

### What is paraphrasing in communication?

Restating the key points of what someone said, in your own words, so they can confirm you understood. It is part of active listening.

### Why repeat back what a client has said?

To check you understood the problem before you try to solve it, to show the client they were heard, and to let them correct you or add details.

### Is paraphrasing the same as repeating?

No. Repeating uses the speaker's exact words. Paraphrasing keeps the meaning but changes and shortens the wording, which shows you understood rather than just heard.

### Is paraphrasing the same as mirroring?

Not quite. Paraphrasing restates the meaning in new words. [Mirroring](/speaking-coach/glossary/mirroring/) usually means repeating a few of their exact words, or matching their tone and posture.

### Should I paraphrase interview questions?

For long or multi-part questions, a quick check like "So you'd like an example of…" can help. Don't do it for every question.

### What if my paraphrase is wrong?

That's fine; it's part of the point. They'll correct you, and you'll both be clearer.
