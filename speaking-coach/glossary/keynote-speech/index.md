# Keynote Speech: Meaning, Structure and Examples

A keynote speech is the main talk at a conference or event, given by a featured speaker to set the theme for everything that follows. Keynotes often run 20 to 60 minutes and are built around one central idea, carried by stories. Because the whole audience hears it, a keynote has to work for mixed levels and leave people with one line they can repeat.

## Meaning

The word comes from music. Wiktionary defines a keynote as the note a musical key is based on, and also as the main theme of a speech or conference. A keynote speech does the same job for an event: it sounds the theme that every other session plays in.

It is also called a keynote address, and the person giving it is the keynote speaker. It is not the same as Keynote, Apple's presentation app, although keynote speakers sometimes use it.

## Types of keynote

| Type | When | Its job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening keynote | Start of the event | Set the theme and the energy |
| Closing keynote | End of the event | Send people home with one message |
| Day or track keynote | Start of a day or a track | Frame that day's sessions |
| Company keynote | A company's own event | Announce products, results or strategy |

## Keynote vs. other conference talks

| Talk | Typical length | Its job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keynote | 20–60 minutes | Set the theme for the whole event |
| Breakout session | 30–60 minutes | Teach one topic to a smaller group |
| Panel | 45–60 minutes, shared | Several views on one question |
| [Lightning talk](/speaking-coach/presentations/lightning-talk/) | About 5 minutes | One point, fast |

## Example structure for a 30-minute keynote

| Part | Time | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 0–3 min | A [hook](/speaking-coach/glossary/speech-hook/): a story or question tied to the theme |
| The big idea | 3–6 min | One sentence the audience could repeat later |
| Three supporting points | 6–24 min | Each with a story, example or piece of evidence |
| What it means for this room | 24–28 min | How the idea applies to their work |
| Close | 28–30 min | Return to the opening story; one clear ask |

At a typical presentation pace of about 130 to 150 words per minute, 30 minutes is roughly 3,900 to 4,500 spoken words. The [speech length calculator](/speaking-coach/tools/speech-length-calculator/) converts other lengths.

## Keynote speech examples

Made-up examples for three events, to show how the opening, the big idea and the close connect.

### A nursing conference

- **Opening:** "Last winter, a patient on our ward told her story four times in one day, once to every new shift."
- **Big idea:** "The best handover is the one the next shift can repeat back to you."
- **Close:** "Before you leave today, write down one handover you'll change on Monday."

### A teachers' conference

- **Opening:** "Every teacher in this room has heard it: 'When will we ever use this?'"
- **Big idea:** "Answer that question in the first five minutes of the lesson, not the last."
- **Close:** "Next time a student asks, don't answer. Ask them to guess. Then build the lesson on what they say."

### A company sales kickoff

- **Opening:** "Last year we lost our biggest customer. In March they came back. Here's what changed."
- **Big idea:** "We win when the customer talks more than we do."
- **Close:** "In your next three calls this week, count who speaks more. Then tell your manager."

Each close points back at the big idea and asks for one small, specific action.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| Five big ideas | One idea, with three points that support it |
| A specialist talk at keynote length | Pitch it for the whole room, not the experts |
| Reading from slides | Learn the structure; use slides for images and a few words |
| Running over | Finish a minute early; the next session depends on it |

## Questions people ask

### What does keynote speech mean?

It means the main speech at an event, the one that sets its theme. "Keynote" comes from the musical note a key is based on.

### Who gives the keynote?

Usually a featured speaker the organisers invite: a leader in the field, an executive, or someone with a story that fits the theme.

### How long is a keynote speech?

Most fall between 20 and 60 minutes. Ask the organiser for your exact slot and whether it includes questions.

### What is the difference between a keynote and a plenary?

A plenary is any session the whole audience attends. A keynote is usually a plenary, but not every plenary session is a keynote.

### What is the difference between a keynote speaker and a guest speaker?

A guest speaker is anyone invited to talk. The keynote speaker gives the headline talk that frames the event; there is often only one per day.

### Do keynote speakers use slides?

Many do, mostly for images and a few words. Some speak without slides. The talk should still work if the projector fails.
