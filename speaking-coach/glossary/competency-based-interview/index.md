# Competency-Based Interview: Meaning, Questions and Examples

A competency-based interview is a structured job interview in which each question asks for a real example of one skill the job needs, such as teamwork, decision-making or communication, and the panel scores your answer against set criteria. It is common in UK public-sector hiring and at large employers. Answer each question with one specific past example, usually in the STAR order: situation, task, action, result.

## Meaning

A competency is a skill or behaviour the job needs. The employer chooses the competencies for the role in advance, often four to six, and decides what a good example of each looks like. Every question asks for evidence of one of them, and your answer is marked against that list. It is also called a competency interview or a competence-based interview.

In the UK Civil Service, recruitment uses a framework called Success Profiles. Its "Behaviours" element works the same way. There are nine behaviours: seeing the big picture, changing and improving, making effective decisions, leadership, communicating and influencing, working together, developing self and others, managing a quality service and delivering at pace. The recruiting manager picks the ones that apply, the job advert names them, and you may be asked for examples of when you have shown each one.

## Competency-based vs. behavioral vs. situational interview

| Type | Typical question | What you give |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competency-based | "Give an example of a decision you made with incomplete information." | A past example matched to a named, scored skill |
| [Behavioral](/speaking-coach/glossary/behavioral-interview/) | "Tell me about a time you missed a deadline." | A past story; the skills may not be named |
| [Situational](/speaking-coach/glossary/situational-interview/) | "What would you do if a key supplier pulled out?" | A plan for a made-up scenario |
| Strengths-based | "What kind of work gives you energy?" | What you enjoy and do well |

## Competency-based interview questions, with what a good answer shows

| Competency | Example question | A good answer shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teamwork | "Tell me about a time you helped a team reach a shared goal." | Your own part, not just the team's |
| Decision-making | "Describe a decision you made with incomplete information." | What you knew, what you didn't, how you weighed it |
| Communication | "Give an example of explaining something complex to a non-expert." | How you adapted to the listener and checked they understood |
| Leadership | "Tell me about a time you motivated others without formal authority." | What you said or did to bring people along |
| Delivering at pace | "Describe a time you met a deadline under pressure." | How you prioritised and what you dropped |
| Customer focus | "Give an example of going out of your way for a customer." | The need you spotted and the result for them |
| Adaptability | "Tell me about a time a plan changed at short notice." | How fast you re-planned and who you told |
| Problem-solving | "Describe a problem you solved that others had given up on." | The steps of your reasoning, out loud |

## Example answer: decision-making

A made-up example for an operations role, in STAR order.

> **Situation:** "Last spring our main packaging supplier closed with two weeks' notice."
>
> **Task:** "I had two days to choose a replacement, with quotes from two suppliers instead of the usual five."
>
> **Action:** "I listed the three things we couldn't compromise on: delivery time, food-safe certification and price. I called a customer of each, then chose the one that could deliver in five days, on a one-month trial."
>
> **Result:** "We didn't miss an order, and we kept that supplier after the trial. Next time I'd keep a back-up supplier approved in advance."

The panel can mark each part. For the method in full, see the [STAR method guide](/speaking-coach/guides/star-method/).

## How answers are judged

Panels usually look for a specific example, not "I always…"; what **you** did, not what "we" did; the behaviours named in the job advert; and a result, ideally with what you learned.

## How to prepare

1. List the competencies from the job advert or person specification.
2. Write two examples for each, in three lines: situation, what you did, result.
3. Use the advert's own words when you describe your action.
4. Say each answer out loud and time it; aim for about two minutes.
5. Practise the follow-ups: "What exactly was your part?" and "What would you do differently?"

## Questions people ask

### What is a competency-based interview in simple terms?

An interview where every question asks you to prove one job skill with a real example from your past, and your answer is scored.

### Is a competency-based interview the same as a behavioral interview?

Very close. Both ask for past examples. Competency-based interviews tie every question to a named skill and usually score it.

### How do I know which competencies will come up?

Read the job advert and person specification. The competencies or behaviours are usually listed there.

### How long should each answer be?

About two minutes. Panels often have a fixed number of questions in a set time.

### Can I use the same example twice?

Better not. Use a different example for each competency so you show range.

### Can my examples come from outside work?

Often yes. The UK Civil Service, for example, accepts examples from work experience or volunteering.
