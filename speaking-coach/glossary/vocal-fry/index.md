# What is vocal fry? Definition and example in speaking

Vocal fry, also called creaky voice, is a low, crackling or popping sound that happens when your voice drops to the bottom of its range, usually at the end of a sentence. Almost everyone does it sometimes. Whether it matters depends on how often it happens and how easily listeners can still hear your words.

## What it sounds like

Take the line "I'll send it over this afternoon." In fry, the pitch sinks on the last word and "afternoon" turns into a slow, rattly creak, like a door hinge or a stick dragged along a fence. The words are still there, but the sound gets quieter and rougher as the sentence ends.

It usually appears:

- on the last word or two of a sentence,
- when you're talking quietly or feel relaxed,
- when you're tired, or first thing in the morning,
- when you're running out of breath.

## Why it happens

Your voice is made by the vocal folds vibrating as air passes through them. When pitch goes very low and there's little air pushing through, they vibrate slowly and unevenly, and you hear individual pops instead of a smooth tone. That's why fry often turns up as a sentence trails off: pitch is falling and the breath is nearly used up.

## How listeners may hear it

In a quiet room, face to face, many listeners won't notice. On a phone line, a video call or a recording, the creaky part is quieter and less clear, so the ends of sentences, often where the key word sits, can be harder to catch. Some listeners also have strong opinions about fry; those say more about the listener than about your voice.

If you want your sentence endings to carry, take a breath before longer sentences and keep a little air for the last word. See [how to breathe while speaking](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-breathe-while-speaking/) and [how to project your voice](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-project-your-voice/).

## Questions people ask

### Is vocal fry bad?

It's a normal part of many people's speech. If your voice is often hoarse or speaking hurts, see a doctor or a speech and language therapist.

### Do men use vocal fry?

Yes. Anyone can, at any age. It depends on pitch and breath, not gender.

### Is vocal fry the same as uptalk?

No. Fry is a creaky sound at a low pitch. [Uptalk](/speaking-coach/glossary/uptalk/) is pitch rising at the end of a statement so it sounds like a question.

### How do I know if I use vocal fry?

[Record yourself](/speaking-coach/guides/record-yourself-speaking/) talking for a minute and listen only to the last word of each sentence.
