Anecdote: Meaning, Examples and How to Tell One in a Speech

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 16, 2026

An anecdote is a short, true account of one small incident, told to make a point concrete, human or funny. In a speech, an interview or a conversation it usually runs 20 to 60 seconds, covers a single moment and ends on one clear point. It is shorter than a full story, which has a longer arc of problem, turning point and change.

What anecdote means

An anecdote is what you tell when someone could fairly ask "For instance?" It is one thing that happened, to you or to someone you know, told briefly because it proves or lightens the point you are making. People use anecdotes in speeches, toasts, lessons, interview answers and small talk. The adjective is anecdotal: "anecdotal evidence" means individual accounts rather than data.

The parts of a good anecdote

PartWhat it doesIn the speech example below
A time and placePuts the listener in one moment"In my first week here"
What happenedThe small incident, in orderAn afternoon spent hunting for the shared drive
One vivid detailMakes it sound real"I even tried guessing the address"
The turnThe surprise or the fixA colleague shows the way in ten seconds
The pointWhy you told it, in one line"Ask in the first hour, not the fourth"

Three short anecdote examples

All three are made-up examples.

In a speech

A talk to new hires about asking questions (about 70 words, 30 seconds):

"In my first week here, I spent an entire afternoon trying to find the shared drive. I searched every folder, I read old emails, I even tried guessing the address. At five o'clock I finally asked the person next to me. She showed me in about ten seconds. So here's my advice for your first week: ask in the first hour, not the fourth."

In an interview answer

Asked "How do you handle mistakes?" for a support role:

"In my second month, I sent a refund to the wrong customer. I noticed twenty minutes later, told my manager before anyone asked, and phoned both customers myself that afternoon. Since then I read the name back before I press send, and it hasn't happened again."

Longer interview stories usually follow the STAR method. An anecdote is the 30-second version.

In small talk

Asked "How was your weekend?":

"Good, thanks. I tried to make bread for the first time. It came out so flat that my daughter asked if it was a biscuit. We ate it anyway. Do you bake at all?"

In conversation, the point can simply be a laugh, and the question at the end hands the turn back.

Anecdote vs. story vs. example

KindLengthWhat it containsSounds like
ExampleA sentenceA general case, no particular moment"Many new starters struggle to find things."
Anecdote20–60 secondsOne specific moment and one point"In my first week, I spent an afternoon looking for the shared drive…"
Story2–5 minutes or moreA full arc: setup, problem, turning point, changeThe whole account of your first year, and how it changed how you onboard people

Anecdotes are the most common of the three in speeches, because they fit almost anywhere. For the longer form, see how to tell a story in a presentation.

How to tell one well

  1. Start in the moment. "In my first week here…", not "So, I'm not sure if I've told you this, but…".
  2. Keep one vivid detail. "Even tried guessing the address" makes it real.
  3. Cut the back-story. Nobody needs to know which month it was.
  4. Say the point in one line right after, so nobody wonders why you told it.
  5. Keep it true, and say whose it is. If it happened to a friend, say "A friend told me…". If you change details for privacy, keep the point honest.

Questions people ask

What is an anecdote in simple words?

A short, true story about one thing that happened, told to make a point or raise a smile.

What is an example of an anecdote?

A speaker telling new hires about the afternoon they once lost looking for the shared drive, to make the point "ask early". The full version is above, with two more.

How long should an anecdote be?

In a speech or an interview, 20 to 60 seconds. That is about 50 to 150 words at a normal speaking pace.

Does an anecdote have to be true?

It's expected to be. If it's made up or a hypothetical, say so: "Imagine you…".

Is an anecdote evidence?

Not on its own. It illustrates a point; in a persuasive talk, pair it with facts.

How many anecdotes should a speech have?

One to three in a five-to-ten-minute talk is plenty. Each should support a different point.
