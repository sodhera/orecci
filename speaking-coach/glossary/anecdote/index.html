What is an anecdote in a speech? Definition and examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

An anecdote is a brief, true account of one small incident, told in a speech to make a point concrete or human. It's shorter than a full story: usually 20 to 60 seconds about a single moment, with one point and no long build-up of conflict and change.

Anecdote, story or example?

KindLengthWhat it containsSounds like
ExampleA sentenceA general case, no particular moment"Many new starters struggle to find things."
Anecdote20–60 secondsOne specific moment and one point"In my first week, I spent an afternoon looking for the shared drive…"
Story2–5 minutes or moreA full arc: setup, problem, turning point, changeThe whole account of your first year, and how it changed how you onboard people

Anecdotes are the most common of the three in speeches, because they fit almost anywhere: an opening, a toast, a team talk, an interview answer.

A short sample

A made-up anecdote for a talk to new hires about asking questions (about 70 words, 30 seconds):

"In my first week here, I spent an entire afternoon trying to find the shared drive. I searched every folder, I read old emails, I even tried guessing the address. At five o'clock I finally asked the person next to me. She showed me in about ten seconds. So here's my advice for your first week: ask in the first hour, not the fourth."

How to tell one well

  1. Start in the moment. "In my first week here…", not "So, I'm not sure if I've told you this, but…".
  2. Keep one vivid detail. "Even tried guessing the address" makes it real.
  3. Cut the back-story. Nobody needs to know which month it was.
  4. Say the point in one line right after, so nobody wonders why you told it.
  5. Keep it true. If you change details for privacy, keep the point honest.

Questions people ask

Does an anecdote have to be true?

It's expected to be. If it's made up or a hypothetical, say so: "Imagine you…".

Can I use someone else's anecdote?

Yes, if you credit them ("A friend told me…") and don't tell it as if it happened to you.

How many anecdotes should a speech have?

One to three in a five-to-ten-minute talk is plenty. Each should support a different point.

Is an anecdote evidence?

Not on its own. It illustrates a point; in a persuasive talk, pair it with facts.
