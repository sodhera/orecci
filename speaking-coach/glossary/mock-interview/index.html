What is a mock interview? Definition and how it works

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

A mock interview is a practice interview that copies a real one: someone plays the interviewer, asks the questions you're likely to get, follows up, and gives you feedback afterwards. It can be run by a friend, a mentor, a university career office, a paid coach or an AI practice partner. Its value comes from answering out loud, under some pressure, before it counts.

How a mock interview works

  1. Pick the real target. Share the job post and your CV with whoever plays the interviewer.
  2. Prepare questions. A mix of common ones ("Tell me about yourself"), behavioral ones and anything specific to the role.
  3. Run it in real conditions. Same format (video, phone or in person), same length, no stopping to restart.
  4. Expect follow-ups. "What exactly did you do?" or "What would you change?" are where most answers are tested.
  5. Get feedback right after, while it's fresh: what worked, what was vague, how you sounded.
  6. Redo the weakest answer straight away, so you leave with an improved version.

What you get from it

  • Answers that hold up when you say them out loud, not just in your head.
  • Practice with follow-up questions, which you can't rehearse alone.
  • Timing: most people find their answers run long the first time.
  • Less surprise on the day, which helps with nerves.

Questions people ask

How many mock interviews should I do?

Two or three before an important interview is common. Leave time between them to work on the feedback.

What should I wear to a mock interview?

For a full run-through, what you'd wear to the real one. It's part of the rehearsal.

Can I do a mock interview alone?

You can record yourself answering a list of questions, but you miss follow-ups. An AI partner or a friend adds those.

Are mock interviews worth it?

They're one of the most direct ways to practise, because they make you speak your answers under realistic pressure.
