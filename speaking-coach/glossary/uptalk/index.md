# What is uptalk? Definition and example of rising intonation

Uptalk is ending a statement with a rising pitch, so it sounds like a question even when it isn't. It is also called upspeak or high rising terminal. Listeners may hear it as friendly and inclusive, or as unsure, depending on the setting and how often it happens.

## Definition

In most English statements, your pitch falls on the last stressed word. With uptalk it rises instead, with the same lift you hear at the end of a yes/no question such as "Are you coming?" Linguists call the pattern a high rising terminal. You will also see it called upspeak or rising inflection. It is part of how many people and many varieties of English speak, so it is a pattern, not a defect.

## Example

Example: the same two sentences from an interview answer, said two ways (↘ falling, ↗ rising).

> "I led the project ↘. We finished two weeks early ↘."

> "I led the project ↗? We finished two weeks early ↗?"

The words are identical. The second version can sound as if you're checking whether you're allowed to claim the result.

## How listeners may read it

| Setting | How uptalk may come across |
| --- | --- |
| Chatting with friends | Warm, inviting the other person to react |
| Telling a story | "Are you with me?" checks, usually fine |
| Interview answer or status update | Unsure of your own facts |
| Stating a price or a deadline | Open to being talked down |

The issue is rarely one rising sentence. It's when every statement rises, including the ones that carry your key facts. See [intonation](/speaking-coach/glossary/intonation/) for how pitch patterns carry meaning more generally.

## How to hear your own

1. Record a 30-second answer to "What do you do?"
2. Listen only to the last word of each sentence and mark it ↗ or ↘.
3. Say the sentences that carry facts again, letting the last word drop.

[Recording yourself](/speaking-coach/guides/record-yourself-speaking/) is the quickest way to notice it, because you rarely hear it while talking.

## Questions people ask

### Is uptalk the same as vocal fry?

No. Uptalk is a pitch pattern at the end of sentences. [Vocal fry](/speaking-coach/glossary/vocal-fry/) is a low, creaky voice quality.

### Is uptalk bad?

Not in itself. It can sound friendly in conversation. It's worth reducing when you need to sound certain, such as in interviews, negotiations or presentations.

### Why do people uptalk?

Often to check that the listener is following, or to soften a statement. Nerves can make it more frequent.

### How do I stop uptalking in an interview?

Pick the sentences that carry your results and numbers, and practise saying them with a falling finish and a short pause after.
