Uptalk (Rising Inflection): Meaning and Examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 16, 2026

Uptalk is ending a statement with a rising pitch, so it sounds like a question even when it isn't: "My name is Dana?" It is also called upspeak, rising inflection or high rising terminal. Listeners may hear it as friendly and open, or as unsure, depending on the setting and how often it happens.

Meaning

In most English statements, your pitch falls on the last stressed word. With uptalk it rises instead, with the same lift you hear at the end of a yes/no question such as "Are you coming?" The words say statement. The melody says question. That is why people describe it as "talking in questions".

Other names for the same thing:

  • Upspeak, or "up talk" written as two words. The habit is called uptalking or up speaking.
  • Rising inflection: the plain description of what the voice does.
  • High rising terminal (HRT) or high rising intonation: the names linguists use.

It is a pattern, not a defect. Wikipedia's article on the high rising terminal notes it in parts of the United States, Canada, Australia and Ireland, among other places. It adds that uptalk is often claimed to be more common among younger speakers and women, though it is not limited to either group.

Examples of uptalk

Made-up examples. The arrows show the pitch on the last word: ↘ falls, ↗ rises.

SituationAs a statementWith uptalk
A plain fact"The report is ready ↘.""The report is ready ↗?"
Interview answer"I led the project ↘. We finished two weeks early ↘.""I led the project ↗? We finished two weeks early ↗?"
Presentation"Sales grew twelve percent ↘.""Sales grew twelve percent ↗?"
Introducing yourself"I'm Dana ↘. I work in finance ↘.""I'm Dana ↗? I work in finance ↗?"
Your name on the phone"It's Dana Reyes ↘, calling about the invoice ↘.""It's Dana Reyes ↗? Calling about the invoice ↗?"
Stating a price"The fee is nine hundred pounds ↘.""The fee is nine hundred pounds ↗?"

The words in each pair are identical. The uptalk version can sound as if you're checking whether you're allowed to say it.

When uptalk is useful

Uptalk does real work in conversation:

UseExampleWhat it tells the listener
Checking understanding"Take the second left ↗, go past the bakery ↗, and it's on your right ↘.""Are you with me so far?"
Holding the floor"So we tested it on Monday ↗, and it failed ↗, so we changed the design ↘.""I haven't finished yet."
Sounding approachable"I thought the second option was stronger ↗.""That's my view, and you can disagree."

The Wikipedia article cites research on Sydney speech in which the rise often discouraged interruption by showing the speaker had not finished, and other research suggesting it helps speakers establish common ground.

When uptalk costs you

SettingHow uptalk may come across
Interview answer or status updateUnsure of your own facts
Stating a price or a deadlineOpen to being talked down
Giving your name or job titleAsking permission to be there
Every sentence of a presentationHard to tell which points matter

The issue is rarely one rising sentence. It's when every statement rises, including the ones that carry your key facts. See intonation for how pitch patterns carry meaning more generally.

How to reduce uptalk, step by step

  1. Record a 30-second answer to "What do you do?"
  2. Listen only to the last word of each sentence and mark it ↗ or ↘.
  3. Underline the sentences that carry facts: your name, numbers, results, decisions.
  4. Say each of those again and let the last word drop, then pause for a beat.
  5. Record again and compare. Leave the friendly rises in casual talk alone.

Recording yourself is the quickest way to notice uptalk, because you rarely hear it while talking.

Questions people ask

What is an example of uptalk?

Saying "I have five years of experience?" with your voice rising on "experience", when you mean it as a fact. Said with a falling pitch, the same words sound certain.

What does rising inflection mean?

Rising inflection means your pitch goes up at the end of a phrase. It is normal in yes/no questions. At the end of a statement, it is called uptalk.

Is uptalk bad?

Not in itself. It can sound friendly in conversation. It's worth reducing when you need to sound certain, such as in interviews, negotiations or presentations.

Why do people uptalk?

Often to check that the listener is following, to show they haven't finished, or to soften a statement. Nerves can make it more frequent.

Is uptalk the same as vocal fry?

No. Uptalk is a pitch pattern at the end of sentences. Vocal fry is a low, creaky voice quality.

How do I stop uptalking in an interview?

Pick the sentences that carry your results and numbers, and practise saying them with a falling finish and a short pause after.
