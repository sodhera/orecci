# What is hedging language? Definition and examples of hedge words

Hedging language is words and phrases that soften or qualify what you say, such as "just", "maybe", "I think", "sort of" and "I might be wrong, but". Hedges are useful when you're genuinely unsure or being tactful, but stacked in front of a point you're sure of, they make it sound weaker than it is.

## Common hedge words

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Minimisers | just, only, a little, a bit |
| Uncertainty | maybe, perhaps, possibly, probably |
| Opinion markers | I think, I feel, I guess, I suppose |
| Vague softeners | sort of, kind of, somewhat |
| Pre-apologies | "This might be a silly question, but…", "I'm no expert, but…" |
| Tag questions | "…, right?", "…, does that make sense?" |

Hedges are different from [filler words](/speaking-coach/glossary/filler-words/) like "um", which fill silence. Hedges change the meaning: they make a claim smaller.

## Before and after

Examples of the same point, hedged and direct.

| Hedged | Direct |
| --- | --- |
| "I just wanted to maybe suggest we could possibly move the deadline?" | "I suggest we move the deadline to the 20th." |
| "I'm not sure, but I sort of think the numbers might be off." | "I think the numbers are off. Row 12 double-counts returns." |
| "Sorry, this is probably a dumb question, but who owns this?" | "Who owns this?" |
| "I kind of led the project, I guess." | "I led the project." |

Notice that "I think" survives in one direct version. One hedge, followed by evidence, is honest. Three in a row sounds unsure.

## When hedging helps

- You genuinely don't know: "I believe it's Thursday, but I'll check."
- Giving sensitive feedback: "It might help to cut the intro."
- Disagreeing with someone senior in a culture where directness reads as rude
- Speaking outside your expertise

## When it weakens you

- Stating your own results in an interview
- Making a recommendation you've already researched
- Asking for something you need: a raise, a deadline, a decision

Try this: record yourself making a recommendation, list every hedge, and keep only the ones that are true. See [how to speak up in meetings](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-speak-up-in-meetings/).

## Questions people ask

### Is hedging always bad?

No. It's accurate when you're unsure and polite when feedback is delicate. The problem is hedging things you're sure of.

### Is "I think" a hedge?

Yes, but a mild one. It's fine for opinions; drop it for facts.

### Why do people hedge so much?

Often to avoid sounding pushy or to protect themselves if they're wrong. Nerves increase it.

### What is the difference between hedging and filler words?

Fillers fill silence ("um", "uh"). Hedges soften the claim itself ("maybe", "sort of").
