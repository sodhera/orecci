Hedging Language: Meaning, Hedge Words and Examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

Hedging language is words and phrases that soften or qualify what you say, such as "just", "maybe", "I think", "sort of" and "I might be wrong, but". In speech, a hedge is useful when you are genuinely unsure or being tactful, but stacked in front of a point you are sure of, hedges make it sound weaker than it is.

Meaning

Linguists call these words hedges. Wikipedia defines a hedge as a word or phrase used to express ambiguity, probability, caution or indecisiveness. A hedge can be a single word ("somewhat"), a clause ("I'm not an expert, but") or a tag question ("isn't it?").

Hedging is not a mistake. It becomes a habit worth noticing when you hedge things you know.

Hedge words: examples by type

TypeHedge words and phrases
Minimisersjust, only, a little, a bit, slightly
Uncertaintymaybe, perhaps, possibly, probably, might, could
Opinion markersI think, I feel, I guess, I suppose, I believe
Vague softenerssort of, kind of, somewhat, more or less
Distanceit seems, it looks like, apparently, tends to
Approximatorsabout, around, roughly, ballpark
Pre-apologies"This might be a silly question, but…", "I'm no expert, but…", "Correct me if I'm wrong…"
Tag questions"…, right?", "…, isn't it?", "…, does that make sense?"

Hedges are different from filler words like "um", which fill silence. Hedges change the meaning: they make a claim smaller.

Examples of hedging in speech: hedged vs direct

Made-up examples of the same point, hedged and direct.

SettingHedgedDirect
Meeting"I just wanted to maybe suggest we could possibly move the deadline?""I suggest we move the deadline to the 20th."
Meeting"I'm not sure, but I sort of think the numbers might be off.""I think the numbers are off. Row 12 double-counts returns."
Interview"I kind of led the project, I guess.""I led the project."
Interview"I'm probably quite good with data?""I'm good with data. I built the weekly sales report."
Presentation"This slide sort of shows that sales are maybe flattening a bit.""This slide shows sales flattening since March."
Asking for a raise"I was just wondering if maybe there's any chance of a review?""I'd like to discuss my salary. Can we set a time this month?"
Asking a question"Sorry, this is probably a dumb question, but who owns this?""Who owns this?"

"I think" survives in one direct version. One hedge, followed by evidence, is honest. Three in a row sounds unsure. A rising pitch at the end works like a hedge too; see uptalk.

When hedging helps

SituationUseful hedge
You genuinely don't know"I believe it's Thursday, but I'll check."
Giving sensitive feedback"It might help to cut the intro."
Disagreeing with someone senior"I see it a bit differently. Can I explain why?"
Speaking outside your expertise"From what I've read, the newer version is faster."
Giving an estimate"Roughly three weeks, depending on testing."

When it weakens you

  • Stating your own results in an interview
  • Making a recommendation you've already researched
  • Asking for something you need: a raise, a deadline, a decision

How to cut unnecessary hedges

  1. Record yourself making a one-minute recommendation.
  2. Write it out or read the transcript, and circle every hedge.
  3. For each one, ask: am I actually unsure about this? Keep the true hedges and delete the rest.
  4. Replace a deleted hedge with evidence: a number, a date or an example.
  5. Say it again, and end each sentence on a falling pitch.

Questions people ask

What are hedge words?

Words that soften a statement or show uncertainty, such as "just", "maybe", "probably", "I think", "sort of" and "kind of".

What is an example of hedging?

"I just think maybe we could try a different approach?" The direct version is "Let's try a different approach." Both say the same thing; the first sounds far less sure.

Is hedging always bad?

No. It's accurate when you're unsure and polite when feedback is delicate. The problem is hedging things you're sure of.

Is "I think" a hedge?

Yes, but a mild one. It's fine for opinions; drop it for facts.

Why do people hedge so much?

Often to avoid sounding pushy or to protect themselves if they're wrong. Nerves increase it.

What is the difference between hedging and filler words?

Fillers fill silence ("um", "uh"). Hedges soften the claim itself ("maybe", "sort of").
