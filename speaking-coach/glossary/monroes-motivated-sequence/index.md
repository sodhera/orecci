# What is Monroe's motivated sequence? Definition and example

Monroe's motivated sequence is a five-step structure for persuasive speeches: Attention, Need, Satisfaction, Visualization and Action. You grab attention, show a problem, offer your solution, help the audience picture the result, then ask them to act. Alan H. Monroe developed it at Purdue University in the 1930s.

## The five steps, with examples

Example topic: a student asking the school to install water refill stations. All details are made up.

| Step | What you do | Example line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Attention** | Grab interest with a hook | "This term, the bin outside our canteen collected 3,112 plastic bottles. I counted." |
| **2. Need** | Show the problem and why it matters to them | "We pay for every one, the vending machine runs out by lunch, and the fountains don't fill a bottle." |
| **3. Satisfaction** | Present your solution and how it works | "Two refill stations, one by the gym and one in the canteen, paid from this year's student fund." |
| **4. Visualization** | Help them picture the result, or the cost of doing nothing | "Imagine next summer: no queue at the machine, cold water after practice, and a bin that's half empty." |
| **5. Action** | Ask for a specific step | "Vote yes on Friday's student fund proposal." |

## Why it works

The order follows how people tend to be persuaded: they need to care before they listen to a solution, and they need to picture a better outcome before they act. The visualization step is the one most speakers skip, and it's often what makes the [call to action](/speaking-coach/glossary/call-to-action/) feel natural.

## Rough timing for a 5-minute speech

| Step | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Attention | 30 seconds |
| Need | 1 minute 15 seconds |
| Satisfaction | 1 minute 30 seconds |
| Visualization | 1 minute |
| Action | 30 seconds |

## Questions people ask

### Who created Monroe's motivated sequence?

Alan H. Monroe, a professor at Purdue University, in the 1930s.

### Can I use it outside speeches?

Yes. It fits sales pitches, fundraising appeals and proposals to a manager.

### What is the visualization step?

Describing what the future looks like if the audience acts, or what happens if they don't, in concrete detail.

### Is it the only persuasive structure?

No. Problem-solution and [ethos, pathos and logos](/speaking-coach/glossary/ethos-pathos-logos/) are other common approaches. Monroe's adds the visualization and action steps.
