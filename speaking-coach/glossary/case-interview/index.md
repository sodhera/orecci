# Case Interview: Meaning, Types and Example Questions

A case interview is a job interview in which you are given a business problem, such as why a company's profit is falling, and asked to solve it out loud with the interviewer, usually in 20 to 40 minutes. It is used mostly for consulting roles and by some strategy, product and finance teams. Interviewers judge how you structure the problem, handle numbers and explain your thinking, more than whether you reach one right answer.

## What "case" means here

A case is a short business scenario, often based on the kind of problem the firm solves for clients. Bain describes the case interview as "a structured conversation where you work through a real business problem", and says it is not looking for a single right answer; BCG says there isn't always one. What counts is the path: a clear structure, careful maths said out loud, and a recommendation a client could act on.

## Types of case interview, with example questions

These example questions are made up, in the style consulting interviews use.

| Type | Example question | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profitability | "Our client's profit fell for two years while sales stayed flat. Why?" | Splitting profit into revenue and costs |
| Market sizing | "How many coffees are sold in this city each day?" | Estimating with round numbers, out loud |
| Market entry | "Should this supermarket chain open stores in a new country?" | Weighing market, competition and cost |
| New product or growth | "A gym chain wants to grow 20% in three years. How?" | Generating and ranking options |
| Pricing | "How should a streaming service price a new family plan?" | Costs, competitors, what customers will pay |
| Acquisition | "Should a bakery chain buy a coffee roaster?" | Fit, value and risks |

Cases also differ in who steers. In an **interviewer-led** case, the interviewer moves you through set questions. In a **candidate-led** case, you decide the order and what data to ask for.

## Example: talking through a case out loud

> "Our client runs 40 gyms. Membership numbers are flat, but profit has fallen over the last two years. What's going on, and what should they do?"

1. **Play it back and clarify.** "So profit is down and members are flat. Have prices changed? And is this all 40 sites, or a few?"
2. **Ask for a moment, then lay out a structure.** "Could I take thirty seconds to organise my thoughts?" Then: "Profit is revenue minus costs, so I'll look at both. Revenue: price per member and extra sales. Costs: rent, staff and equipment."
3. **Say where you'll start.** "I'll start with costs, since revenue looks flat." Clear [signposting](/speaking-coach/glossary/signposting/) helps the interviewer follow.
4. **Do the maths out loud.** "If rent rose 10% across 40 sites at roughly £100,000 each, that's about £400,000 a year."
5. **Say what it means.** "So rent explains part of it, but not all. Can I look at staffing?"
6. **Recommend first, then give reasons.** "I'd renegotiate the leases at the five most expensive sites, because…"

The numbers are illustrative; in a real case the interviewer gives you the data. Answer-first endings follow the [pyramid principle](/speaking-coach/glossary/pyramid-principle/).

## Case interview vs. other interview types

| | Case interview | [Behavioral interview](/speaking-coach/glossary/behavioral-interview/) | [Situational question](/speaking-coach/glossary/situational-interview/) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Asks about | A business problem you've never seen | What you did in the past | What you would do in a scenario |
| Example | "Should this retailer sell online?" | "Tell me about a time you missed a deadline." | "What would you do if a client rejected your plan?" |
| How to answer | Structure, data, maths, recommendation | One true story, often in STAR form | Your steps and reasons |

Many consulting interviews mix both: a few behavioral questions, then the case.

## How to practise

1. Do one market-sizing estimate a day out loud, with round numbers.
2. Run full cases with a partner, then swap roles.
3. Record your final recommendation and keep it under 45 seconds.

## Questions people ask

### What is a case interview?

An interview where you solve a business problem out loud with the interviewer. It tests structure, numbers and clear speaking, not a memorised answer.

### What is an example of a case interview question?

"Our client's profit has fallen for two years while sales are flat. Why, and what should they do?" Market sizing questions, such as how many coffees a city sells a day, are also common.

### How long is a case interview?

The case itself usually takes 20 to 40 minutes, often after some behavioral questions.

### Can I use paper or a calculator?

Bring pen and paper. Calculators are often not allowed, so practise mental arithmetic with round numbers and check the rules with your recruiter.

### What if I get the answer wrong?

A clear, well-reasoned path with a small slip often scores better than a lucky right answer with no structure. If you spot an error, say so and correct it.
