What is the rule of three in speaking? Definition and examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

The rule of three is grouping ideas, examples or words in threes, because a set of three is easy to follow, easy to remember and feels complete. In a speech it can shape the whole talk (three main points) or a single line, such as "faster, cheaper and safer".

Definition

Listeners can't reread what you said, so they rely on patterns. Two items can feel like a comparison, and long lists start to blur. Three gives a beginning, a middle and an end, with a natural rhythm that tells people the list is finished. That is why so many talks have three main points and so many closing lines have three beats.

Example lines

These are made-up examples of the pattern for different occasions.

OccasionExample line
Wedding toast"Sam is loyal, Sam is funny, and Sam is, finally, on time."
Team update"This week we fixed the login bug, cleared the backlog and hired our new designer."
Sales pitch"It's quicker to set up, cheaper to run and easier to train people on."
Interview answer"I'd bring three things: process, patience and a lot of spreadsheets."
Eulogy"She taught us to cook, to argue fairly and to forgive."
Presentation close"Start small, measure weekly, and share what you learn."

Notice the trick in the toast: the first two items set a pattern and the third bends it for a laugh.

Where to use it

  • Structure: three main points in a 5- to 10-minute talk. Announce them with signposting: "I'll cover three things."
  • Lists: three examples instead of seven.
  • Closings: a final line in three short parts, said slowly.

Mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it hurtsInstead
Inventing a weak third itemThe filler point is the one people rememberUse two strong points
Every sentence in threesIt starts to sound like a sales scriptSave it for key moments
Three long clausesThe rhythm disappearsKeep each item to a few words

Questions people ask

Why does the rule of three work?

Three items are few enough to remember and enough to form a pattern, so the list sounds complete.

Do I always need three main points?

No. If you have two strong points, use two. The rule is a tool, not a requirement.

What is a tricolon?

The rhetorical name for a sentence made of three parallel parts, such as "quick, clear and kind."

Can I use the rule of three in interviews?

Yes. "I'd say three things" helps you and the interviewer keep track. Back each with an example.
