Diaphragmatic Breathing for Speaking: Meaning and Exercises

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

Diaphragmatic breathing, also called belly or abdominal breathing, means breathing low, so your belly and lower ribs expand as you breathe in, rather than lifting your chest and shoulders. For speaking, it gives you a steadier supply of air, so your voice stays even, carries without strain and doesn't run out mid-sentence. This page gives general practice tips for speakers, not medical advice.

What it means

The diaphragm is a large, dome-shaped muscle at the base of your lungs, as Cleveland Clinic describes it. When you breathe in, it tightens and moves down, the lungs fill, and your belly moves out to make room. Diaphragmatic breathing simply lets that happen fully instead of breathing high in the chest.

For speakers, singers and actors, it is the base of what voice teachers call breath support: enough air, released steadily, so the voice doesn't wobble or fade at the end of a sentence.

Chest breathing vs. diaphragmatic breathing

Chest breathingDiaphragmatic breathing
What movesUpper chest and shoulders riseBelly and lower ribs expand
Breath sizeOften short and shallowFuller, slower
VoiceCan sound thin, tight or shakySteadier, easier to project
Common whenNervous, rushed, slumpedRelaxed, upright

Nerves tend to push breathing up into the chest, which is one reason a voice shakes before a speech. See how to stop your voice shaking.

How to tell which one you're doing

  • Hand check: one hand on your chest, one on your belly. Breathe in. If the top hand moves most, you're chest breathing.
  • Lying down: on your back, with a book on your belly. The book should rise as you breathe in. This is often the easiest place to feel it.

A 3-minute exercise

Example practice:

  1. Sit or stand upright with your shoulders relaxed. Put one hand on your chest and one on your belly.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for about 4 counts. Aim for the belly hand to move out while the chest hand stays mostly still.
  3. Breathe out slowly on a gentle "sss" for about 6 to 8 counts.
  4. Repeat 5 times.
  5. Take the same low breath and count aloud from 1 to 10 on one breath, keeping the volume even to the last number.

Step 5 is the link to speaking. If the last numbers fade or squeeze, you started with too little air or let it out too fast.

Examples: where to breathe when you speak

Mid-speech, you won't think about your diaphragm. You can control where you breathe. Examples:

MomentWhere to breatheExample
Before a presentationOne slow, low breath before your first wordReach the front, breathe, then "Good morning."
In a presentationA small top-up at each full stop"Sales are up. / [breath] Costs are flat. / [breath] So margin is up."
In a job interviewWhile the interviewer finishes the questionBreathe in as they say "…tell me about a time", then answer
In a toastBefore you raise your glass"[breath] So please raise your glasses. To Ana."
On a video callSitting upright, feet flat, not slumpedSlumping makes a low breath harder

A speaking breath is quick and small, a sip rather than a gulp. Taking one at every phrase break is covered in how to breathe while speaking.

Common mistakes

MistakeInstead
Pushing the belly out without breathing inLet the breath move the belly, not your muscles
Gulping a huge breath before every sentenceSmall, frequent top-ups
Lifting the shoulders to "get more air"Keep shoulders down and loose
Practising only lying downDo step 5 standing, the way you'll speak

Stop if you feel dizzy, and breathe normally. If you have asthma, another breathing condition or ongoing voice problems, ask a doctor or a qualified voice professional before starting breathing exercises.

Questions people ask

What does diaphragmatic breathing mean?

Breathing low, so the diaphragm moves down and your belly and lower ribs expand as you breathe in. It is also called belly breathing or abdominal breathing.

How do you breathe from your diaphragm when speaking?

Take a low breath, belly out and shoulders still, before your first word, then small top-ups at full stops and natural pauses. Practise it slowly first, then while reading aloud.

Does diaphragmatic breathing help with nerves before a speech?

Many people find a few slow, low breaths before they speak help them feel steadier. It is a general practice tip, not a treatment; if fear of speaking is severe, talk to a professional. There is more in how to overcome fear of public speaking.

Should I breathe through my nose or mouth when speaking?

At natural pauses, a quick breath through the mouth is normal. The nose is easier for slow practice breaths and for settling before you start.
