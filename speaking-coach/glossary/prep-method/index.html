What is the PREP method? Definition and example answer

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

The PREP method is a four-part way to give a clear answer: Point (your answer), Reason (why), Example (proof) and Point again (restate it). It suits short answers in meetings, interviews and impromptu speaking, and a full PREP answer usually takes 30 to 60 seconds.

The four parts

PartWhat you sayLength
PointYour answer, in one sentenceAbout 5 seconds
ReasonWhy you think soOne or two sentences
ExampleA specific case, number or story that proves itTwo or three sentences
PointThe answer again, often with a next stepOne sentence

The repeat at the end isn't wasted. Listeners who drifted during the example get the answer a second time, and it tells everyone you've finished.

A compact example

Example: in a meeting, your manager asks, "Should we keep the Friday stand-up?" Made-up details.

Point: "I'd drop the Friday stand-up."

Reason: "By Friday most of the week's work is done, so it repeats what we already said on Thursday."

Example: "I checked the last four Fridays' notes, and three of them had no new blockers at all."

Point: "So let's drop Friday and use that 15 minutes for the weekly demo instead."

About 70 words, roughly 30 seconds.

When to use it

  • Opinion questions in meetings: "What do you think?"
  • Interview questions that aren't stories: "What makes a good manager?"
  • Impromptu speeches and table topics
  • Any time you're tempted to think out loud

For behavioral "tell me about a time" questions, a story structure such as CAR or STAR fits better. For more practice ideas, see the PREP method guide.

Questions people ask

What does PREP stand for?

Point, Reason, Example, Point.

Can I give more than one reason?

Yes, but two is usually the maximum for a short answer. Give one example per reason.

What if I don't have an example ready?

Use a quick hypothetical ("Say a customer calls on Friday…") or a general observation, then return to your point.

Is PREP good for interviews?

Yes, for opinion and "how would you" questions. Use a story structure for questions about past experience.
