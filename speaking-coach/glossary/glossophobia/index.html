What is glossophobia? Definition of fear of public speaking

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Glossophobia is the term for fear of public speaking. It covers anything from strong nerves before a presentation to anxiety that makes someone avoid speaking in front of others altogether. This page is general information, not a diagnosis: if the fear is severe or affects your work or daily life, a doctor or mental health professional can help.

Where the word comes from

It comes from two Greek words: glōssa, tongue, and phobos, fear. It's said GLOSS-oh-FOH-bee-uh. In everyday speech, most people just say "fear of public speaking" or "stage fright".

Common signs

WhenSigns people often describe
Days beforeDread, putting off preparation, trying to swap out of the talk
Just beforeRacing heart, a dry mouth, needing the toilet, cold hands
DuringA shaky voice, talking too fast, the mind going blank, avoiding eye contact
AfterReplaying every mistake, feeling sure it went worse than it did

Many of these are the body's normal stress response. On their own they don't mean anything is wrong.

Nerves, stage fright and glossophobia

TermUsually means
NervesNormal tension before speaking that often fades once you start
Stage frightFear in the moment of performing: speaking, acting, playing music
GlossophobiaFear of public speaking specifically, often used when it's strong or leads to avoidance

The lines between them are loose; people use them interchangeably.

Questions people ask

Is glossophobia a mental disorder?

It's an informal name, not a diagnosis. When the fear is severe, it can be part of an anxiety problem that a professional can assess.

Is glossophobia common?

Yes, some nervousness about speaking in public is very common. Feeling it doesn't mean you can't become a good speaker.

Can glossophobia be treated?

Professionals offer help for severe cases. For everyday nerves, regular low-stakes practice is what many people find helps most.

What's the difference between glossophobia and shyness?

Shyness is general unease around people. Glossophobia is tied to speaking in front of a group, and confident, outgoing people can have it too.
