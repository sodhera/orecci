# Glossophobia (Fear of Public Speaking): Meaning and Examples

Glossophobia is the fear of public speaking: strong nerves, dread or avoidance when you have to speak in front of an audience. It can show up as a racing heart and a shaky voice before a talk, or as turning down chances to speak at all. It is an everyday name, not a diagnosis; if the fear is severe or gets in the way of work or study, a doctor or mental health professional can help.

## Meaning and where the word comes from

The word joins *glosso-*, from the Ancient Greek *glôssa* ("tongue" or "language"), and *-phobia*, from the Greek *phóbos* ("fear"). It's usually said GLOSS-oh-FOH-bee-uh. In everyday speech, most people just say "fear of public speaking" or "stage fright".

It covers a wide range: from butterflies before a wedding toast to dreading every meeting where you might be asked to speak.

Example sentences:

- "I have mild glossophobia: I'm fine in meetings, but a conference talk terrifies me."
- "Glossophobia is why I always volunteer to make the slides instead of presenting them."

## Common signs

| When | Signs people often describe |
| --- | --- |
| Days before | Dread, putting off preparation, trying to swap out of the talk |
| Just before | Racing heart, a dry mouth, needing the toilet, cold hands |
| During | A [shaky voice](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-stop-voice-shaking/), talking too fast, the [mind going blank](/speaking-coach/guides/mind-goes-blank-while-speaking/), avoiding eye contact |
| After | Replaying every mistake, feeling sure it went worse than it did |

Many of these are the body's normal stress response. On their own they don't mean anything is wrong.

## Examples of glossophobia in everyday life

Made-up examples of how it can look:

- **At school:** a student who knows the material well asks to hand in a written report instead of presenting it.
- **At work:** an analyst stays silent in meetings, because speaking up feels like being on stage.
- **In an interview:** a candidate who rehearsed an answer perfectly at home blanks when the interviewer asks it.
- **At a family event:** someone turns down giving a toast at their sister's wedding, then regrets it.
- **In a weekly update:** a manager's voice shakes for the first minute of every update, then settles.

## Nerves, stage fright, glossophobia and shyness

| Term | Usually means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nerves | Normal tension before speaking that often fades once you start | Butterflies before a team update, gone by the second slide |
| [Stage fright](/speaking-coach/glossary/stage-fright/) | Fear in the moment of performing: speaking, acting, music, sport | Freezing in the wings before a school play |
| Glossophobia | Fear of public speaking specifically, often used when it's strong or leads to avoidance | Taking a lower grade rather than present |
| Shyness | General unease around people, not tied to speaking to a group | Quiet at parties, yet fine giving a prepared talk |

The lines between them are loose, and people use the first three interchangeably. A wider fear of being judged in many social situations, not only when speaking, is something a doctor or mental health professional can assess.

## How to handle it: first steps

These are everyday practice steps, not treatment.

1. **Start smaller than feels necessary.** Say your talk to one person, then a few, then a small group.
2. **Prepare your first 30 seconds word for word,** so the start runs on its own while your nerves settle.
3. **Rehearse out loud,** not in your head. Saying it is a different skill from reading it.
4. **In the last minutes before you speak,** breathe out slowly and say your first line quietly to yourself.
5. **If your mind goes blank,** pause, look at your notes and repeat your last point. A short pause looks calmer than it feels.
6. **If the fear is intense, lasts a long time or makes you turn down work or study,** talk to a doctor or mental health professional.

## Questions people ask

### What is glossophobia in simple words?

It's the fear of speaking in front of a group, from mild nerves to avoiding speaking altogether.

### How do you pronounce glossophobia?

It's usually said GLOSS-oh-FOH-bee-uh, with the stress on "FOH".

### Is glossophobia a mental disorder?

It's an informal name, not a diagnosis. When the fear is severe, it can be part of an anxiety problem that a professional can assess.

### Is glossophobia common?

Yes, some nervousness about speaking in public is very common. Feeling it doesn't mean you can't become a good speaker.

### Can you get over glossophobia?

Many people find everyday nerves ease with regular, gradual practice in front of small, friendly audiences. For severe fear, a professional can advise on the help available.

### What's the difference between glossophobia and stage fright?

Stage fright covers any performance, from music to sport. Glossophobia is the fear of public speaking specifically.
