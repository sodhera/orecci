# What is the STARR method? Definition and example answer

The STARR method is STAR with a fifth step, Reflection: Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection. After the result, you add one or two sentences on what you learned or would do differently, which shows the interviewer that you grow from experience.

## The five parts

| Part | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Situation** | Where were you, and what was going on? |
| **Task** | What did you have to do? |
| **Action** | What did you do, step by step? |
| **Result** | What happened, ideally with a number? |
| **Reflection** | What did you learn, or what would you change? |

The first four are the standard [STAR method](/speaking-coach/glossary/star-method/). Some people call the last step "Reflect" or "Learning"; it's the same idea.

## What the extra R adds

Interviewers often ask "What would you do differently?" as a follow-up anyway. STARR answers it before they ask. It is most useful for:

- Questions about failure, mistakes or conflict, where the lesson is the point
- Roles that value self-awareness, such as management, teaching and healthcare
- Graduate and competency-based interviews that score "learning" or "development"

## Example closing lines

Example: the end of an answer about a missed handover, with made-up details.

> **Result:** "We caught the error before the patient was affected, and I wrote it up the same day."

> **Reflection:** "What I took from it is that I now read back the medication list at every handover, even when we're rushed. It adds a minute and it's caught two errors since."

Good reflections are specific and show a changed habit.

| Weak reflection | Stronger reflection |
| --- | --- |
| "I learned communication is important." | "I now send a two-line summary after every client call." |
| "I'd do everything the same." | "I'd involve finance a week earlier." |
| "It taught me a lot." | "I learned to ask for the deadline in writing." |

## Questions people ask

### What does STARR stand for?

Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection.

### Should every answer use STARR?

No. Use the reflection when the question invites it, such as failures, conflicts and mistakes. For straightforward success stories, STAR is often enough.

### How long should the reflection be?

One or two sentences, about 10 seconds.

### Can the reflection be positive?

Yes. "What I'd keep doing" is a valid reflection, as long as it's specific.
