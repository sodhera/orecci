What is intonation in speaking? Definition and examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Intonation is the way your pitch rises and falls across a sentence. It tells listeners things your words don't: whether you're stating or asking, finished or still going, sure or unsure. In English, a falling pitch at the end usually signals a statement, and a rising pitch often signals a yes/no question.

Statements and questions

How English pitch commonly moves at the end of a sentence:

SentencePitch at the endWhat it signals
"You finished the report."FallsA statement of fact
"You finished the report?"RisesSurprise, or checking
"Did you finish the report?"Usually risesA yes/no question
"When did you finish the report?"Usually fallsA question asking for information
"I wrote the report, the summary and the slides."Rises on each item, falls on the lastA list, and that it's complete

The list pattern is useful when presenting: a fall on the last item tells people you've finished, so they don't wait for more.

Intonation and meaning

Pitch also marks which word matters. Say each line, stressing the bold word with a small jump in pitch:

  • "I didn't say we'd cancel it." (Someone else said it.)
  • "I didn't say we'd cancel it." (We might postpone it.)
  • "I didn't say we'd cancel it." (We might cancel something else.)

Same words, three meanings.

Patterns that can confuse listeners

  • Rising at the end of statements. It can make a firm statement sound like a question. That's called uptalk.
  • Very little movement at all. Every sentence sounds equally important: a monotone delivery.
  • Trailing off. The pitch and volume drop so far that the last words get lost.

Intonation patterns differ between languages and between English accents, and none of them is "wrong". What matters is whether listeners can follow what you mean. Ways to add variety are in how to stop sounding monotone.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between intonation and stress?

Stress is the extra weight on a syllable or word. Intonation is the overall rise and fall of pitch across the sentence. They work together.

Why does intonation matter?

It tells listeners whether you're asking or telling, finished or continuing, and which word matters.

Is intonation the same in every language?

No. Languages use pitch differently, which is why intonation can carry over when you speak a second language.

How can I hear my own intonation?

Record yourself saying a few statements and questions, then listen for whether your pitch rises or falls at the end.
