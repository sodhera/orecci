Rapport: Meaning, How to Build Rapport and Examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

Rapport is a relationship of ease and mutual understanding with someone, in which you both feel comfortable and communicate well. When you speak, it makes people more willing to listen, trust you and say what they really think. You build it through attention and small, genuine signals: remembering what they said, matching their energy and finding real common ground.

Meaning

The word came into English from French and is said "ra-POR": the final t is silent. You have rapport with someone when talking feels easy; building rapport (or establishing it) is what you do to get there.

Example sentences:

  • "She has great rapport with her students."
  • "Spend the first two minutes building rapport before you get to the agenda."
  • "We had no rapport at all. Every answer felt like a test."

Signs you have rapport

  • The conversation flows, and you both talk roughly as much as each other.
  • People tell you more than you asked.
  • Short silences feel comfortable, not awkward.
  • Small jokes land, and they pick up your words or phrases.
  • Disagreeing doesn't end the conversation.

Behaviours that build rapport, with example lines

BehaviourExample
Remembering details"How did your daughter's recital go?" a week after they mentioned it
Matching pace and energySlowing down and lowering your voice with a calm, careful caller
Real common ground"You studied in Leeds too? Which part of town did you live in?"
Showing you listened"So the move was the hard part, not the job itself?"
A little self-disclosure"I'm terrible at that too. I still write my lists on paper."
Using their name, once or twice"Thanks, Maria, that's helpful."
A warm open and close"Thanks for making time. I know Thursdays are busy for you."

Active listening underlies most of these.

Rapport in different settings

Example lines to adapt:

SettingWhat builds itExample line
Job interviewEasy chat on the walk in; referring back to what they said"You mentioned the team doubled this year. That's part of why I applied."
Sales or client callAsking about their situation before your product"Before I show you anything, what's prompting the change now?"
PresentationNaming a problem the room shares"Most of us have sat through a forty-slide update. This one is six."
New teamAsking for their knowledge"What do you wish someone had told you in your first week?"
Video callA personal follow-up before business"Good to see you again. Did the move go okay?"

In an interview you have only a few minutes, so rapport comes from small things. It doesn't replace good answers, but it makes the interviewer more relaxed with you and more curious. See small talk before an interview for what to say on the walk in.

TermWhat it isHow it relates to rapport
RapportEase and mutual understanding between peopleThe result

TrustBelief that someone is reliable and honestRapport makes it easier; trust takes longer

What breaks rapport

  • Interrupting, or finishing their sentences for them.
  • Flattery that sounds scripted: "What a fascinating company!" with nothing specific behind it.
  • Copying too obviously. Some mirroring is natural; mimicking every gesture feels odd.
  • Talking only about yourself, or answering every story with a bigger one.
  • Checking your phone while they talk.

How to build rapport in a conversation

  1. Give full attention for the first minute: phone away, face them.
  2. Open with something you share right now: the place, the event, the meeting.
  3. Ask one question about something they just said.
  4. Share a small, real detail about yourself in return.
  5. Roughly match their pace and energy. Don't copy them.
  6. Remember one detail and use it at the end: "Good luck with the move."

Questions people ask

What is rapport in simple words?

Rapport is when two people get on easily and understand each other, so talking feels comfortable.

What does "build rapport" mean?

It means creating that ease on purpose, through attention, listening and small genuine signals, rather than waiting for it to happen.

How do you build rapport quickly?

Give full attention, ask one question about something they just said, and share a small, genuine detail about yourself.

What is rapport in an interview?

A relaxed, two-way feel between you and the interviewer. It comes from a warm greeting, listening closely and referring back to what they told you.

Can you build rapport on a phone or video call?

Yes. Without visual cues, your voice does more of the work: a warm tone, short acknowledgements and using their name.

Is rapport the same as being liked?

Not quite. Rapport is ease and trust in the conversation. You can have it with someone who disagrees with you.
