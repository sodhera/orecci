# Articulation in Speech: Meaning, Exercises and Examples

Articulation is how clearly you form the sounds of words with your lips, tongue, teeth and jaw, especially consonants and word endings. Clear articulation keeps "next Tuesday" as two distinct words; slurred articulation runs them into "nexTuesday". When you speak to a group or on a call, it decides whether people catch your names, numbers and key words the first time, and it is about clarity, not accent.

## Meaning

Every consonant is made by a movement: lips closing for "p" and "b", the tongue tapping behind the teeth for "t" and "d", the back of the tongue lifting for "k" and "g". Articulation is how completely you make those movements. When they're quick and light but complete, words separate cleanly. When they're skipped, sounds merge and endings vanish.

The word has neighbours that mean something slightly different:

- **Articulate** (adjective): able to express ideas clearly in words. Clear articulation is one part of sounding articulate; choosing the right words is the other.
- **To articulate** an idea: to put it into words, as in "She articulated the plan in two sentences."
- **In speech therapy**, articulation means how a person produces speech sounds. If a child or adult has ongoing trouble making certain sounds, a speech and language therapist is the person to ask.

## Examples: clear vs slurred speech

| What you mean | Slurred | Clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did you eat yet? | "Jeet yet?" | "Did you eat yet?" |
| I asked the client | "I ass the client" | "I askt the client" |
| Fifteen products | "Fif'y produc's" | "Fif-teen pro-ducts" |
| Next Tuesday at two | "Nexchoosday a'two" | "Next Tuesday, at two" |
| Thank you all for coming | "Kyall f'comin'" | "Thank you all for coming" |
| Probably twenty | "Prob'ly twenny" | "Probably twenty" |
| The project budget | "The projeh budge" | "The project budget" |

In casual chat some blending is normal and nobody minds. It matters most with names, numbers, technical terms, phone and video calls, large rooms, and listeners who speak English as a second language. "Fifteen" heard as "fifty" in a price or a deadline is the classic slip.

## Articulation vs. pronunciation vs. diction

| Term | Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Articulation | How clearly you form each sound | Sounding the "t" in "next" |
| Enunciation | Saying words distinctly; in everyday use, nearly the same as articulation | "Did you eat yet?", not "Jeet yet?" |
| Pronunciation | Which sounds a word uses and where the stress goes | "REcord" the noun vs "reCORD" the verb |
| [Diction](/speaking-coach/glossary/diction/) | Word choice, or clear speech in general | "Use" rather than "utilise" |
| Accent | The sound pattern of where you learned the language | Not a clarity problem in itself |

You can be fully clear with any accent; see [how to be understood clearly with an accent](/speaking-coach/guides/speaking-clearly-with-an-accent/).

## Why it slips

- Speaking fast, so movements get cut short
- Nerves, which tighten the jaw
- Dropping volume at the end of sentences
- Looking down at notes, which muffles sound

## 5 articulation exercises

1. **Pa-ta-ka.** Say "pa-ta-ka" ten times slowly, then faster, keeping each sound crisp. It works the lips, tongue tip and back of the tongue in turn.
2. **Open vowels.** Say "ma, may, mee, my, mo, moo", opening your jaw a little more than feels natural on each one.
3. **Final consonants.** Read one paragraph aloud and exaggerate the last sound of every word: "las**t**", "projec**t**", "aske**d**".
4. **Numbers over a bad line.** Read a phone number, a date and three prices as if the listener is on a weak connection.
5. **Back to normal.** Read the same paragraph again at your usual pace. The crispness tends to stay.

Slowing down helps all five; see [how to slow down when speaking](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-slow-down-when-speaking/). For more drills and tongue twisters, see [how to speak more clearly](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-speak-clearly/).

## Questions people ask

### What is articulation in speech?

How clearly you form the sounds of words, especially consonants and word endings, so listeners can tell one word from the next.

### What is the difference between articulation and pronunciation?

Pronunciation is which sounds a word uses and where the stress goes. Articulation is how clearly you produce those sounds.

### Is articulation the same as enunciation?

In everyday use, nearly. Both mean speaking words distinctly.

### What does it mean to be articulate?

To express your ideas clearly and fluently in words. Clear articulation helps, but being articulate is mostly about saying the right thing simply.

### Does mumbling come from poor articulation?

Often, combined with low volume and a barely moving jaw.

### Can I improve articulation as an adult?

Yes. Short daily read-aloud practice at a slower pace usually helps quickly. If you have an ongoing speech difficulty, a speech and language therapist can assess it.
