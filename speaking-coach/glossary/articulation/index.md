# What is articulation in speaking? Definition and examples

Articulation is how clearly you shape the sounds of words with your lips, tongue and jaw, especially consonants and word endings. Clear articulation makes "next Tuesday" two distinct words; slurred articulation runs them into "nexTuesday". It is about clarity, not accent.

## Definition

Every consonant is made by a movement: lips closing for "p" and "b", the tongue tapping behind the teeth for "t" and "d", the back of the tongue lifting for "k" and "g". Articulation is how completely you make those movements. When they're quick and light but complete, words separate cleanly. When they're skipped, sounds merge and endings vanish.

## Clear vs slurred examples

| What you mean | Slurred | Clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did you eat yet? | "Jeet yet?" | "Did you eat yet?" |
| I asked the client | "I ass the client" | "I askt the client" |
| Fifteen products | "Fif'y produc's" | "Fif-teen pro-ducts" |
| Next Tuesday at two | "Nexchoosday a'two" | "Next Tuesday, at two" |
| Thank you all for coming | "Kyall f'comin'" | "Thank you all for coming" |

In casual chat some blending is normal and nobody minds. It matters most with names, numbers, technical terms, phone and video calls, large rooms, and listeners who speak English as a second language.

## Why it slips

- Speaking fast, so movements get cut short
- Nerves, which tighten the jaw
- Dropping volume at the end of sentences
- Looking down at notes, which muffles sound

## A 2-minute warm-up

1. Say "pa-ta-ka" ten times, slowly, then faster, keeping each sound crisp.
2. Read one paragraph aloud, exaggerating every final consonant.
3. Read it again at normal pace. The crispness tends to stay.

Articulation is separate from accent. You can be fully clear with any accent; see [how to be understood clearly with an accent](/speaking-coach/guides/speaking-clearly-with-an-accent/) and [how to speak more clearly](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-speak-clearly/).

## Questions people ask

### What is the difference between articulation and pronunciation?

Pronunciation is which sounds a word uses and where the stress goes. Articulation is how clearly you produce those sounds.

### Is articulation the same as enunciation?

In everyday use, nearly. Both mean speaking words distinctly.

### Does mumbling come from poor articulation?

Often, combined with low volume and a barely moving jaw.

### Can I improve articulation as an adult?

Yes. Short daily read-aloud practice at a slower pace usually helps quickly. If you have an ongoing speech difficulty, a speech-language pathologist can assess it.
