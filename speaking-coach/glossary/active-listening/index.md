# What is active listening? Definition and example responses

Active listening means giving the speaker your full attention and showing that you understood them: you listen without planning your reply, check what you heard, and respond to what they actually said. In conversation it sounds like short reflections, specific follow-up questions and summaries, not just nodding.

## The parts of active listening

| Part | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| **Attention** | You let them finish, and you leave a second of silence before replying. |
| **Acknowledging** | "Right." "That makes sense." "Oh, really?" |
| **Reflecting** | "So the deadline moved and nobody told you." (See [paraphrasing](/speaking-coach/glossary/paraphrasing/).) |
| **Asking** | A [follow-up question](/speaking-coach/glossary/follow-up-question/) about something they just said. |
| **Summarising** | "So two things are worrying you: the budget and the new hire." |

## Example responses

A coworker says: *"I've been on this report all week, and my manager just changed the brief."*

| Response | Active listening? |
| --- | --- |
| "That happened to me last month too. My manager…" | No: it turns the topic to you. |
| "Just ask for an extension." | No: advice before understanding. |
| "A whole week. So the work you've done might not fit the new brief?" | Yes: reflects the frustration and the facts. |
| "What changed in the brief?" | Yes: a specific follow-up. |
| "That sounds frustrating. Do you want ideas, or did you just need to say it?" | Yes: names the feeling and asks what they need. |

## What it isn't

- **Agreeing.** You can show you understood and still disagree afterwards.
- **Waiting for your turn.** If you're rehearsing your reply, you've stopped listening.
- **Repeating word for word.** That sounds like a script. Put it in your own words.

In interviews, active listening shows when you answer the question that was asked, and when your own questions at the end pick up on something the interviewer said earlier.

## Questions people ask

### What are three examples of active listening?

Reflecting ("So you're saying…"), asking a specific follow-up question, and summarising the main points before you respond.

### Is active listening the same as agreeing?

No. It means showing you understood. You can then add a different view.

### How do I show active listening on a video call?

Use short spoken acknowledgements, since nods are easy to miss on camera, and refer back to what people said by name: "Building on Sam's point…"

### Why do interviewers care about active listening?

Most jobs involve customers, colleagues or patients. Answering exactly what was asked is the first evidence that you listen.
