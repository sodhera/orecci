What does BLUF mean? Bottom line up front, definition and example

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

BLUF stands for "bottom line up front": put your main point or request in the first sentence, then give the details. It comes from US Army writing guidance and is now common in business emails, briefings and spoken updates.

Meaning

The bottom line is whatever the listener most needs: the decision, the answer, the request or the headline. BLUF means it comes first, and background comes after, only as much as they need. The opposite is the chronological story, which saves the point for the end.

Origin

The phrase comes from US Army correspondence guidance, Army Regulation 25-50, which asks for writing that is concise and puts the main point at the beginning. From military briefings it spread to business writing, and it works even better spoken: a listener can't skip ahead to find your point.

Example spoken update

Example: a weekly update on a website launch, with made-up details.

Before (story order):

"So on Monday we started testing, and Ana found a problem with the payment page on older phones, and we looked into it with the vendor, and it turns out it's their library, so they're patching it, which should be Thursday, so we'll probably need to move the launch."

After (BLUF):

"Bottom line: launch moves from the 3rd to the 10th, and I need your OK to tell the client today. The reason is a payment bug on older phones; the vendor will patch it Thursday. Everything else is on track."

The second version is shorter, the decision is clear, and the manager can ask about the details if they want them.

A simple BLUF template

  1. Bottom line: the answer, decision or request in one sentence.
  2. Why: one or two sentences of reason.
  3. What's next or what I need: a date, an owner or an approval.

BLUF is close to the pyramid principle, which adds a structure for the supporting points. For meetings, see how to give a clear team update.

Questions people ask

What does BLUF stand for?

Bottom line up front.

Can I say "BLUF" in an email subject?

Some teams write "BLUF:" as the first word of an email. If your readers don't know the term, just put the point first without the label.

Isn't BLUF rude?

Not if the tone is warm. Getting to the point respects people's time. Add one friendly line if the relationship needs it.

When shouldn't I use BLUF?

When the listener needs context before the news can land, such as very bad personal news. Even then, don't delay long.
