Pyramid Principle (Minto): Meaning, Structure and Examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

The pyramid principle is a way of structuring a message answer-first: you state your main point, group the supporting reasons beneath it, and give the details under each reason. Barbara Minto set it out for business writing, and it works just as well for spoken updates, presentations and interview answers, because the listener hears your conclusion first and can stop you at any level.

Meaning

It is often called the Minto pyramid principle, after Barbara Minto. Her book Pyramid Principle dates from 1987, and the later edition is The Minto Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing, Thinking and Problem Solving. Her own site describes the idea as presenting your ideas as a pyramid under a single point.

In speech it means: conclusion first, then reasons, then evidence. Most people do the reverse and tell the story in the order it happened, leaving the listener asking where it's going.

The structure

Picture a pyramid with your answer at the top:

  • Top: the one main point or recommendation.
  • Middle: two to four reasons that support it, grouped so they don't overlap.
  • Bottom: the facts, numbers and examples under each reason.

Each level sums up the one below it. You say it top-down, so a listener who stops you after one sentence already has the answer.

The SCQ opening

Minto's method also uses a Situation, Complication, Question (SCQ) opening to find the question your listener actually has.

PartWhat it doesExample
SituationA fact everyone agrees on"We open six days a week."
ComplicationWhat has changed or gone wrong"Sunday is now our busiest day for online orders, and customers keep asking to pick up in store."
QuestionThe question that raises"Should we open on Sundays?"
AnswerThe top of your pyramid"Yes, from 10 to 4, starting next month."

Example spoken update

Example with made-up details. A manager asks, "Should we open on Sundays?"

Answer: "Yes, I recommend we open on Sundays from 10 to 4, starting next month."

Reason 1: "Demand is there. Our online orders on Sundays are the highest of the week, and customers ask for in-store pickup."

Reason 2: "It pays for itself. Two staff for six hours costs less than the pickup sales we'd expect from current orders."

Reason 3: "Staff are willing. Four people have already asked for Sunday shifts."

Close: "If you agree, I'll draft the rota this week."

Before and after: story order vs. pyramid

SituationBefore (story order)After (pyramid)

Interview answer"I started in retail, then I moved to the office, and over time…""My biggest strength is calming upset customers. Two examples show it…"

Pyramid principle vs. BLUF vs. PREP

MethodWhat it gives youBest for
Pyramid principleAnswer first, then grouped reasons, then detailsRecommendations, reports, board updates
BLUFOne rule: the main point goes firstEmails and quick updates
PREPPoint, Reason, Example, PointSpoken answers of 30 to 45 seconds

All three put the answer first. The pyramid adds a way to group and order everything below it, which matters most in board and executive presentations, where time is short and questions come early.

Common mistakes

  • Opening with "Some context first" and burying the answer.
  • Reasons that overlap, such as "it's cheaper" and "it saves money".
  • Details that support none of your reasons.
  • Ending without the ask: what you need from the listener.

How to practise it

  1. Write the question your listener has, in one line.
  2. Answer it in one sentence you can say in one breath.
  3. List your reasons, merge any that overlap, and keep the strongest two to four.
  4. Add one fact or example under each.
  5. Say it out loud, top-down, in under a minute. Ask someone to stop you after the first sentence: do they already know your answer?

If you wander before reaching the point, see how to stop rambling.

Questions people ask

What is the pyramid principle in simple terms?

Say your main point first, then the reasons for it, then the evidence. The listener gets the answer before the detail.

Who created the pyramid principle?

Barbara Minto, who set it out in her books Pyramid Principle and The Minto Pyramid Principle, as a method for business writing.

What is an example of the pyramid principle?

"Yes, we should open on Sundays. Demand is there, it pays for itself and staff are willing." Then a fact under each reason.

What is SCQ in the pyramid principle?

Situation, Complication, Question: a short opening that sets out what everyone knows, what has changed and the question that follows, before you answer it.

How many reasons should I give?

Usually two to four. More than that is hard to follow out loud.

Is it the same as BLUF?

Close. Both put the answer first. The pyramid principle also tells you how to group and order what follows.
