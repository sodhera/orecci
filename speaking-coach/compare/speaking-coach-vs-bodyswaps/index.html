Who we are, and what we checked

We make Speaking Coach. We read Bodyswaps' home page, pricing pages and App Store page in October 2026, and our facts about it come only from those pages.

The main difference is who buys it. Bodyswaps is sold to organizations: universities, colleges, career-readiness programs, hospitals and companies. They choose the scenarios, set the assessment criteria and roll it out to learners. Speaking Coach is bought by one person for themselves.

Your school already provides Bodyswaps: what now?

Use it first. It's free to you, your educators may have built scenarios for your course, and some programs track completion. Then fill the gaps.

  1. Do the roleplays your course assigns. If your educators set assessment criteria, that's what you're being measured on.
  2. Note which situations it doesn't cover for you. Typical gaps: an interview for one specific job, a language your course doesn't offer, or everyday conversations outside the curriculum.
  3. Practice those on your own. This is where a personal app helps: paste the actual job post, add your CV, and the interviewer asks about your placement or project. Our campus placement interview guide lists what to expect.
  4. Keep your access in mind. Organization licenses often end when you leave the course or job. A personal app goes with you.

Where Bodyswaps is stronger

  • Immersion. VR simulations put you in a room with a virtual person, which some learners find closer to the real thing than a phone.
  • Educator control. Instructors can build roleplays for a specific module, such as a clinical conversation for nursing students, and assess everyone the same way.
  • Accessibility and inclusion. Its site lists WCAG 2.2 AA alignment and difficulty levels for SEND and ESOL learners.
  • Healthcare and sector scenarios. Its examples include breaking bad news to a patient and handling a guest complaint.

Where Speaking Coach is stronger

  • You can get it today, on your own. As of October 2026, Bodyswaps' individual offering wasn't open yet.
  • It practices your specific event. Your CV, your job post, your slides.
  • It covers life outside the course. Small talk at a career fair, meeting someone new, a first date, saying no to a flatmate, asking for a raise.
  • More languages. 20, including Hindi, Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Turkish, Indonesian, Filipino and Vietnamese.
  • Daily habit. A 30-second daily prompt and a streak, built for a few minutes a day.

What it doesn't have: VR, educator dashboards, or assessment that your school can see.

Questions people ask

Can I buy Bodyswaps as an individual?

Not yet, as of October 2026. Its individual learner page says it is "currently working on our B2C offering" and invites you to join a beta waitlist.

Do I need a VR headset for Bodyswaps?

No. Its site says students can practice on desktop, browser, mobile or VR, and the browser option needs no headset or install.

My university uses Bodyswaps. Do I need Speaking Coach too?

Not necessarily. If the roleplays cover your upcoming interview or presentation, use them. Add a personal app when you need practice for a specific job, another language, or everyday conversations.

Which languages does Bodyswaps support?

Its site lists English (US and UK), French, Spanish, German, Portuguese, Polish and Welsh. Speaking Coach works in 20 languages.

Is Speaking Coach available for schools?

Speaking Coach is a personal subscription on the App Store and Google Play. It doesn't include tools for educators to assign or assess practice.
