Final Round AI Alternatives: What to Use to Practice Interviews?

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

If you want to get better at interviews rather than get answers fed to you during one, pick a practice tool: Huru or Aced's free peer mocks cost nothing, Speaking Coach and Yoodli give you an AI partner who reacts to what you say, and interviewing.io or Aced coaching give you a human interviewer. As of October 2026, Final Round AI's own paid plan centers on a live copilot, so check finalroundai.com for current prices before you renew.

Why people look for something else

We make Speaking Coach, one of the tools on this page, so read our picks with that in mind. We checked every competitor's own site in October 2026 and only state what we saw there.

Final Round AI is built mainly around a live interview copilot. As of October 2026, its home page says the desktop app listens to your call and "delivers real-time answers in a private window only you can see", with a Stealth Mode that keeps its windows out of screen shares and recordings. It also has practice interviews in eight scenario types, but the paid Pro plan (listed from $25 a month, with no free trial) is what unlocks live sessions. Its terms say subscriptions renew automatically until you cancel, and its refund policy offers a 72-hour money-back window with conditions.

People usually leave for one of three reasons:

  • They want to improve, not be prompted. Reading a suggested answer in an interview doesn't make you better at the next one.
  • Cost and renewal. A monthly plan adds up across a long job search.
  • Risk. An employer that found out could treat hidden live help as cheating. We cover that on the Speaking Coach vs Final Round AI page.

Free options

Huru. As of October 2026, Huru's site says job seekers get every feature free, with no subscription. You can run a live interview with an AI interviewer that adapts to your answers, pick Supportive, Standard or Demanding pressure, and set a length from 5 to 20 minutes. Each answer gets a score and a rewritten version. It works in a web browser and an iOS app; the site points Android users to the web app. For a free AI mock, it is hard to beat.

Aced peer mock interviews. Aced (the new name of Exponent) matches you with another candidate for about an hour. You switch roles halfway, so you also learn what interviewers notice. Types include product management, coding, system design, behavioral and finance. Free users get monthly credits; transcripts and AI feedback need a paid membership.

Practice with a friend. Give them five questions and one rule: after every answer, ask "Why?" or "What would you do differently?" That single follow-up is most of the value of a mock interview.

AI mock interviewers that react to you

These are the closest replacements for Final Round AI's practice mode, without the copilot.

  • Speaking Coach (ours). On iPhone and Android, you add your CV, the job post or notes, and an AI voice interviewer asks about your real experience and follows up on what you actually said. You choose Gentle, Realistic or Tough pressure. Afterwards you see your pace in words per minute, filler words, long pauses, and tips that quote your words. It works in 20 languages. It is a subscription; if you just want to test one answer, the free Speaking Coach app in ChatGPT reviews a recording in English, two reviews a day.
  • Yoodli. Strong if you also practice presentations and sales conversations. As of October 2026 its individual plans run on the web, starting with five free lifetime sessions.
  • Big Interview. Better if you want structured lessons first: it pairs video lessons with practice questions and AI feedback, and sells one-time access rather than open-ended monthly billing.

Human mock interviews

An AI partner is good for repetition. A human who has hired for your role is better for judgment: whether your story is senior enough, whether your design would pass. For software roles, interviewing.io offers anonymous mocks with engineers, and its FAQ says interviews start at $179. Aced sells 60-minute coaching sessions; its page says most people book three to five. A cheaper middle ground is one human mock near the end, after you have used free or AI practice to fix the basics.

How to choose in two minutes

  1. No budget: Huru for AI mocks, Aced peer mocks for human practice.
  2. Interview in a week, want to hear yourself improve: an AI partner that follows up, used daily for 10 to 15 minutes.
  3. Senior or technical role: add one paid human mock in your last week.
  4. Not in English: check language support first. Speaking Coach works in 20 languages; Huru lists five.

Whichever you pick, practice out loud. Start with the questions that sink most people, like tell me about yourself and the STAR method for behavioral rounds.

Questions people ask

Is there a free alternative to Final Round AI?

Yes. As of October 2026, Huru says its AI interview practice is free for job seekers, and Aced gives free monthly credits for peer mock interviews. Final Round AI's own free plan covers goal setup and job post parsing, not live sessions.

Does Final Round AI have a free trial?

Its pricing page said "There is no free trial" for live sessions as of October 2026. Check finalroundai.com for the current offer.

How do I avoid being charged again by Final Round AI?

Its terms say the subscription renews automatically and must be cancelled before it renews to avoid the next charge. Its refund policy describes a 72-hour window with conditions, so act quickly if you were charged.

Is it okay to use an AI copilot during a real interview?

That depends on the employer's rules, and many interviews ask you not to use outside help. If an employer finds hidden help, it can cost you the offer. Practicing beforehand carries no such risk, and what you learn stays with you.

Which alternative is best for coding interviews?

For coding and system design, use human or peer mocks: Aced peer mocks for free, interviewing.io for paid sessions with experienced engineers. Use an AI speaking partner for the behavioral round.
