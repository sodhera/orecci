Toastmasters or a Speaking App: Which Helps You Improve Faster?

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

They improve different things, so combining them is faster than picking one. Toastmasters gives you a real audience, human evaluators and a community, a couple of times a month. A speaking app like Speaking Coach gives you short daily practice in private, measured feedback, and a partner for interviews and conversations. Not the same kind of thing

Toastmasters is a club: people who meet, give speeches, and evaluate each other. A speaking app is a tool you use alone. Asking which is better is a bit like asking whether a running club or a running app makes you faster. The honest answer is: the one you'll actually show up for, and ideally both.

We make Speaking Coach, a speaking app, so we're not neutral. But we think a club does things no app can, and we say so below.

Where Toastmasters wins

  • A real audience. Nothing simulates the feeling of 15 faces looking at you, and that feeling is what you're training for.
  • Human judgment. An evaluator can say "your story about your grandmother was the moment we leaned in." An app can't tell what moved a room.
  • Body and room skills. Where to stand, how to use the space, eye contact with a crowd.
  • Commitment. People expect you on Tuesday. That's the best habit system there is.
  • Leadership practice. Running meetings and taking roles.

Where an app wins

  • Volume. At a club, your speaking time in a month might be one prepared speech and a Table Topic. With an app, 10 minutes a day is over five hours a month.
  • Private practice. You can be bad at it with nobody watching. For very nervous speakers, that's often what makes starting possible.
  • Measured feedback. Words per minute and filler counts from your actual voice, instead of a tally sheet.
  • Instant retries. Speaking Coach's "Retry this moment" gives you 90 seconds on the exact part you fumbled, right away.
  • Other conversations. Interviews, asking for a raise, a toast, a first date. Club speeches don't cover those directly.

How to combine them

  1. Draft your club speech, then rehearse it out loud daily for a week with an app or a recording. Time it; for an Ice Breaker, that's 4 to 6 minutes.
  2. Do a 2-minute impromptu answer each day to train for Table Topics. Our impromptu speaking guide has a simple structure.
  3. Deliver at the club and write down your evaluator's top point.
  4. Practice that one point in the app until the next meeting.

No club near you, or evenings impossible? Toastmasters' club finder lets you filter for clubs that accept online attendance, and our Toastmasters alternatives page covers other options.

Questions people ask

Is Toastmasters worth it?

If you want real audiences, peer evaluation and a community, yes. Its site lists $72 every six months plus any club dues and a one-time $25 fee, as of October 2026, and you can visit as a guest first.

Can an app replace Toastmasters?

No. An app gives you many more private repetitions and measured feedback, but not a real room or a community. Use it alongside a club, not instead.

Is a speaking app good for someone too scared to join a club?

Yes, as a first step. Practicing privately with a partner who reacts can make the first club visit feel less like a leap.

Can I use Speaking Coach to practice Table Topics?

Yes, in the sense that it asks unexpected follow-ups and lets you describe any situation, such as "ask me a surprise question and give me a minute to answer."

Do Toastmasters clubs meet online?

As of October 2026, Toastmasters says clubs meet in person, online or hybrid, and its club finder lets you filter for online attendance.
