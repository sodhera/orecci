What it means and when people ask it

"How is your week going?" is asked while the week is still under way, most often from Tuesday to Thursday. It does two different jobs.

  • At work, it's mostly courtesy. It opens emails, Slack and Teams messages, and the first minute of calls. The person is being friendly before they get to their real question. They expect a line, not a report.
  • From a friend, a relative or someone you're dating, it's a real check-in. They want to know how you are, and they're giving you an opening to talk or to plan the weekend.

The exception at work is a one-to-one with your manager. There the question is often meant literally: how is your workload, and is anything stuck?

Replies at work

Every reply on this page is an example. Put your own details in.

In an email reply: one line, then business

Put your answer straight after the greeting, keep it to one sentence, return the wish, and start a new paragraph for the real subject. Example:

Hi Priya,

Going well, thanks. It's a full week with the audit, but we're on schedule. I hope yours is going smoothly too.

On the invoice: finance confirmed this morning that it was paid on the 12th.

Other one-liners for the top of an email:

  • "All good here, thanks, and I hope the same goes for you."
  • "Busy but productive, thanks for asking."
  • "A hectic one, but I can't complain. I hope yours is calmer."
  • "Good, thank you. The office is quiet this week, which helps."

If the email says "I hope your week is going well", that's a wish, not a question. You can thank them and wish them the same, or go straight to business. Both are polite.

On a call

Out loud, keep it shorter than you would in writing, and hand the question back so you're not the only one talking. There's more on those first minutes in what to say at the start of a video call.

  • "Good, thanks. Busy, but the good kind. How's yours?"
  • "Not bad at all. It's been back-to-back calls, so this one's a nice change. How are things on your side?"
  • "Pretty well. It's finally stopped raining here, which helps. How about you?"

To your manager

In a one-to-one, give a real answer: status first, then the one thing you need.

  • "Good overall. The report's nearly done. I'm stuck on the numbers from sales, so I'd like five minutes on that."
  • "Busy, honestly. I've got three things due on Friday. Can we check which one matters most?"
  • "Smoother than last week. It's quieter, so I've got room if anything needs picking up."

For the lighter moments, see small talk with your boss.

To a client

Stay positive and brief, and steer the conversation towards them. A client doesn't need to hear that you're swamped.

  • "Very well, thanks. We've been looking forward to this one. How's your week been?"
  • "Good, thank you. It's been a full one. How are things at your end after the launch?"
  • "Going well, thanks. I know it's a heavy month for your team, so I'll keep this short."

Casual replies for friends and dates

Here the detail matters more than the mood word. Give them something to ask about.

  • "Long. It's only Wednesday and I've already had two cancelled trains. Yours?"
  • "Pretty good, actually. I started that pottery class last night, and I'm terrible at it."
  • "Fine, nothing special. I'm counting down to Friday. Are you around this weekend?"
  • "Better since Tuesday. Work has calmed down. How's the new job going?"
  • "Good, but slow. I'm looking forward to Saturday. Are we still on for seven?"

Honest replies for a hard week

You don't have to say "good" when it isn't. An honest reply that doesn't overshare has three parts:

  1. Name it. Say that it's been a tough week.
  2. Size it. Give one clause on why, as general as you like: deadlines, family things, not sleeping well.
  3. Close it. Say you're coping, or say what would help. This tells the other person whether to ask more.

Examples:

  • To a coworker: "Honestly, a heavy one. Two deadlines landed on the same day. I'll be fine by Friday."
  • To your manager: "Stretched, to be honest. I can't finish both the audit and the client deck by Thursday. Can we move one?"
  • To a friend: "Rough, actually. Work's been a lot and I'm not sleeping much. Can I call you tonight?"
  • When it's personal and you'd rather not say: "It's been a hard week outside work, but I'm managing. Thanks for asking."

Replies that ask back

A plain "And you?" is fine. A specific question is better, because it shows you remember something about their week.

  • "Good, thanks. How's yours? Did the move go okay?"
  • "Can't complain. How's the new team treating you?"
  • "Not bad so far. How about you? Is the big presentation this week or next?"

The question changes with the day. On Monday people ask "How was your weekend?" instead, and on a Saturday it becomes "How is your weekend going?".

Which tense: "is going", "has been" or "was"?

QuestionTenseWhen it's usually askedStart your reply with
How is your week going?Present continuousTuesday to Thursday, with the week in progressIt's going…
How has your week been?Present perfectWednesday to Friday, looking back at the part that's doneIt's been…
How was your week?Past simpleFriday evening, the weekend, or about last weekIt was…

A few details:

  • "How's your week been?" is short for "How has your week been?" Here "How's" means "How has", not "How is".
  • "So far", as in "How has your week been so far?", only stresses that the week isn't over. It doesn't change your answer.
  • "Hows your week" is quick texting for the same question, with the apostrophe and "going" dropped.
  • "How was the week?" means the same as "How was your week?", a little less personal.

People mix these freely, and nobody minds if your reply uses a different tense. Matching the question simply sounds most natural.

Questions people ask

What does "How is your week going?" mean?

It's a friendly check-in asked while the week is still in progress. At work it's usually a polite opener before the real topic. From a friend or a date it's an invitation to talk.

How do I reply to "How is your week going?" in an email?

Write one sentence straight after the greeting, such as "Busy but productive, thanks for asking." Then start a new paragraph and get to the subject of the email.

Do I have to answer "I hope your week is going well"?

No. It's a wish, not a question. A short thanks is friendly, and going straight to business is also fine.

Is it "How is your week going?" or "How has your week been?"

Both are correct. "How is your week going?" treats the week as in progress. "How has your week been?" looks back at the days that have passed. The answers are almost the same.

What do I say if my week is going badly?

Name it, give one general reason and close it: "Honestly, a heavy one. Two deadlines landed on the same day. I'll be fine by Friday."

How do I answer "How's your week going?" from someone I like?

Give one real detail and leave an opening for a plan: "Fine, nothing special. I'm counting down to Friday. Are you around this weekend?"
