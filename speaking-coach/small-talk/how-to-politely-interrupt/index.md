# How to Politely Interrupt Someone Who Won't Stop Talking

To interrupt politely, wait for a breath or the end of a sentence, say their name, acknowledge their point in a few words, then make yours: "Sam, that's a really good point about the budget. Can I add one thing?" Keep your voice calm and slightly louder, not faster. In meetings, tie the interruption to the meeting's goal or time so it isn't personal.

## Interrupting is sometimes the polite thing

Many people were taught never to interrupt. But when one person talks for ten minutes at a dinner, or takes over a meeting, waiting forever isn't polite. It leaves everyone else out, wastes time, and often means the person talking doesn't realise they've gone on too long.

The goal is not to cut someone off. It's to **step in gracefully**, so the conversation becomes shared again.

## The four-part polite interruption

1. **Timing:** wait for a natural gap, like a breath, the end of a sentence or a pause to sip a drink. Even talkers have them.
2. **Their name:** saying someone's name is the gentlest way to get their attention. "Sam…"
3. **Acknowledge:** a few words that show you were listening. "That's a great point about the deadline."
4. **Your line:** a short, clear question or point. "Can I add one thing?" or "Can I ask a quick question?"

All together:

> "Sam, that's a really good point about the deadline. Can I jump in with one thing?"

That takes about four seconds. Then make your point, and make it short. A long point after an interruption looks like you just wanted the floor.

## What to do with your voice and body

How you interrupt matters as much as the words.

| Do | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Speak a little louder than your normal volume | It cuts through without sounding sharp |
| Keep your pace calm, even slow | Rushing sounds anxious and pushy |
| Lean in slightly, or raise a hand a little | Signals you're about to speak before you do |
| Make eye contact with the speaker | Shows respect, not dismissal |
| Keep going if they talk over you at first | Finish your short sentence calmly |

A small raised hand, or an "Mm, can I…" during their breath, prepares them to stop. People rarely stop instantly. You may need to say your opening line twice, calmly. If your voice tends to get lost, the [voice projection guide](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-project-your-voice/) helps.

## Lines for social situations

| Situation | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| A friend on a long story | "Wait, back up. Did she actually say that?" (an interested interruption) |
| One person dominating a group | "Jess, that's hilarious. Hang on, Tom, didn't something similar happen to you?" |
| Someone repeating the same complaint | "That sounds really frustrating. What do you think you'll do about it?" |
| You need to leave | "I'm so sorry to cut you off. I have to run, but I want to hear the end next time." |
| A topic you'd like to steer away from | "Can I change the subject for a second? I've been dying to ask about your trip." |

The first line is a useful trick. Interrupting with a question about *their* story feels like interest, not rudeness, and it gives you a turn. Inviting someone else in ("Tom, didn't something similar happen to you?") rebalances a group without singling out the talker.

If you need to move the topic on entirely, see [how to change the subject](/speaking-coach/small-talk/how-to-change-the-subject/).

## Lines for meetings

In meetings, interruptions land better when they're about the meeting, not the person.

| Situation | What to say |
| --- | --- |
| Someone running over time | "I'm going to jump in because we've got ten minutes left and three items to go." |
| A tangent | "This is a good discussion. Can we take it offline and come back to the agenda?" |
| You have a point that fits now | "Can I add to that before we move on?" |
| Someone repeating themselves | "I think we've got that point. Shall we hear from anyone who hasn't spoken yet?" |
| You were cut off | "I'd like to finish my thought, then I'm keen to hear yours." |
| You're running the meeting | "Thanks, Raj. I'm going to pause you there so we hear from everyone." |

If you're the one chairing, interrupting is part of your job. People are usually grateful when someone keeps a meeting on track. If you're new or quiet, the [speaking up in meetings guide](/speaking-coach/guides/how-to-speak-up-in-meetings/) covers how to get your first words in.

## When someone interrupts you back

If you're cut off while making your point:

- **Keep going calmly** for one more sentence, a little louder: "Let me just finish this point."
- **Name it lightly:** "Hang on, I wasn't quite done."
- **Come back to it:** "Going back to what I was saying before…"

You don't need to be aggressive. A calm, steady voice is usually enough.

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking over them at full speed | Sounds like a fight for the floor | Wait for a breath, then use their name |
| No acknowledgement | They feel dismissed | A few words about their point first |
| A long speech once you're in | Looks like you only wanted to talk | One short point, then hand it back |
| Sighing, eye-rolling, checking your phone | Passive-aggressive and noticed | Use words, kindly |
| Apologising over and over | Undermines your point | One "sorry to jump in" at most |

## Questions people ask

### How do you politely interrupt someone?

Wait for a breath, say their name, acknowledge their point briefly, then ask to add something: "Sam, good point. Can I add one thing?"

### Is it rude to interrupt someone who talks too much?

Not if you do it kindly. Letting one person dominate leaves others out. A polite interruption makes the conversation shared again.

### How do you interrupt in a meeting without being rude?

Tie it to the meeting: time, agenda or hearing from others. "We've got ten minutes left, so I'm going to jump in."

### What do I say if someone keeps interrupting me?

Keep going calmly for a sentence, then say "Let me just finish this point." A steady voice works better than a loud one.

### How do I end a conversation with someone who won't stop talking?

Wait for a breath, use their name, and give a warm reason to leave: "I have to go, but it was great catching up."
