How to Talk to Your Crush Without Freezing Up (What to Say)

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Start with something you both share right now, like the class, the shift or the event, then ask one question about them and listen for a detail to follow up on. Keep the first conversation short and light, and end it while it's still going well. Showing interest is mostly remembering what they said and coming back to talk again.

Why you freeze, and why it matters less than you think

With most people, small talk is easy because nothing rides on it. With someone you like, every word suddenly feels like a test. Your mind races, your voice speeds up, and the line you planned disappears.

Two things help. First, the other person can't hear your heartbeat. A slightly nervous "Hi" usually comes across as friendly, not awkward. Second, you don't need a perfect first conversation. You need a pleasant one that makes a second one easy.

Step 1: Open with the thing you share

The best opener is not a clever line. It's a comment or question about the place, task or moment you're both in. It feels natural because it is.

Where you know them fromOpener
A class or lecture"Did you understand what she meant about the second question? I lost her halfway."
Work"How's your week going? You looked busy in that meeting."
The gym or a club"How long have you been coming to this class? It's brutal."
A friend's party"How do you know Sam?"
A coffee shop or regular spot"Is that the oat latte? Is it any good? I always get the same thing."
Online, moving to talking in person"It's weird finally talking properly. You're taller than your photos suggest."

If they've already said hi to you before, use their name. It's warmer and it shows you remembered.

Step 2: Ask about them, then follow the detail

After the opener, ask one easy, open question about them:

  • "What made you sign up for this class?"
  • "Are you from around here?"
  • "What are you up to this weekend?"

Then listen for a detail and ask about that, not a brand-new topic. If they say "I'm going to my sister's wedding", ask "Oh, nice, are you in the wedding party?" That one follow-up shows interest more clearly than any compliment. The guide to keeping a conversation going has more ways to build on what someone says.

Share something about yourself too. A conversation where only one person answers questions feels like an interview, even with someone you like.

Step 3: Keep it light and short

The first real conversation should probably last somewhere between two and ten minutes. Short is fine. Ending while it's going well leaves both of you with a good feeling and a reason to talk again.

Good light topics with a crush:

  • Plans for the weekend or an upcoming holiday.
  • Food, coffee, a new place nearby.
  • Something funny or annoying about the shared setting (the teacher, the queue, the weather).
  • Shows, music, books, games, but ask what they like rather than testing their taste.

Topics to save for later: exes, money, heavy family stuff, and anything that feels like a big confession.

Step 4: Show interest without overdoing it

You want them to sense that you like talking to them, without dropping a big declaration too early. Signals that work:

  1. Remember details. Next time: "How was your sister's wedding?"
  2. A small, specific compliment. "You explained that really well." "That's a great jacket." Compliment a choice, not their body.
  3. Seek them out. Say hi when you arrive, not just when you happen to be next to them.
  4. Suggest a small next step. "We should get coffee after class sometime. Thursday?"

Keep the first compliment about something they chose or did. It's easier to accept, and it often leads to a story ("Oh, I found it in a charity shop in Glasgow").

Step 5: End well

How you leave matters as much as how you started. A clean, warm exit:

"I have to run, but this was fun. See you Thursday?"

"I'll let you get back to it. Good luck with the presentation tomorrow."

The second one gives you a natural follow-up next time ("How did the presentation go?"). For more exits, see how to end a conversation.

When you freeze mid-conversation

It happens. Your mind goes blank and there's a pause. What to do:

What happensWhat to say
You lose your train of thought"Sorry, I completely lost what I was saying. Anyway, what about you?"
A silence after their answerAsk about the last thing they said: "Wait, you said you climb? Indoor or outdoor?"
You said something clumsyLaugh it off: "That came out wrong. What I meant was…"
You feel your face going redIgnore it. They're less aware of it than you are. Keep talking.

Naming the slip lightly is almost always better than pretending it didn't happen. It shows confidence. The mind goes blank guide covers this in more depth.

MistakeWhy it backfiresTry instead

Agreeing with everythingYou seem to have no views of your ownShare your real opinion, kindly
Talking fast to fill silenceShows nerves and buries what you saySlow down; let a short pause sit

Practise the opening out loud

Freezing usually happens at the start, so practise your first two lines and one follow-up out loud until they come easily. Questions people ask

What do I say to my crush to start a conversation?

Comment on or ask about something you share right now: the class, the event, the work. "Did that lecture make sense to you?" is enough to start.

How do I show my crush I'm interested without being obvious?

Remember what they tell you, ask about it next time, say hi first, and give a small specific compliment. Then suggest one low-key plan.

What if my crush gives short answers?

They may be shy, busy or not interested. Try one more open question and share something about yourself. If answers stay short, end politely and try another day.

How long should the first conversation be?

A few minutes is plenty. End it while it's going well so you both look forward to the next one.

Should I text or talk in person?

If you see them in person, start there. Text works well afterwards to follow up on something you talked about.
