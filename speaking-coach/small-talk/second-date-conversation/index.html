What to Talk About on a Second Date (Going Deeper)

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

On a second date, skip the job-and-hometown basics you covered last time and build on them: call back to something they told you on date one, then ask questions one layer deeper, about why they chose things, what they're looking forward to and what matters to them. Share at the same depth they do, and if it's going well, float a light plan for next time.

What changes on a second date

The first date answered "Is this person normal and fun to talk to?" The second date asks "Do I actually like who they are?" That means less interviewing, more real conversation, and a little more of yourself.

You've already done the hard part. You have material: everything they told you last time.

Start with a callback

The strongest opener on a second date is something they mentioned on the first. It shows you listened, and it skips the awkward warm-up.

"Did you end up trying that ramen place you were talking about?"

"How did the big presentation go? You were dreading it last week."

"I've been thinking about your story about the camping trip. Did you ever go back?"

If you can't remember much, be honest and curious: "Remind me, what was the thing your sister was doing in Spain?" That's still a callback.

Questions one layer deeper

Go from the what to the why and the how it felt. Here's how first-date questions grow on a second date:

First date askedSecond date asks
"What do you do?""What made you go into that? Is it what you expected?"
"Where did you grow up?""What was it like growing up there? Would you ever move back?"
"Do you have siblings?""Are you close with your family? Who are you most like?"
"What do you do for fun?""What's something you got into recently that surprised you?"
"Have you travelled much?""What's a trip that changed how you see something?"
"Do you like your job?""If money didn't matter, what would you spend your days on?"

You don't need to ask all of these. Pick two or three, and follow the answers with your own follow-up questions.

Share at the same depth

Going deeper only works if both people do it. If they tell you something real, offer something similar back:

"That makes sense. I almost studied architecture, actually, but I got scared off by the maths. I still wonder about it sometimes."

Keep it balanced. A second date isn't the time for your hardest life stories or a full relationship history. Think "honest and a bit personal", not "therapy session".

Topics that work well on a second date

  • What they're looking forward to: trips, projects, the next few months.
  • Small opinions and tastes: favourite comfort food, the best book they've read this year, morning person or night person.
  • Stories: "What's the most spontaneous thing you've done?" Stories reveal more than facts.
  • What they're like with friends: "What would your friends say you're like on a holiday?"
  • Light values: what they care about in friends, work or how they spend weekends.

Talking about a next time

If it's going well, a light plan near the end makes the date feel like it's going somewhere, without pressure:

"You said you've never been to the night market. Want to go next weekend?"

"I'd like to do this again. Are you free sometime next week?"

If you're not sure, it's fine to end warmly without a plan: "I had a really nice time tonight." Clarity can come by message later.

Questions people ask

What do you talk about on a second date?

Start with something from the first date, then ask questions that go one layer deeper: why they chose their work, what they're looking forward to, stories that reveal what they're like.

How deep should a second date conversation go?

A bit more personal than the first date, but not heavy. Share honest preferences and stories, and save difficult history for later.

Is it okay to talk about exes on a second date?

A brief mention is fine if it comes up, but don't dwell, compare or go into detail.

Should I bring up what I'm looking for on a second date?

A light, honest line is fine if it comes up, such as "I'm open to something serious, but no rush." A long conversation about expectations can wait.

How do I ask for a third date?

Mention something specific to do together: "You said you love jazz. There's a gig on Friday, want to go?" It feels natural, not pushy.
