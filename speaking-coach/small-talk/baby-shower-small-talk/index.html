What to Talk About at a Baby Shower When You Barely Know Anyone

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

At a shower where you only know the guest of honor, your easiest opener is the connection you share: "How do you know Emma?" Then ask about the other guest's own life, offer to help the host, and use the games and gifts as built-in topics. Avoid questions about birth plans, bodies, weight, and "when are you having kids?".

Why showers feel tricky

At a baby shower or bridal shower, the guest of honor is usually busy: opening gifts, greeting people, being the centre of attention. The guests are often a mix of groups who don't overlap: family, school friends, work friends, neighbors, the partner's family. You might know one person in the room, and she's unavailable.

The good news: everyone there has one thing in common, the guest of honor, and showers come with built-in activities that give you things to talk about.

The opener that always works

"Hi, I'm Lena. How do you know Emma?"

It's simple, everyone has an answer, and it often leads to a story ("We met in a terrible first-year flat share"). Then share your own connection: "I sit next to her at work. She's the reason I survived my first year there."

Follow-ups that keep it going:

  • "How long have you known her?"
  • "Did you come far today?"
  • "Have you met any of the others? I only know Emma."
  • "Have you been to many showers? I'm not sure what the games involve."

That last one is a gift for nervous guests. Admitting you're a bit lost makes it easy for someone to help you, and people like being helpful.

At a bridal shower, swap in wedding topics: "Are you in the wedding party?", "Have you seen the venue?", "How did they meet? I've only heard her version."

Questions to avoid

Showers bring up topics that can be painful for some guests in ways you can't see.

AvoidWhy

Birth horror storiesNot what an expectant parent wants to hear
"Is it planned?"Intrusive
Comments on names they've chosenThey may be sensitive about it

If kids come up naturally ("My two are with their dad today"), follow the other person's lead. Just don't be the one to ask.

Joining a group

Many guests will already be in small groups. To join one:

  1. Stand at the edge with a drink or plate, and listen for a moment.
  2. Catch a gap and add to the topic: "Sorry, I couldn't help overhearing, did you say you went to school with Emma? Was she always this organised?"
  3. Introduce yourself once you're in.

The joining a group conversation guide has more on reading the moment. If it's a big shower, look for other people standing alone. They're usually relieved when someone comes over.

Helping the host

Offering to help is one of the best ways to get comfortable when you know no one:

"Can I do anything to help? I'm happy to refill drinks or take photos."

It gives you a job, a reason to talk to people ("Can I get you a top-up?"), and the host will be grateful. Taking photos during gift opening is particularly useful, and the guest of honor often loves having them later.

Getting through the games

Shower games are often a bit silly, and that's the point. They're designed to get strangers talking. Lean into it:

  • Team up with whoever's next to you: "Want to be partners? I'm terrible at guessing baby food."
  • Laugh at yourself: if you guess the bump size wildly wrong, enjoy it.
  • Use the game as a topic afterwards: "I can't believe you got the jar of sweets exactly right."

If you'd really rather not play, it's fine to say "I'll cheer you on from here" and help with photos instead.

Talking to the guest of honor

You'll get a few minutes with them. Make it warm and short, since many people want their attention:

"You look so happy. I'm really excited for you. Is there anything I can do in the first few weeks? I'm good at batch-cooking."

A specific offer of help is often the most valued thing a guest can give, especially a new parent.

Questions people ask

What do you talk about at a baby shower?

How you each know the guest of honor, the food, gifts and games, and other guests' own lives: work, plans, where they live.

What should you not say at a baby shower?

Avoid asking others about having kids, commenting on the mother's body, sharing birth horror stories, and asking whether the pregnancy was planned.

How do I start a conversation with strangers at a shower?

"Hi, I'm Lena. How do you know Emma?" works every time, because everyone there shares that connection.

What if I'm not into shower games?

Join a team and enjoy it lightly, or offer to take photos or help the host instead. Either way you'll have people to talk to.

How long should I stay at a baby shower?

Usually through the main parts, like gifts and any games, unless the invitation says otherwise. Say goodbye to the host and guest of honor before you go.
