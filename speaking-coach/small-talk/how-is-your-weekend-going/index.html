What "How is your weekend going?" means

The question is in the present tense, so it's asked while the weekend is still on: Saturday morning or Sunday afternoon. It's a small way of saying "I thought of you. What are you up to?"

People send it for four reasons:

  • To start a conversation. The question is the excuse to chat.
  • To find out if you're free. If your reply mentions a gap, they can suggest something.
  • To show interest. From a crush or a match, a mid-weekend message often means they'd like to be part of your weekend.
  • To be polite before a request. On a work chat, it softens the real message that comes next.

"How was your weekend?" is a different question. It comes on Monday, when the weekend is over, and has its own page.

Replies by situation

To a friend

  • "Pretty good. I slept in and now I'm pretending to clean. You?"
  • "Busy. It's my nephew's birthday, so I'm surrounded by six-year-olds. Save me."
  • "Good. I'm heading into town around four if you want to join."

To a coworker or your boss on chat

A weekend message from work is usually a lead-in. Answer in one line and make it easy for them to get to the point.

  • "Good, thanks. I hope yours is too. What do you need?"
  • "Going well, thanks. I'm out today, but I can look at it tonight."
  • "Nice and quiet, thanks. Is this about Monday's demo?"
  • "Good, thanks. I'm away from my laptop until Monday. Is it urgent?"

The last one sets a limit politely.

To someone you're dating or a match

Give them something to reply to, and leave a door open.

  • "Really good. I just tried that bakery near the station. Have you been?"
  • "It's nice, but quieter than I'd like. What are you doing tomorrow?"
  • "Good. I'm helping a friend move, so I've earned a drink later. What about you?"

If it's someone you like and haven't been out with yet, talking to your crush covers the next steps.

To a family member

  • "Good, Mum. I did the food shop and I'm about to go for a walk. How's Dad's knee?"
  • "All fine here. We're taking the dog to the beach. I'll send pictures."

When your weekend is boring

A boring weekend is still an answer. Name one small thing, or use the gap as an invitation.

  • "Very quiet, honestly. The highlight so far is a really good sandwich. Yours?"
  • "Nothing to report. Laundry and a podcast. Tell me yours is more interesting."
  • "Slow. I could easily be talked into doing something."

When it's going badly

Say that it's bad, say how bad in a few words, and say what you'd like: distraction, company or space.

  • "Not great, to be honest. The car broke down this morning. Distract me. How's yours?"
  • "Not my best. I've got a cold, so I'm in bed with tea. Nothing serious."
  • "A bit low this weekend, honestly. I'm okay. A call later would be nice."

When you're busy and want to keep it short

  • "Good but hectic. We've got people staying. I'll message you tomorrow!"
  • "Flat out today, all good though. Talk on Monday?"
  • "Good, thanks! Can't chat right now. I hope yours is going well."

A reply with no question back tells them, kindly, that you're closing the chat for now.

How to turn your reply into a plan or a longer chat

  1. Give them something to picture. A place or an activity beats a plain "good".
  2. Ask something more specific than "you?" A question about the gig or the trip they mentioned gets a real answer.
  3. Mention a gap. Saying you have nothing on tomorrow afternoon tells them you're free.
  4. Or just ask. A plan with a day and a time is easier to say yes to than "we should hang out sometime".

Example of all four in one reply:

"Pretty good. I spent the morning at the flea market. How was the gig last night? I've got nothing on tomorrow, if you fancy a coffee around two."

Does "so far" change the meaning?

No. "How's your weekend going so far?" is the same question. "So far" only points out that the weekend isn't over, so a part-way answer is what they expect. You can echo it:

  • "So far, so good. Football this morning, and a barbecue later if the rain holds off."

Other forms of the same check-in:

  • "How's the weekend going?" and "How is your weekend?" mean exactly the same.
  • "How has your weekend been?" looks back at the part that's already gone, so it usually arrives on a Sunday. Start your reply with It's been…
  • "Hows your weekend going" and "How your weekend going" are quick texting, with the apostrophe or the "is" left out. In careful writing, use "How's" or "How is".

The midweek version, "How is your week going?", works differently, because it turns up in work emails.

"How is your Saturday going?" and "How's your Sunday going?"

These narrow the question to today, so answer about today.

On a Saturday most of the weekend is still open. It's a good moment to mention tonight or tomorrow:

  • "Good. I'm about halfway through clearing out the garage. Are you doing anything tonight?"
  • "Slow start. I've only just had breakfast. What are you up to today?"

On a Sunday the mood is quieter, and replies often look ahead to Monday:

  • "Lazy, in the best way. Roast in the oven, phone on silent. How's yours?"
  • "Fine. I'm doing the boring jobs before Monday. How has your weekend been?"

Three replies that stall the chat

  • "Good, you?" gives them nothing to answer. Add one thing you're doing.
  • A long paragraph covering your whole weekend is hard to reply to by text. Send one detail and a question.
  • "Busy." on its own reads as "leave me alone". If you don't mean that, add when you'll be free.

Questions people ask

What does "How is your weekend going?" mean from a guy or a girl?

Most often it means they were thinking of you and want to talk. One message can't tell you more than that, so look at the pattern: if they ask often, reply quickly and suggest plans, the interest is probably real.

Is "How is your weekend going?" correct grammar?

Yes. It's the present continuous, used because the weekend is still in progress. "How's your weekend going?" is the same sentence with a contraction.

What is a good reply to "How's your weekend going so far?"

Echo the "so far" and add one detail: "So far, so good. Football this morning, and a barbecue later if the rain holds off."

How do I reply if I don't want a long chat?

Answer warmly and don't ask a question back: "Good, thanks! Can't chat right now. I hope yours is going well."

What is the difference between "How is your weekend going?" and "How was your weekend?"

The first is asked during the weekend, usually by message. The second is asked afterwards, mostly on Monday at work, and is answered in the past tense.
