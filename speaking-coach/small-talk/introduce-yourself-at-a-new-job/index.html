How to Introduce Yourself at a New Job: 11 Examples

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 17, 2026

Use one formula and change only the length: your name, your role, where you've come from or what you'll work on, one human detail, and an invitation to talk. Say it in 15 to 30 seconds at a team meeting and 30 to 45 seconds at an all-hands; write it in under 150 words for an email and under 80 for Slack or Teams. With senior leaders, two sentences are enough.

The formula: five parts

Every new-job introduction uses the same five parts; only the length changes.

PartExample line
Name"Hi, I'm Priya."
Role"I've joined the sales team as a data analyst."
Where you came from, or what you'll work on"I'll be taking over the weekly sales reports."
One human detail"I've just moved to Leeds."
An invitation"If you use the reports, I'd love ten minutes with you."

Say where you came from if people will wonder about your background, and what you'll work on if they need to know how you'll affect them. The invitation is the part most people skip, and it's what starts conversations later. Keep the human detail light: a move, a hobby, a pet.

Setting, length and what to include

SettingLengthWhat to include
Team meeting or stand-up15–30 seconds (35–70 words)All five parts
All-hands or town hall30–45 seconds (70–110 words)Thanks to the host, then all five parts
One-to-one at a desk10–15 secondsName, role, why you came to them, a question
Introduction emailUnder 150 wordsAll five parts, plus where to find you
Slack or Teams message40–80 wordsAll five parts, ending with something easy to reply to
Video call15–30 secondsName said slowly, role, where you're based, detail
Senior leadersTwo sentencesName, team, what you're working on
New manager meeting the teamUnder 90 secondsBackground, how you'll start, how you work, a question

Word counts assume a typical pace of 120 to 160 words per minute. Time your draft with the speech length calculator.

11 example introductions

These are made-up examples. Keep the shape and swap in your own details.

1. A team meeting or stand-up (15–30 seconds)

"Hi everyone, I'm Priya. I've joined the sales team as a data analyst, and I'll be taking over the weekly sales reports from Tom. Before this I was in logistics for four years. I've just moved to Leeds, so lunch tips are welcome. If you use the reports, I'd love ten minutes with you this week."

2. An all-hands or town hall (30–45 seconds)

Thank whoever introduced you, and slow down.

"Thanks, Sam. Hi everyone, I'm Daniel Okafor, and I've joined finance as financial controller. I spent eight years in finance at a large hospital, so I'm used to budgets that never quite stretch. Here, I'll look after month-end reporting and help teams plan next year's budgets. Outside work I coach an under-11s football team, so if I look tired on Mondays, that's why. If you own a budget, please come and say hello."

3. One-to-one at someone's desk

Keep it short; your question is the point.

"Hi, are you Marta? I'm Aisha, I've just joined HR as the advisor for the warehouse sites. Everyone says you know how the shift patterns really work. Is now a good time, or could I book 15 minutes this week?"

4. An introduction email to the team

Subject: Hello from the new product designer

Hi everyone,

I'm Lucy, and I joined the mobile team this week as a product designer. I've come from five years at a design agency, working on banking and travel apps.

Here, I'll be working on the checkout and account screens, so if you have opinions about either, I'd love to hear them.

Outside work, I'm usually walking a retired greyhound who is lazier than you'd think.

I'm in the office Tuesday to Thursday and always on Teams. Expect an invite for a short coffee this month.

Lucy

5. A Slack or Teams message

Post it in your team's channel. End with something people can answer in one line.

"Hi all, I'm Rahul, the new account executive for the north region. I spent three years selling software to schools, so education customers are my comfort zone. I'm also probably the last person alive who still prints maps. If you've worked with any of our northern customers, I'd love a quick chat this week."

6. A video call when you're remote

Camera on, unmute, wait a second, then say your name slowly.

"Hi everyone, I'm Sofia, the new QA engineer. I'm working remotely from Lisbon. I'll be testing the mobile app before each release, so I'm the one who'll ask awkward questions just before launch. If you hear meowing, that's my cat. I'll send each of you an invite for a short call."

7. Introducing yourself to senior leaders

Say who you are and what you're working on, then stop. If they ask how it's going, mention something you've enjoyed. More in small talk with senior leaders.

"Hi, I'm Grace. I joined three weeks ago as a team lead in customer support, and I'm working on cutting reply times for our business customers."

8. As a new manager meeting your team

Your team wants to know who you are, what will change and how you work.

"Hi everyone. I'm Nadia, your new manager. For four years I led a support team at a travel company, and before that I answered customer calls myself, so I know what a hard day on the queue feels like.

My first month is mostly listening. I'll book a one-to-one with each of you this week to hear what's working, what gets in your way, and what you'd change. Nothing changes until I've heard from everyone.

One thing about me: I'd always rather hear about a problem early than late. What would you like to ask me?"

9. An intern

Say how long you're there and what you study, and offer to help.

"Hi, I'm Leo, the marketing intern for the next ten weeks. I'm in my third year studying business, and I'll be helping with the social media calendar and the customer survey. If you ever need an extra pair of hands, please ask."

10. A career changer

Name your old career in one line and link it to the new one.

"Hi, I'm Hannah, the new junior UX researcher. Until this summer I was a primary school teacher, so I'm new to tech but not to explaining things to people who'd rather be somewhere else. I'll be interviewing users of the booking app, so if you know them well, I'd love your help."

11. Returning after a career break

You don't owe anyone an explanation. Mention the break in one plain clause, or not at all.

"Hi, I'm Rob, and I've joined facilities as a project coordinator. I took three years out to look after my children, and before that I spent seven years running office moves. I'll be coordinating the second-floor refurbishment, so if you sit there, I'd like to hear what you'd change."

Questions people ask

How do I introduce myself to new coworkers?

Name, role, what you'll work on, one light personal detail and an invitation. In a group, 15 to 30 seconds is enough; one to one, say it in 10 seconds, then ask about their work.

What is the best self-introduction for the first day at work?

A short, specific one that ends with an invitation: "I'm Priya, the new data analyst. I'll be running the sales reports, so tell me what you use them for."

Should I send an introduction email when I start a new job?

Ask your manager first; some prefer to send it themselves. Keep it under 150 words and send it to your own team.

How long should a self-introduction at a new job be?

15 to 30 seconds in a team meeting, 30 to 45 at an all-hands and 10 one to one. A new manager can take about a minute.
