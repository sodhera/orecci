# How to Make Small Talk at a BBQ or Cookout With New People

At a BBQ or cookout, use what's around you: the food, the grill and the games. Ask the person at the grill "What's your secret?", compliment a dish and ask who made it, or join a lawn game with "Can I get in on the next round?" Offering to help carry, serve or clean up gives you an easy way in, and outdoor parties make it simple to drift between groups.

## Why cookouts are easier than most parties

A backyard BBQ, cookout or picnic is one of the friendliest settings for small talk with strangers. People are relaxed, nobody is stuck at a fixed seat, and there's always something happening: food cooking, kids running, a game starting. Every one of those is a ready-made opener.

You don't need clever lines. You just need to use what's in front of you.

## Grill-side openers

The grill is the social centre of most cookouts. The person cooking is usually stuck there and glad of company.

| Situation | Opener |
| --- | --- |
| Someone's at the grill | "That smells amazing. What are you cooking?" |
| They seem to know what they're doing | "Okay, what's the secret? Mine always burn." |
| Charcoal vs gas | "Charcoal or gas guy? I've heard strong opinions." |
| A homemade marinade or rub | "Is that a homemade sauce? What's in it?" |
| It's taking a while | "Want me to grab you a drink while you're stuck here?" |
| You want to help | "Can I take anything over to the table for you?" |

"What's your secret?" works because almost everyone who grills has a method they're proud of. Be ready to hear about it for a few minutes.

## Food is the safest topic there is

At a cookout, most people bring something. Every dish is a conversation:

- "Who made this potato salad? It's the best one here."
- "Is this a family recipe?"
- "What did you bring? I want to make sure I try it."
- "I've never had elote before. Is it hard to make?"
- "Okay, controversial question: ketchup on a hot dog, yes or no?"

A light food debate like ketchup on hot dogs, pineapple on burgers, or the best barbecue sauce gets a group laughing fast. Keep it playful.

If you brought something, mention it lightly: "I made the pasta salad. Be honest." It gives people an easy compliment to offer and something to ask about.

## Lawn games as a way in

Cornhole, frisbee, croquet, bocce, giant Jenga, a kickabout with the kids. Games are the easiest way to meet people without having to "make conversation".

1. **Watch for a minute**, then ask: "Can I get in on the next round?"
2. **Team up with someone you don't know:** "Need a partner?"
3. **Chat during the slow moments:** "How do you know Dave?" between throws.
4. **Be a good sport:** cheer good shots on both sides, laugh at your own misses.

You'll learn more about people from 15 minutes of cornhole than from an hour of standing around. And after the game, you have a shared story: "That last throw was unbelievable."

## Moving from the food to the person

After the opener, move to one question about them:

- "How do you know the host?"
- "Do you live around here?"
- "Do you do this every summer, or is this a special occasion?"
- "Any good plans for the rest of the summer?"

Then share something of yours. A cookout pace is slow, so there's time for a proper chat. For more general topics, see [small talk topics](/speaking-coach/small-talk/small-talk-topics/).

## Helping is the best icebreaker

If you're nervous, give yourself a job. It's polite, and every task comes with a conversation.

| Task | Line it gives you |
| --- | --- |
| Carrying food to the table | "Where do you want these?" |
| Refilling drinks or ice | "Can I get you anything?" |
| Helping with cleanup | "I'll grab the plates. Where's the trash?" |
| Watching kids for a parent | "Want me to keep an eye on them while you eat?" |
| Running the music | "Any requests?" |

The host will appreciate it, and you'll talk to more people than if you'd stayed in one spot.

## Moving between groups

Outdoor parties make it easy to drift. People move between the grill, the cooler, the food table, the games and the shade, and nobody notices if you do too.

- **Use natural exits:** "I'm going to grab another plate. Good to meet you." "I'll go see if they need a fourth for cornhole."
- **Join groups at the food table or cooler**, where everyone is coming and going anyway.
- **Bring someone with you:** "Come and meet Ana, she's the one who made the brownies."

If a group seems tight, the [joining a group conversation guide](/speaking-coach/small-talk/how-to-join-a-group-conversation/) has more on timing.

## If it's your neighbor's cookout

Neighborhood BBQs are a great way to meet the people on your street. Use the event as a starting point for longer-term connections. Remember names, learn which house is whose, and follow up next time you see them: "Great cookout last weekend. Did those ribs all get eaten?" The [neighbor small talk guide](/speaking-coach/small-talk/talking-to-your-neighbors/) covers what comes next.

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Criticising someone's grilling | They're proud of it, and it's their party | Ask about their method |
| Standing by the host all day | They need to mingle too | Branch out after the first chat |
| Arriving empty-handed with no offer to help | Can look like you just came to eat | Bring something or help out |
| Heavy topics like politics | Kills the relaxed mood | Food, summer plans, games |
| Never joining a game | You miss the easiest way in | Ask for the next round |

## Practise your first few lines

If walking into a backyard full of strangers makes you freeze, rehearse a grill-side opener and a follow-up question out loud before you go. ## Questions people ask

### What do you talk about at a BBQ with strangers?

The food, the grill, the games, how you know the host, and summer plans. Food is the safest topic, and everyone has an opinion.

### How do I start a conversation at a cookout?

Go to the grill and ask "What are you cooking?" or "What's your secret?", or compliment a dish and ask who made it.

### Should I bring something to a cookout?

It's usually welcome, even if it's just drinks or a dessert. It also gives you an easy conversation starter.

### What if I don't know anyone at the BBQ?

Offer to help, join a lawn game, or stand near the grill. All three give you a reason to talk without a big introduction.

### How do I leave a conversation at an outdoor party?

Use a natural reason to move: grabbing food, joining a game or getting a drink. "Good to meet you, I'm going to grab another plate."
