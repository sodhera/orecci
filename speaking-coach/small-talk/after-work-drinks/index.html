How to Make Conversation at After-Work Drinks With Coworkers

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

At after-work drinks, start with the shared day ("Glad that week's over?") and move to life outside work within a few minutes: weekend plans, where people live, hobbies, food. You don't need to drink alcohol to join in; order anything and nobody will notice. If you don't know the group, sit next to one friendly person, ask them to introduce you, and leave after an hour if you want to.

What after-work drinks are for

After-work drinks are low-stakes. Nobody expects big conversations, and most people are tired. What they're for is seeing coworkers as people: the analyst who plays in a band, the manager with three dogs. That's what makes Monday's meeting easier.

So you don't need to be fun or funny. You need to be present for an hour and ask a few questions.

The first ten minutes

  1. Arrive with someone if you can. Walking in with a colleague solves the "where do I stand" problem.
  2. Get a drink, any drink. Having something in your hand gives you something to do.
  3. Join the nearest friendly face: "Mind if I join you?"
  4. Open with the shared day: "How was your week? Yours looked busy."
  5. Let work talk run briefly, then turn the corner.

Turning from work to everything else

Work topics come first because they're shared. That's fine. After a few minutes, use a turning question:

"Anyway, enough work. Doing anything good this weekend?"

"Where are you based? Is the commute okay?"

"What do you do when you're not answering my emails?"

"Have you been here before? Any good food nearby?"

Once someone mentions anything personal (a trip, a dog, a sport, a show), follow it with two questions. That's usually enough to carry a conversation for ten minutes. For the follow-up skill itself, see how to keep a conversation going.

If you don't drink, or don't drink much

You can go to after-work drinks and not drink alcohol. Most people won't notice, and those who do won't care.

  • Order something you like: soda and lime, a soft drink, a mocktail, tea. It looks like any other drink.
  • If someone asks, keep it short and light: "Not tonight, I've got an early start" or simply "I don't drink, but I love a good lemonade." You don't owe a reason.
  • If a round is going, join it with your own drink so you're part of the rhythm.
  • Leave before things get loud, if that suits you. The best conversations often happen in the first hour anyway.

If you don't know the group

New starters and people from other teams often feel like outsiders at drinks. A few moves help:

SituationWhat to do or say
Everyone's in one big circleStand at the edge, listen, and add one comment on the current topic
The group shares in-jokesAsk: "Okay, I need the backstory on that one"
You know one person"Could you introduce me to a few people?"
You end up aloneFind someone else on their own: "I'm newish here too, I'm Ana"
Conversation splits into pairsTurn to whoever's next to you: "We haven't met, I'm Ana"

What to avoid

MistakeWhy it backfiresInstead
Venting about your managerDrinks loosen talk, and words travelKeep complaints for friends outside work
Talking shop all eveningOthers came to unwindTurn the corner after a few minutes
Pressuring others to drinkUncomfortable for many peopleOffer, accept "no" without comment
Matching the heaviest drinkerYou may say things you regretSet a limit before you arrive
Staying silent at the edge for an hourNobody gets to know youOne question to one person is enough

How to leave

You don't need a reason, but a line makes it easy:

"I'm going to head off, but this was fun. See you Monday."

"I promised myself one drink, and I'm keeping my word. Have a great weekend."

Say goodbye to the person you were talking to, and wave to the rest. Leaving after an hour is completely normal.

Practice the turning question

The hardest part for many people is the shift from work talk to personal talk, because it feels forward. Say your turning questions out loud a few times so they come out naturally. Questions people ask

What do you talk about at happy hour with coworkers?

Start with the week, then ask about weekend plans, where they live, hobbies, food and travel. Follow anything personal they mention with a couple of questions.

Is it okay to go to after-work drinks and not drink?

Yes. Order any drink you like. If someone asks, a short "Not tonight" is enough, and you don't need to explain.

How long should I stay at after-work drinks?

An hour is plenty. Arrive early, talk to a few people, and leave when you want to.

What if I don't know anyone at work drinks?

Ask one colleague to introduce you, or find someone else on their own and introduce yourself. Asking about in-jokes is a good way in.

Should I talk about work at after-work drinks?

A little, at the start. Then move to life outside work, and avoid complaints about people.
