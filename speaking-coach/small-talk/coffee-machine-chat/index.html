What to Say at the Office Coffee Machine or Kitchen

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

At the office coffee machine, say hi, make a quick comment about the day or the machine, and ask one easy question, like 'Busy morning?' or 'Any plans for the weekend?'. Keep it to the time it takes to make your drinks, then leave with a friendly line such as 'Good luck with the rest of the day.'

The shape of a coffee machine chat

A kitchen encounter has a built-in time limit: the length of a coffee, maybe two minutes. That makes it one of the easiest kinds of small talk. You don't need a deep conversation; you need a friendly two minutes and a clean exit.

A simple shape:

  1. Greet. "Morning!" or "Hi, Jess."
  2. Comment. On the day, the machine, the queue, the weather.
  3. Ask one question. Something easy to answer in a sentence or two.
  4. Follow up once on what they say.
  5. Exit when the coffee's done.

Things to talk about in two minutes

The best topics are short and light. A good test: could they answer it while pressing buttons?

  • The machine or the kitchen: "Does anyone know how to make the milk frother work?"
  • The day or week: "Busy morning?" "Nearly Friday."
  • Weekend: before, "Any plans for the weekend?"; after, "Do anything fun at the weekend?"
  • Food: "Is the lunch place downstairs open again?"
  • Something shared at work, kept positive: "Are you going to the team drinks on Thursday?"
  • A callback: "Did your son like the climbing party?"

Avoid starting anything you can't finish in two minutes: a complaint about a manager, a big personal story, a debate. The kitchen is not the place, and people come and go.

Callbacks turn coffee chats into relationships

The kitchen is where you see the same people again and again. That makes it the perfect place to build on last time.

Tuesday: "I'm presenting to the board on Thursday. Bit nervous." Friday: "How did the board presentation go?"

Remembering one detail from last time does more for a work relationship than any opener. Over a few weeks, these small chats turn coworkers into people you actually know.

How to exit gracefully

The coffee finishing is your exit. Use it.

"Right, back to it. Good luck with the presentation." "That's me. Have a good afternoon." "Enjoy the weekend if I don't see you."

If they want to keep chatting and you need to go: "I've got a call in five minutes, but let's catch up at lunch." If they're the one who needs to go, let them: "Don't let me keep you."

When to skip the chat

Not every kitchen visit needs a conversation. Signs someone would rather not talk:

  • They're on the phone or wearing headphones.
  • They're reading something on their phone and don't look up.
  • They're clearly rushing: "Sorry, I've got a meeting."

A smile and "Morning" is enough. It's still friendly, and it doesn't put anyone on the spot.

In a hybrid or remote office

If you're only in the office some days, the kitchen is where most informal chat happens. Use it: "I'm only in on Tuesdays, so I'm trying to actually meet people. What team are you on?" In a fully remote team, the first minute of a call does the same job. See what to say at the start of a Zoom call.

Questions people ask

What do you talk about at the office coffee machine?

The day, the week, weekend plans, food and the machine itself. Keep it light and short enough to finish while your coffee's made.

How do I start a conversation with a coworker I don't know?

Introduce yourself with your name and team, and mention that you've seen them around: "Hi, I'm Dev from data. I see you here every morning but we've never met."

How do I end a conversation in the office kitchen?

Use the coffee finishing as your cue: "Right, back to it. Have a good afternoon."

Is it rude not to talk at the coffee machine?

No. A smile and a quick "Morning" is friendly enough, especially if someone looks busy.

How do I build relationships with coworkers through small talk?

Remember one thing they tell you and ask about it next time. Over a few weeks, those callbacks add up.
