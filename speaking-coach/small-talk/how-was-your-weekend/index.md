# How to Answer 'How Was Your Weekend?' and Keep It Going

Answer "How was your weekend?" with a one-word mood plus one specific detail, then ask back: "Good, actually. I finally fixed my bike and rode to the coast. How about yours?" Even a quiet weekend has a detail worth naming, like a meal, a show or a long sleep. The detail gives the other person something to ask about.

## What the question is really for

On a Monday morning, "How was your weekend?" is a ritual more than a question. Most of the time the asker wants a friendly 30-second exchange, not a full report. But it's also one of the easiest ways coworkers get to know each other, a little at a time, week after week.

"Fine, you?" "Fine." closes that door. One extra detail opens it.

## The formula: mood, detail, return

1. **Mood:** one or two words. "Really good." "Quiet." "Busy, but fun."
2. **Detail:** one specific thing. A place, a food, a person, a thing that went wrong.
3. **Return:** "How was yours?" or "Did you get up to anything?"

That's about 10 seconds. If they pick up the detail, you have a conversation. If they don't, you've still been friendly and you can both get on with the day.

## Example answers for every kind of weekend

These are examples to adapt, not scripts.

| Your weekend | Flat reply | Reply with a detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did nothing | "Nothing really." | "Gloriously lazy. I didn't leave the flat until Sunday afternoon. How was yours?" |
| Housework and chores | "Just chores." | "Very grown-up. I assembled a wardrobe and only had two screws left over." |
| Family visit | "Saw family." | "My parents came to stay. My dad reorganised my kitchen without asking. Yours?" |
| Kids' activities | "Busy with the kids." | "Two football matches and a birthday party. I'm more tired now than on Friday." |
| Something fun | "Good, went out." | "Great. We tried that new Lebanese place on the high street. Have you been?" |
| Sport or exercise | "Went for a run." | "I did my first 10k on Sunday. Slowly, but I finished." |
| Rough weekend | "Not great." | "Bit of a rough one, honestly, nothing dramatic. Glad it's Monday. How about you?" |
| Watched something | "Watched TV." | "I watched all of that new detective series. I need someone to talk about the ending with." |

The "nothing" weekend is the one people worry about, but it's the most relatable answer there is. Say it with some warmth ("gloriously lazy", "properly recharged") and it lands well.

## Turning it into a real conversation

If they react to your detail ("Oh, which Lebanese place?"), give one more line, then turn it back to them. Good follow-ups when they share their weekend:

- **Ask about the detail they gave:** "How was the concert? Who was playing?"
- **Ask how it felt, not just what happened:** "Was the drive worth it?"
- **Connect it to you, briefly:** "I've been meaning to go there. Would you go back?"
- **Remember it next week:** "Did you get the garden finished in the end?"

That last one is the quiet power move. Remembering last Monday's detail on this Monday tells a coworker you actually listened. Over a few weeks, that's how work acquaintances become people you like working with.

## Reading the moment

Not every "How was your weekend?" wants a chat.

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Passing in a corridor | Mood and detail in one breath, smile, keep walking |
| Waiting for the kettle or coffee | Full formula; two or three exchanges is ideal |
| Start of a team call | One line, then let the next person speak; see [video call small talk](/speaking-coach/small-talk/video-call-small-talk/) |
| Your manager asks | Same as anyone; keep it light and ask back |
| They look busy or stressed | Short and kind: "Good, thanks. Hope Monday's treating you okay." |

At the [coffee machine](/speaking-coach/small-talk/coffee-machine-chat/), you usually have about the time it takes for a drink to pour, which is perfect for this formula.

## What to keep to yourself

Monday small talk is light by design. A few things are better left out at work, at least until you know someone well:

- Detailed hangover stories or how much you drank.
- Dating details, arguments with your partner, or family conflict.
- Health problems, unless you'd like support and trust the person.
- Anything you'd be uncomfortable hearing repeated to your manager.

If the weekend was genuinely hard, "Bit of a tough one, but I'm okay" is honest without inviting questions. You can always tell a friend at work more later, privately.

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it falls flat | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Fine, you?" every week | Gives nothing to build on | Add one detail, even a small one |
| A five-minute replay | Monday is busy; they lose interest | One detail, then hand it back |
| Not asking back | Feels like you don't care about theirs | Always end with "How was yours?" |
| Topping their story | "You went to Paris? I went to Rome." shuts them down | Ask about theirs first |
| Complaining about work being back | Sets a gloomy tone for the week | Keep it light or neutral |

## Questions people ask

### What do you say if you did nothing at the weekend?

Own it warmly: "Gloriously lazy, I barely left the sofa." Add one small detail like a meal or a film, then ask about theirs.

### Do coworkers really care about my weekend?

Mostly they're being friendly. But the details you share add up over time, and that's how colleagues get to know you.

### How much detail should I give?

One specific thing, in one or two sentences. If they ask more, give a bit more.

### Is it rude to answer "fine" and walk away?

Not rude, but it can come across as closed if you do it every week. Adding one detail and "How was yours?" takes about five extra seconds.

### What if I don't want to share what I did?

Give a mood and a safe detail: "Quiet, good to recharge. Read a lot." You don't owe anyone the full story.
