How Was Your Weekend? 33 Replies and How to Answer

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated November 16, 2026

Answer "How was your weekend?" with a one-word mood plus one specific detail, then ask back: "Good, actually. I finally fixed my bike and rode to the coast. How about yours?" Even a quiet weekend has a detail worth naming, like a meal, a show or a long sleep. The detail gives the other person something to ask about.

What the question is really for

On a Monday morning, "How was your weekend?" is a ritual more than a question. The asker wants a friendly 30-second exchange, not a full report. But it's also one of the easiest ways coworkers get to know each other, a little at a time.

"Fine, you?" "Fine." closes that door. One extra detail opens it.

This page covers the past-tense question, asked once the weekend is over. A message on a Saturday asking "How is your weekend going?", or a midweek "How is your week going?", works differently. Each has its own page.

The formula: mood, detail, return

  1. Mood: one or two words, such as really good, quiet, or busy but fun.
  2. Detail: one specific thing. A place, a food, a person, a thing that went wrong.
  3. Return: "How was yours?" or "Did you get up to anything?"

That takes about 10 seconds. If they pick up the detail, you have a conversation. If not, you've still been friendly.

Replies by who is asking

These are examples to adapt, not scripts. Swap in your own details.

A coworker

  • "Good, actually. I finally fixed my bike and rode to the coast. How about yours?"
  • "Quiet one. I made a big curry on Sunday and that was the main event. You?"
  • "Busy. My sister moved flat and I was the van driver. Did you get up to much?"
  • "Really nice. We had friends over on Saturday and it went on far too late. How was yours?"

Your boss

The same formula, a little tidier. You can move straight on to work.

  • "Good, thanks. I got out for a long walk on Sunday, which I needed. How was yours?"
  • "Lovely, thanks. My daughter had her first swimming gala. Did you get away in the end?"
  • "Restful, which was the plan. How about you?"
  • "Good, thanks. Mostly gardening. I've read the notes for the ten o'clock, by the way."

A friend

You can be more honest here, good or bad.

  • "So good. I slept until ten both days and I regret nothing. You?"
  • "Chaotic. The boiler broke on Saturday, so I spent the weekend in three jumpers. Tell me yours was better."
  • "A bit flat, honestly. I didn't see anyone. Do you fancy doing something this week?"
  • "Great. I finally went to that climbing wall and my arms don't work today. What did you do?"

Someone you're dating

Show that you thought of them, or point at the next plan.

  • "Good, but too short. I saw my brother on Saturday and did nothing on Sunday. How was yours?"
  • "Really nice. I went to that market you told me about. You were right about the dumplings."
  • "Pretty quiet. I watched the first two episodes of the series you mentioned, and I have questions."
  • "Busy with family, so I'm glad it's Monday. Are you still free on Thursday?"

By text

Keep it to two or three short lines. End with a question if you want the chat to continue.

  • "Good thanks! My cousin's wedding was on Saturday and my feet still hurt. How was yours?"
  • "Pretty lazy, honestly. Pizza and three films. You?"
  • "Really good. I'll tell you properly when I see you. How was the trip?"
  • "Long. I worked Saturday. Free now though. Did you do anything fun?"

The "nothing" weekend is the one people worry about, but it's the most relatable answer there is. Say it warmly and it lands well.

"How was your long weekend?" and "How was your Sunday?"

After a long weekend, people expect to hear what you did with the extra day, even if the answer is nothing. Example replies:

  • "Really good. We drove up to the lakes and I didn't open my laptop for three days. Did you go away?"
  • "It went fast. I painted the hallway and it took all three days. How was yours?"

"How was your Sunday?" asks about one day, often by text on Sunday night. Answer about that day only:

  • "Slow. Lunch at my mum's, then asleep on the sofa by nine. Yours?"
  • "Good. I went for a run in the morning and did nothing useful after that. How was yours?"

Turning it into a real conversation

If they react to your detail ("Oh, which Lebanese place?"), give one more line, then turn it back to them. Good follow-ups:

  • Ask about the detail they gave: "How was the concert? Who was playing?"
  • Ask how it felt, not just what happened: "Was the drive worth it?"
  • Connect it to you, briefly: "I've been meaning to go there. Would you go back?"
  • Remember it next week: "Did you get the garden finished in the end?"

Remembering last Monday's detail tells a coworker you listened. Two things end the chat early: a five-minute replay of your weekend, and topping their story with a bigger one.

What to keep to yourself

Monday small talk is light by design. At work, leave these out until you know someone well:

  • Detailed hangover stories or how much you drank.
  • Dating details, arguments with your partner, or family conflict.
  • Health problems, unless you'd like support and trust the person.
  • Anything you'd be uncomfortable hearing repeated to your manager.

If the weekend was genuinely hard, the "rough one" reply in the table above is honest without inviting questions.

Grammar: "How did your weekend go?" or "went"?

PhraseCorrect?Why
How was your weekend?YesThe standard form. One weekend, so was.
How did your weekend go?YesMeans the same. After did, the verb stays as go.
How did your weekend went?NoDid already marks the past, so went is wrong.
How was the weekend?YesThe same question, a little less personal.
How were your weekends?Only for several"How were your weekends while I was away?"
How was your weekends?NoWas is singular and weekends is plural.

To reply, match the verb: It was… after "How was", It went… after "How did it go". And "how my weekend went" is not a question. It's part of a statement: "Let me tell you how my weekend went."

Questions people ask

What do you say if you did nothing at the weekend?

Own it warmly: "Gloriously lazy. I didn't leave the flat until Sunday afternoon." Then ask about theirs.

Is it "How did your weekend go" or "How did your weekend went"?

"How did your weekend go?" is correct. After "did", the main verb stays in its base form, so "went" is wrong.

How do you reply to "How was your weekend?" by text?

Send two or three short lines: a mood, one detail and a question back, such as "Pretty lazy, honestly. Pizza and three films. You?"

What if I don't want to share what I did?

Give a mood and one safe detail, such as a quiet weekend and a book you read. You don't owe anyone the full story.
