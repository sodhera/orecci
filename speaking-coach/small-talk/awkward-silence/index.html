What to Say When a Conversation Goes Quiet: Awkward Silence

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

When a conversation goes quiet, the easiest restart is a callback: go back to something they mentioned earlier ('You said you'd just been to Porto. How was it?'). You can also comment on what's around you or ask a fresh open question. A pause of a few seconds is normal, though; not every silence needs filling.

First, check: is it actually awkward?

Not every silence is a problem. Pauses of two or three seconds happen in every conversation. People are thinking, drinking, or deciding what to say. If you jump in instantly, you can cut off something they were about to add.

Type of silenceWhat it usually meansWhat to do
A pause of a few secondsNormal thinking timeWait; they often continue
Silence after a topic runs outThe thread is finishedRestart with a callback or new question
Silence while eating or walkingNobody needs to talkLet it be
Silence after big or sad newsSpace to take it inStay quiet, or say something simple and kind
Silence with a close friendComfortable, not awkwardEnjoy it
Silence after a joke that didn't landMild embarrassmentSmile, move on with a new topic
Long silence and they look away or at their phoneThey may want to leaveOffer an easy exit

The silence that needs a restart is the third kind: you've both run out of the last topic and are looking at each other.

Restart line 1: the callback

The strongest restart is going back to something they said earlier. It shows you were listening, and it's a topic they've already shown interest in.

"Wait, you mentioned earlier that you're training for a half marathon. When is it?" "Going back to your new flat, did the landlord ever fix the boiler?" "You said you'd just been to Porto. Did you get to the coast?"

To make this easy, notice details while they talk. Anything they mention in passing (a trip, a pet, a project, a sibling) is a future restart.

Restart line 2: the setting

Comment on something around you. It's neutral, so it works with anyone.

"This place has filled up fast. Do you come here often?" "Is that a lemon tree? In this climate?" "I think they've started the music. Do you know who's playing?"

Restart line 3: a fresh open question

If there's no callback and nothing around you, ask a new open question. Choose one that invites a story, not a yes or no.

"What's keeping you busy at the moment, outside work?" "Anything fun planned for the summer?" "What's the best thing you've watched lately?"

Restart line 4: share a thought

Questions aren't the only way back. Sharing something small gives the other person something to respond to without feeling quizzed.

"I've been trying to learn to cook Thai food, and it's going terribly." "I just realised I've lived here five years and never been to the market everyone talks about."

Restart line 5: name it, lightly

Sometimes acknowledging the pause with a smile breaks the tension better than pretending it didn't happen.

"And that's the end of everything I know about wine." "Sorry, I completely lost my train of thought. What were we saying?"

Keep it light and use it once. Doing it often draws attention to the silences.

When to let it be

Silence is fine, and even good, when:

  • You're eating, walking, driving or doing something together.
  • You're with someone you know well.
  • They've just told you something heavy. A quiet "That sounds really hard" is better than changing the subject.
  • It's a short ride, such as in a lift or taxi, and neither of you started talking.

People who seem relaxed in conversation are often just people who don't panic in pauses. Taking a breath and a sip of your drink is a perfectly good response to two seconds of quiet.

When the silence means it's time to go

If restarts keep falling flat, and they're looking around or at their phone, the conversation may have run its course. That's not a failure. End it kindly:

"Anyway, I'm going to grab some food. Really nice to meet you."

How to have fewer silences in the first place

Most dead ends come from closed replies. "Nice" and "Same" end threads; a follow-up or a share keeps them going. The method is in how to keep a conversation going. In short:

  1. Follow up on details in what they say.
  2. Share something small back.
  3. Keep a mental list of threads to return to.

Practise the restart out loud

Restart lines are hard to find in the moment, because silence makes your mind go blank. Saying two or three of them out loud beforehand means one is ready when you need it. Questions people ask

How long is an awkward silence?

There's no set length, but a pause of two or three seconds is normal in conversation. It usually only feels awkward when both people have clearly run out of things to say.

What do you say to break an awkward silence?

Go back to something they mentioned earlier, comment on your surroundings, or ask a fresh open question that invites a story.

Is silence in a conversation always bad?

No. Silence while eating or walking, with close friends, or after hard news is normal and often kind.

How do I stop my mind going blank in conversations?

Notice details while they talk so you have threads to come back to, and practise a few restart lines out loud. For speaking in general, see what to do when your mind goes blank.

What if the silence happens on a first date?

Use a callback to something from earlier in the evening, or ask a question that invites a story. A short pause on a date is normal; don't let it rattle you.
