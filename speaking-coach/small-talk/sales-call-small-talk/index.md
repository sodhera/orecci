# How Much Small Talk Before a Sales or Client Call? What to Say

Plan on about one to three minutes of small talk before a sales or client call, then less if they're clearly busy and more if they lead it. Open with something specific to them, not the weather, and move to business with a clear bridge line that confirms the time and the agenda. Let the client set the length; your job is to notice.

## How long to chat

There's no fixed number, but this rough guide works for most calls:

| Situation | Small talk | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First call with a new prospect | 1–2 minutes | They don't know you yet; respect their time |
| Regular call with a client you know | 2–4 minutes | The relationship is part of the value |
| Senior buyer or tight 15-minute slot | Under a minute | They want you to get to the point |
| They start chatting at length | Follow them | They're telling you they want it |
| Call starts late or someone's rushed | Skip it | "I know we're tight on time, so let's dive in" |

The best signal is how they answer your first question. A long, friendly answer with a question back means stay a little. A quick "Good, thanks" means move on.

## Openers that work better than the weather

Weather and "How are you?" are fine, but specific openers build rapport faster because they show you've paid attention.

**For a first call:**

> "Thanks for making time. I saw your team just opened the Leeds office. How's that going?"

> "I noticed you moved over from retail last year. Is the new role what you expected?"

**For a client you know:**

> "Last time you were about to go on holiday. Did you make it to the coast?"

> "How did the board meeting go? You were deep in prep when we spoke."

**When you don't have anything specific:**

> "How's your week shaping up?"

> "Are you working from the office or home today?"

Keep notes after each call about one personal thing they mentioned. Next time, that's your opener.

## Moving to business without sounding abrupt

The bridge is where many calls go wrong: either small talk drags, or the switch feels like a door slamming. Use a line that marks the shift and confirms the plan:

> "I'd love to hear more about that sometime. To make the most of your time, should we jump in?"

> "We've got thirty minutes. I was thinking we'd cover where things stand, then the pricing question you raised. Does that still work?"

> "Before we get going, has anything changed on your side since last week?"

The last one is especially useful. It feels like small talk but often surfaces the most important information of the call.

## What to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five minutes of chat on a 20-minute call | Eats into their time | Watch the clock; aim for one or two minutes |
| Obviously researched personal details | Can feel like surveillance | Stick to work news they've shared publicly or told you |
| Talking about yourself at length | It's their call | One sentence about you, then a question back |
| Fake enthusiasm ("Wow, amazing!") | Sounds like a script | Plain, genuine interest |
| Pitching inside the small talk | They feel ambushed | Keep a clear line between chat and business |
| Skipping hello entirely | Feels cold | Even 20 seconds of warmth helps |

## Video, phone and in-person

**Video calls** often start with people joining at different times. Use those first moments for light chat, and don't start the agenda until everyone who matters is there. See [what to say at the start of a video call](/speaking-coach/small-talk/video-call-small-talk/).

**Phone calls** have no visual cues, so pauses feel longer. Keep openers short and listen for tone. [Small talk on the phone](/speaking-coach/small-talk/phone-call-small-talk/) covers this.

**In person**, the walk from reception or getting coffee is the small talk. By the time you sit down, you can often start almost straight away.

## A simple call opening, step by step

1. **Greet by name and thank them:** "Hi Maya, thanks for making the time."
2. **One specific question** about them or their week.
3. **Listen and respond to the answer**, with one follow-up if they're chatty.
4. **Bridge:** confirm the time and the agenda.
5. **Ask what's changed** since you last spoke.
6. **Start the business**, ideally with a question rather than a monologue.

Say this opening out loud a few times before an important call, especially the bridge line. Rehearsing it in your head isn't the same as hearing yourself say it. ## Questions people ask

### Is small talk necessary on a sales call?

A little, yes. Even 30 seconds of warmth makes the call feel like a conversation instead of a transaction. But a busy client will appreciate you getting to the point.

### How do you transition from small talk to business?

Use a bridge line that names the shift and the plan: "To make the most of your time, should we jump in? I was thinking we'd cover X and Y."

### What if the client wants to keep chatting?

Let them, within reason. They're signalling they value the relationship. Watch the time and bridge gently once ten minutes remain on the agenda.

### What are good small talk questions for a client call?

Ask about something specific to them: a recent project, a team change, a trip or event they mentioned last time. "How's your week shaping up?" works when you have nothing specific.

### Should I research the client's personal life before a call?

Look at their work background and public company news. Avoid bringing up personal details you found online; let them share those themselves.
