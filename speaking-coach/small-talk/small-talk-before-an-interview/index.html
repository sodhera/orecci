What to Say in Small Talk Before a Job Interview Starts

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Before the interview, keep small talk short, warm and positive: greet the receptionist by name if you can, answer "Did you find us okay?" with a sentence and a thank-you, and ask one easy question on the walk, such as how long they've been with the company. Avoid complaints about traffic or nerves, and let the interviewer decide when the formal questions start.

Why the first two minutes matter

Before the first real question, the interviewer already has a sense of you: how you greet people, whether you seem calm, whether you're easy to talk to. Small talk isn't scored, but it sets the tone for everything that follows. The good news is that it's short and predictable. You can prepare almost all of it.

The four moments, and what to say in each

MomentTypical lengthYour goal
Reception30 secondsPolite, clear, friendly
Waiting2–10 minutesCalm; small chat only if invited
The walk to the room30–90 secondsOne easy exchange
First minute in the room30–60 secondsWarm, brief, ready to start

1. At reception

Give your name, who you're seeing and the time, in one sentence:

"Hi, I'm Daniel Okafor. I have a 10 o'clock interview with Sarah Lin."

Thank them, and use their name if they give it. Receptionists sometimes share impressions with the hiring team, and either way, being kind to everyone is the point.

2. While you wait

Sit, put your phone away or keep it on silent, and review your notes quietly. If the receptionist chats, answer warmly and briefly:

"It's a lovely building. Have you been here long?"

Don't start a long conversation if they're busy.

3. On the walk to the room

This is where most interview small talk happens. The interviewer will often ask one of three questions. Have a one-sentence answer for each, then one question back.

They askYou say
"Did you find us okay?""Yes, thanks, the directions were really clear. Is this whole floor your team?"
"How's your day going?""Good, thanks. I've been looking forward to this. How about yours?"
"Have you come far?""About forty minutes on the train, it was easy. Do most people here commute in?"

Other easy questions to ask:

"How long have you been with the company?"

"Is the team mostly in the office, or hybrid?"

"This space is great. Did you move in recently?"

4. The first minute in the room

Take the seat they offer, put your things down neatly, and wait for them to begin. A short line is enough:

"Thanks for making the time today."

If they continue chatting, follow along. When they say something like "So, let's get started," your small talk is done. The next thing will very often be Tell me about yourself, so have that ready.

Video and phone interviews

Online, small talk happens in the first 30 seconds of the call. Join a minute or two early, check your sound, and have a line ready:

"Hi, can you hear me okay? Thanks for having me."

If there's a small delay or a technical hiccup, stay relaxed and say so plainly: "Sorry, you froze for a second there, could you repeat the last part?" For more, see video interview tips.

If nerves make small talk hard

Nerves usually show in pace: you rush, or your answers come out clipped. Two things help:

  1. Prepare your three walk answers above, word for word if needed, and say them out loud the night before.
  2. Breathe out slowly before you stand up to greet them. It slows your first sentence.

Questions people ask

Should I make small talk before a job interview?

Yes, briefly. Answer friendly questions with a full sentence and ask one back. It shows you're easy to work with, but don't let it run long.

What do you say when the interviewer asks "How are you?"

"Good, thanks, I've been looking forward to this. How's your day going?" Positive, short, and it hands the conversation back.

Is it okay to talk about the weather?

Yes, if it comes up. Keep it positive or neutral: "Lovely to see the sun today." Don't complain about it.

What if the interviewer doesn't make small talk?

Some interviewers go straight to questions. Follow their lead. Smile, say thanks for having you, and start answering.

Does the receptionist's opinion matter?

It can. Some companies ask front-desk staff how candidates behaved. Be polite and friendly to everyone you meet in the building.
