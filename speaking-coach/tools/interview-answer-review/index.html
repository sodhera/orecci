How to use it

  1. Read the question in the box. Press Another prompt to move through all 12.
  2. Press Start speaking and answer out loud. Don't look at notes for the first try; you won't have them in the room.
  3. Press Stop. Check the time, the transcript and the numbers.
  4. Answer the same question again before moving on. The second answer is where the practice happens.

The questions are the ones that come up in most interviews regardless of the job. For questions specific to your role, use interview questions by role.

How long should each answer be?

Your result shows the time and the word count. Use them against these rough targets:

Question typeExamples from the listTarget lengthWords at 140 per minute
IntroductionTell me about yourself60–90 secondsabout 140–210
MotivationWhy this job, why leaving, five years30–60 secondsabout 70–140
Strengths and weaknessesGreatest strength, greatest weakness45–75 secondsabout 105–175
Story questionsChallenge, mistake, disagreement, leadership, pressure90 seconds to 2 minutesabout 210–280

These are working targets, not interview rules. If your story answers run past three minutes, the interviewer is probably waiting for the result while you're still setting the scene. The STAR method keeps the situation short and the action long.

How to read your results

What you seeWhat it usually meansTry this
Several fillers in the first sentenceYou didn't know how to startWrite down your first sentence only, and say it the same way each time
Long pauses in the middle of a storyYou lost the thread between situation and resultNote three words for the story: the problem, your action, the outcome
Pace over 170 words per minuteNerves, or rushing through a part you're unsure ofPause for a breath after the situation, before your action
The transcript repeats "we"The interviewer can't tell what you didSay "I" for your part of the work
No number or result in the transcriptThe answer ends without a payoffClose with what changed, even a small result

The check can't tell whether your example was the right one to choose or whether you answered the question asked. Read the transcript next to the question and ask yourself that directly.

What a practice round can't copy

Real interviewers follow up. "What would you do differently?" or "What was your role exactly?" is often where an answer falls apart. Speaking Coach's phone app gives you a voice interviewer who reads your CV or the job post and asks follow-ups based on what you said. Before then, have a friend read the questions and add one "why?" after each answer.

Questions people ask

Should I memorize my interview answers?

Memorize your first sentence and the key facts of each story, not whole paragraphs. Recited answers sound flat and break when the interviewer rephrases the question.

How many times should I practice each answer?

Two or three times out loud is usually enough to find a good first sentence and a natural length. Past that, practice new questions instead.

Is a low filler count enough to pass?

No. Browser speech recognition often drops "um" and "uh", and fillers are a small part of an interview. Clear examples and a direct answer matter more.

Does it work for video and phone interviews?

Yes. Phone and video interviews are spoken, so practicing out loud fits them well. Practice at the desk and with the headset you'll use on the day.

Is my answer saved anywhere?

No. Your browser turns speech into text and the counting happens on your device. Speaking Coach receives nothing.
