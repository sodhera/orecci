Small talk practice drill: answer openers out loud

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Read the opener, press start and reply out loud as if a stranger just said it to you, then press stop to see your words, pace, filler words and transcript. A reply that keeps small talk going answers the opener, adds one detail and hands a question back, in about 10 to 20 seconds.

How to use it

  1. Read the opener in the box. Imagine where you are: a party, a coffee line, the seat next to someone at a conference.
  2. Press Start speaking and reply out loud straight away. Hesitating is part of what you're practicing.
  3. Press Stop and look at the word count and transcript.
  4. Press Another prompt and keep going. Fifteen openers take about ten minutes.

Answer, add, ask

Most small talk dies because the reply closes the door. "Good, thanks" is polite but leaves the other person to do all the work. A reply that keeps it going has three parts:

  1. Answer the question honestly and briefly.
  2. Add one specific detail they can pick up on.
  3. Ask something back, ideally linked to your detail.
OpenerReply that closes itReply that keeps it going
"How's your day going?""Fine, thanks.""Better now. I spent the morning moving furniture, so sitting down feels great. What about you, have you come from work?"
"How do you know the host?""We work together.""We sit next to each other at work. She talked me into coming, and she said the food would be good. How about you?"
"Busy week?""Yeah, pretty busy.""Very. We launched something on Tuesday, so I'm running on coffee. Yours?"
"Are you from around here?""No.""No, I moved here in spring from the coast. I'm still finding the good coffee places. Any tips?"

These are examples of the pattern. Use your own real details; true ones are easier to say and to follow up on.

What to look for in your results

  • Words. A reply of roughly 25 to 50 words is about 10 to 20 seconds. Under 10 words usually means you closed the door. Over 80 usually means you've started a monologue.
  • Fillers at the start. "Um, uh, yeah, so" before your answer is normal on the first try. On the second try, start with the answer itself.
  • A question at the end. Check the transcript. If your reply has no question in it, the other person has to restart the conversation.
  • Pace. Small talk is usually relaxed. A very fast reply can sound like you want it to be over.

Tips for keeping it going

  • Give them a hook. "Moving furniture" or "launched something" are easy things to ask about. "Fine" isn't.
  • Ask open questions. "What brought you here?" goes further than "Is this your first time?"
  • Notice their details. When they mention something specific, ask about that before you change topic.
  • Have a way out ready. Knowing how you'll leave makes starting easier. See how to end a conversation politely.

When someone keeps giving short answers, the problem may not be you. See what to do with one-word answers. For practice beyond the first reply, Speaking Coach's phone app has a stranger who says hi and keeps talking, so you practice the second and third turns too.

Questions people ask

What should I say when I don't know what to talk about?

Comment on what you share right now: the place, the event, the food, the weather, the line you're both standing in. Then ask a question about it.

Is it rude to ask "What do you do?"

Not usually, but it can feel like an interview opener. "What keeps you busy these days?" invites work or anything else.

How do I sound less awkward?

Practice the first few seconds out loud. Most of the awkward feeling comes from not knowing how to start your reply, and that improves with repetition.

What if someone asks something too personal?

Give a short, friendly non-answer and turn it back to something general: "Oh, long story. How about you, have you been here before?"
