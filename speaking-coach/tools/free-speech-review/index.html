How to use it

  1. Press Start speaking and allow the microphone when your browser asks.
  2. Deliver your speech the way you will on the day: standing, at full volume, with the notes you'll really have. A toast, a class presentation, a pitch: anything works.
  3. Press Stop when you finish. Results appear under the button.
  4. Read the transcript first, then the numbers. Pick one thing to change and run it again.

If your browser can't turn speech into text, open Or paste what you said, paste a transcript and enter how many minutes it took. You'll get pace and fillers, but no pause count.

What the review measures

MeasureHow it's worked outWhat to look for
PaceWords heard, divided by the time from your first word to your lastUnder 110 words per minute is flagged as slow, over 170 as fast
Filler wordsMatches common English fillers: um, uh, like, you know, I mean, basically, actually and othersClusters, especially in your first 20 seconds
Long pausesGaps of more than two seconds where nothing new was heardPauses in the middle of a sentence, which usually mean you lost your place
Words and timeTotal words in the transcript and total recording timeWhether you fit your slot; the speech length calculator shows what fits
TranscriptWhat your browser's speech recognition heard, with fillers highlightedSentences that run on, and words that came out differently from your notes

Conversational English is commonly cited at about 120 to 160 words per minute, and presentations often land around 130 to 150. These are typical ranges, not rules: a eulogy can sit lower.

What it can't tell you

  • It doesn't judge content. It can't tell you whether your opening grabs attention, whether a joke lands or whether your point is clear.
  • It misses some hesitation sounds. Speech recognition is built to produce clean text, so "um" and "uh" are often dropped. Your real filler count is usually higher.
  • A pause isn't always bad. A two-second pause after a punchline or before your key line is deliberate. The tool counts it anyway; you decide which pauses were planned.
  • Accents and noise affect the transcript. A misheard word is the recognizer's mistake, not yours. Nothing here rates your accent.

A three-run routine

  1. Run 1, cold. Don't fix anything. Just get a baseline.
  2. Run 2, one fix. Choose the single biggest issue: your fillers before the first point, or a pace over 170. Change only that.
  3. Run 3, full delivery. Stand where you'll stand, look up from your notes, and compare the numbers with run 1.

Three runs of a five-minute speech take about 20 minutes. More in how to practice a speech.

Questions people ask

Is my speech recorded or saved?

No audio is saved, and Speaking Coach receives nothing. Your browser's own speech service turns speech into text (Google or Microsoft in Chrome and Edge, Apple in Safari), and the counting happens on your device.

How long can I talk for?

There is no set limit, but one to ten minutes works best: browser speech recognition can stop and restart during very long sessions.

Is this AI feedback?

No. It counts and measures; it doesn't interpret. AI feedback that quotes your words is in the ChatGPT app (free, English) and the phone app described above.

Why is my pace different every time?

Nerves, how well you know the material and whether you're reading all change your speed. Compare runs of the same speech, not different speeches.

Can I use it for a speech in another language?

This English page listens for English. Where the page is available in your language (see the language menu at the top), that version listens in your language and looks for its common fillers.
