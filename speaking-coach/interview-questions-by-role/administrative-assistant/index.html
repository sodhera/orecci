Administrative assistant interview questions and example answers

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Administrative assistant interviews test how you handle competing requests, double-booked calendars, confidential information and everyday office software. Answer with the system you use, not just a trait: how you decide what comes first, who you check with, what you say, and one real example that shows it working.

What the hiring manager is picturing

Most admin assistants support more than one person. The interviewer is imagining a Tuesday where three people ask you for something urgent before 10 a.m., a calendar is double-booked and someone leaves a salary spreadsheet on the printer. They want to hear calm, a system and good judgment about when to ask.

"I'm very organized" is the most common answer and the least convincing. Describe how you're organized.

Prioritizing requests from several people

Interviewers want a decision rule, plus evidence that you tell people where they stand.

  1. Sort by deadline and consequence. What breaks if this is late? A client meeting tomorrow beats a filing project.
  2. Check unclear urgency. "When do you need this by?" saves most conflicts.
  3. Escalate real clashes to the person who sets priorities, usually your manager.
  4. Tell people when to expect it. "I'll have it to you by 2."

Example answer (made-up details):

"I supported four account managers. One Monday, two asked for client packs for Tuesday morning and a third wanted a travel booking. I asked the third when she was flying; it was Thursday, so I booked it after lunch. Both packs were genuinely for Tuesday, so I did the one for the earlier meeting first and told the second manager I'd have hers by 4. I keep a shared request list so they can see where their item is without asking."

If the clash is between two managers, see what to do when two managers give conflicting tasks.

Calendar clashes

This is often asked as a scenario. Show that you don't just pick one: you find out which meeting matters more, propose an alternative and confirm.

"First I'd check which meeting has outside guests or a fixed deadline, because internal meetings are usually easier to move. If I wasn't sure, I'd ask my manager in one line: 'You're double-booked at 3 with the budget review and the vendor call. Shall I move the vendor call to Thursday at 10?' Then I'd contact the other side with two new times and update everyone's calendars once it's confirmed."

Offering a specific suggestion is what makes the answer strong. It saves your manager a decision.

Confidentiality

Admin assistants often see salaries, disciplinary letters, legal documents and personal details. Interviewers want to know you keep it to yourself even when it's tempting, and that you handle the awkward moments.

SituationWhat you do
A colleague asks if the rumors about layoffs are true"I can't talk about anything I see in my manager's work. You'd need to ask her."
You find an HR letter left on the shared printerTake it to the owner directly or put it in a sealed folder for them
Someone asks to see your manager's calendar detailsShare availability, not meeting contents
You're asked to email a file with personal dataCheck you have the right recipient and use the company's secure method

"In my last job I prepared offer letters, so I knew salaries before anyone else. A friend on the team once asked what a new hire would be earning. I said, 'You know I can't tell you that,' and changed the subject. She didn't ask again."

The tools you use

Don't list software. Name the tool and say what you did with it.

  • "I managed calendars for three directors in Outlook, including recurring meetings across two time zones."
  • "I built a simple Excel tracker for purchase orders with a column that turned red when an invoice was overdue."
  • "I set up our Teams channels and showed the team how to use shared files instead of email attachments."

If the job post lists a tool you haven't used, say what similar tool you know and how quickly you learned the last one.

Mistakes to avoid

  • "I can multitask." Every candidate says it. Give the system.
  • Pretending you never make mistakes. Pick a small scheduling error and what you now do to prevent it.
  • Gossip, even mild. Talking about a past boss's private life ends the interview.
  • A tools list with no examples.

Questions people ask

What's the difference between an administrative assistant and an executive assistant interview?

Admin assistant interviews focus on supporting a team and running office tasks. Executive assistant interviews go deeper into managing one leader's time, travel and communications. See executive assistant interview questions.

Will there be a typing or software test?

Some employers include a short test of typing, Word or Excel. Ask the recruiter beforehand so you can practice.

How do I answer "What would you do if you had no work?"

Show initiative: offer to help a colleague, tidy a shared drive, update a process document or learn a tool the team uses.

I've never had an admin title. What examples can I use?

Any role where you organized things for other people: scheduling volunteers, running a club's calendar, handling bookings in a family business.
