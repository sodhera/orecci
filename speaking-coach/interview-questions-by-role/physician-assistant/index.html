Physician assistant interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Physician assistant interviews, for a job or for PA school, ask why you chose PA over medical school, for a patient care story, how you work with supervising physicians, and how you'd handle an ethics scenario. Give a clear, positive reason for the PA role, use a real patient you cared for, and reason through ethics out loud instead of jumping to an answer.

"Why PA and not MD?"

The interviewer has heard "I didn't want the debt" and "school is shorter" many times. Those may be true, but don't lead with them. Give a reason that is about the PA role itself, backed by something you saw.

Good reasons to build on: working in a team with a physician, the chance to change specialties during your career, more time with patients in some settings, and a role you watched up close and wanted.

Example answer for a PA school applicant who worked as a medical assistant:

"I worked for two years as a medical assistant in a family practice with two physicians and two PAs. I saw the PAs carry their own patient list, then walk down the hall to talk through a tricky case with a physician. That's how I want to practise: independent day to day, with a team I can check my thinking with. I also like that PAs can move between specialties. I'm drawn to primary care now, but I want room to grow."

Never criticise physicians or medical school. You will work alongside both.

A patient care example

Pick a patient where you did more than a task. Show what you noticed, what you did, and what you learned about patients.

"As an EMT, I transported an older woman who kept saying she was fine, but she couldn't tell me what day it was. Her daughter said that was new. I made sure the ED nurse heard that it was a change from her baseline, not just confusion. It turned out to be an infection. It taught me how much the history from family matters, and that 'I'm fine' isn't always the whole story."

Working with physicians

They want to hear that you know your scope, ask for help early, and also speak up.

"I see it as a partnership. I'd manage my patients within my scope and our practice's agreements, and I'd ask early when something is outside my experience, rather than at the end of the day. If I disagreed with a plan, I'd raise it privately with my reasoning and the evidence, and if it was a safety issue, I'd say so clearly and follow the practice's process."

The ethics scenario

There often isn't one right answer. What scores is how you reason. Work through it out loud in this order:

  1. Restate the dilemma in one sentence.
  2. Name who is affected and what each person needs.
  3. Name the principles in tension, for example patient autonomy against harm to others.
  4. Say what you'd find out first before acting.
  5. Give your decision, who you'd involve, and what you'd do if it went badly.

Example answer to "A patient asks you not to tell their family about a serious diagnosis":

"This is the patient's right to privacy against the family's wish to know. First I'd make sure the patient has the capacity to decide, and ask what's behind the request. Is it fear, or family conflict? I'd respect their choice, offer to help them tell their family when they're ready, and make sure they have support. I'd document the conversation and involve the supervising physician if there was any doubt about capacity."

This is general interview preparation, not legal or clinical guidance. In a real situation, follow your program's or employer's policies and the law where you work.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Answering "why PA" with only time and money.
  • Telling a patient story where you were a bystander.
  • Jumping straight to a decision in ethics questions without showing your reasoning.
  • Pretending you'd never need help from a physician.

Say your "why PA" answer out loud until it's under 90 seconds and sounds like you, not a personal statement read aloud. The interview answer review shows your pace and filler words; Speaking Coach adds an interviewer who asks a follow-up on what you just said.

Questions people ask

What is an MMI in PA school interviews?

A multiple mini interview: short timed stations, each with a scenario or question, often on ethics or teamwork. Practice answering scenarios in two to three minutes using the steps above.

How many patient care hours should I talk about?

Don't lead with the number. Talk about what you did and learned during those hours. The number is already on your application.

How do I answer if I applied to medical school before?

Be honest and positive. Say what you learned about yourself and the PA role since, and why PA is now your clear first choice.

What questions should I ask at the end of a PA job interview?

Ask how supervision and collaboration work day to day, how new PAs are onboarded, and how many patients a PA sees per day once fully ramped up.
