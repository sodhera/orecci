Paralegal interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Paralegal interviews test whether you can be trusted with deadlines, find the right authority quickly, keep several attorneys happy at once and protect confidential information. Answer with your actual systems: how you calendar and double-check dates, how you research, how you prioritize competing requests and what you do when something sensitive lands in the wrong place.

What attorneys are really hiring

A paralegal interview is usually with a supervising attorney, a senior paralegal or an office manager. Their biggest fear is simple: a missed deadline or a confidentiality slip that hurts a client. Their second fear is a paralegal who needs everything explained twice. Your answers should show you're careful, organized and able to work without hand-holding.

Systems beat adjectives. "I'm very detail-oriented" means nothing. "I enter every deadline in the docketing system and a second person checks it" means a lot.

Deadlines

Expect at least one question on how you track them. Describe your system step by step.

  1. Capture it the same day a deadline is triggered: a court order, a served document, a filing.
  2. Calculate it carefully using the applicable rules, and note how you calculated it.
  3. Enter it in the firm's docketing or calendar system with reminders before the due date.
  4. Get a second check from another paralegal or the attorney.
  5. Review upcoming deadlines weekly with each attorney you support.

Example answer (made-up details):

"At my last firm I supported three litigators. Every Monday I sent each one a list of their deadlines for the next 30 days, color-coded by how close they were. Once I noticed an opposing party's filing had been served electronically, which changed the response date we'd calendared. I flagged it to the attorney, we corrected the date and I added a note to our checklist about service method."

Legal research

Firms want to know you can start a research assignment without wasting hours, and that you know when to stop and report back.

"I start by making sure I understand the exact question and the jurisdiction. I begin with a secondary source to get the lay of the land, then find the primary cases or statutes it cites. I check that each authority is still good law before I rely on it. If I haven't found anything useful in about an hour, I go back to the attorney with what I have and ask if I should keep going or change direction. I send a short memo: the question, the answer, the key authorities and anything uncertain."

Name the research tools you've used. If you haven't used the one the firm uses, say how you learned a previous one quickly.

Managing several attorneys

This is the classic conflict question. Don't secretly pick a winner. Make the conflict visible.

"Two partners once asked for urgent work due by 5 p.m. I knew I couldn't do both well. I told each one, 'I also have a filing due for the other partner by five. Which deadline is court-imposed?' One was a court filing; the other was a client draft that could move to the morning. The second partner agreed. If they hadn't, I'd have asked the supervising attorney to decide."

The conflicting tasks question covers the same idea for other jobs.

Confidentiality

Show habits, not just the principle.

SituationWhat you'd say you do
Talking about work outside the officeNever name clients or matters, even to family
EmailsCheck recipients twice before sending, especially with auto-complete
Papers and screensLock your screen, never leave files on a printer or desk
Documents sent to you in errorStop reading, tell the supervising attorney straight away, follow their direction
Conflict checksFlag any personal connection to a party at once

Mistakes to avoid

  • "I've never missed a deadline." Fine if true, but show the system that makes it true.
  • Giving legal advice in your examples. Paralegals support attorneys; your answers should show you know where that line is.
  • Choosing between attorneys silently. Make competing priorities visible.
  • Being vague about software. Name what you've used and how fast you learn new tools.

Questions people ask

Do I need a paralegal certificate?

It depends on the firm and the country. Many firms value certificates or degrees, but experience and strong systems count too. Check the job post.

What if I'm moving from a legal assistant role?

Show the substantive work you already do: research, drafting, case management. Lead with that, not with scheduling and filing.

How do I answer questions about my weaknesses?

Pick a real one that isn't about accuracy or confidentiality, and show what you do about it. See the greatest weakness question.

What should I ask at the end?

Ask how many attorneys you'd support, what practice areas, how deadlines are docketed and how paralegals are trained on firm systems.
