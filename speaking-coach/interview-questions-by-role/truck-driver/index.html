Truck driver interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

CDL truck driver interviews focus on safety and reliability. Expect questions about your driving and inspection record, how you plan around hours of service, what you'd do if you broke down on the road, and how you handle time away from home. Answer with specific numbers (years, miles, clean inspections) and show you'd never run illegal hours to make a delivery.

What the recruiter or safety manager wants to hear

Trucking interviews are usually with a recruiter, a terminal manager or a safety manager. They're thinking about one thing: will this driver keep the truck, the freight and the company's safety record intact? Every answer should come back to safe, legal and reliable. Specific numbers help: years driving, miles, how long since your last violation.

Your safety record

Have your record ready and say it plainly. The company will see your driving record and history anyway, so honesty is the only strategy.

Example answer for an experienced driver:

"I've driven Class A for six years, mostly regional dry van, about 600,000 miles. No preventable accidents. I had one equipment violation three years ago for a brake light I missed on my pre-trip, and since then I walk the trailer lights with the flashers on every morning. My last four roadside inspections were clean."

If you're a new CDL holder, talk about your school's training, your road test, and the habits you're building, like your pre-trip routine.

Hours of service

Expect a scenario where you're short on hours. They want to hear you'll stop, not stretch it.

For property-carrying drivers, federal rules allow up to 11 hours of driving after 10 consecutive hours off duty, within a 14-hour window. You also need a 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving, and there's a weekly limit of 60 hours in 7 days or 70 hours in 8 days. Exceptions exist, so know the ones that apply to the work you'd do.

"If I couldn't make the delivery legally, I'd stop. I'd call dispatch as early as I could see it coming, not at the last minute, so they can tell the customer or reschedule. Then I'd find safe parking and take my break. I plan my day backwards from the appointment time, so this doesn't happen often, but when traffic or a slow shipper eats my hours, I don't push it."

Don't guess at rules you're unsure of in the interview. "I'd check with safety" is a fine answer to a tricky exception.

A breakdown on the road

Show a calm, ordered response.

  1. Get off the road safely if you can, as far from traffic as possible.
  2. Turn on hazards and set out your warning triangles.
  3. Make sure you're safe, then call the company's breakdown line or dispatch.
  4. Give your exact location and describe the problem clearly.
  5. Protect the load and stay with the truck unless it's unsafe to.
  6. Log it, and keep dispatch updated until help arrives.

"I had an air line fail on the interstate at about 2am. I got onto the shoulder, put out my triangles, and called our breakdown line with my mile marker. While I waited I called dispatch so they could tell the receiver. It was three hours, but the load arrived the same day and nobody got hurt."

Time away from home

Recruiters ask this because drivers who aren't ready for it often quit early. Be honest about what you can manage, and show you've thought about it.

"I've done two to three weeks out with a few days home, and that works for my family. We video call every night, and I plan my home time in advance. If the route here is longer, I'd want to talk about how home time is scheduled."

Asking about home time is a reasonable question, not a red flag.

Interviews are often by phone. Say your answers out loud before the call so they come out short and steady; the pace checker shows how fast you talk when you're nervous.

Questions people ask

What should I bring to a truck driving interview?

Your CDL, medical card, any endorsements and a list of past employers with dates. Recruiters often need your employment history to verify it.

How do I answer if I'm a new CDL driver?

Be honest that you're new, talk about your training, and say you're ready to learn with a trainer or on a dedicated route. Many companies hire new drivers.

Will they ask about drug and alcohol testing?

Yes. Expect questions about previous tests and consent to testing. Answer directly.

What questions should I ask the recruiter?

Ask about home time, pay structure, the type of freight and equipment, how often trucks are replaced, and how breakdowns and detention time are handled.
