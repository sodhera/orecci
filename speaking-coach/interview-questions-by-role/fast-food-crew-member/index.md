# Fast food crew member interview questions and how to answer them

Fast food crew interviews are short, often ten to fifteen minutes, and the manager mainly wants to know three things: that you'll show up for the shifts you say, that you can stay quick and accurate when it's busy, and that you'll stay friendly when an order goes wrong. Answer simply and honestly, with an example from school, sport or any job.

## What the manager is deciding

For a crew job, the manager isn't comparing CVs. They're asking themselves: "Will this person turn up on Saturday, and can I put them on the register at noon?" That's why the most important answers are the simplest ones: your availability, a real example of being reliable, and a calm way to handle a mistake.

If it's your first job, say so. Then use examples from school, sports, clubs, chores, volunteering or looking after siblings. Managers hire first-timers all the time.

## Availability: be exact

"What days and hours can you work?" is often the deciding question. Have your answer ready before you walk in.

| Weak answer | Better answer |
| --- | --- |
| "I'm pretty flexible." | "Weekdays from 4 p.m. to close, and all day Saturday and Sunday." |
| "Whenever you need me." | "Any time except Tuesday evenings, when I have class." |
| "I'm not sure yet." | "Until June it's evenings and weekends. In summer I can do full days." |

Only offer hours you can really work. Saying "anytime" and then turning down shifts is the fastest way to lose the job.

## Speed and accuracy

"How would you stay fast and accurate at rush?" Speed matters, but managers worry more about wrong orders, because every remake slows the whole line.

> Example answer for a first job: "I'd read the order back to the customer before I send it, especially at the drive-thru, because fixing it then takes two seconds. I'd learn the menu and the screen in my first week so I'm not searching for buttons. And during the rush I'd focus on one order at a time instead of trying to remember five."

If you've had any job with a rush, like a café, a school event or a sports concession stand, use it as your example.

## A wrong order

"A customer says their order is wrong. What do you do?" Keep it short: apologize, fix it fast, don't argue.

> Example answer: "I'd say, 'Sorry about that, let me fix it right now,' and check the receipt with them. If it's our mistake, I'd get it remade straight away and tell the kitchen so it doesn't happen again. If they're really upset, I'd get my manager. I wouldn't argue about whose mistake it was, because it doesn't help them get their food."

Not arguing is the key phrase. Even if the customer ordered wrong, the fix is usually the same.

## Teamwork at rush

"Tell me about a time you worked in a team." Pick something short where you helped without being asked.

> Example answer: "On my school's sports day I worked the snack stand with three others. When the line got long, I noticed the person on drinks was falling behind while I had nothing to do on the till between orders, so I started pouring drinks between customers. The line moved a lot faster. I like jobs where you just jump in."

If they ask what you'd do if a coworker isn't helping during a rush, say you'd keep going, ask them directly and politely, and tell the shift lead only if it keeps happening. More on that is on [what to do if a coworker isn't doing their share](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-if-a-coworker-is-not-doing-their-share/).

## Honesty and rules

You might hear "What would you do if you saw a coworker giving away free food?" The answer is simple: you wouldn't do it yourself, and you'd tell the manager. Say it calmly; you don't need a long speech.

## Mistakes to avoid

- One-word answers. "Yes" and "No" make you sound unsure. Add one sentence.
- Saying you want the job "just for the money". It's fine to need money, but add something real: "I want my first job, and I like working fast with people."
- Looking at your phone while waiting to be interviewed.
- Arriving at a busy time and asking for the manager at the register. Ask when a quieter time is.

## How to practice

Say your availability and the wrong-order answer out loud a few times. Each answer should take 20 to 40 seconds. The free [interview answer review](/speaking-coach/tools/interview-answer-review/) shows your pace and filler words so you can hear whether you sound sure.

## Questions people ask

### What should I wear to a fast food interview?

Clean, simple clothes: a plain shirt and trousers or jeans without holes. You don't need a suit.

### What if I've never had a job?

That's normal for crew roles. Tell them you're reliable, give one example from school or home, and be clear about your hours.

### How long is a fast food interview?

Often 10 to 15 minutes, sometimes in the dining room. Some places hire on the spot or call you within a few days.

### Should I ask questions at the end?

Yes, one or two: "What does training look like?" or "What shifts do you need covered most?" Both show you're serious.
