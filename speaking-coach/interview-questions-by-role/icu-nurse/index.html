ICU nurse interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

ICU nurse interviews test critical care judgment: what you saw and did when a patient deteriorated, how you set priorities with two unstable patients, how you care for a dying patient and their family, and how you speak in family meetings. Answer with one real patient, give the numbers and drips that mattered, say who you called and when, and finish with the outcome and what you learned.

What an ICU panel is listening for

ICU interviews are often panels: the nurse manager, a charge nurse, sometimes an educator or a staff nurse. Many include scenario questions. They're checking three things: can you recognize and act on deterioration fast, can you keep two critically ill patients safe at once, and can you stay human with families on the worst day of their lives.

General nursing answers won't stand out here. Critical care detail does: the trend, the drip, the call and the result.

A deteriorating patient

Tell it in order and give the numbers that mattered.

Example answer (made-up details):

"I had a septic patient on one vasopressor whose mean arterial pressure had been holding in the high sixties. Over an hour it drifted to the high fifties, his urine output dropped and his lactate came back higher. I titrated within the order's parameters while I called the intensivist, and I gave her an SBAR: what had changed, what I'd done, and that I thought he needed a second agent and more fluid assessment. She came to the bedside, we added a second vasopressor and she ordered an echo. Within two hours his pressure was stable and his urine output picked up. My lesson from that shift is to call on the trend, not wait for the threshold."

What works: you noticed a trend, acted within your orders, called with a recommendation, and stayed until it stabilized.

Critical care priorities

A favorite scenario: two patients, both getting worse. Say your reasoning out loud.

  1. Find the most immediate threat to life. Airway and breathing before blood pressure, blood pressure before a lab value.
  2. Get help to the other patient. Ask the charge nurse or a neighboring nurse to watch them, or call the rapid response team.
  3. Communicate quickly. "I need someone in bed 4 now. Their pressure is dropping and I'm with bed 3, who's desaturating."
  4. Come back and reassess both once the first is safe.
Patient APatient BFirst move
Self-extubated, oxygen saturation fallingBlood pressure trending down on a dripAirway first; get the respiratory therapist and team to A, a colleague to B
New chest pain, ECG changesAgitated and pulling at linesChest pain assessment; ask a colleague or tech to stay with B for safety

The panel cares less about the "right" pick than about your reason and your call for help.

End-of-life care

Many candidates skip past this. ICU nurses do a lot of it, so give it real attention.

Example answer:

"I cared for an elderly woman when her family decided to move to comfort care. Before we stopped the ventilator I asked the family what mattered to them. Her grandson wanted to play her favorite music, so we did. I stopped unnecessary monitoring alarms in the room, kept her comfortable with the medications ordered, and explained what changes in her breathing might look like so nothing surprised them. I stayed close but gave them space. Her son told me afterwards that it was peaceful. I went to the debrief the unit offered and took a few minutes before my next patient."

Family meetings

You may not lead the meeting, but your role matters. Show what you contribute.

  • Before: tell the physician what the family has been asking and what they understand.
  • During: describe what you see at the bedside in plain words, and notice who looks lost.
  • After: stay with the family, answer what you can and ask "What did you hear?" to check understanding.

Line you might use with a family:

"I know that was a lot of information. What questions are coming up for you now?"

Questions people ask

Can a new graduate get an ICU job?

Some hospitals hire new grads into critical care residency programs. Use placement stories, show strong assessment habits and say you'd lean on your preceptor. See new grad nurse interview questions.

Do ICU interviews include clinical tests?

Some include scenario questions, rhythm strips or drip calculations. Ask the recruiter what to expect and review your fundamentals.

How do I talk about a patient who died?

Focus on the care you gave, how you supported the family and how you processed it afterwards. Keep details de-identified.

What should I ask at the end?

Ask about nurse-to-patient ratios, orientation length, the patient population, support after difficult deaths and how the unit handles rapid response calls.
