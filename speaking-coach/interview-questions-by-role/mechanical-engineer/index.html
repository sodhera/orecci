Mechanical engineer interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Mechanical engineer interviews ask about a design you improved, a part that failed and how you found the root cause, how you set or worked with tolerances, and how you work with manufacturing, quality and other teams. Answer with the numbers (loads, dimensions, costs, failure rates), the method you used, and what changed in the drawing or process as a result.

What the interviewer is listening for

The hiring manager is usually a lead or principal engineer. They want to know that you think from requirements to design to test, that you look for evidence before blaming a part, and that manufacturing will be glad to see your drawings. Technical depth is checked with specific questions. The spoken answers show how you work.

Speak in numbers and units. "We cut the bracket mass by 18% and kept a safety factor of 2" is the kind of line they remember.

A design you improved

Use four parts: the problem with the old design, how you measured it, what you changed, and the result in numbers.

Example answer (made-up details):

"Our pump housing used a cast bracket that cracked in about one in 200 units during vibration testing. I ran a modal analysis and found the bracket's first natural frequency was close to the motor's running speed. I added a rib and changed the fillet radius from 2 mm to 5 mm, which moved the frequency well above the operating range. We tested 40 units with no cracks, and the casting cost went up by less than a dollar."

The interviewer will almost certainly ask a follow-up, such as "How did you validate the analysis?" Have that answer ready.

Failure analysis

They're testing method. A strong answer goes through the steps in order and doesn't jump to the fix.

  1. Contain it. What you did so no more bad parts went out.
  2. Gather evidence. Fracture surfaces, batch numbers, operating conditions, test data.
  3. List possible causes. Design, material, manufacturing, assembly, use.
  4. Test each one. Rule causes out with evidence, not opinion.
  5. Fix and verify. Change the design or process, then show the failure stopped.
  6. Prevent it elsewhere. Update the design guide, the inspection plan or the supplier spec.

Example answer (made-up details):

"A shaft was failing in the field after around 8 months. The fracture surface showed beach marks, so we knew it was fatigue. We checked material certificates and hardness, which were fine, and then found the failures all started at a keyway machined with a sharp corner, though the drawing called for a radius. The supplier had changed tooling. We added the radius as a critical dimension on the drawing and the inspection plan."

Use whichever method your employer used (5 Whys, fishbone, fault tree) and name it.

Tolerances

They want to hear that you set tolerances based on function, cost and process, not by default.

Weak answerStronger answer
"I use the standard title block tolerance.""I start from what the part has to do, tighten only the features that affect fit or function, and leave the rest at the general tolerance."
"Tighter is safer.""Every tighter tolerance costs machining time and inspection. I check with manufacturing what their process holds before I specify it."
"I don't really do stack-ups.""For the hinge assembly I ran a worst-case stack-up on five parts, found we could bind, and opened up one clearance."

Working across teams

Mechanical engineers sit between design, manufacturing, quality, suppliers and sometimes electrical and software. Pick a story where another team pushed back.

"The manufacturing lead said my sheet metal part needed a bend too close to a hole. Rather than argue, I went to the press brake with him, saw the distortion, and moved the hole 3 mm. Now I send early drawings to manufacturing before the design review, not after."

That last line, what you changed in how you work, is what interviewers listen for.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Only describing the CAD model. Talk about requirements, loads and tests, not just geometry.
  • Blaming the supplier or the operator before you've shown evidence.
  • Trusting simulation without a check. Say how you validated it: a hand calculation, a test, or a comparison with a known design.
  • No numbers. Dimensions, loads, cycles and costs make your answer real.

Explaining a failure investigation clearly, step by step, is easier after you've said it out loud a few times. Speaking Coach can play an engineering manager who keeps asking for the evidence.

Questions people ask

What technical questions do mechanical engineer interviews ask?

Expect questions on statics, materials, stress, heat transfer or GD&T depending on the role, and sometimes a short design problem. The technical interview guide covers how to think out loud through them.

What if I don't have a failure investigation from work?

Use a university project or a test that didn't go to plan. Show the same steps: evidence, possible causes, testing them, and the fix.

How do I talk about a design mistake I made?

Say what the mistake was, how it was caught, what it cost, and the check you now use so it doesn't happen again. Keep it factual and short.

Should I bring drawings or models to the interview?

If you can share them without breaking confidentiality, yes. Otherwise bring a sketch or describe the design in general terms and say why you can't show the details.
