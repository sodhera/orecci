Bookkeeper interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Bookkeeper interviews ask how you reconcile bank and card accounts, how you work with small business owners who send paperwork late, how you catch and fix errors, and which accounting software you know. Answer with your monthly routine, a real error and how you traced it, and what you did in the software, not just its name.

What the interviewer is listening for

You might be interviewed by a small business owner, an accounting firm's manager or an office manager. All of them want the same things: the books balance every month, you notice when something looks wrong, you can deal with owners who are busy and disorganised, and you keep their numbers private. They'll also want to know you can start working in their software with little training.

Reconciliations

Describe your routine as steps. It shows you've done it many times.

  1. Pull the bank or card statement for the period.
  2. Match each line to a transaction in the books, using bank feeds or rules where the software has them.
  3. Investigate anything unmatched: missing entries, duplicates, wrong dates or amounts.
  4. Check that the ending balance in the books equals the statement.
  5. Note any reconciling items and follow up the ones older than a month.

When it doesn't balance, explain how you hunt:

"First I check whether the difference matches a single transaction, then whether it's divisible by 9, which often means two digits were swapped. Then I look for duplicates around the start and end of the month, where cut-off mistakes happen."

Catching an error

Pick an error with a clear amount and cause. Interviewers like stories where your routine found it, not luck.

Example answer (made-up details):

"During a monthly reconciliation for a landscaping client, the books were $1,480 higher than the bank. I found a customer payment that had been recorded twice, once from the bank feed and once when the owner entered it by hand from the invoice. I deleted the manual entry, checked the previous three months for the same pattern and found one more. Then I asked the owner to stop entering payments himself and send me remittances instead."

The last part, what you changed, is what interviewers listen for. See making a mistake if they ask about an error that was yours.

Small business clients

Many small business owners see bookkeeping as a chore. Show that you make it easy for them without letting standards slip.

ProblemWhat you can say you do
Receipts arrive late"I send a short list of exactly what's missing on the 5th, not a general reminder."
Personal and business spending mixed"I flag them as 'ask owner' and clear them in a 10-minute call each month."
Owner doesn't read reports"I send three numbers with one line each: sales, profit, cash in the bank."
Year-end rush"I keep the books current monthly so year-end is a check, not a catch-up."

Example line for explaining a report:

"Your sales were up this month, but profit dropped because the new oven was recorded as a repair. I'll move it to equipment, and you'll see profit come back up."

Software

Name the programs you've used and one specific thing you did in each. "I set up bank rules in QuickBooks Online that matched about two-thirds of transactions automatically" is far stronger than a list. If you haven't used their system, say which similar one you know and how quickly you learned it.

Integrity and confidentiality

Expect a scenario: a client asks you to record something wrongly. Answer calmly.

"I'd explain that I can't record a personal holiday as a business expense, and why it could cause trouble for them later. If they insisted, I'd raise it with my manager or decline the entry and note it."

For confidentiality: say you don't discuss clients' figures with anyone outside the job, you lock your screen and you share files only through the agreed system.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Listing software with no task. Say what you set up or fixed in it.
  • "I'm very detail-oriented." Show it with your reconciliation steps instead.
  • Complaining about disorganised clients. That's the job; show how you handle it.
  • Guessing on a technical question. Say what you'd check and where.

Questions people ask

Do I need a qualification to be a bookkeeper?

It depends on the country and employer. Many roles value experience and software skills; some ask for a bookkeeping certificate. Check the job post and mention any course you're taking.

What test might a bookkeeping interview include?

Some employers give a short practical test, such as reconciling a sample statement or posting a few transactions. Work carefully and say what you'd check if you had more time.

How do I answer if I've only done bookkeeping for my own business or family?

Use it. Describe the accounts, the software and your monthly routine just as you would for a paid job, and mention any error you caught.

What's the difference between a bookkeeper and an accountant interview?

Bookkeeper interviews focus on accurate day-to-day recording, reconciliations and clients. Accountant interviews add month-end close, analysis and reporting. See the accountant interview questions for that side.
