Sales development representative interview questions and answers

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

SDR and BDR interviews test three things: can you pick up the phone to a stranger, can you hear no forty times a day and keep going, and do you really want a sales career. Expect a mock cold call, answer rejection questions with a real story from any part of your life, and explain why sales with a specific reason, not 'I like people'.

What the hiring manager is screening for

SDR is often a first job, so most candidates have no sales numbers. That's fine. Managers are listening for coachability, energy on the phone, comfort with rejection and a clear reason for wanting sales. Your answers can come from retail, a part-time job, sport, a student society or fundraising.

SDR and BDR are used almost interchangeably. Where a company separates them, SDRs often follow up inbound leads and BDRs do outbound prospecting. Ask your recruiter which this role is.

Why sales as a first role

"I'm a people person" is the answer every manager has heard. Give a reason tied to the job itself.

Example answer for a graduate (made-up details):

"I ran ticket sales for our university's spring ball, which meant calling alumni who'd never heard of me. I liked that every call was a small puzzle, and I liked that I could see my number go up each week. We sold out a week early. SDR is that every day, with coaching and a path to closing deals, which is where I want to be in two years."

What makes it work: a real moment of selling, a reason you enjoyed it and a link to the career path.

Cold calling: what they ask and how to answer

You'll be asked how you'd approach a call, then often asked to do one. A simple opening structure for a cold call:

  1. Who you are, fast. "Hi Sam, it's Priya from Northwind. I'll be quick."
  2. Why them. "I saw your team just opened a second warehouse."
  3. A problem, not a product. "Teams that grow that fast often find stock counts drift between sites."
  4. A question. "Is that something you're seeing?"
  5. A small ask. "Would 20 minutes on Thursday be worth it to see how others fixed it?"

The goal of the call is a meeting, not a sale. Saying that out loud in the interview shows you understand the role.

The mock call

The interviewer will play a prospect, usually a rushed one. They want to see you stay calm, ask a question and try for the meeting again without being pushy.

Prospect saysWhat a strong SDR says next
"I'm busy.""Understood. Thirty seconds and you can hang up on me?"
"Just send me an email.""Happy to. So it's useful, what's the one thing you'd want it to cover?"
"We already use a competitor.""Makes sense. What would you change about it if you could?"
"Not interested.""Fair enough. Is that because it's not a priority, or because it's solved?"
"Call me next quarter.""Will do. What changes next quarter?"

If you get stuck, it's fine to pause and ask a question. A two-second pause sounds far better than a rushed pitch. For a related exercise, see sell me this pen.

Handling rejection

Pick a story where you heard no many times and kept a steady method, not just "I didn't give up."

Example answer:

"Last summer I knocked on doors for a solar company. The first week I got one appointment from about 200 doors and went home deflated every day. I started tracking which streets and times worked, changed my opener to a question about their electric bill, and took ten minutes after every 20 doors to reset. By week four I was booking eight to ten appointments a week. I learned that no is about the timing or the pitch, not about me."

Questions people ask

Do I need sales experience to become an SDR?

Usually not. Show transferable proof instead: customer-facing work, fundraising, competitive sport or any target you chased and hit.

What is a good answer to "What motivates you?"

Be honest that targets and earning potential motivate you, then add learning and progression. Sales managers want people who like a visible number. See what motivates you.

How do I prepare for the mock cold call?

Learn who the company sells to and one problem they solve. Write a five-line opener, then practice it out loud against five objections until you can answer each without stopping.

What should I ask at the end of an SDR interview?

Ask how SDRs are coached, what the daily activity targets are, and how many SDRs were promoted to account executive in the last year.
