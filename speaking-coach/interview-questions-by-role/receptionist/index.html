Receptionist interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Receptionist interviews test how you greet people, what you do when the phone rings while someone is standing at the desk, how you stay polite with a rude caller and how you juggle several small tasks. The interview itself is part of the test, so answer warmly and clearly, and include the exact words you would say at the desk.

You're being interviewed as the first voice of the company

For a receptionist role, an office manager or salon owner is asking one question underneath all the others: would I be happy for this person to be the first thing every client sees and hears? That means your tone, your pace and your smile count as much as your answers. Speak a little slower than feels natural and finish your sentences.

The questions below cover office and salon front desks.

First impressions

Don't answer with "I'm friendly." Describe what you actually do in the first ten seconds.

"I look up and greet people as soon as they come through the door, even if I'm on the phone; a smile and a raised hand says 'I've seen you.' Then: 'Good morning, welcome to Lane Studio. Who are you here to see?' I use their name once I have it, offer them a seat and a drink, and tell them roughly how long they'll wait."

The detail interviewers listen for is acknowledging people immediately, even when you're busy.

The phone rings while a visitor is waiting

This is the classic front-desk scenario. A good answer protects both people.

  1. Finish your sentence with the visitor, or say, "Excuse me one moment."
  2. Answer the phone within a few rings with your standard greeting.
  3. Ask the caller to hold or take a quick message: "Can I put you on hold for one minute? I'm just helping someone at the desk."
  4. Return to the visitor, finish helping them, then go back to the caller.
  5. Don't leave a caller on hold without checking back.

Example answer (made-up details):

"At the salon I worked at, Saturdays meant a full waiting area and the phone ringing every few minutes. If I was checking someone in, I'd say, 'Sorry, one second,' answer with 'Thanks for calling Bloom, can you hold for just a moment?' and finish the check-in. If the call was a booking, I'd take it fully, because empty chairs cost the salon money. If it was a long question, I'd take a number and call back within the hour."

Rude callers

Interviewers want to hear that you stay polite, don't take it personally and know where your limit is.

Instead ofTry
"He's busy.""He's in a meeting until 3. Can I take a message so he can call you back this afternoon?"
"I can't help you.""I can't book that myself, but I'll make sure the right person has your message within the hour."
"You don't need to shout.""I want to help you. Can I get your name and number first?"
Hanging up"I'm going to end the call now. Please call back when we can talk calmly." Then tell your manager.

"A caller once insisted on being put straight through to our director and swore at me when I said she was in a meeting. I kept my voice level, said I'd make sure she got his message right after her meeting, and read his number back to him. I wrote 'urgent, upset' on the note and handed it to her when she came out. He called back to apologize later that week."

Multitasking at a front desk

Receptionists sign for deliveries, book rooms, answer emails and greet visitors in the same hour. Show the habit that keeps nothing from slipping.

"I keep one running list on paper next to the phone. Anything that can't be done right away goes on it: a callback, a room to book, a package to hand over. I clear it whenever the desk is quiet, and before I leave I check every item is crossed off or handed to someone."

Simple systems are convincing because they're believable.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Mumbling or rushing. Your speaking voice is part of the job. Slow down.
  • Saying you love being busy without saying how you cope.
  • Gossiping about past visitors or coworkers. Discretion is part of the job.
  • Forgetting the visitor in the scenario. Interviewers notice if your answer drops the person at the desk.

Questions people ask

What should I say when answering the phone in a receptionist interview scenario?

Use three parts: a greeting, the company name and an offer to help. "Good morning, Harper and Lowe, this is Sam. How can I help?" Say it at a calm pace.

What if I have no reception experience?

Use any role where you greeted or served people: retail, hospitality, volunteering at events. Focus on how you handled several people at once.

How is a medical receptionist interview different?

Medical front desks add insurance, scheduling rules and patient privacy. See medical receptionist interview questions.

What should I wear to a receptionist interview?

Dress one step smarter than the staff at that front desk. For an office that usually means business casual; for a salon, neat clothes that fit its style.
