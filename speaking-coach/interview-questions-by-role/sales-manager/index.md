# Sales manager interview questions and how to answer them

Sales manager interviews test whether you can make other people sell: how you coach reps, how accurate your forecast is, what you did with a team below quota and how you hire. Answer with your team's real numbers, one named rep or deal per story, and what changed in the team because of what you did.

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence. Updated 2026-10-22. Source: https://www.orecci.com/speaking-coach/interview-questions-by-role/sales-manager/

## What a VP of sales listens for

Hiring a sales manager is a bet that you'll raise a whole team's number. So the panel looks past your own selling and asks about the four parts of the job: coaching, forecasting, fixing a team that's behind and hiring. Your best deal story as a rep matters much less now.

Know these numbers before you walk in: team size, team quota and attainment for the last two years, how many reps hit quota, your forecast accuracy if you tracked it, and how many people you hired. Round honestly.

## Coaching reps

Avoid "I lead by example." Show that you diagnose before you coach.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "One rep had a full pipeline but closed half the rate of the team. I listened to six of his recorded calls and the pattern was clear: he demoed in the first meeting and never found out who signed. We agreed on one change for a month: no demo until he'd asked three discovery questions and named the economic buyer. I sat in on two calls a week and we reviewed one recording every Friday. His close rate came close to the team average by the end of the next quarter."

What makes it strong: you looked at evidence, picked one behavior, set a time frame and checked in.

## Forecasting

Say how you build it and how you protect it from optimism.

| Step | What you say |
| --- | --- |
| Stage definitions | "A deal is only in commit if the buyer has agreed a signing date and we've met whoever signs." |
| Weekly review | "Every Monday each rep walks me through their top five deals: what changed, what's next, what could kill it." |
| Your own judgment | "I adjust rep calls by their history. If a rep's commits usually land at 80%, I plan for that." |
| Calling it early | "If the number moves, my VP hears it from me on Monday, not at quarter end." |

If you tracked forecast accuracy, give the number. If you didn't, say how you'd start.

## A team below quota

This is the story that matters most. Show you diagnosed, acted and were honest upward.

1. **The gap.** "Halfway through the quarter we were at 60% of where we needed to be."
2. **The diagnosis.** "Pipeline was fine; deals were stalling at legal review."
3. **The action.** "I got a standard contract approved with our legal team and coached reps to send it in the first meeting."
4. **The upward message.** "I told my VP we'd likely land at 85%, not 100%, and why."
5. **The result.** "We landed at 88%, and the next quarter we beat quota because those stalled deals closed."

A miss you handled well is more convincing than a perfect record.

## Hiring salespeople

Say what you test for and how. Many managers look for coachability, curiosity and resilience. Show how your process tests them rather than just naming them.

Example line:

> "In the second interview I give a short mock call, then feedback, then ask them to run it again. How much they change after one piece of feedback tells me more than their résumé."

Add one hire that didn't work and what you changed in your process afterwards.

## The top performer who ignores the process

A favorite follow-up. The panel wants to see you hold standards without losing the rep.

> "I'd talk to them privately, explain why the CRM notes matter for the forecast, not for me, and agree the minimum I need. If they still won't, it becomes a performance conversation like with anyone else. The team watches how I treat the top rep."

## Mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking about your own deals | They're hiring a coach, not a closer | Stories about reps you developed |
| No team numbers | Looks like you're hiding them | Attainment, team size, hires |
| "We always hit quota" | Unbelievable, and teaches them nothing | A miss you managed honestly |
| "I'd fire low performers fast" | Sounds like you don't coach | Diagnose, coach with a deadline, then decide |

## Questions people ask

### How do I answer if I've never managed salespeople?

Use team lead, mentoring or onboarding experience: a new rep you ramped, a playbook you wrote. Be clear about what you haven't done yet and how you'd learn it.

### What should my 30-60-90 day plan include?

Listen first (ride along on calls, meet every rep), then fix one process, then set targets for the team. Keep it to one page. See [first 90 days](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-would-you-do-in-your-first-90-days/).

### How long should a coaching story be?

About 90 seconds: the rep's problem, your diagnosis, the one change, the result.

### Should I mention commission structures?

Only if asked or late in the process. If you have views on plan design, keep them brief and tied to behavior you wanted to change.
