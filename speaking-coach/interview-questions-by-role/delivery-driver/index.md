# Delivery driver interview questions and how to answer them

Delivery driver interviews are short and practical. Expect questions about what you'd do when your route runs late, how you'd handle a damaged package, your driving record, and how you deal with customers at the door. Keep answers brief and honest: show you drive safely, tell dispatch early when something goes wrong, and stay polite with every customer.

## What the hiring manager wants to know

Delivery interviews often take 15 minutes or less, sometimes by phone. The manager is checking four things fast: you're a safe driver, you're reliable, you'll call when something goes wrong, and customers won't complain about you. Short, direct answers work best here. Twenty to forty-five seconds per question is plenty.

## "Your route is running late. What do you do?"

The trap is saying you'd drive faster. The answer they want: stay safe, tell dispatch early, and work smart.

Example answer:

> "First I'd let dispatch know as soon as I can see I'll be late, not at the end of the day, so they can tell customers or move stops to another driver. I'd check my route for anything I can reorder, like grouping stops on the same street. But I wouldn't speed or skip safety checks to catch up. A late package is better than an accident."

## "A package is damaged. What do you do?"

Show that you follow the process and don't hide it.

1. **Don't deliver it as if nothing happened.**
2. **Take a photo** and note the damage in the scanner or app if your company uses one.
3. **Follow company policy:** some say deliver and report it, others say return it to the depot.
4. **Tell the customer honestly** if you're delivering it: "The box got damaged on the way. I've reported it, so if anything's wrong inside, you can contact customer service."

> "I'd photograph it and report it in the app straight away, then do whatever our policy says. If we deliver damaged items, I'd point out the damage to the customer so it's not a surprise. Leaving a crushed box on the doorstep and hoping no one notices just creates a bigger problem."

## Your driving record

Be honest. Employers usually check it. A minor ticket from years ago, explained calmly, rarely costs you the job; hiding it can.

| Your record | How to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Clean | "Clean record, no accidents or tickets in the last five years." |
| One old ticket | "One speeding ticket three years ago. I've had nothing since." |
| A minor accident | "One fender-bender two years ago, my fault. It made me leave more space, and I haven't had one since." |
| Something serious | Say it plainly, say what changed, and ask whether it affects eligibility for this role |

## Customer contact at the door

You're the only person from the company most customers ever see. Show you're polite, quick and calm.

> "I'd keep it short and friendly: a hello, confirm the name, hand it over. If someone's angry their package is late, I'd say, 'I'm sorry it's late. I've got it here now,' and not argue about why. If they want to complain, I'd give them the customer service number. I'd never get into a heated conversation on someone's doorstep."

Other door situations to be ready for:

- **Nobody's home, no safe place:** follow policy. Leave a card, try a neighbour if allowed, or bring it back. Don't leave it in the rain to save time.
- **A dog at the gate:** don't go in. Report it and follow policy.
- **Signature required, wrong person answers:** don't hand it over unless your rules allow it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- "I'd drive faster to make up time."
- Hiding tickets or accidents.
- Saying you'd leave packages anywhere to finish your route.
- Arguing with customers at the door.

## Questions people ask

### What do delivery driver interviews usually include?

A short conversation, a check of your licence and driving record, and sometimes a drug test or road test. Ask what the steps are when they call you.

### How do I answer if I've never driven for work?

Talk about your everyday driving, any jobs where you were on time and worked alone, and how comfortable you are with maps and route apps.

### What should I say about working in bad weather?

Say you're fine working in rain, heat and cold, and that you'd dress for it, but that you'd slow down and follow company guidance in dangerous conditions.

### Do I need to know the area?

It helps. If you do, say so. If you don't, say you'll learn it quickly and that you're comfortable using navigation apps.
