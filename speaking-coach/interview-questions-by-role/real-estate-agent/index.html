Real estate agent interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Real estate agent and brokerage interviews ask how you'll generate leads, what happened when a deal fell through, how you negotiate for a client, and whether you're ready for commission-only income. Answer with a weekly lead plan with numbers, a lost deal you learned from, a negotiation where you got your client something besides price, and a realistic budget for your first months.

What the broker is listening for

When a brokerage interviews you, they're deciding whether you'll produce enough business to be worth their training, desk and brand. When a seller interviews you, it's whether they trust you with their biggest asset. Either way, the questions come back to three things: can you find clients, can you keep a deal together, and can you survive the months before your first commission.

The brokerage interview is also your chance to interview them: training, lead sources, splits and fees. Ask.

Lead generation

Brokers hear "I'll use my sphere and social media" all the time. Give a plan with activities and numbers per week.

ActivityExample weekly targetWhy it works
Calls to your sphere (friends, family, past colleagues)25People who already know you are the easiest first conversations
Open houses for other agents' listings1–2Meets buyers who aren't working with an agent yet
Follow-ups with past leads15Many buyers take months to decide
Local content (a short video or post on your area)2Shows you know the neighbourhood
Door-knocking or notes near a new listing or sale50 doorsNeighbours often think about selling when one sells

"I'd put every contact in a CRM in my first week and call five people a day from it. I'd track conversations, appointments and signed clients so after two months I know what's working. If open houses bring buyers, I'd ask for more of them."

Use your own targets, but show you think in activities and conversion, not hope.

A deal that fell through

Every agent has one. Interviewers want to see that you kept the client, learned something and changed your process.

Example answer (made-up details):

"My buyers were three weeks from closing when the inspection found a foundation crack the seller hadn't mentioned. The seller refused to fix it or lower the price, and the deal collapsed. My clients were upset, so I met them that evening, went through the inspection report with them and helped them see it was the right call. We found another house five weeks later. Since then I push for inspections early and I warn buyers up front that this can happen, so it's less of a shock."

Negotiation

Show that negotiation is about more than price. Terms often matter as much.

  • Price: grounded in comparable sales, not opinion.
  • Closing date: a seller who needs to move fast may take a lower offer for a quick close.
  • Repairs or credits: agreed after inspection.
  • Contingencies: fewer contingencies can make an offer stronger without raising the price.

Example answer (made-up details):

"In a multiple-offer situation, my buyers couldn't go above their limit. I called the listing agent and learned the sellers needed to stay in the house for a month after closing. We offered at their limit with a free 30-day rent-back. We won against a higher offer."

The overpriced seller

"I'd show them the three most similar homes that sold in the last few months and what they sold for. Then I'd say: 'If we list at your number, we'll likely sit for weeks and have to cut the price, and buyers will wonder why. If we list here, we're likely to get more interest early.' If they still want to start higher, I'd agree a date to review the price together."

Commission income

Brokers ask this because the first commission can take months to arrive, and they want to know you'll still be working when it does. Be practical.

"I've saved enough to cover about six months of living costs, and I'll keep my part-time job for the first three months while I build my pipeline. I know deals can take months from first contact to closing, so I'm planning for that."

Mistakes to avoid

  • No numbers in your lead plan. Brokers want to hear activity targets.
  • Blaming the other side when a deal fell apart.
  • Promising sellers a price to win the listing.
  • Avoiding the money question. It shows you haven't planned.

Questions people ask

What questions should I ask a brokerage in an interview?

Ask about training for new agents, where leads come from, the commission split, desk or tech fees, and how many new agents are still with them after a year.

What if I'm newly licensed with no sales?

Talk about your network, any sales or customer service experience, and your weekly plan. Brokers hiring new agents know you have no track record; they want to see work habits.

How do I answer an ethics question about hiding a property problem?

Say you'd tell the client that known problems need to be disclosed under the rules where you work, and that you'd involve your broker if they insisted. Then move on.

How should I answer "Why real estate?"

Give a real reason, such as helping your family through a move or loving your local area, and connect it to the work: finding clients, negotiating and guiding people through a stressful process.
