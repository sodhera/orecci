# Social media manager interview questions and how to answer them

Social media manager interviews test whether you can learn from a post that flopped or caused a backlash, pick metrics that match business goals, write in a brand's voice and build a content plan someone can actually run. Answer with real posts and real numbers from your work, say what you changed because of them, and expect to critique the company's own channels.

## What hiring managers are looking for

Anyone can post. The interview is checking whether you can connect posts to business results, recover when things go wrong and sound like the brand rather than like yourself. Expect at least one practical question about their own accounts, so scroll through their channels for the last month before you go in.

Bring two or three examples with screenshots or numbers: one success, one flop and one moment you handled under pressure.

## A post that flopped, or went wrong

These are two different questions, so prepare both.

### A post that flopped

Interviewers want to see that you look at data, form a reason and change something.

Example answer (made-up details):

> "I made a carousel explaining our new subscription, and it got about a third of our usual saves. Looking at it, the first slide was a block of text with no reason to swipe. I tested the same content with a first slide that asked a question our customers often asked in DMs. That version beat our average. Now every carousel I make starts with a customer question."

### A post that caused a backlash

Here they're testing calm and judgment. Show the order you worked in.

1. **Pause scheduled posts** so nothing cheerful goes out on top of it.
2. **Understand what happened** and who's upset, and why.
3. **Escalate** to your manager or comms team, especially if it touches legal, safety or a sensitive issue.
4. **Respond** with a short, human statement, removing or correcting the post if appropriate.
5. **Review** what went wrong in the approval process.

> "A product photo we posted accidentally showed a competitor's packaging in the background, and comments started piling up. I paused our scheduled posts, told my manager and agreed with her to delete it and post a light correction within an hour. We added a second-person check on product photos."

## Metrics

Show that you choose metrics based on the goal, not on what looks good.

| Goal | Metrics that matter | Metrics that can mislead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Reach, video views, follower growth from the target audience | Total likes on one viral post |
| Engagement and community | Comments, shares, saves, replies to DMs | Raw follower count |
| Traffic and sales | Link clicks, conversions, revenue from tracked links | Impressions alone |
| Customer care | Response time, resolved issues | Number of posts published |

> "For leadership I report three numbers each month, tied to the quarter's goal. Last quarter the goal was newsletter sign-ups, so I led with sign-ups from social, then click-through rate, then saves on our how-to posts, which predicted sign-ups well."

## Brand voice

You might be asked to describe their voice, or to rewrite a post. Prepare by picking three words for their voice from their recent posts, and an example of each.

> "Your voice reads as warm, practical and slightly playful. You use short sentences and talk to one person. I'd keep that, but your replies to complaints sound more formal than your posts, so I'd bring those closer to the same voice."

That last part shows you noticed something real. Be honest but respectful; the person interviewing you may have written those posts. The [what would you improve question](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-would-you-improve-about-our-company/) covers how to critique tactfully.

## A content plan

Walk through it in steps rather than listing post ideas.

1. **Start with the goal** for the month or quarter.
2. **Set three or four content pillars** that serve it, like education, product, community, behind the scenes.
3. **Choose the platforms and formats** where the audience is.
4. **Set a realistic cadence** you can sustain and keep quality high.
5. **Plan time for reacting** to trends and comments.
6. **Review weekly** and move effort toward what works.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Talking about likes and followers only.** Connect to the business goal.
- **Having no flop story.** Everyone has posts that didn't work. Pick one you learned from.
- **Trashing their channels.** Suggest improvements as opportunities.
- **Using your personal voice everywhere.** Show you can write as the brand.

## Questions people ask

### Should I bring a portfolio?

Yes. A short deck or document with three to five examples, each with the goal, what you did and the result, is usually enough.

### How do I talk about a post that flopped without looking bad?

Keep it brief, own it and spend most of the answer on what you changed. See [how to answer the failure question](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/tell-me-about-a-time-you-failed/).

### What if I've only run my own accounts or a small business's?

Use them. Real numbers from a small account show more than vague claims about a big one.

### What should I ask at the end?

Ask who approves posts, how social results are measured, what support exists for community management and how they've handled a crisis before.
