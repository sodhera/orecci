Virtual assistant interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Virtual assistant interviews ask how you work remotely across time zones, how you manage several clients without dropping anything, which tools you use and how a client can trust you with their inbox, calendar and passwords. Answer with your working hours and response times, the system you use to track tasks for each client, and the specific security habits you follow.

What the client is listening for

A virtual assistant interview is often a 20-minute video call with a business owner or an agency recruiter. The client is about to hand you their inbox, calendar and maybe their passwords. They want to know three things: you'll respond when you say you will, you won't let tasks slip, and you'll be careful with their information. Calm, specific answers win this call.

Your setup is part of the interview. A clear camera, good audio and a quiet room show you'll be easy to work with remotely. See video interview tips.

Working across time zones

Be specific about your hours, the overlap with theirs and your response times.

Example answer (made-up details):

"I'm based in Manila, so my morning overlaps with your late afternoon in New York. I'd keep two hours of overlap each weekday, from 7 to 9 a.m. my time, for anything that needs a live answer. Outside that, I reply to messages within four working hours. I'd work through your inbox and calendar before you wake up, so you start the day with a short summary of what needs you."

Mention how you handle the occasional urgent request outside your hours, and whether that costs extra.

Managing several clients

Clients worry they'll be your lowest priority. Show a system.

ProblemWhat you can say you do
Keeping tasks separate"Each client has their own board in my task manager, with due dates."
Not mixing up accounts"I use separate browser profiles for each client."
Two urgent tasks at once"I tell both clients when they'll have it, honestly, and do the one with the harder deadline first."
Capacity"I cap my client list so every client gets the response time I promised."

Example answer (made-up details):

"I work with four clients at the moment. Every Monday I plan my week in my task manager by client and deadline, and every Friday I send each client a three-line summary: done, in progress, needs you. That Friday note is the thing clients mention most."

Tools

Name the tools you've used and one task in each. Good categories to cover:

  • Email and calendar: filtering, labels, scheduling across time zones.
  • Task management: boards, recurring tasks, sharing with the client.
  • Documents: shared drives, templates, permissions.
  • Communication: chat apps, video calls, short recorded videos.
  • Password management: a shared vault rather than passwords sent by email.

"If you use a tool I haven't worked with, I'll learn it in my own time before the first week. I learned a new CRM for one client in about two days by setting up a test pipeline."

Trust and security

This is the question that decides many VA interviews. Give habits, not reassurance.

"I'd ask you to share logins through a password manager, so I never see or store passwords in plain text, and you can remove my access any time. I use two-factor authentication on everything, I don't work from public Wi-Fi without a VPN, and I'd never share your information or use it for another client. If something I'm asked to do seems off, like a payment request from an unknown sender, I check with you directly before acting."

You can also offer a small paid trial task so they can see your work before committing.

A mistake for a client

"I once booked a call in the wrong time zone because the invite showed my local time. I apologised, rebooked it within the hour, and since then I always write both time zones in every invitation."

Short, owned, and followed by the habit you changed.

Mistakes to avoid

  • "I'm available 24/7." It sounds unsustainable and clients don't believe it.
  • Listing dozens of tools with no task attached.
  • Vague security answers. Name the password manager approach and two-factor authentication.
  • Not asking about their work. Ask what's taking most of their time right now.

The first client call sets the tone. Speaking Coach can play a busy client so you can practise answering the trust and time-zone questions clearly.

Questions people ask

How should I answer questions about my rates?

State your rate or range clearly, say what it includes, and mention how you handle extra hours or urgent tasks. Don't apologise for it.

What if I'm new to virtual assistance?

Use admin, customer service or office experience, and offer a short paid trial task. Show your systems for organising work and protecting information.

What questions should I ask the client?

Ask what they'd hand over first, how they prefer to communicate, what a good week looks like, and how they measure whether a VA is working out.

Should I have a portfolio?

It helps. A few examples, such as a cleaned-up inbox structure, a travel itinerary or a process document, with private details removed, show your work better than a list of skills.
