# Barista interview questions and how to answer them

Barista interviews are short and friendly, but they check four things: how you'd cope with a rush, what you'd do when a drink is wrong, how you'd treat regulars, and why you want to work with coffee. Keep answers to 30–45 seconds, sound warm, and show you'd fix problems quickly without fuss. No coffee experience is fine if you show you'll learn.

## What a cafe manager looks for

Most barista interviews take 10 to 20 minutes, often at a table in the cafe, sometimes with a short trial shift after. The manager can teach you to pull a shot. What they can't easily teach is staying friendly at 8am with twelve people waiting. So they listen for warmth, calm and willingness to learn.

If this is your first job, use school, sport, volunteering or helping at home. "I worked the snack stand at every home game" is a real answer.

## Handling a rush

Example answer for a first job:

> "I'd focus on one drink at a time and not panic about the length of the line. I'd keep the tickets in order, call names clearly, and say hi to people waiting so they know they've been seen. If I got behind, I'd tell whoever's on register so they can warn customers or jump in. I worked the concession stand at our school's basketball games, and halftime was basically a rush every week."

Experienced baristas can be more specific: "I batch milk for two drinks when I can, and I keep the bar wiped as I go so I'm not stuck cleaning at the end."

## Remaking a drink without fuss

*"A customer says their drink is wrong."* Managers want three things: no arguing, quick fix, and a kind word.

Example answer:

> "I'd say sorry and offer to remake it straight away, without asking whose fault it was. I'd check exactly what they wanted, like oat milk or an extra shot, so the second one's right. Then I'd make it next, not at the back of the queue, and hand it over with a 'Sorry about that, enjoy.' If it was my mistake, I'd remember it for next time."

## Regulars

Cafes run on regulars, and this question checks whether you notice people.

Example answer:

> "I'd try to learn names and orders. It feels good when someone starts your drink as you walk in. I'd ask small questions, like how the new job's going, but read whether they want to chat or just want their coffee. Some people are in a hurry, and remembering their order is the friendliest thing you can do."

The [small talk with customers guide](/speaking-coach/small-talk/small-talk-with-customers/) has easy lines for this.

## Why coffee?

You don't need to be a coffee expert. You do need a real reason.

| Weak | Stronger |
| --- | --- |
| "I need a job" | "I want a job where I'm talking to people all day" |
| "I like coffee" | "I've been making pour-overs at home and want to learn espresso properly" |
| "It seems easy" | "I like fast-paced work where you can see what you've done" |
| Nothing about this cafe | "I come here before school and I like how the staff know everyone" |

Example answer:

> "I'm a morning person and I like busy places, and I want to learn to make coffee properly. I picked this cafe because I've been coming here for a year, and the staff always seem like a team."

## The trickier questions

- **A coworker isn't helping during a rush.** Ask them directly and kindly to take something specific: "Can you take the cold drinks?" If it keeps happening, mention it to the shift lead. Don't complain to customers or other staff.
- **Describe your favorite drink.** They're testing how you'd talk to customers. "A flat white, it's smaller and stronger than a latte, with silky milk" is perfect.
- **Availability.** Be exact and honest, including weekends and early mornings. Changing it later causes more problems than a smaller offer now.

## How to prepare

1. Visit the cafe once and notice what's popular and how busy it gets.
2. Prepare three short stories: a busy time, a mistake you fixed, and a team moment.
3. Say your answers out loud, with a smile. The [interview answer review](/speaking-coach/tools/interview-answer-review/) shows your pace and filler words.
4. Know your availability before you walk in.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need barista experience?

Not for most cafes. Show you're friendly, reliable and keen to learn, and mention any customer-facing or team experience.

### Will there be a trial shift?

Many cafes do one. Ask questions, stay busy, clean without being told, and talk to customers. They're watching attitude more than latte art.

### What should I wear?

Something neat and practical, close to what the staff wear. Clean shoes you can stand in.

### How do I answer "What's your weakness?" for a cafe job?

Pick something real and small, like being slow on the register at first, and say how you're improving it. Here's the [full guide](/speaking-coach/interview-questions/what-is-your-greatest-weakness/).

### What should I ask at the end?

Ask what a typical shift looks like, how training works, and what their best baristas do well.
