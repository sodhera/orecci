Cashier interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Cashier interviews are usually short and practical: can you handle money accurately, stay friendly when the line is long, deal politely with a customer who says the price is wrong and follow the store's rules if you suspect theft. Short, honest answers with one example work best, and examples from school, volunteering or home are fine if this is your first job.

What the store manager wants to know

Cashier interviews are often 10 to 20 minutes with a store or front-end manager, sometimes as a group interview. They are not looking for a perfect speech. They want to see that you're friendly, honest, careful with money and that you'll follow store rules.

If this is your first job, that's expected. Use examples from school, sports, volunteering, babysitting or helping at home. What matters is that the example shows the same habit.

Cash handling accuracy

Say exactly how you'd avoid mistakes. Simple steps sound more convincing than "I'm good with numbers."

  1. Count your drawer at the start of the shift and report any difference right away.
  2. Say the amount out loud when a customer hands you cash: "Out of twenty."
  3. Leave the bill on the drawer edge until you've given change, so there's no confusion about what they gave you.
  4. Count change back into the customer's hand.
  5. Never mix your own money with the drawer, and never leave it open or unattended.

Example answer for a first job (made-up details):

"I haven't worked a register yet, but I ran the snack table at our school basketball games for two seasons. I counted the float at the start, kept the bills in order and counted change back to people. At the end we always checked the cash against the tally sheet, and it matched every week except once, when we were three dollars over and found someone had overpaid. I'd bring that same habit to your register."

A long line

Show that you stay friendly, keep moving and ask for help early.

"I'd keep scanning at a steady pace and greet every customer, even with a quick 'Thanks for waiting.' If the line got past four or five people, I'd call for backup the way the store does it, like a bell or a call to the manager, rather than waiting until people complain. I wouldn't rush so much that I made mistakes, because fixing one takes longer."

The detail that impresses is calling for help before customers get angry.

A customer disputing a price

The interviewer wants you to stay polite, check rather than argue and follow store policy.

"That's what the computer says.""Let me check that for you."
"You must have read the sign wrong.""Can you show me where you saw the price?"
"I can't change it.""I'll call my supervisor so we can sort it out."

"I'd say, 'Let me check that for you,' and ask where they saw the price. If I could check quickly, or someone could check the shelf, I would. If the sign was wrong, I'd follow the store's policy, which usually means a supervisor approves the change. If it was a misunderstanding, I'd explain kindly and offer to take the item off if they didn't want it."

Suspected theft

This question tests whether you'll follow the rules and stay safe. Many stores tell cashiers not to confront anyone.

"I wouldn't accuse or confront the person. I'd stay polite and normal, and tell my manager or security as soon as I could, quietly, using whatever process the store has. My job is to report what I saw, not to chase anyone."

If a friend or coworker asks you to give them a discount or ring up items for free, the answer is the same: say no and tell your manager. Interviewers sometimes ask this directly.

Mistakes to avoid

  • One-word answers. "Yes, I'm good with money" needs one sentence of proof.
  • Saying you'd chase a shoplifter. It sounds brave but goes against many store policies and isn't safe.
  • Vague availability. Know exactly which days and hours you can work before you go.
  • Speaking too fast. Nerves speed people up. Pause before each answer.

If you're nervous about your first interview, say your answers out loud a few times beforehand, especially tell me about yourself. Speaking Coach's AI interviewer can ask you these questions and follow up on what you actually say.

Questions people ask

What should I say if I have no experience?

Say it's your first job, then give an example of being responsible: handling money for a club, showing up on time for practice, helping a neighbor. Managers hiring cashiers expect beginners.

What should I wear to a cashier interview?

Clean, neat clothes a step above what cashiers wear at that store, such as plain trousers and a collared shirt or simple top.

How do I answer "Why do you want to work here?"

Give a real reason: you shop there, it's close to home or school, or you like the hours. Add one thing about yourself that fits, such as liking to talk to people.

Will they test my math?

Some stores ask you to count change or do a simple calculation. Practice counting back change from a $20 and a $50 bill before the interview.
