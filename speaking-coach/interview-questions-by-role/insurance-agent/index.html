Insurance agent interview questions and how to answer them

By the Speaking Coach team at Sodhera Intelligence · Updated October 22, 2026

Insurance agent interviews ask how you'd find prospects, how you'd explain a policy in plain words, how you'd handle objections like "it's too expensive", and where you are with licensing. Answer with a weekly prospecting plan with numbers, a 30-second plain-language explanation, and an objection you answer with a question instead of pressure.

What the agency owner is listening for

Whether it's a captive agency, an independent broker or a call-centre sales team, the person hiring you wants to know three things: you'll pick up the phone and keep picking it up, you can make a policy sound simple without misleading anyone, and you'll stick around long enough to build a book. Insurance has a lot of rejection. They're listening for whether you've thought about that.

Prospecting

A vague "I'd network and use social media" won't pass. Give a weekly plan with sources and numbers.

SourceWhat you'd doWeekly target to mention
Warm marketTell friends, family and past colleagues you've started; ask who they know10 conversations
Existing book (if the agency has one)Review clients with only one policy and call about gaps20 calls
PartnersCar dealers, mortgage brokers, real estate agents2 meetings
CommunityA local business group or a talk at a workplace1 event
ReferralsAsk every new client for one name at the end of the saleEvery sale

"I'd treat it like a numbers game for the first 90 days and track calls, conversations, quotes and sales in the CRM every day. After a month I'd know which source turns into sales and spend more time there."

Use your own estimates, and say they're estimates. The point is that you think in numbers.

Explaining a policy simply

The interviewer may ask you to explain a product to them as if they'd never bought insurance. Keep it to about 30 seconds: what it does, when it pays, what it costs, and what it doesn't cover.

Example for term life insurance (general wording to adapt to the real product):

"You pay a fixed amount each month for a set number of years, say 20. If you die during that time, your family gets a lump sum to cover things like the mortgage and school costs. If you're still alive at the end, the cover stops. It's the cheapest kind of life insurance because it only covers that window."

Then check understanding: "What questions does that bring up for you?" If you want to improve this skill, the explaining something complex page has more.

Handling objections

Interviewers often role-play this. The strongest move is a question, not a counter-argument.

ObjectionWeak replyBetter reply
"It's too expensive.""It's actually great value.""What were you hoping to spend each month? Let's see what cover fits that."
"I need to think about it.""This price won't last.""Of course. What part are you unsure about?"
"I already have cover through work.""That's not enough.""Do you know what happens to that cover if you change jobs?"
"I don't trust insurance companies."Defending the company"That's fair. What happened that made you feel that way?"

Avoid pressure tactics in your answer, even if you think they work. Many interviewers screen for them.

Rejection

"I expect most calls to end in no. I don't take it personally, because they're saying no to the timing or the product, not to me. I set a daily call target and I don't stop until I hit it, even on a bad day. I also note why each no happened, because patterns tell me what to fix in my pitch."

Licensing

Be specific about where you are: licensed in which lines, exam booked for which date, or ready to study. Ask how the agency supports new agents through it. Requirements differ by country and state, so don't guess at details you haven't checked.

Mistakes to avoid

  • "I'm a people person." Show it with a prospecting plan and a persuasion story.
  • Using jargon like "riders" and "exclusions" without explaining them.
  • Overpromising. Never suggest a policy covers more than it does.
  • Avoiding the pay question. Show you understand commission and have a plan for the ramp-up months.

Objection handling only gets smooth with practice out loud. Speaking Coach can play a sceptical prospect so you can try different replies.

Questions people ask

Do I need a license before the interview?

Not always. Many agencies hire people and sponsor their licensing. Say clearly where you are in the process and how soon you could be licensed.

What if I have no sales experience?

Use any job where you persuaded people or dealt with rejection: retail, fundraising, call centres, recruiting. Show the habits, like daily targets and follow-up.

Should I prepare for a role play?

Yes. Expect to be asked to explain a product or handle an objection. Practise your 30-second explanation and two or three objection replies out loud. The sell me this pen guide covers the same skill.

How do I answer questions about commission-only pay?

Say you understand how it works, that you've planned for the first few months financially, and ask what new agents typically earn in their first year at this agency.
